Zelensky says Kyiv is 'done' pausing attacks after Moscow kept hitting Ukrainian power plants, while accusing Washington of cutting intelligence and throttling Starlink to push it along.

The wire said: Russia accuses Ukraine of attacking oil refinery after Zelensky says strikes to resume · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · Geopolitics3 min read

Ukraine has resumed attacks on Russian oil refineries, hours after President Volodymyr Zelensky told the Financial Times that Kyiv was 'done' with an energy ceasefire. Russia's defence ministry said Ukraine attempted a large-scale attack on a refinery in Ryazan, south of Moscow, and that falling drone debris started a fire. Moscow said it intercepted four missiles and dozens of drones. The ceasefire, in short, lasted as long as only one side was observing it.

According to the BBC, Zelensky said he restricted Ukrainian strikes for three days while Russia kept hitting Ukrainian energy infrastructure. He said strikes left hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians without power and that Russia ignored the proposal within hours. The BBC notes the Kremlin never said it agreed to the deal. Translation: an energy ceasefire is a truce in which one party announces it, a second party complies, and a third party does not attend.

Black Sea Tanker Fires Test NATO's Favorite Tool: A Meeting About Having a Meeting

Microsoft and Japan Shut Down Scam Marketplace That Ran Like a Normal Online Store

Microsoft says it disabled more than 20,000 accounts tied to YAM Store and two phishing kits blamed for over a billion malicious messages since January 2026.

Microsoft said it helped dismantle a marketplace that supplied scammers with the materials needed to send phishing messages, according to The Verge. The investigation, run with Japan's National Police Agency, led to YAM Store and two phishing kits called CoGUI and Iron Man. Phishing, for the uninitiated, means a message pretending to be a bank, delivery firm or government office in order to get you to hand over a password or payment details. Translation: the parking ticket you did not earn was part of a supply chain.

The numbers are large. Microsoft says that since January 2026 the three platforms collectively enabled more than one billion malicious messages aimed at over 75,000 organizations in more than 150 countries. More than 750 million of those messages were directed at Microsoft customers alone. Targets included consumers, healthcare organizations, financial institutions and other businesses, and the operators earned tens of millions of dollars, according to the company's report.

Wholesale Prices Jump 6.3%, Giving Fed Fresh Reason to Look Busy

September's producer price index beat forecasts by a wide margin, and traders now put the odds of a rate hike this month at nearly 34%.

The producer price index, which measures what businesses receive for their goods and services, rose 1.3% in September and 6.3% from a year earlier, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Thursday, according to CNBC. Economists polled by Dow Jones had expected 0.6% and 5.5%. The monthly gain was the largest since March 2022 and the annual gain the largest since December 2022, which is the economy's way of saying it remembers that period and would like to revisit it.

Translation: the producer price index is inflation measured before the price tag reaches you. It tracks what companies charge each other, which is why it is often treated as a preview of what you will be charged later, with the cheerful implication that the trailer is worse than the film.

Energy did much of the damage. CNBC reported that the Middle East conflict curtailed oil supply and sent prices surging. A narrower measure excluding food, energy and trade still rose 0.5% on the month and 5% on the year. So removing the obvious culprit lowers the number, but leaves a figure no one would describe as relaxed.
