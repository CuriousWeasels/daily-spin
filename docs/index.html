Oil Falls on Peace Hopes While Tankers Keep Catching Fire in Hormuz

Crude retreated from $110 intraday highs on diplomacy hopes, though at least 18 ships have been fired on in two weeks and the November contract expires Tuesday.

The wire said: Oil prices fall as traders hope for diplomatic progress in Middle East · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & Economy2 min read

Crude oil fell on Tuesday as traders bet on diplomatic progress between the United States and Iran, CNBC reported. U.S. West Texas Intermediate futures for November slipped 0.9% to $102.75 a barrel, well below a session high of $110.48. Brent, the global benchmark, fell 1% to $108.31 after touching $111.99. The market, in other words, found a reason for optimism in a week when optimism was not obviously supplied by events.

The events in question: Kpler, a ship tracker, counts at least 18 vessels fired on in and around the Strait of Hormuz over the past two weeks. Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said two "non-compliant" oil tankers were set ablaze after hitting mines laid by its naval unit, according to the Iranian state-affiliated Tasnim news agency. Translation: the party that laid the mines is describing the ships that hit them as the ones at fault.

Zelensky Flies to Oslo to Ask Washington for Intelligence, Interceptors and Internet

Ukraine's president meets US envoys in Norway as Rubio denies withholding intelligence, fuel prices bite, and Washington's sanctions policy bends toward Russian diesel.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky met US special envoy Steve Witkoff in Oslo, the BBC reported, in talks that Norway and Finland agreed to host at his request. Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre said the aim was to steer relations toward "a more constructive path". It is a notable arrangement: the leader of a country at war needed two Nordic governments to arrange a meeting with his main ally.

The backdrop is an argument over intelligence. Serhii Beskrestnov, a top military adviser to Zelensky known as "Flash", told the BBC that unreliable US warnings, including about ballistic missile launches, meant lives were being lost. "This looks very strange," he said, noting that Kyiv sometimes gets information before an attack and sometimes does not. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the accusation "scandalous" and denied that anything was being withheld.

Hacker Outsources 100 Break-Ins to AI Agents While Army Drafts Its Own

Norforge says one attacker used autonomous agents to breach more than 100 websites, as documents show Army Cyber Command wants self-spreading AI that does the same, on purpose.

A hacker used an army of AI agents to break into more than 100 websites, mostly without supervising them, according to findings from the AI security firm Norforge. Norforge did not name the victims, but Reuters reported they included John Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins. The human involved appears to have done what many managers aspire to: set a goal, leave the room, and collect the results.

The tool was Cairn, an open-source platform that calls itself a "general-purpose problem-solving engine." Translation: you tell the software where to start and where to finish, and it documents its own progress along the way. Norforge says the same design works nicely for cyberattacks, which is the sort of thing a general-purpose engine tends to be good at.

Musk and Anthropic Pledge Share Giveaways Ahead of Very Large Stock Listings

Anthropic will put 5% of its shares into a public fund while Musk promises one SpaceX share per American child, details to follow.

Elon Musk replied by saying he would give one SpaceX share to every child in America next year. Anthropic said none of the fund's shares would be used for lobbying or political donations, in the US or abroad. Translation: the money is meant to be charity, and the company would like that in writing before anyone asks. Home Buyers Discover 7% Mortgage Rates, Promptly Discover Their Current Apartments

Pending home sales fell 7.3% in September to a record low as the 30-year mortgage rate climbed from about 6.8% to 7.6%, and builders are cutting prices anyway.

Pending home sales, which count signed contracts on existing homes, fell 7.3% in September from August and 11.3% from a year earlier, according to the National Association of Realtors. MarketWatch described the result as the lowest level on record, a 25-year low. The housing market has not so much cooled as been placed in a walk-in freezer.

The cause is not mysterious. According to Mortgage News Daily, cited by CNBC, the average 30-year fixed rate began September around 6.8% and reached 7.6% by month's end. Translation: a buyer who signed in late September agreed to pay noticeably more every month for the same house, which is a reliable way to make the house feel less like a dream and more like a spreadsheet.

Lawrence Yun, the NAR's chief economist, said that while job growth and income gains can support purchases, affordability is more affected by changes in mortgage rates. In other words, your raise is welcome, but the bank has already spent it.
