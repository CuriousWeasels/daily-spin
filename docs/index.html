Brent Touches $110 as Three Separate Conflicts Agree on One Thing: Oil Should Cost More

Brent closed at $109.86 Friday as Iranian attacks on Hormuz shipping, Houthi missiles and a collapsed Ukraine-Russia truce combined to lift diesel to $6.33 a gallon.

The wire said: Brent oil touches $110 as Iran escalates Hormuz attacks, Ukraine vows to hit Russian refineries · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & Economy2 min read

Brent crude futures touched $110 a barrel on Friday for the first time since May, then eased to close at $109.86, up 3.7%, CNBC reported. U.S. West Texas Intermediate settled at $101.41, up 3.5%. For the week, WTI gained more than 10% and Brent nearly 5%, which suggests the market is capable of panic in more than one benchmark at a time.

The causes arrived in a tidy set of three. Iran escalated attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, Houthi militants fired missiles at Saudi Arabia, and Ukraine vowed to keep hitting Russian refineries. Translation: the world's oil supply is being threatened at the source, along the shipping route, and at the refinery, which is thorough.

Meta Updates Business Terms So Publishers' Websites Can Be Read, Learned From, and Not Invoiced

New commercial terms taking effect October 30 let Meta crawl any site linked to a business account, and critics say that includes train-on-it rights, while Meta says nothing is changing.

Meta will update its commercial terms on October 30, and according to Ars Technica and The Verge, the new language lets the company use automated software to crawl websites associated with any business account. The terms say Meta may use that content to "develop, improve, research," and "train" its products. Businesses that decline may "stop all access or use" of Meta's products for commercial purposes, which is a contract's way of saying the exit is over there.

Translation: a crawler is a program that reads web pages automatically, and robots.txt is a file where a website politely asks crawlers to stay out. It works like a sign on a lawn. Legal experts told Nieman Lab the new terms could let Meta argue its commercial contract outranks that sign, including any licensing agreement a publisher has negotiated. The sign remains, but the contract says the lawn is already part of the deal.

Wholesale Prices Rise 6.3%, Fed Told Its Work Is Not Done, Again

September's producer price index rose 1.3% on the month, more than double forecasts, as the Iran war's energy shock spread into the rest of the economy and put a rate hike back on the table.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Thursday that the producer price index rose 1.3% in September and 6.3% from a year earlier, according to CNBC. Those were the largest monthly and annual gains since March 2022 and December 2022, respectively. Economists polled by Dow Jones had expected 0.6% and 5.5%, which means the forecasters were off by roughly the amount of the thing they were forecasting.

Translation: the producer price index measures what businesses receive for their goods and services before the rest of us encounter them at a register. It is the part of inflation that is still in transit. Think of it as a package tracking page for your future grocery bill.

Energy did much of the work. CNBC noted that gasoline prices rose 20%, and the BLS said almost half of the September increase in processed goods for immediate demand was tied to diesel. Diesel runs the trucks, and trucks deliver everything else, so a fuel shock has a way of becoming a shock to everything. Brent crude stood at 126.55 in the latest official data.
