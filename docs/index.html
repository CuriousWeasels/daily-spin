France Answers Weekend of Riots With Curfews, a Restitution Law and a Refused Resignation

After more than 2,500 arrests and damage estimated in the hundreds of millions of euros, the government is giving mayors curfew powers and promising that rioters will pay the bill.

The wire said: Curfew imposed in parts of central Paris and other cities after rioting across France · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · Geopolitics3 min read

French authorities have imposed overnight curfews in parts of central Paris and several other cities after a weekend of rioting and looting, the BBC reported. In Paris, the curfew runs from 23:00 to 06:00 in the Place de la République and the Les Halles shopping area. Partial restrictions on young people or gatherings were announced for Marseille, Bordeaux, Nice, Lyon and Lille. The protest movement began with students asking for better schools, which is a reasonable request that has now acquired a fire brigade.

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez admitted that police had "lost control" in some cities. Translation: the people responsible for control reported, with admirable candor, that they did not have it. He offered his resignation on Sunday, and President Emmanuel Macron refused, citing, according to the Élysée Palace, "the need for continued command of law enforcement." The same statement told Nuñez to be transparent about what Macron called "intelligence and foresight failings." The minister has thus been kept in his job so that he can explain why he was not doing it well.

Alberta Votes on Starting Process That Could Lead to Leaving Canada, Expected to Decline

Polls suggest the motion will fail, but as many as one in three voters may back it, which is the number everyone in Ottawa will actually be reading.

Anthropic Pledges 5% of Itself to Public, Retains 100% of Say in How

The AI company's new benefit fund is a wholly owned LLC, not a nonprofit, and arrives just ahead of an IPO that could value it near $2 trillion.

Anthropic said on Sunday that it plans to put 5 percent of its shares into a new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, pending shareholder approval. According to The Decoder and TechCrunch, the fund could make large donations to traditional nonprofits or explore direct payments to specific populations, with a focus on workers whose jobs AI may threaten. It is a generous-sounding gesture from a company that sells the technology it is generous about.

The fund could receive another 2 percent of shares for every 50 percent rise in Anthropic's valuation above a set baseline. Translation: the pledge grows as the company gets richer, which is how most charitable pledges work and also how most executive stock packages work. Anthropic pitches it as the opposite of the latter. The Decoder reports that the top-ups are at the board's discretion, and the milestones that would trigger them have not been named.

Russian Satellite Spends Weekend Loitering Next to Ukraine-Linked Imaging Craft, Insists It Was Just Passing

LeoLabs says Kosmos 2612 closed from hundreds of kilometers to within 500 meters of a US commercial satellite, in what it calls the first known unsanctioned rendezvous in low-Earth orbit.

A Russian military satellite, Kosmos 2612, maneuvered in what the orbital tracking firm LeoLabs called "extreme proximity" to ICEYE-X36, a US-operated commercial imaging satellite, according to Ars Technica's report on the company's findings. LeoLabs described it as the first known unsanctioned rendezvous in low-Earth orbit. In a field where spacecraft normally try to avoid each other, this was a deliberate visit nobody had been invited to.

The sequence, as LeoLabs tells it, was methodical. Kosmos 2612 closed from hundreds of kilometers away, circled the ICEYE craft, stayed nearby for an extended period, and then began moving away. By Friday it was holding station 2 to 3 kilometers off before shifting to positions ahead of and behind its neighbor. Over the weekend the two were within 500 meters (1,640 feet) of each other, and "likely quite a bit closer than that," said LeoLabs vice president Adam Marsh.

Junk Bond Market Sends Mixed Signals, Which Is Not a Signal, Investors Note

MarketWatch reports credit markets are giving unusually contradictory messages, with the riskiest corporate debt flashing a warning while other indicators stay calm.

MarketWatch reported that credit markets are sending unusually mixed messages to investors, and that this is making some people nervous. The article's headline goes further, describing the riskiest junk bonds as sending a warning and the market as having entered uncharted territory. The available excerpt is short on detail, so what follows leans on the market data supplied alongside it.

Translation: junk bonds are debt issued by companies with weaker credit ratings, which pay higher interest to compensate lenders for the higher chance of not being repaid. When investors get worried, they demand even more compensation, and the gap between junk yields and safe government yields widens. That gap is why credit markets are treated as a kind of smoke detector for the economy.

The mixed messages come from the fact that other detectors are not beeping in unison. The latest data shows the S&P 500 fund SPY at 766.48 as of October 19, and the VIX, the so-called fear gauge, at 19.99 as of October 16. Translation: the VIX measures how much volatility options traders expect, and a reading near 20 is generally regarded as ordinary unease rather than panic.
