Lula Wins Fourth Term After Losing Round One; Rival Asks for Do-Over

Brazil's 80-year-old president beat Flávio Bolsonaro 51.79% to 48.21%, a 4.4 million vote gap that his opponent says should be annulled anyway.

The wire said: Lula wins historic fourth term as Brazilian president in defeat of far-right Flávio Bolsonaro · The Guardian

By The Daily Spin Desk · Geopolitics2 min read

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term as Brazil's president on Sunday, taking 51.79% of the vote to Flávio Bolsonaro's 48.21%, according to The Guardian and the BBC. With every vote counted, the margin was 4.4 million. Bolsonaro, a 45-year-old senator, declined to accept it, which in Brazilian politics is now less a reaction than a family tradition.

Lula's win is historic in two ways. He is the first Brazilian to secure a fourth term, which would run until 2030. He is also the first candidate since civilian rule returned in the 1980s to lose the first round and then win the runoff. The Guardian noted that no first-round runner-up had ever done this. Lula had trailed 47% to 45% on 4 October.

Serbian Voters Retire Vučić, Who Had Not Planned to Be Retired

A student-led list took 52.7% of the vote to the ruling party's 39.2% in snap elections, and Aleksandar Vučić conceded after 14 years in power.

SpaceX Rents $40 Billion of Chips, Calls It Financing, Predicts Rivals Will Be Eaten

An Apollo-managed entity will buy the GPUs and lease them to SpaceX for about five years, while Elon Musk forecasts doom for 200-plus smaller AI landlords.

SpaceX said Monday in a regulatory filing that it has a binding commitment for about $40 billion to pay for AI chips and related data center gear. According to TechCrunch, an entity managed by Apollo Global Management will buy the equipment and lease it to SpaceX. SpaceX called it the "largest committed GPU financing," a category that, until recently, did not need a superlative because it barely existed.

Translation: this is a very large rent-to-own arrangement for computers. Apollo, a private equity firm, pays for the chips, SpaceX uses them, and SpaceX sends back monthly checks for roughly five to five-and-a-quarter years, starting as each batch is delivered. The leases are expected to begin in the first half of 2027, and the deal is supposed to close by year-end, subject to closing conditions.

With about 12,250 sites worldwide and 16 US states weighing bans or pauses, the buildings that run the internet have become a campaign issue.

Mortgage Rates Above 7% Declared 'New Normal' by Economists, Who Also Say Not to Call It That

Housing forecasters expect borrowing costs to stay above 7% well into 2027, ending hopes that the 3% mortgage will return from retirement.

Housing economists have settled on a phrase for mortgage rates above 7%: the new normal. According to CNBC, the term has appeared in public comments from Lawrence Yun of the National Association of Realtors and Robert Dietz of the National Association of Home Builders. Fannie Mae forecasts rates could average 7.5% in 2027, while the Mortgage Bankers Association expects them to stay above 7%. The organizations that sell and build houses, in other words, are telling buyers to get comfortable with expensive money.

The mechanism starts with the 10-year Treasury yield. Translation: the interest rate the U.S. government pays to borrow for a decade, which lenders use as a yardstick for pricing mortgages. Mortgages are usually paid off or refinanced well before their 30-year term ends, so the 10-year is the closer comparison. The latest official figure puts that yield at 4.96%, which explains why a 7% mortgage does not feel like an aberration.

Inflation is the first culprit. When prices keep rising, bond investors demand higher yields to make up for the shrinking value of the money they get back later. CNBC notes that the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, the PCE price index, has not been below the central bank's 2% target since February 2021. Consumer price inflation was 3.74% year over year as of September, per the latest official data, so the target remains a place the economy visits only in forecasts.
