France Discovers Student Complaints About Crumbling Schools Can Be Ignored Only Until They Aren't

Arrests reached 1,200 by one official count and at least 1,800 by NPR's, as protests that began over school conditions widened into a national fight over prices, inequality and who is listening.

The wire said: France's Macron vows to hold accountable those who 'sought to bring down the Republic' · NPR World

By The Daily Spin Desk · Geopolitics2 min read

Police in France arrested at least 1,800 people after nationwide demonstrations that drew hundreds of thousands, NPR reported, as violent groups clashed with officers and looted shops. The protests began as a complaint about schools. They now cover prices, inequality and the general sense that the government has stopped answering its mail.

The numbers differ by source, which is normal for a protest and for arithmetic done under pressure. DW reported that Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu put arrests at about 1,200 and police injuries at 150. NPR's later figure is at least 1,800. Lecornu said it was a miracle the toll was not higher, which is a sentence officials use when the toll is already high enough to need a miracle.

Trump Pauses Russia Sanctions for 60 Days to Keep Moscow Talking, Study Economic Damage

Two days before penalties were due to take effect, the president suspended them, while Russia hit Ukraine's power grid and Kyiv warned of a winter blackout.

US President Donald Trump blocked a set of Russia sanctions on Friday night, two days before they were due to take effect, according to the BBC. He said on Truth Social that he is negotiating peace and did not want to "foolishly" drive Russia away from the bargaining table. The sanctions, it should be noted, were passed by Congress and signed into law by Trump himself in September, so the president has paused a law he signed, in order to protect a negotiation.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio described the move as a 60-day pause, during which officials would analyse the potential impact of the sanctions on the US and global economies. Translation: the penalties designed to hurt Russia's economy are being reviewed for whether they hurt everyone else's too. Kirill Dmitriev, a Russian presidential envoy, called the suspension "an important and truly historic moment" and said the measures would have caused "serious damage" to the US economy and global markets. When the sanctioned party and the sanctioning party agree the sanctions are painful, the disagreement is mostly over who should feel it.

Anthropic Stops Hiring Juniors as Its AI Works Six Times the Hours and Humans Attend More Meetings

An internal report says junior hires in technical roles fell from 27 percent to 9 percent, while meeting time per employee rose 20 percent.

Anthropic is hiring far fewer junior employees in technical roles, according to an internal study the company published. The Decoder and TechCrunch both reported that the junior share of technical positions fell from 21 percent to 14 percent since early 2025. The junior share of new technical hires fell more sharply, from 27 percent to 9 percent. A company that builds machines to do work has, by its own account, found that it needs fewer beginners.

Anthropic points to its AI agents as the reason. According to The Decoder, by August 2026 those agents were logging six times as many work hours as human employees. Translation: the software now puts in the overtime, and it doesn't ask for a title change. Junior hiring dropped most in roles where Claude handles the most complex work, which is where a junior employee would once have been handed the tedious parts.

Chip Stocks Tumble, Finance Minister Says Boom Will Last Until at Least Next Year

SK Hynix fell 20.6% below a key moving average as South Korea's finance minister promised strong demand through 2027, while China's CXMT posted a profit swing and still lost 6.5%.

South Korean finance minister Lee Hyoung-Il told CNBC on Saturday that the chip boom should last at least through 2027. He spoke on the sidelines of the IMF-World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok, after chip stocks fell sharply during the week. Investing.com noted that SK Hynix had dropped 20.6% below its 20-period moving average. Translation: the stock is trading far under its recent average price, which traders treat as a sign of either a bargain or a warning, depending on which they already own.

Lee's case rests on supply and demand. 'The gap between demand and supply for semiconductors is still very wide, and supply is still being expanded,' he said. This is a comforting sentence with a small wrinkle: the same sentence says supply is growing, and growing supply is what eventually ends shortages.

CNBC reported that the selloff was blamed on investor concerns about interest rate hikes and rising competition from Chinese companies. Nvidia and other U.S. giants fell along with SK Hynix and Seoul Semiconductor. The latest market data shows the Fed funds rate at 3.88% while the 10-year Treasury yield sits at 5.1%. Translation: long-term borrowing costs are well above the short-term policy rate, which makes investors less willing to pay generous prices for profits that arrive years from now.
