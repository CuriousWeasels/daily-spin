Trump's point is that this time Washington paid nothing, which would be remarkable if it were the whole story.

Drones Strike Sudan Funeral Convoy and Fuel Market; Wit Declines to Attend

More than 300 people were reportedly killed in drone strikes across West Kordofan and Abyei, raising tensions between Sudan and South Sudan over a region neither has managed to settle.

Drone strikes on Sunday and Monday reportedly killed more than 300 people in Sudan's West Kordofan state and in Abyei, the BBC reported. The Sudan War Observatory, which tracks the conflict, was told by sources that 150 people died on Sunday evening, including 145 when a truck carrying about 200 mourners was hit as it arrived in the town of Al-Dabab. A tent where mourners had gathered was also struck, killing five more. Abyei's information minister later put the death toll at the Monday market attack there at 153.

The Abyei strike hit Amiet Market, about 10 kilometres from Abyei town, at around 11:30 local time on Monday. A military source told the BBC the army carried out the attack, describing the market as a fuel supply site for the paramilitary Rapid Support Forces. DW reported that South Sudanese officials also blamed the army but offered no evidence. Sudan's army has not responded to the allegations about the Sunday strikes, but says it does not attack civilians even when it believes RSF fighters are among them.

Meta Pauses Muse Sharing After 'Private' Links Turn Out to Be Searchable

Security firm Claroc found 6,800 Muse conversation links through public DNS records, including loan applications and a confidential executive-search call.

Meta has paused the sharing feature in Muse, its new AI agent, after researchers at the security firm Claroc reported that they could find thousands of supposedly private shared conversations with a single query. According to Ars Technica, the researchers enumerated 6,800 share links. Meta told Ars and The Verge it paused the feature while it reviews the report and considers changes to how the links can be discovered.

Share links normally work like an unlisted door. The page exists, but the address is a long string of random characters that nobody should be able to guess. Translation: the security rests on the address being a secret, so the address had better stay one.

Musk Says Merging His Two Trillion-Dollar Companies Is 'Good Idea,' Asks Committees to Please Not Tell Him How

Elon Musk confirmed that special committees at Tesla and SpaceX are studying a combination that would be the largest merger ever, though he is not allowed in the room.

He made the remark on SpaceX's earnings call, where he also said special committees at both companies are studying the matter. No terms have been agreed, and Musk said there is no guarantee they will be. It is the rare corporate announcement in which the person most likely to benefit says he is not allowed to take part.

Those committees are made up of independent directors, which is the corporate way of saying people who are not Musk. He also described even commenting on the topic as "tap dancing through a minefield," which is a fair description of securities law.

SpaceX Beats Estimates, Floats Merging With Tesla, Calls Process 'Tap Dancing Through a Minefield'

SpaceX reported $14.6 billion in quarterly revenue and Elon Musk said a Tesla combination has merit, pending committees, a shareholder vote and a high hurdle.

SpaceX stock rose 10% on Wednesday after the company reported third-quarter revenue of $14.6 billion, up 161% from a year earlier, according to CNBC. Analysts surveyed by LSEG had expected $12.72 billion. Elon Musk then used the earnings call to say that merging SpaceX with Tesla is a good idea, which is an unusual thing to say on a call about one company's earnings, in the sense that it is about a second company.

The numbers were strong by any measure. Operating income was $2.8 billion, and net income was $4.4 billion, against a net loss of $1.05 billion a year earlier. Translation: the company that used to lose money launching rockets now makes money renting out computers and satellite internet, which is a more reliable way to be a space company.

The biggest driver is what SpaceX now calls Super Intelligence, or SI. Translation: artificial intelligence, with a rebrand. That segment is now the largest business unit, with revenue up ninefold to $7.56 billion from $813 million. CFO Bret Johnsen credited that growth, along with recurring revenue from Starlink, for the quarter.
