According to NPR, military reinforcements are moving toward the Middle East, and some personnel have left the region. NPR's reporting says the pattern points to a potential attack on Iran. Both movements at once is the military equivalent of clearing the table before a large dinner: nobody announces the menu, but everyone notices the furniture.

NPR also reported that President Trump and Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu have been suggesting a new U.S.-Israeli attack is on the table. Translation: when leaders say an option is on the table, they are telling an adversary to worry while telling their own public that nothing has been decided. Uganda Reports Zero Ebola Cases, While Two Neighbors' Patients Both Pass Through Its Airport

Kenya and Nigeria's first Ebola patients both transited Entebbe on October 3, and Switzerland has contradicted Uganda's account of the Nigerian woman's travels.

Health officials in Africa are trying to work out how Nigeria's first Ebola patient in more than a decade became infected, after Switzerland contradicted the account Uganda had given of her travels. According to DW and The Guardian, the Swiss foreign ministry said the woman, a Swiss national of Ugandan origin married to a Swiss diplomat in Abuja, had not traveled to the Democratic Republic of Congo. Uganda's health ministry had said preliminary findings showed she went to eastern DRC on business first. One of these accounts is wrong, and the virus is not waiting for the paperwork.

The Africa Centres for Disease Control and Prevention said the Nigerian patient and Kenya's first confirmed patient both passed through Entebbe International Airport on October 3. Dr Wessam Mankoula, the agency's head of emergency preparedness and response, said investigators are checking whether their movements overlapped. DW notes it has not been established that the two met or that any transmission happened there. Translation: two people were in a large airport on the same day, which describes most days at most airports.

AI Buildout Has Rocky Week as Neoclouds Slide, Default Insurance Soars, and an IPO Is Quietly Withdrawn

CoreWeave's default-protection costs passed 11%, Meta's free cash flow turned negative, and Solidigm shelved its IPO, while Nvidia's CEO says demand is off the charts.

The Nasdaq fell again on Friday and ended the week down 0.7%, according to CNBC. The index has not had an across-the-board sell-off. It has instead had a series of unpleasant discoveries, each occurring in a different part of the AI supply chain.

Start with the neoclouds, companies that rent out AI computing power to customers who would rather not build their own data centers. Translation: they are landlords for chips, and they borrowed heavily to buy the buildings. CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN each fell sharply this week, leaving each down well over 30% for October, CNBC reported.

CoreWeave's credit default swaps, which are insurance contracts against a company failing to repay its debt, widened past 11%. Translation: the price of insuring its bonds has reached what markets generally treat as a distress level. Yields on its 2031 and 2032 unsecured notes topped 15%, which is what lenders charge when they would like to be paid for worrying.
