Europe Asks Washington to Please Stop Bombing Things Near Its Heating Bill

Western European governments that stayed quiet in February now urge restraint on Iran, as gas prices are up about 140 percent and Trump muses about a Cuban stopover.

The wire said: How is the US-Israeli war on Iran causing havoc in Europe? · Al Jazeera

By The Daily Spin Desk · Geopolitics3 min read

Al Jazeera reported that Germany, Britain and other Western European powers, which said little when the US and Israel began bombing Iran, are now calling for diplomacy and warning against a renewed campaign. The shift did not come from a sudden discovery of pacifism. It came from the gas bill.

The numbers explain the change. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said on October 16 that Europe's imported fossil fuel bill had risen by 106 billion euros, gas prices were up about 140 percent since late February, and diesel prices had doubled. Translation: the continent is paying a war surcharge on a war it was not consulted about, and it arrives monthly.

Google Amends Promise Not to Use Your Gmail for Ads, Adds the Word 'Unless'

A October 16 privacy policy update lets data from Gmail, Drive and Photos feed ad personalization if users connect it to AI features.

Google updated its privacy policy on October 16 to say it will not use your Drive, Gmail or Photos content for personalized ads, unless you choose to make that data available to other Google services that support personalized ads. The Verge and Ars Technica both reported the change, which was first spotted by Android Authority. The earlier version contained no such clause, which is the sort of detail that tends to be the whole point of a clause.

Here is the mechanism. Personal Intelligence lets users connect their Google apps to AI products such as the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. Translation: you hand the AI your email and photos so it can answer questions about your life, and in return it answers questions about your life. The Gemini app does not currently show ads, according to Ars Technica, but AI search tools may carry sponsored content. That is where the two systems meet.

Google Tells Publishers the Web Is Thriving, Publishers Check Their Traffic and Disagree

Since Google put AI summaries atop search results, publishers report falling clicks, while Google says click volume is 'relatively stable' and offers pilot programs.

The Verge reported that Google's AI search features have reshaped online publishing, and not in the direction publishers had hoped. For years the deal was simple: publishers filled Google's index with content, and Google sent readers back. Translation: the web was a restaurant that paid the delivery app to bring in customers, and the app has now begun serving the food itself.

The mechanism is straightforward. AI Overviews, launched in 2024, put a generated summary at the top of results, with source links below it. A reader who gets the answer in the summary has less reason to visit the page the answer came from. The Press Gazette found Overviews appeared on almost a quarter of news-related searches in May 2024, and by August they were displacing media sites that had previously held the top spots for some queries.

France Keeps Its Credit Rating, Loses Its Patience With Everything Else

Moody's held France at Aa3 with a negative outlook while 10-year yields hit their highest since 2002 and protesters planned more marches.

Moody's on Friday left France's credit rating at Aa3, which it still calls "high grade," and kept its negative outlook, CNBC reported. Translation: the grade is fine, but the teacher has written "concerning" in the margin and has not ruled out a phone call home.

The agency said the outlook reflects the risk that political fragmentation "will continue to impair" the work of the legislature. In plain terms, Moody's is less worried about whether France can afford its budget than about whether anyone can agree to vote for one. It said it could restore a stable outlook if institutions manage to narrow the deficit.

The market did its own grading. According to CNBC, the yield on 10-year French bonds, known as OATs, rose 11 basis points on Thursday to its highest since 2002, passing 5%. Translation: a basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point, and the yield is the interest rate investors demand to lend to the government. The gap over German Bunds, the euro zone's safe-bond benchmark, is the widest since November 2011.
