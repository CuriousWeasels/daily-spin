Ebola Investigators Trace Two Patients to One Airport, and One Country's Denial

Nigeria's first Ebola case in over a decade has no confirmed source, and Uganda and Switzerland disagree on where the patient went.

Health officials across Africa are trying to work out how Nigeria's first Ebola patient in more than a decade became infected, according to The Guardian. The patient, a woman based in Abuja, is the fourth country's case in an outbreak the Guardian describes as the most geographically widespread in history. The one thing everyone agrees on is that she has Ebola. Where she got it is the subject of a dispute.

Uganda's Health Ministry said preliminary findings showed she travelled to eastern DR Congo for business, then passed through Uganda without symptoms. Translation: if she caught it in Congo, it is not Uganda's problem. Switzerland's foreign ministry then told Reuters that she and her husband, a Swiss diplomat in Abuja, did not travel to Congo at all, and that she was believed to have been infected while travelling in Uganda. DW noted that this Swiss assessment has not been independently confirmed.

Auditors Spend Three Weeks Inside OpenAI, Find Safety Monitor Missing Quite a Lot of AI

Researchers from METR and Redwood Research estimate 6 to 25 percent of some internal agent traffic went unmonitored, and the public gets a short summary of the 61-page report.

Two researchers spent three weeks inside OpenAI checking whether the company's monitoring system for rogue AI behavior actually monitors things, The Decoder reported. The answer, in the careful language of auditors, was: not always. They had access to the codebase and internal documents and interviewed about a dozen employees, which is roughly the point at which a smoke detector inspection becomes a conversation about the batteries.

The headline number is that an estimated 6 to 25 percent of internal agentic Responses API traffic from models of the GPT-5.5 class and above was not covered by monitoring during the audit period. Translation: agentic traffic means AI systems taking actions on their own rather than just answering questions, and the monitor was not watching a meaningful slice of it. METR says the uncertainty is significant in both directions, so the true figure could be better or worse than the range suggests.

France Keeps Its Credit Rating, Loses Its Calm, Pays Investors Like It's 2002

Moody's left France at Aa3 with a negative outlook while 10-year yields topped 5% and more protests were scheduled for the weeks ahead.

Moody's said on Friday that it was keeping France's credit rating at Aa3, a level it still calls high grade, while maintaining a negative outlook, according to CNBC. Translation: the agency has not downgraded France, but it has left the door open and put a note on it.

The reasoning was blunt. Moody's said the negative outlook reflects the risk that political fragmentation will keep impairing how the legislature functions. It added that instability, visible in the current social tensions, could hamper the government's ability to address the deficit, the rising debt burden and higher borrowing costs. It said the outlook could return to stable if institutions manage to narrow the deficit.

The market had already offered its own review. The yield on France's 10-year bond, known as the OAT, rose 11 basis points on Thursday to its highest since 2002, and CNBC reported it passed 5% this week. Translation: a basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point, and a yield is what investors demand to lend you money. France is being asked to pay more for the privilege. The gap over German Bunds, the euro zone's benchmark, is the widest since November 2011.
