Lula Wins Fourth Term After Losing Round One, Which Brazil's Voters Said Was Optional

The 80-year-old president beat Flávio Bolsonaro 51.75% to 48.25%, becoming the first first-round runner-up to win a Brazilian runoff since democracy returned.

The wire said: Lula wins fourth term as Brazil president after historic upset over far-right Flávio Bolsonaro · The Guardian

By The Daily Spin Desk · Geopolitics2 min read

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won a fourth term as president of Brazil on Sunday, defeating Flávio Bolsonaro in a runoff. With nearly 99% of votes counted, the Guardian reported Lula had 51.75% to his rival's 48.25%. Brazil's Superior Electoral Court called the race shortly after 9pm Brasília time, according to Al Jazeera. The count was settled faster than the argument over it.

The result is historic in a technical sense. Since democracy returned in the 1980s, no candidate who finished second in the first round had won the runoff. Lula had lost that round by 2.2 million votes. He won the second by 4.3 million, which is a swing of 6.5 million votes achieved in three weeks, mostly by persuading people to leave the house.

Israel Heads to Polls Tuesday, Where Coalition Math Matters More Than Winning

Polls show Netanyahu's bloc and the opposition almost level, with a former army chief leading the challenge and half a million first-time voters deciding how close is close.

Israel holds a general election on Tuesday, and according to the BBC, the final polls, published Friday as Israeli law requires, put the governing coalition and the main opposition bloc almost neck-and-neck. About half a million Israelis are voting for the first time, and roughly 70,000 are reportedly flying home from abroad just to cast a ballot. This is what is known as civic engagement with a baggage allowance.

The central figure on the challenger side is Gadi Eisenkot, a former military chief of staff whose centrist Yashar party has regularly edged Netanyahu's Likud for first place in recent polls, the BBC reported. Translation: his party might get the most votes, which is not the same as getting the job. Israeli politics, the BBC notes, is the art of coalition-building, so the winner is usually whoever can assemble 61 seats in the 120-seat parliament. Finishing first earns a lead in the race and a lot of phone calls.

OpenAI Discovers Customers Prefer Cheaper Things, Offers Them Up to 90 Percent Off

Rippling, Replit and DataCamp are shifting AI spending from Anthropic to OpenAI, The Information reports, as a price war replaces the usual talk of benchmarks.

OpenAI is offering discounts of up to 90 percent to lure large customers away from Anthropic, and it appears to be working, The Decoder reported, citing The Information. Companies including Rippling, Replit and DataCamp are moving more of their AI spending to OpenAI. In a market where every vendor claims to have the smartest model, it turns out that a sale still gets attention.

The numbers are specific. Rippling CEO Parker Conrad said OpenAI's share of his company's coding workload rose from 16 percent to 52 percent, while Anthropic's fell from 57 percent to 18 percent. Translation: a customer that once mostly bought from one vendor now mostly buys from the other, which is the kind of thing a procurement department calls optimization and a sales team calls a bad quarter.

Data Centres Become Rare Issue Uniting Both US Parties, Neither Of Which Can Stop Building Them

With about 12,250 sites worldwide and 16 US states weighing curbs, the buildings that run the internet are now a midterm campaign issue, according to the BBC.

The BBC reported that opposition to data centres has become a significant issue in the United States ahead of the midterm elections on 3 November, with both Republicans and Democrats coming out against their development. President Donald Trump has dismissed the pushback as a "sick conspiracy". In a polarised country, this is notable: voters have found something they can agree to dislike, and it is a warehouse.

The scale explains the attention. According to Data Center Map, which tracks both operational and planned sites, there are about 12,250 data centres worldwide, up from around 8,000 operational sites in 2021. The US has 4,767 of them, and leads by an even wider margin in total capacity, with China second and Europe far behind. Translation: capacity is the amount of electricity a site can use, which is a better measure of how much computing is going on than counting buildings, since some data centres fit in a garden shed.

SpaceX Borrows $40 Billion for Chips, Predicts Rivals Will Be Unable to Do the Same

Elon Musk says the company closed the debt deal for Nvidia-style GPUs and forecasts a wipeout among more than 200 AI cloud upstarts.

Elon Musk said on X on Sunday that SpaceX has closed $40 billion in debt financing to buy graphics processing units, according to CNBC. GPUs are the chips that power AI systems. It is a large sum to borrow for hardware, though it is the sort of figure the AI industry now treats as a rounding error with ambitions.

Musk said the company is investment grade, meaning rating agencies consider it likely to repay, and that it earns revenue from Starlink satellite internet and rocket launches. In a follow-up post, he said the financing is mainly callable debt for GPUs, expected to arrive in the first and second quarters of 2027. Translation: callable debt is a loan the borrower can repay early. The money is announced now and delivered later, which is a fine way to get a head start on next year's shopping.

Bloomberg reported earlier, citing people familiar with the matter, that the plan used a special purpose vehicle to keep the debt off SpaceX's balance sheet and protect its investment-grade ratings. Translation: a special purpose vehicle is a separate legal entity that holds the borrowing, so the parent company's books look tidier. Bloomberg also said Goldman Sachs was leading talks with investors, including Apollo Global Management. Musk's posts did not describe the structure, so how the final deal compares with the reported one is unconfirmed.
