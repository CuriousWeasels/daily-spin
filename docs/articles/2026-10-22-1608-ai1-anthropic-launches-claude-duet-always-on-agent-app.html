Anthropic Releases Claude Duet, an Assistant That Works While You Sleep and Asks Permission Before Spending

The research-preview app runs in the background on its own cloud computer, connects to more than 6,000 plugins, and will not move your money between accounts under any circumstances.

The wire said: Anthropic takes on Grok Bot, Meta's Muse, and OpenAI's Dots with its always-on agent "Claude Duet" · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 22, 2026 16:08 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Anthropic has launched a research preview of Claude Duet, a standalone app in which an AI agent handles work tasks in the background. It joins a crowded field that includes Grok Bot, Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots.

Why it matters

Agents that can send messages and make purchases for you turn AI from a thing you consult into a thing that acts, which makes the approval rules and security safeguards the real product. For small businesses, the pitch is fewer dropped invoices; the risk is trusting a system that, by its maker's own admission, can misjudge urgency.

Anthropic has released a research preview of Claude Duet, an app in which an AI agent keeps working on tasks while the user is away, according to The Decoder and TechCrunch. It runs on iOS, Android and the web, is built on Claude Opus 5.5, and is open through a waitlist to Pro and Max subscribers in the US. Team and Enterprise plans are not included yet. The company has, in effect, built an employee that never leaves the office and is also not in the office.

The main difference from the regular Claude app is direction. In the chat app, you show up and ask for things. With Duet, The Decoder reported, the agent shows up first: a short morning summary of the important items from connected apps, then silence unless something needs attention. Translation: the assistant has moved from waiting by the phone to calling you.

Tasks are assigned by text or voice in a single ongoing thread, and the work happens on a virtual machine. Translation: a virtual machine is a rented computer in a data center with its own browser, which is why your laptop can be closed and your phone can be in a drawer. Duet plugs into services such as Gmail, Slack, Stripe, Docusign and Shopify through more than 6,000 connectors and plugins from the Claude Marketplace. Anthropic's examples include tracking down unpaid invoices, drafting payment reminders and getting payroll ready, which is to say the tasks small business owners avoid most.

Then comes the part that matters most: permission. Anthropic says Duet can read, draft and prepare on its own, but before it sends a message, makes a payment, signs a document or deletes anything, it shows what it plans to do and waits for approval. Trading securities and moving money between accounts are off limits even if the user says yes. For purchases it uses Stripe Link with a virtual card, so the real card number is never shared, and TechCrunch adds that Shopify's Shop Pay is also supported.

The safeguards go further. The Decoder reported that the agent never sees passwords or login tokens directly, and that Anthropic applied defenses from its browser research against prompt injections. Translation: a prompt injection is a hidden instruction planted in a web page or email telling the AI to do something its owner never asked. Anthropic also built a new classifier, a screening system that checks each message for leaks of sensitive data. By default it should refuse requests for credit card numbers and government ID details, and health or personal finance information gets through only when the user explicitly says so.

Anthropic is notably candid about limits. The early version can misread a message thread, misjudge urgency and miss things that arrive between checks. It is weaker at personal errands such as booking tickets, and TechCrunch noted it cannot show its browser use as it happens. Anthropic says it checks connected accounts only every few hours, because constant checking would burn through subscription limits much faster. The assistant that never sleeps, it turns out, is rationed.

The market context is a pile-up. The Decoder said SpaceXAI opened the Grok Bot beta in August, followed in September by Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots. TechCrunch's list of rivals also includes Hark, Instinct and Fo, and noted that many competitors are leaning toward business use. Anthropic's answer is to position Duet for professionals and small business owners with, in its words, more on their plates than they can track.

What to watch next: how Anthropic tunes when the agent should interrupt, and how Duet eventually fits with the main Claude app, a question the company calls open. Until then the two run separately, which is at least one more arrangement in which the user is the one coordinating the assistants.
