Russia and Ukraine Agree on Something: A Four-Hour Bus Schedule in Oleshky

A ceasefire brokered by Melania Trump let 25 buses carry up to 750 people out of the ruined, Russian-occupied city, while the Kremlin said the truce is not for everyone else.

The wire said: Hundreds evacuated from Russian-occupied Oleshky during rare Ukraine ceasefire · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 30, 2026 17:17 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Oleshky is a Russian-occupied city in the Kherson region of Ukraine, left without gas, electricity or running water. A ceasefire across Kherson began on Tuesday, mainly so a road out of the city could be cleared of mines.

Why it matters

For the people of Oleshky, the window is a rare chance to leave a place with no basic services. For everyone else, it tests whether a localized truce can exist without becoming a template for a wider one, which the Kremlin says it cannot.

Hundreds of residents of Oleshky were evacuated on Friday during a humanitarian ceasefire brokered by US First Lady Melania Trump, according to the BBC. Russian and Ukrainian authorities both said the operation went smoothly, which in this war counts as a diplomatic breakthrough. Russia's human rights ombudsman, Yana Lantratova, said it went without incident and that both sides noted the "high level of organisation."

The arrangements were modest and precise. Residents had a four-hour window, until 14:00 local time, and a convoy of 25 buses was provided for up to 750 people, along with ambulances for those with limited mobility. The ceasefire, in force across the whole Kherson region since Tuesday, was used to demine the road out. Translation: the truce exists partly so that leaving does not involve stepping on something.

The route is not short. Evacuees travel first into Russian-occupied territory and on to the southern Russian city of Anapa, then board a Russian government plane to Gomel in Belarus, then drive into Ukraine's Chernihiv region. Ukrainian military positions are 5km from Oleshky, but a straight line is apparently not among the terms agreed. Ukraine's ombudsman, Dmytro Lubinets, will escort them and become the first Ukrainian official to visit Russia since the invasion began.

Lubinets and Lantratova also travelled to Oleshky together from Armiansk in Crimea, making Lubinets the first Ukrainian official to visit Russian-occupied parts of Ukraine since the war began. He said he cooperated to save lives. The BBC reported that some Ukrainians recoiled at seeing him beside a representative of the occupying power that has killed many of the city's residents. Both officials posted videos, so the cooperation was at least well documented.

Many people chose to stay. The BBC reported that several hundred remain despite the devastation, and Lubinets said they will receive humanitarian assistance. Russia said it prepared "dozens of tonnes" of aid, including food, medicine and fuel for generators. One resident told the BBC she stayed because officials "wouldn't take my animals." Another, Olena, said older relatives do not want to leave, and that locals carry the wounded and dead to the hospital in garden wheelbarrows.

Then comes the politics. The Kremlin said on Friday that the Oleshky success should not be read as willingness to extend the truce to the rest of Ukraine. Residents of Kherson told the BBC they were overjoyed by the first peace in the region since February 2022, but fear Russia is using the pause to build up forces on the Dnipro riverbank ahead of an attack when the ceasefire expires at the end of Sunday 1 November.

Many Ukrainians also suspect Moscow's real aim is to earn credit with the White House and give President Donald Trump a lift before next week's midterm elections, the BBC reported. Moscow is expected to present the evacuation, with a handful of invited foreign journalists watching, as a mission of mercy. Whatever the motive, the buses ran on time.

What to watch: whether the Kherson ceasefire lapses on Sunday as scheduled, and whether anyone now finds it harder to explain why a truce is possible for four hours and impossible for the rest of the map.
