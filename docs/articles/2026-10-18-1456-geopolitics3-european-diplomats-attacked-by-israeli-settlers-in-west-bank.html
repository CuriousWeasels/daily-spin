Diplomats Attacked in West Bank; Israel Condemns Attack, Questions Whether It Happened

Belgium, France and Poland protest after masked settlers confronted a European convoy, while the prime minister's office urges caution about the footage and the IDF says no soldiers were there.

The wire said: European diplomats attacked by Israeli settlers in West Bank · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 18, 2026 14:56 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Israeli settlers have carried out near-daily attacks on Palestinians during the olive harvest in the occupied West Bank, according to the BBC. On Saturday, European diplomats visiting the village of Taybeh to assess the situation were themselves attacked.

Why it matters

When settler violence reaches diplomats and journalists, it becomes a problem for Israel's relations with Europe and the US, not just for the Palestinians who face it daily. It also tests whether condemnations will ever turn into prosecutions or EU sanctions.

A delegation of European diplomats was attacked by masked Israeli settlers in the occupied West Bank on Saturday, the BBC reported. The envoys, from Belgium, France, Poland and the Catholic Church, had traveled to Taybeh, a largely Christian village near Ramallah, to see how things stood after weeks of settler attacks on Palestinians trying to bring in their olive harvest. They found out.

Video from the scene showed masked men confronting the convoy, and reports suggest stones were thrown. Belgium said a window of a consulate car was smashed. A French mayor was also assaulted, and footage showed a man wearing a French tricolor sash slumped beside a vehicle. The mayor, Bernard Baudoux of Aulnoye-Aymeries, told AFP that settlers kicked him and beat him with iron bars. "It's the gratuitous nature of the violence that stuns me," he said.

Reactions arrived in the usual order. Belgium's foreign minister, Maxime Prevot, said Israel must bring those responsible to justice and that Belgium will keep pressing for EU sanctions against them. Translation: sanctions are penalties such as asset freezes or travel bans, aimed at individuals, which are cheap to propose and notoriously slow to agree among 27 member states. France's foreign minister, Jean-Noël Barrot, urged Israel to "urgently halt this criminal violence." Poland asked for a "firm response." The EU asked for "full accountability."

Israel's response came in several versions. Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar strongly condemned the violence by what he called "Israeli rioters." He then noted it was unfortunate that the visit was not coordinated with the IDF in advance, which would have helped safeguard the visitors. This is the diplomatic equivalent of regretting that the victim did not file an itinerary. The IDF also condemned "the severe act of violence" and said a preliminary inquiry found no soldiers were present, which is a fact that answers one question and raises another.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's office took a third line. It urged "caution before drawing conclusions," said social media images do not establish the full sequence of events, and pointed to concerns about a "coordinated campaign to smear Israel" during the olive harvest. It also said police and forensic investigators had opened an investigation. So the government is investigating an incident that its own foreign ministry and army have already condemned, while its leader's office wonders if the reports are a smear. Both can be true only if one reads very carefully.

The attack came a day after a separate settler assault in another village injured several Palestinians and Heidi Levine, a Washington Post photographer. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio called it "disturbing" and "not a good look." US ambassador Mike Huckabee, a staunch Israel ally, called it a "despicable act of terror carried out by masked cowards" and demanded prosecution. When that vocabulary comes from that source, it suggests the criticism is not confined to Israel's usual critics.

Mayor Baudoux argued that the attack showed the tolerance of Israel's right-wing government for settler violence. He added that he would go home and be fine, while those who live there continue to suffer the most. The investigation now has diplomats, video footage and an unusually long list of governments watching it. What to watch is whether anyone is charged, since condemnation has so far been abundant and arrests have not.
