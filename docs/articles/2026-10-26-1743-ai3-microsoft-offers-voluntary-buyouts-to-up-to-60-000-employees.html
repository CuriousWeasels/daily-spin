Microsoft Invites 60,000 Employees to Leave, Describes It as an Opportunity

The company is offering voluntary exits to up to 60,000 staff in four countries, months after laying off 4,800 and while its AI teams are only partly invited.

The wire said: Microsoft offers buyouts to 60,000 employees · The Verge AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 26, 2026 17:43 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Microsoft has about 223,000 employees and has been trimming its workforce this year, including a layoff of 4,800 and a separate retirement program for long-serving staff. The new offer is a broader, voluntary version.

Why it matters

Buyouts let a company shrink headcount while avoiding the headlines of a layoff, and they show where a tech giant thinks it needs fewer people. For workers, the question is whether a voluntary offer today becomes a less voluntary one later.

Microsoft is offering voluntary exit packages to up to 60,000 employees in the US, UK, Canada, and India, The Verge reported. In a message to staff, chief people officer Amy Coleman described it as a chance to leave "with financial support and career planning resources." The company is, in other words, trying to reduce its workforce without calling it a layoff, which is a distinction that matters a great deal to everyone except the person leaving.

Translation: a voluntary exit offer is a buyout. The company puts a package on the table, and employees decide whether to take it. It is cheaper in public relations than a layoff, and it has the further advantage that the people who accept are the ones who already had one foot out the door. It has the disadvantage that the company does not choose who leaves.

The eligibility list is where the strategy shows. According to sources who spoke to The Verge, staff in AI experiences, Copilot, CoreAI, Windows + Devices, Xbox, finance, and human resources are eligible. Cloud + AI, MicrosoftAI, and corporate, external, and legal affairs are partially participating. Marketing, the commercial business, discovery and quantum, and security are not included. So the company is encouraging departures from some of its AI teams while it spends heavily to be seen as an AI company, which is the sort of contradiction that tends to resolve itself in a spreadsheet.

The timing is not subtle. The offer arrives months after Microsoft laid off 4,800 people, mostly in Xbox and commercial sales, and Fortune reported that Xbox CEO Asha Sharma is planning further layoffs. Earlier this year Microsoft also ran a voluntary retirement program for about 9,000 US employees whose age plus years of service reached 70. This one is much wider: an internal note seen by The Verge says it is not based on age, length of service, or performance.

Coleman wrote that the offer "is new for us" and that "we'll learn from it." Few employees enjoy being the pilot group for a learning exercise, but 60,000 of them now have the chance. The internal note also says Microsoft may consider another voluntary exit offer "where there is a business and workforce need," which is a polite way of saying the first offer may not be the last.

What to watch is the uptake. If too few people accept, the company still has its headcount target and several other tools for reaching it, and those tools are generally not described as opportunities.
