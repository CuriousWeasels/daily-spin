Chip Stocks Slide as Investors Remember Memory Can Be Oversupplied, Briefly Forget Again

Tesla's lower spending target and Lam Research's weak forecast hit chipmakers on Thursday, while Nvidia's Jensen Huang said he sees no memory glut by 2029.

The wire said: Two big reasons Micron and other chip stocks are falling on Thursday · MarketWatch

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 22, 2026 16:08 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Memory stocks have fallen for a week after a South Korean opinion column warned the AI boom could slow and flood the market with supply by 2029. On Thursday, MarketWatch said Tesla's spending cut and Lam Research's guidance added to the pressure.

Why it matters

Memory chips are a core part of AI hardware, so falling prices would squeeze the profits of Micron, Samsung and SK Hynix. Anyone with an index fund is exposed to how long this AI trade keeps its nerve.

Chip stocks fell Thursday, with MarketWatch pointing to two causes: Tesla's cut to its near-term spending target and a weaker-than-expected forecast from Lam Research. Lam makes chipmaking equipment, so when it guides low, investors infer that its customers are planning to buy fewer machines. Translation: the shovel seller says fewer people are digging, and the market took note.

CNBC reported that memory stocks had already been battered over the past week. The trigger was an Oct. 15 opinion column in a prominent South Korean publication urging the government to brace for a slowdown in the AI boom. It cited two experts who expected a 'flood of new supply' that would turn memory into 'a red ocean' by 2029, per CNBC's translation.

Translation: a glut means too many chips chasing too few buyers, which pushes prices down and squeezes profit margins at SK Hynix, Samsung and Micron. Elon Musk added to the mood by saying China 'will be making a LOT of RAM in 2028.' The selloff was also fed by China's ChangXin Memory Technologies, or CXMT, announcing a new design architecture that could make it more efficient and let it take share from the incumbents.

Nvidia's Jensen Huang, whose chips depend on memory, offered a different view in a CNBC interview at GTC Berlin. 'You can always use more of it,' he said, which is the sort of forecast one expects from a company that buys the memory. He said HBM, a high-performance type of RAM, will be used for years, and that Nvidia is putting phone-style LPDDR memory into data centers.

Huang also said Chinese manufacturing deserves to be taken very seriously, and that US regulation had 'definitely accelerated' China's chip ambitions. That is a notable admission about export controls, which restrict Beijing's access to top Nvidia chips. CNBC noted that some Chinese firms still reach that computing power through data centers in Southeast Asia.

The wider market showed the same unease. CNBC's Investing Club recap said another wave of selling hit the AI trade, with Huang's remark about a 'scarcity of capital' for neoclouds like CoreWeave, Meta's AI funding comments and Lam's guidance all possibly contributing. GE Vernova, whose gas turbines power the AI buildout, fell almost 10% a day after rising on bullish comments from SpaceX. As one portfolio director put it, the market has 'no memory from one day to the next,' which is an unusual flaw in a memory-focused sell-off.

Money moved into defensive sectors, with healthcare and consumer staples the best performers in the S&P 500. Bond yields fell even as oil rose, which CNBC described as a flight to safety. Meta dropped nearly 6% despite strong revenue growth, with the club suggesting its plan to create a new class of non-voting stock to help fund AI spending might be the overhang.

For context, the 10-year Treasury yielded 5.12% as of Oct. 20, and the S&P 500 tracker SPY closed at 764.95 on Oct. 22. With safe assets paying that much, investors have a fresh reason to ask whether a 2029 chip glut is worth worrying about now. Watch whether Lam's caution spreads to other equipment makers, and whether anyone outside Nvidia's earnings call agrees that memory demand has no ceiling.
