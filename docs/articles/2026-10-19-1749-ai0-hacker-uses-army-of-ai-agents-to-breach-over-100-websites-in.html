Hacker Delegates Breaking Into 100 Websites to AI Agents, Takes Rest of Day Off

Security firm Norforge says one attacker used an open-source AI platform to breach more than 100 sites, with the agents doing most of the work.

The wire said: A hacker broke into John Deere and SharkNinja with an army of AI agents · The Verge AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 19, 2026 17:49 UTC · 1 min read

Catch up

Norforge, an AI security firm, found exposed instances of Cairn, an open-source platform for directing AI agents. One of them showed a hacker running largely autonomous intrusions against companies in four countries.

Why it matters

If a short instruction can send software off to break into websites with little supervision, the cost of attacking drops while the defenders' workload does not. Companies, including their forgotten support sites and vendor-run pages, are now easier targets.

A hacker broke into more than 100 websites using AI agents that mostly worked on their own, according to findings from the security firm Norforge. Norforge does not name the victims, but Reuters reported that they included John Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins. The human involvement was reportedly limited, which is the first time in history that the phrase 'hands-off management' has been used to describe a felony.

The trail began with exposed work on Cairn, an open-source platform that calls itself a 'general-purpose problem-solving engine.' Translation: you tell the software where to start and where to finish, and it figures out the middle and keeps notes. Norforge notes that this is just as useful for planning a cyberattack as for anything else, which is the problem with general-purpose tools.

The attacker in question was the 'most active' of the exposed Cairn instances Norforge found, per Reuters, and targeted companies in the US, Canada, Australia and Japan. According to Reuters, the hacker described preferred intrusion tactics in fewer than 100 Chinese characters, and successful breaches took about 23 hours. That is a shorter brief than most corporate offsites produce, and with a clearer outcome. Norforge counted more than 20 exposed Cairn instances it considers 'likely malicious.'

The companies' responses follow the standard script, which is to emphasize how small the damaged thing was. Cummins spokesperson Melinda Koski told The Verge the company 'is investigating the report of a potential cyber-attack on a website operated by one of our third-party vendors.' Translation: if it happened, it happened in someone else's building. A John Deere spokesperson told Reuters there is 'no indication that sensitive information was accessed.' SharkNinja said the breach hit a standalone site with product support information and that it has contained the threat. Nobody mentioned whether the vacuum manuals are safe, but the vacuum manuals are presumably relieved.
