Brazil Ends Campaign With Million-Person Beach Rally and a Statistical Tie

Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro finish the runoff race level in the polls, with Minas Gerais, a state that has never picked a loser, likely to decide it.

The wire said: More than one million Lula supporters rally on eve of Brazilian election runoff · The Guardian

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 25, 2026 01:53 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Flávio Bolsonaro, a senator and son of former president Jair Bolsonaro, won the first round on 4 October by 47% to 45% over President Lula. The two meet in a runoff on Sunday, with nearly 159 million Brazilians eligible to vote.

Why it matters

The result decides whether Latin America's largest country keeps a left-wing government or moves right, and whether the jailed Jair Bolsonaro's political circle returns to power. No first-round loser has won a runoff since democracy returned in the 1980s, so either outcome is historically notable.

On the final day of campaigning, Brazil's two runoff candidates did what candidates do and gathered as many people as possible in as many places as possible. According to the Guardian, Rio's City Hall said about 1.1 million people filled Copacabana beach for President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. Lula also attended a rally on Paulista Avenue in São Paulo, and Flávio Bolsonaro drew supporters in Minas Gerais. Crowd size is the one poll in which every campaign finishes first.

The actual polls are less generous. Al Jazeera reported that a CNT/MDA survey released Saturday had Bolsonaro at 46.3% and Lula at 45%, with a margin of error of 2.2 percentage points. Translation: the margin of error is bigger than the lead, so the poll is shrugging. A separate Datafolha poll, cited by Al Jazeera, put the two at 50% each, which is a shrug with better manners.

Lula is trying something that has not happened since the 1980s: winning a runoff after losing the first round. He is 80 and seeking a fourth non-consecutive term, having first won in 2002. The Guardian said he has recently gained ground after a run of scandals involving his opponent, including a magazine claim from Piauí that Bolsonaro attended a boat party during the height of the 2020 Covid outbreak. The Guardian presented that as the magazine's claim, not an established fact.

The Lula side has built a fairly modern machine. Many marchers belong to "Lula's Army," a WhatsApp group of 10 million members formed after the first round. Translation: a group chat that is also a field operation, which is the first time a family group chat has been asked to do something useful.

The Bolsonaro side filled Belo Horizonte's Praça da Liberdade, where the Guardian described a soundtrack of AI-generated funk tracks praising the candidate. Many supporters waved American flags alongside Brazilian ones. Lula seized on this, telling the crowd, according to Al Jazeera, that voters must choose between someone who kisses the national flag and someone who kisses the American flag. One supporter told the Guardian he wanted alignment with the US, and when reminded of Venezuela's current situation after Donald Trump's January invasion, said a right-wing government there was still "in transition."

Minas Gerais is the real contest. It has the second-largest electorate after São Paulo, and the Guardian noted that no candidate has won the presidency without carrying it. Bolsonaro led there by just under 5% in the first round. This is the sort of statistic that makes every other state feel like a warm-up act.

The issues are familiar: crime, the economy and the future of the Bolsonaro family. The younger Bolsonaro has promised hundreds of thousands more prison places and cuts to government spending, according to Al Jazeera. Lula's supporters point to the elder Bolsonaro's 27-year sentence for plotting a coup after the 2022 election, and the Guardian said Brazilian press reports suggest some of his fellow plotters could get senior military and police jobs under a new Bolsonaro government. Bolsonaro's supporters, meanwhile, say Lula did nothing for the country, and one told Al Jazeera that welfare breeds dependence.

The voters themselves are not uniformly thrilled. Event organiser Gabriela Ribeiro Viana told AFP that the choice was between "the worst, the horrible, and the lesser evil." She did not say which was which, which is the most democratic thing anyone said all weekend.

Polls open Sunday. After weeks of rallies, a million-person beach and a 10 million-member group chat, the result may come down to a few thousand voters in one state who were, presumably, asleep at the time of the Copacabana rally.
