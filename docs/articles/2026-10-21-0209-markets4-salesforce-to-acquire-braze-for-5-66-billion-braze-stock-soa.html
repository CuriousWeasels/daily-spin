Salesforce Pays $5.66 Billion to Own the Software That Sends Its Customers Emails

Braze shares jumped 51% on a $47.50-per-share cash offer, a day after closing at $30.04, in Salesforce's second big AI-flavored purchase in months.

The wire said: Salesforce announces $5.66 billion deal for Braze, sending BRZE stock soaring 51% · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 21, 2026 02:09 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Salesforce, the giant business-software company, agreed Tuesday to buy Braze, a customer engagement platform that helps companies manage customer data and send marketing messages. The price is about $5.66 billion in cash, and the deal follows Salesforce's roughly $3.6 billion agreement to buy AI customer service platform Fin.

Why it matters

Consolidation among enterprise software vendors shapes what companies pay for marketing tools and which AI products they end up using. For Braze shareholders, it also shows how quickly a stock can reprice when a larger buyer decides it wants what you have.

Salesforce announced Tuesday that it will buy Braze for about $5.66 billion, paying $47.50 in cash for each share of Braze Class A common stock, according to CNBC. Braze closed Monday at $30.04, so the offer was a premium of roughly 58%. Translation: Salesforce looked at the market's price for Braze and politely declined to pay it.

Investors reacted in the way investors do when handed a number larger than the one they had in mind. After a brief premarket trading halt, Braze stock rose more than 51% to close at $45.57. That is $1.93 below the offer price, a gap that is the market's way of saying the deal will probably happen, but that nobody has signed the final paperwork on the universe yet.

The deal is not expected to close until fiscal 2028, and it needs shareholder and regulatory approval, according to a Salesforce blog cited by CNBC. Translation: the people who sold their shares Tuesday at $45.57 were paid in certainty, and the people who hold on are being paid in patience. The spread between the trading price and the offer is what a long wait and some regulatory risk look like when expressed in dollars.

Braze makes artificial intelligence marketing tools that manage customer data and messaging. In plain terms, it is a large part of the reason a retailer knows to send you a message about the sweater you looked at once in March. Braze CEO Bill Magnuson said in a statement that Salesforce's platform will help it "innovate faster," which is the customary sentence an acquired chief executive says and also, in this case, may even be true.

The purchase follows Salesforce's announcement just months ago that it would buy Fin, an AI customer service platform, for about $3.6 billion, a move CNBC said was meant to strengthen its agentic AI offerings. Translation: agentic AI means software that does tasks on its own rather than waiting to be asked. Together the two deals total roughly $9.3 billion, which is a considerable sum to spend on teaching software to talk to customers so employees do not have to.

Salesforce also expanded its partnership with Anthropic in August, including a plugin called "Claudeforce" that helps salespeople write emails and update records. The pattern is fairly legible: rent a frontier AI model, buy the tools that deliver messages, buy the tools that answer replies. The result is a company assembling the full conversation with a customer, from first email to complaint, inside one subscription.

The broader market backdrop is not especially cheap for borrowing or buying. The 10-year Treasury yield stood at 5.15% as of Oct. 19, and the Fed funds rate at 3.88%. Salesforce is paying cash, which makes the deal a bet that a large software company's money is better spent buying growth than sitting somewhere earning interest.

What to watch is the regulatory review, which has until fiscal 2028 to find its opinions. Until then, Braze remains independent, its shareholders remain pleased, and its customers remain on the receiving end of marketing messages from a company that will eventually be owned by another company that sends marketing messages.
