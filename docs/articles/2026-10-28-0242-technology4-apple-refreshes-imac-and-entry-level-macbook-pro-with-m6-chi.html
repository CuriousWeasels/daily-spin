Apple Updates iMac and MacBook Pro With M6 Chip, Skips M5 Entirely

The iMac jumps from the M4 straight to the M6, while the entry-level 14-inch MacBook Pro gets a single chip option and a larger storage ceiling.

The wire said: Apple refreshes the iMac, entry-level MacBook Pro with the M6 chip · Ars Technica

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 28, 2026 02:42 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple introduced its M6 chip in the Mac mini several weeks ago and is now spreading it across more of its computer lineup. The iMac and the entry-level 14-inch MacBook Pro are the latest to get it, according to Ars Technica.

Why it matters

Anyone with an older iMac or an entry-level MacBook Pro now has a clear reason to ask whether their machine is still current. The configuration limits also show how much Apple's naming and port choices decide what you can buy.

Apple announced refreshed versions of the iMac and the entry-level, 14-inch MacBook Pro, both now running the M6 chip, Ars Technica reported. The chip debuted in the Mac mini several weeks ago, so this is less a launch than a rollout. Apple has finished the first showing and is now moving the chip through the rest of the house.

The iMac gets the more dramatic change. Its previous update brought the M4, and Apple skipped the M5 and went straight to the M6. Translation: if you own an M4 iMac, you are now two generations behind, despite the fact that no M5 iMac ever existed for you to be behind.

The iMac comes in two chip variants, and the distinction is oddly specific. Choose the "M6 chip with two Thunderbolt ports" and you get a 10-core CPU and a 10-core GPU, with a ceiling of 24GB of RAM and 1TB of storage. Choose the "M6 chip with four Thunderbolt ports" and you get 12 cores of each, plus up to 32GB of RAM and 2TB of storage.

Translation: the number of ports on the back of your computer now determines how much memory it is allowed to have. Ars Technica did not say why the options are bundled this way. Shoppers who want more memory must also accept more ports, and shoppers who want more ports must also accept more processor cores.

The MacBook Pro is simpler. There is one M6 option, with a 12-core CPU and a 12-core GPU, capped at 32GB of RAM but available with up to 4TB of storage. That is a menu with one chip on it, which is at least easy to read.

As for performance, Ars Technica noted that Apple's claims for the Mac mini applied here: up to 40 percent faster CPU performance, up to double the graphics performance, and nearly five times faster on-device AI, compared with the M4. Those figures largely matched what Ars saw when it tested the M6 Mac mini last month, and it expects new iMac users to see similar numbers. Note that these are comparisons against the M4, so MacBook Pro owners should check which chip they have before doing the math.

The practical advice is the usual one. If your machine works, the M6 will still be there next year, and Apple will still be skipping something.
