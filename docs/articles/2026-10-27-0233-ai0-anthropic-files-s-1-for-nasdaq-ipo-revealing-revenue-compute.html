Anthropic Files to Go Public, Discloses $80.7 Billion Loss and Possible Existential Risk

The AI lab reported $16.3 billion in first-half revenue, a first operating profit and roughly $780 billion in compute bills, with founders keeping 50.1% of the votes.

The wire said: Anthropic officially files for its public debut · TechCrunch AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 27, 2026 02:33 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude models, publicly filed its S-1 (the IPO prospectus) with the SEC after weeks of leaks and an earlier confidential filing in June. It plans to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT.

Why it matters

If the IPO goes ahead, ordinary investors and index funds can own a piece of a leading AI lab, along with its fixed compute obligations. The filing also shows how much of the AI boom rests on one company's growth outrunning its bills.

Anthropic filed its public IPO prospectus on Monday, planning to list on the Nasdaq as "ANT," with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading the offering, according to The Decoder. The company reported $16.3 billion in revenue for the first half of 2026, against $1.2 billion in the same period last year, per TechCrunch. It also reported a net loss of $80.7 billion, which is the sort of number that normally arrives with a bankruptcy notice and not a ticker symbol.

The loss is less dire than it sounds. TechCrunch reported that it was largely driven by noncash accounting adjustments tied to convertible debt, and The Verge, citing earlier Wall Street Journal reporting, said most of it came from accounting charges on notes the company issued for funding. Translation: convertible debt is a loan that can turn into shares, and when the company's value soars, the accounting says the lenders' upside is a cost. Anthropic is, in effect, penalized on paper for being too successful.

The operating picture is better, though the sources differ on the details. TechCrunch put the first-half operating loss at $693 million, down from $2.1 billion a year earlier. The Decoder reported that the second quarter alone produced a first GAAP operating profit of $974 million on $11.6 billion in revenue. These are compatible: a bad first quarter and a strong second one can add up to a smaller loss over six months. GAAP, for the record, means the profit survived standard accounting rules and not just the founders' optimism.

Customers are spending heavily. More than 11,900 pay at least $100,000 a year, about 250 pay $10 million or more, and The Decoder estimated net revenue retention at roughly 900 percent, meaning existing customers spend nine times what they did a year ago. About 80 percent of revenue is billed per token, the small unit of text an AI model processes, so revenue rises only when people actually use the product. No customer exceeds ten percent of revenue, but five together account for 25 percent.

Then come the bills. Anthropic has compute commitments of roughly $780 billion to $810 billion with providers including Broadcom, Google, Amazon and Microsoft, according to The Decoder. The Verge listed some of them: at least $114.5 billion with Google through 2033, $110 billion with Amazon over ten years, $44.9 billion with Microsoft through 2033, and about $3.1 billion a month to SpaceX for Nvidia GPU access once capacity is delivered. Translation: the costs are fixed and long-term, while revenue depends on customers continuing to use the product. The company must therefore keep growing quickly, which is a business plan and also a hostage situation.

Governance is the other notable feature. Seven cofounders, including Dario and Daniela Amodei, will form a "Founder LLC" holding 50.1% of voting power, which Anthropic says protects its AI safety mission. Its Long-Term Benefit Trust will appoint most of the board. TechCrunch noted that voting power will not match economic ownership, leaving public shareholders little practical control over a company that, by its own filing, builds technology posing "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity." Buyers will be offered a stake, but not a steering wheel.

The risk factors are candid. The company cites the possibility of its models developing "forms of sentience," copyright disputes over training data, and "AI-specific taxes." It also said no single insurer, and potentially not the insurance industry in aggregate, could cover a catastrophic AI incident, according to The Verge. Insurance, it turns out, has limits.

Several questions remain open. Anthropic has not said how much it hopes to raise or at what price, and The Decoder noted that third-quarter figures are missing from the filing, though the company already has them. Wall Street expects a valuation around $2 trillion, per Yahoo Finance as cited by The Decoder, up from $965 billion privately. The next filing should supply a price, and investors will find out how much they are willing to pay for a vote they will not have.
