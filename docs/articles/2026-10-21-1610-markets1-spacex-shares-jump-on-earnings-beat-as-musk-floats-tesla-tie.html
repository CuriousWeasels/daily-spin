SpaceX Beats Estimates, Floats Merging With Tesla, Calls Process 'Tap Dancing Through a Minefield'

SpaceX reported $14.6 billion in quarterly revenue and Elon Musk said a Tesla combination has merit, pending committees, a shareholder vote and a high hurdle.

The wire said: SpaceX stock jumps 10% after earnings beat and Musk hints at Tesla tie-up · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 21, 2026 16:10 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

SpaceX went public on the Nasdaq in June in the largest IPO ever, at $150 a share. Speculation about a combination with Tesla has grown as the two companies share chip, AI and product work.

Why it matters

A SpaceX-Tesla merger would be the largest in history and would tie Tesla shareholders to a company whose biggest unit is now AI infrastructure. It also gives the AI cloud trade a data point after a week of worry about how that buildout is financed.

SpaceX stock rose 10% on Wednesday after the company reported third-quarter revenue of $14.6 billion, up 161% from a year earlier, according to CNBC. Analysts surveyed by LSEG had expected $12.72 billion. Elon Musk then used the earnings call to say that merging SpaceX with Tesla is a good idea, which is an unusual thing to say on a call about one company's earnings, in the sense that it is about a second company.

The numbers were strong by any measure. Operating income was $2.8 billion, and net income was $4.4 billion, against a net loss of $1.05 billion a year earlier. Translation: the company that used to lose money launching rockets now makes money renting out computers and satellite internet, which is a more reliable way to be a space company.

The biggest driver is what SpaceX now calls Super Intelligence, or SI. Translation: artificial intelligence, with a rebrand. That segment is now the largest business unit, with revenue up ninefold to $7.56 billion from $813 million. CFO Bret Johnsen credited that growth, along with recurring revenue from Starlink, for the quarter.

SpaceX also guided to first-quarter 2027 revenue of $33 billion to $37 billion. The average analyst estimate, according to LSEG, was $22.2 billion. Guidance is a company's own forecast of its future, and in this case the forecast arrived well above where the people paid to forecast it had landed.

Then came the merger. Musk said the boards of both companies have formed special committees to discuss a consolidation, and that there is merit given that, in his words, 'we are pretty much in the superintelligence singularity.' CNBC also reported he said it would be a good idea, but the shareholders have to be supportive. On the Tesla side that means a vote of 50% of all shares, which he called 'quite a high hurdle.' He described the question as tap dancing through a minefield, which is a fair description of discussing a merger of two public companies on a public call.

There is a logic to the idea, and CNBC lays it out. The companies are building a semiconductor factory with Intel in Grimes County, Texas, and Tesla already uses Grok, the AI technology developed by SpaceX, in customer cars and to help control its Optimus humanoid robots. Musk pointed to overlap in AI software, chip design, Terafab and robotics.

The two companies are in different moods. Tesla stock is down 14% this year, behind all its megacap tech peers, and its revenue fell in three of four quarters of 2025 as Chinese rivals such as BYD and Xiaomi gained ground. Tesla's capital spending is expected to top $25 billion this year, up from about $8.5 billion in 2025. Analysts expect earnings of 43 cents a share and revenue of $27.9 billion when Tesla reports after the close on Wednesday. SpaceX, meanwhile, is trading above its $150 IPO price. In a merger, one of these companies is the more attractive partner, and shareholders can probably guess which.

Analysts also found a broader message. Truist said the call supported 'continued pricing power and shorter payback periods' for AI cloud providers, and that demand looks insatiable, with SpaceX saying it could rent out its capacity several times over. Nebius and Coreweave traded marginally lower, while Iren and Applied Digital rose. Space stocks such as Rocket Lab, Intuitive Machines and AST SpaceMobile also climbed.

What to watch next is Tesla's call tonight, where Musk will again be asked about a merger, and about when Full Self-Driving might become unsupervised. Both questions have been asked before, and the answers have historically arrived on a flexible schedule.
