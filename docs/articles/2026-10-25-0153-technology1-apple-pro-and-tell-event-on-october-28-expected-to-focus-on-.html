Apple Schedules Third Event This Fall to Sell Laptop It Once Said Nobody Wanted

Apple's 'Pro and tell' event on October 28th is expected to deliver a touchscreen OLED MacBook Pro, plus a pricier OLED iPad Mini, according to The Verge.

The wire said: What to expect from Apple’s ‘Pro and tell’ event · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 25, 2026 01:53 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple has announced a 'Pro and tell' event for October 28th at 1PM ET in Los Angeles, shortly after its Welcome Home event and the iPhone Duo announcement. The Verge says the long-rumored touchscreen MacBook Pro is the likeliest star, though nothing is confirmed.

Why it matters

If the rumors hold, buyers face a new category of Mac that is probably expensive, alongside price increases that The Verge attributes to component shortages. Anyone planning a laptop, iMac or iPad Mini purchase may want to wait until Wednesday before paying.

Apple has scheduled a product event for October 28th at 1PM ET in Los Angeles, and The Verge reports it will most likely focus on the MacBook Pro. The event is called 'Pro and tell.' Apple has confirmed nothing about its contents, which is how Apple confirms things.

The headline rumor is a MacBook Pro with an OLED touchscreen. Translation: OLED means each pixel makes its own light, so blacks are properly black and the bill is properly large. The Verge says this device has been in the rumor mill since at least early 2023, which makes it less a product than a long-running tradition. It would be Apple's first touchscreen laptop and first OLED laptop, and it supposedly gets a Dynamic Island, the iPhone's pill-shaped cutout.

Do not expect an iPad-Mac hybrid. The Verge says the interface will not be dramatically revamped, with perhaps a magnification effect when you touch the screen and some new gestures in native apps. A colleague at The Verge separately argues that macOS 27 contains hints of touch-friendly changes, and hopes for Apple Pencil support, including on the trackpad. That writer also says he does not believe Apple will build a standard 2-in-1 convertible, a design Windows makers have sold for years.

The chip situation is less tidy. The touchscreen model is expected to keep the M5 Pro and M5 Max, and there may be no M6 Pro or Max at all, with Apple possibly waiting for the M7 next year. Meanwhile, the base MacBook Pro could move to the M6, which debuted in the Mac Mini in August, and may get a hole-punch camera. The iMac is also expected to skip the M5 entirely and go straight to the M6, nearly two years after its last revision, with possibly new colors as its main visible change.

Then there is the price. The Verge says the base MacBook Pro is expected to cost more as component shortages ripple through the lineup. The OLED iPad Mini, which would swap its LCD for a better screen and its aging A17 chip for the A20 from the iPhone 18, may cost about $100 more. Translation: you are being asked to pay extra for a screen, a chip, and the global parts supply.

The Verge's opinion writer notes that Apple's recent design record is mixed, citing the HomeView's lack of apps, a somewhat messy iPhone Duo, and the not-so-distant butterfly keyboard fiasco. After years of insisting on keeping touch away from the Mac, he says, Apple has to make the change worth it.

Apple will find out Wednesday whether customers agree. Until then, the company's third event of the season has the advantage of a very low bar: the laptop has been rumored for so long that merely existing would count as news.
