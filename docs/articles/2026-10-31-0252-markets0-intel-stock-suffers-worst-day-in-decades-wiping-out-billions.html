Intel Stock Falls 29%, Worst Day Since 1970s, Dragging Government Stake Down With It

A weak forecast, a shuttered Israeli plant and a PC market squeezed by memory prices erased about $12 billion from the U.S. government's holding in a single session.

The wire said: Why Intel’s stock saw its worst day in decades — and a $140 billion wipeout · MarketWatch

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 31, 2026 02:52 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Intel shares had risen about 150% this year on demand for processors used in AI workloads. Then the company gave a forecast that came in well below what analysts expected.

Why it matters

Intel is the only U.S.-based maker of advanced logic chips, and the government and Nvidia both own large stakes. A sharp drop shows how much pension funds, taxpayers and index investors now depend on one turnaround story.

Intel shares fell 29% on Friday, their worst day since the 1970s, after the chipmaker issued a forecast weaker than Wall Street expected, CNBC reported. The company blamed a plant shutdown in Israel and continuing softness in the PC market. A stock that gained about 150% this year before earnings gave back a meaningful share of that in roughly six and a half hours.

The numbers explain the mood. Intel said it expects adjusted earnings per share of 25 cents this quarter on revenue of $15 billion to $16 billion. Analysts surveyed by LSEG had expected 43 cents and $17.1 billion. Translation: Intel did not narrowly miss the bar. It walked under it, and the bar was set by people who get paid to be right.

The PC business is the main drag. CNBC reported that soaring costs for memory and other components are hammering the market, and Counterpoint Research said global PC shipments fell 4% in the second quarter from a year earlier. MarketWatch added that corporate IT departments have less budget for laptops. In plain terms, memory got expensive, so computers got expensive, so companies are keeping the old ones a while longer. Intel's finance chief, David Zinsner, said the slowdown should last at least through the first half of next year.

The Israel plant adds a separate cost. An explosion at a fab earlier this month killed two workers and seriously injured a third. Zinsner said the shutdown will cost about $600 million in lost revenue over two quarters, and CEO Lip-Bu Tan called it an 'unfortunate and tragic accident.' The plant's extended shutdown affects shipments of 10-nanometer chips.

The share price also matters to the owners. The Trump administration steered the U.S. government into a 10% stake over a year ago, and CNBC reported that the holding lost about $12 billion in value on Friday, though it remains profitable overall. Nvidia, which also took a large stake, lost about $6 billion. Being a strategic shareholder means getting the geopolitical benefits, and also the daily quote.

Wider markets did not help. The Nasdaq Composite fell 2.9% on Friday after a week of rough tech earnings. Alphabet had its worst day on record on Thursday, down more than 12%, and fell again Friday. Amazon slipped 1.4% after missing on revenue and profit while keeping its 2026 capital spending forecast at $220 billion and reporting negative free cash flow for a second straight quarter. Nvidia fell 4%, Broadcom more than 5% and Qualcomm 11%. MarketWatch reported that AMD also slid, as investors wait to learn whether it faces the same problems.

Analysts were split on how worried to be. D.A. Davidson downgraded Intel to neutral in a report titled 'Not fixed yet,' saying the current multiple is hard to justify given the weakness. Translation: the stock was priced for a recovery and received a delay notice. Matt Orton of Raymond James told CNBC he does not see a 'moment of reckoning,' but that positioning in the sector remains crowded.

The turnaround case still rests on manufacturing. CNBC reported that Intel is in high-volume production of its 18A chip at a new Arizona fab, but it still has no external customers for its new chips. Tan said on the call that a couple of customers should be ready to announce over the next two quarters. Investors have now heard the promise and will wait to see names attached to it.
