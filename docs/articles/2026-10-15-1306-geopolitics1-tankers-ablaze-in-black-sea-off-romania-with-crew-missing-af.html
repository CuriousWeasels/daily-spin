Black Sea Tanker Fires Test NATO's Favorite Tool: A Meeting About Having a Meeting

After a drone strike left eight of 12 crew missing off Romania, two NATO members are weighing Article 4, which permits consultations and little else.

The wire said: Eight crew missing after drone attack leaves tanker ablaze off Romania's coast · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 15, 2026 13:06 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Russia and Ukraine have intensified strikes on merchant ships in the Black Sea, with several vessels hit off Bulgaria and Romania in recent days. Russia says it targets weapons cargo, while Kyiv and its allies say grain and civilian ships are being attacked.

Why it matters

Attacks on commercial shipping threaten the lives of crews from many countries and disrupt grain and energy supplies, which can raise freight costs and food prices far from the Black Sea. They also push the war closer to NATO members' own waters.

The San Marino-flagged tanker Wine Trader caught fire about 13 nautical miles east of the Danube's Sulina mouth on Thursday, and eight of its 12 crew are missing, according to the BBC. Romanian President Nicușor Dan later confirmed the ship was struck by drones. Four crew are confirmed alive, and Romania has sent two Black Hawk helicopters and two rescue boats into very high waves. The search is the one part of this story with a clear objective.

DW gave a somewhat different count, reporting five of 12 safe and seven missing, and noted that Romanian officials had not explicitly said drones caused the blazes. Defense Minister Radu Miruta told Digi24: "Whether it was struck by a drone or not, an investigation is currently underway." Romanian media cited by the BBC quoted nearby crews as saying two drones hit the ship. Both outlets agree that a second tanker, the Yara J, burned earlier in the same area, and that its crew was reported safe.

The leaders of Romania and Bulgaria are weighing whether to invoke Article 4 of the NATO treaty. Translation: Article 4 lets a member request consultations with allies when it feels its territory or security is threatened. It is the alliance's way of saying something is wrong, short of the clause that obliges anyone to do anything. Dan is due to meet NATO chief Mark Rutte in Brussels with Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev, and said the talks would cover equipment to protect shipping. He also said: "there will be a response from the Romanian state."

The pattern is hard to miss. On Tuesday two merchant ships were hit off Bulgaria and one sank, leaving 11 crew missing, according to the BBC, and Bulgaria summoned Russia's envoy. DW counts at least five other cargo ships struck off Romania and Bulgaria in 10 days, with at least 23 sailors lost. Moscow says it hit two cargo ships carrying military cargo to Odesa, without naming them. Kyiv and its allies say civilian vessels are being attacked, and Ukraine also struck an oil tanker at Sochi last week.

Ships are crowding near Romania and Bulgaria for a reason. Russian strikes on Ukrainian ports have pushed Ukrainian vessels toward the Danube and coastal routes near both NATO members, according to DW. Russian drones have also repeatedly strayed into Romanian airspace over four years. Put a war zone's shipping lane next to two NATO coastlines, and incidents become a matter of geometry.

Diplomacy is busy. Bulgaria says the EU summit will discuss a proposed moratorium on attacks on civilian ships. India, which supplies over 12% of the world's seafarers, is pressing both sides for safe passage, and Ukraine's foreign minister called its proposal the broadest of four offers. Russia has not endorsed a binding deal, and has repeatedly rejected calls for a naval ceasefire, blaming Ukraine for hitting Russian merchant ships. President Trump announced an energy ceasefire over the weekend, though its scope and mechanism remain unclear, and Ukraine alleges Russia has already breached it.

The costs reach well beyond the sea. DW reports that attacks have sharply cut grain shipments from both Ukraine and Russia, and an Indian economist warned of higher freight on Russian crude and pressure on edible oil prices. In practice, a drone off Sulina can reach a grocery bill in Delhi.

Next to watch: whether Romania and Bulgaria formally request Article 4 consultations, and whether the EU's proposed moratorium becomes something a drone operator is told about. Consultations are guaranteed to take place. Whether the drones read the minutes is a separate question.
