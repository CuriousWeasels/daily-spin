Trump Calls Ukraine's Refinery Strikes a 'Disgrace' After Buying Diesel From the Refineries' Owner

Zelensky says Kyiv is only defending itself, while Washington's new fuel deal with Moscow turns Russian oil infrastructure into something of an American supplier.

The wire said: Zelensky says Ukraine has right to defend itself after Trump calls latest strikes a 'disgrace' · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 16, 2026 15:38 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Ukraine has been striking Russian oil facilities while both sides hit each other's energy infrastructure. Last week Trump announced an energy ceasefire, which Moscow said it never agreed to, and Kyiv resumed its strikes after three days.

Why it matters

US fuel prices have risen sharply since the war with Iran began in February, and a diesel deal with Russia is now colliding with Ukraine's targeting of Russian refineries. For ordinary drivers and truckers, the price at the pump has become entangled with a war's targeting list.

President Donald Trump has called Ukraine's latest strikes on Russian oil refineries a "disgrace", and President Volodymyr Zelensky has replied that he is "only standing up for Ukraine's interests", the BBC reported. Trump made the comments in an interview with Newsmax, saying Zelensky was "more difficult to deal with than" Vladimir Putin. It is a rare diplomatic position: criticizing the country being invaded for hitting the country doing the invading.

The reason is diesel. According to the BBC, the US recently struck a deal with Moscow under which 300,000 tonnes of Russian diesel would go to "the American and global marketplace". Translation: the United States has become a customer of the refineries that Ukraine is trying to set on fire. Trump's complaint is that Ukraine's strikes could stop Moscow from delivering its side of the bargain.

The motive is domestic. The BBC says US fuel and especially diesel prices have skyrocketed since the war with Iran began in February, hurting Trump's standing with voters. Midterm elections are next month. Seen from that angle, a Ukrainian drone over a Russian refinery is not a military operation but a campaign problem with a flight path.

Zelensky has called the diesel deal a gift to Putin that would "put more money straight into Putin's pocket to fund this war". He also said he could not accept it because a gas agreement could come next. "If I don't stand up to this... who else will?" he told the EU summit in Brussels. Asked whether Trump was using him as a scapegoat for domestic political problems, he sat through a 15-second pause, then grinned and said, "This is my answer."

The energy ceasefire that Trump announced last week did not survive contact with the other party. Kyiv said it would stop if Russia did, Moscow said it had never agreed, and Russian attacks continued. After three days Ukraine resumed strikes, and NPR reported drones hitting a refinery in Russia's Ryazan region. Russian authorities also said a major refinery southeast of Moscow was struck, which they described as retaliation for attacks on Ukrainian energy infrastructure.

Washington has other ways of expressing displeasure. Starlink, the US-owned internet network that is critical to Ukraine's military, was restricted this week. Zelensky called the slowdown a "very dangerous and unpleasant surprise" and said speeds were still not fully restored. Residents and the government said several deadly Russian strikes on Tuesday night came with no prior warning. The Trump administration had also previously warned it could withhold intelligence-sharing if Kyiv kept hitting the refineries.

Europe, meanwhile, offered a sympathetic audience and a smaller cheque than the war requires. Zelensky told the European Council the war would cost €155bn next year, with at least €70bn needed from the EU for defence and weapons. He said Ukraine could make up to three times more drones but lacks the money. He arrived saying it was good to be among "real leaders", a remark the BBC noted as a possible slight to Trump.

The fighting continues regardless of the diplomacy. Russia's defence ministry said 644 Ukrainian drones were intercepted over 26 regions, and Moscow's airspace was closed for several hours. In Ukraine, local governors said about 270,000 households lost power in the Volyn region and several people were killed in a Russian drone strike on Kharkiv. BBC Verify reported that Russia has nearly finished a new drone base near Millerovo in six weeks, which one analyst said signals a higher tempo of strikes this winter.

The next test is whether Moscow can deliver diesel while its refineries are being hit, and whether Washington decides which of these facts to blame first. Zelensky has made clear he does not intend to stop.
