Ukraine Ends Three-Day Energy Ceasefire Russia Never Agreed To, Resumes Refinery Strikes

Zelensky says Kyiv is 'done' pausing attacks after Moscow kept hitting Ukrainian power plants, while accusing Washington of cutting intelligence and throttling Starlink to push it along.

The wire said: Russia accuses Ukraine of attacking oil refinery after Zelensky says strikes to resume · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 15, 2026 13:06 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

US President Donald Trump announced an 'energy ceasefire' on Sunday that the Kremlin never publicly confirmed. Ukraine says it paused strikes on Russian energy sites for about three days while Russian attacks on its own grid continued.

Why it matters

Refinery strikes squeeze Russian fuel revenue, but the US is trying to lower pump prices before the midterms, so Kyiv's battlefield strategy now collides with Washington's fuel politics. Ukraine's air defences also depend on US intelligence and satellite links, which gives that collision real consequences.

Ukraine has resumed attacks on Russian oil refineries, hours after President Volodymyr Zelensky told the Financial Times that Kyiv was 'done' with an energy ceasefire. Russia's defence ministry said Ukraine attempted a large-scale attack on a refinery in Ryazan, south of Moscow, and that falling drone debris started a fire. Moscow said it intercepted four missiles and dozens of drones. The ceasefire, in short, lasted as long as only one side was observing it.

According to the BBC, Zelensky said he restricted Ukrainian strikes for three days while Russia kept hitting Ukrainian energy infrastructure. He said strikes left hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians without power and that Russia ignored the proposal within hours. The BBC notes the Kremlin never said it agreed to the deal. Translation: an energy ceasefire is a truce in which one party announces it, a second party complies, and a third party does not attend.

The dispute is partly about diesel. The BBC reported a US-Russia arrangement to release 300,000 tonnes of diesel to the US and global market; Al Jazeera put the figure at 1.8 million tonnes. The sources disagree on the number, though not on the motive. The BBC describes the deal as Trump's latest effort to lower fuel prices before the midterm elections, after the Iran war pushed up petrol and especially diesel costs.

Zelensky called this the US effectively investing in Russia's war effort. 'The question will be: when will the next deal be?' he told the FT. 'Maybe it will be gas. Definitely it will be gas.' His case is that Ukraine hits refineries to cut the revenue funding Russia's war, while Washington would like those same refineries to keep producing diesel that Americans buy.

Zelensky also accused the US of withdrawing intelligence-sharing on Russian strikes to pressure Kyiv. He quoted US counterparts as saying: 'if you will not stop [the strikes], we will block intelligence.' Trump denied halting intelligence-sharing. Translation: Ukraine relies on American early-warning alerts to know which missiles are coming, so a pause in them is a significant form of persuasion.

Starlink added to the confusion. The BBC reported that a speed restriction on drones using the service on Tuesday initially looked like a technical glitch. Elon Musk appeared to deny that and point to the Trump administration, and Al Jazeera reported he wrote that only the US military and Trump had the authority to limit Starshield. 'It was not a technical problem, it was the will,' Zelensky said. Ukrainian defence sources told the BBC the function had since been restored and officials were still seeking the reasons for the restrictions. Musk separately wrote 'Time for Zelensky to go,' and Al Jazeera noted that Trump called on Saturday for Zelensky to be replaced.

The cost of the past day was counted in people, not just infrastructure. Local officials reported at least 13 people killed across Ukraine, and Zelensky said more than 850 drones were launched at dozens of cities. In Kirovohrad, nearly 360 drones struck in a 10-hour barrage, the region's largest attack since the full-scale invasion began, and at least three people died there. Deaths were also reported in Donetsk, Kherson and Volyn.

Zelensky says he would pause refinery strikes again only if the US supplied more Patriot missiles, which the BBC says have been in short supply since the Iran war drained stockpiles. He met UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham in London, who said the UK would speed up delivery of lightweight multirole missiles. European leaders meet in Brussels on Thursday. The EU has pledged $50 billion for Ukraine next year, against the $175 billion Kyiv plans to spend on defence, according to Al Jazeera.

Ukraine's position is now fairly clear: it will stop hitting Russian fuel if someone supplies the means to defend its own. Whether Brussels can supply that, given a gap of $125 billion, is the next thing to watch.
