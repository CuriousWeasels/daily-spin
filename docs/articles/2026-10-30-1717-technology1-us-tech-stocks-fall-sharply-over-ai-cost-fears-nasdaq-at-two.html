Tech Stocks Slide as Investors Notice AI Costs Money Before It Makes Any

Intel, Amazon, Alphabet and others fell sharply after earnings showed heavy AI spending, with the Nasdaq opening down 2.5% at a two-month low.

The wire said: US tech stocks see sharp falls over AI cost fears · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 30, 2026 17:17 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Big technology companies have been spending heavily on artificial intelligence infrastructure, and investors have so far been willing to wait for the payoff. This week's earnings reports tested that patience.

Why it matters

Index funds and retirement accounts hold large stakes in these companies, so a loss of confidence in the AI build-out can move ordinary savings. It also hints at whether the spending boom that has driven the market can continue.

US tech stocks kept falling on Friday as worries grew over the rising cost of the artificial intelligence build-out, the BBC reported. The Nasdaq opened down 2.5%, its lowest level in more than two months. Investors had spent months asking when AI would pay for itself, and this week several companies answered with a bill.

Intel was the worst case. Its shares fell as much as 30% in early trading after the chipmaker warned of a weaker computer market while still planning to spend more on AI. The BBC said Intel was on track to lose more than $130bn in value. Finance chief David Zinsner said the company did not expect to bring in more cash than it spent this year, and forecast lower-than-expected fourth-quarter revenue. Translation: the company expects to spend slightly more than it earns, and it would like you to see this as a plan.

Zinsner also said, "we see the PC slowdown continuing at least through the first half of next year." He added that equipment investment had risen significantly in the third quarter and would be "even more significant" in the next three months. So demand for the core product is soft, and the response is to buy more equipment. This is the corporate version of buying a bigger restaurant because the dining room is empty.

Amazon revealed on Thursday that it, too, was spending more cash than it was making, and that spending had risen six-fold over three months. Its earnings fell short of expectations. The shares dropped as far as 6% overnight, recovered somewhat, and were down about 1.6% on Friday.

Alphabet had the largest dollar loss. Its value fell by more than $500bn on Thursday after it disclosed that its own AI summaries were affecting core search advertising revenue, and it slipped another 1% on Friday. Translation: Google built a tool that answers questions directly, and people are now clicking fewer of the ads next to the answers. The company is, in effect, competing with its own business model, and the business model is losing.

Others felt the effect downstream. Reddit fell 26% after reporting weaker daily user figures and warning that Google's growing AI-driven search was hurting referral traffic. That means fewer people are arriving from search results. Roblox dropped nearly 20% as its daily active users declined.

Chris Low of FHN Financial told Reuters the large companies were "showing some pretty significant growing pains as they aggressively pursue the AI build-out." He said the fear was that "the entire sector is going to slow down." Microsoft also reported this week, and analysts said there were questions about the payout from all the investment.

Nobody is claiming that AI has failed. The complaint is narrower and more awkward: the spending is arriving this quarter and the profits are scheduled for a later one. Investors, who like both of those things in the right order, will be watching the next round of earnings for a change in sequence.
