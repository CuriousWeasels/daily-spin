South Korea Finance Minister Says Chip Boom Will Last Through Next Year, Citing Shortage of Chips

Lee Hyoung-Il tells CNBC the supply-demand gap is wide, even as a local newspaper column warns of a 'red ocean' by 2029.

The wire said: Semiconductor market to remain solid ‘at least through next year,’ South Korea finance minister says · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 18, 2026 14:56 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Semiconductor stocks fell sharply this week on worries about interest rate hikes and growing competition from Chinese firms, dragging down SK Hynix, Seoul Semiconductor and Nvidia. South Korea's market is unusually dependent on chipmakers.

Why it matters

When one industry makes up a large share of a national economy, its mood swings become the country's mood swings, and the tax receipts that fund public services follow. Investors holding chip stocks, directly or through index funds, are exposed to the same dependency.

South Korea's finance minister, Lee Hyoung-Il, said on Saturday that the semiconductor boom should last at least through 2027. He told CNBC on the sidelines of the IMF-World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok that the gap between chip demand and supply is still very wide. The remark arrived after a week in which chip stocks fell sharply, which is the kind of timing that makes a reassurance sound like a reassurance.

Lee's reasoning is straightforward: demand exceeds supply, and supply is still being expanded. Translation: there are not enough chips, and factories are being built to make more, which is both the reason the boom continues and, in other circumstances, the usual way booms end. Lee did not dwell on the second half of that sentence.

The week's sell-off, according to CNBC, was driven by investor worries about interest rate hikes and rising competition from Chinese companies. SK Hynix and Seoul Semiconductor fell alongside U.S. giants such as Nvidia. For context, the U.S. Fed funds rate stands at 3.88%, the 10-year Treasury yields 5.1%, and inflation was 3.74% as of the latest reading, so the rate-hike anxiety is not coming from nowhere.

Lee also addressed the volatility of South Korea's stock market this year. He blamed the chip sector's large weight in the market, noting that semiconductors make up a bigger share of the economy there than in other countries. Translation: when one industry is that large, the national index mostly measures how that industry feels today. Lee still called the broader economy 'solid.'

A less festive view appeared Thursday in the Maeil Business Newspaper, a prominent South Korean publication. According to a CNBC translation, a column urged the government to prepare for an eventual slowdown in the artificial intelligence boom. It cited two experts who expect a 'flood of new supply' that would turn the memory market 'into a red ocean' by 2029. A red ocean, in business jargon, is one where many competitors fight over the same customers until the water is the color of margins.

The minister's timeline and the columnist's are not strictly in conflict. Lee said the growth lasts through next year, while the column pointed to 2029. That leaves roughly two years in which everyone is correct and nobody has to plan, which is a comfortable place for a government to be.

The fiscal side is where Lee sounded most content. He said the country expects to collect more tax than initially projected this year and will end up in a stronger fiscal position because of the chip boom. In other words, the stock market gets the nerves and the treasury gets the money.

What to watch: whether the new factory capacity arrives before the demand leaves. Both are possible. Only one of them shows up in the budget.
