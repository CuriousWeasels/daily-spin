Microsoft Offers 60,000 Staff a Way Out, Keeps GPUs for Those Who Stay

The company is extending voluntary buyouts to about a quarter of its workforce in four countries, as it tries to pay for AI infrastructure by employing fewer humans.

The wire said: Microsoft’s AI future has fewer jobs · The Verge AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 30, 2026 03:06 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Microsoft has cut jobs repeatedly over the past couple of years while spending heavily on AI infrastructure. This week, according to The Verge, it widened an earlier retirement offer into a much larger voluntary exit program.

Why it matters

The biggest software companies are testing whether they can fund an AI buildout by shrinking payrolls, and workers elsewhere will be watching how it goes. For Microsoft employees, the practical question is whether to take a standard severance package now or wait to see what comes next.

Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to around 60,000 employees across the US, UK, Canada, and India, The Verge reported. That is roughly a quarter of the company, and it is the first time Microsoft has made such an offer to so many people. It expands on a retirement offer made earlier this year to about 9,000 employees. Nobody is being told to leave, which is a distinction of real comfort to the people being asked.

The stated logic is arithmetic. According to The Verge, the effort is meant to avoid cutting AI infrastructure investment by lowering operating costs and headcount. Translation: data centers are expensive, and the cheapest line item to shrink is the one that eats lunch.

CEO Satya Nadella sketched this plan almost two years ago on a podcast. "Our total people costs will go down, our cost per head will go up, and my GPU per researcher will go up," he said. Translation: fewer people, paid more, each supplied with more computing power. It is the rare corporate forecast that the company has gone on to follow carefully.

The numbers support it. Microsoft employed about 223,000 people as of June 30th, down 5,000 from 228,000 in 2025, according to The Verge. It is the first year-over-year decline in a decade. The new offer arrives months after Microsoft laid off 4,800 employees, hitting Xbox and commercial sales teams hardest, and the company laid off 4 percent of staff in July last year. The annual layoff, once a predictable seasonal ritual, now appears closer to a subscription service.

Whether the buyouts will thin the ranks much is unclear. More than 30 percent of the 9,000 employees eligible for the earlier retirement offer accepted it. The Verge expects far fewer to take this one, because sources say its terms are practically the same as regular Microsoft severance: cash based on time at the company, accelerated stock vesting, some subsidized health coverage, and several months of career services. Translation: it is the standard exit package, now with a voluntary label. Even a 5 percent uptake would mean 3,000 departures. Employees have until December to decide, and The Verge notes rumors of further layoffs in November and January. That timing gives the word voluntary a certain flexibility.

Some people who know the company think it has too many staff. "Microsoft needs fewer people," said Merill Fernando, a former Microsoft Entra product manager, who described "layers on layers" and silos. Nadella has acknowledged the internal-rivalry meme, saying last year that "social cohesion is not a goal, winning in the marketplace is a goal." That is a notable position for a CEO often credited with building a culture around empathy.

The Verge frames the open question as whether Nadella can remake Microsoft for AI without dismantling that culture. Employees deciding by December will have to answer a simpler one: whether to take the offer on the table or bet that the next one is better. Management has made its position on the GPUs perfectly clear.
