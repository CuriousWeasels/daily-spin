Chip Index Posts Longest Losing Streak Since 2008 as AI Trade Meets Its Invoice

The Nasdaq had its worst week since June, the VIX topped 22, and traders bought bets that fear will last through November.

The wire said: Chip index SOX posts longest losing streak since 2008 as AI worries deepen · Investing.com

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 18, 2026 01:40 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Stocks tied to artificial intelligence sold off last week as inflation ran hotter than expected and worries grew about how much debt companies are taking on to build AI infrastructure. Chip stocks, neocloud providers like CoreWeave and cybersecurity names were hit hardest.

Why it matters

If inflation keeps borrowing costs high, the debt-funded AI buildout gets more expensive, and the index funds in many retirement accounts hold a lot of AI-linked stocks. Diversification, long considered boring, briefly looked clever.

The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index, known as SOX, posted its longest losing streak since 2008, according to Investing.com. CNBC reported that the iShares Semiconductor ETF fell nine straight sessions, its longest run in more than a decade. Translation: an ETF is a basket of stocks you can buy in one click, and this basket went down nine days in a row.

The Nasdaq fell 4.5% for the week, ending a four-week winning streak and marking its worst week since June, CNBC said. The S&P 500 fell 2%, while the Dow rose 0.7% as investors moved into healthcare, consumer staples and other sectors that do not depend on a chatbot. The Dow, a collection of old companies that sell things people physically need, had a rare moment of being the sensible one.

The sell-off sped up Wednesday after September consumer prices rose 3.7% from a year earlier, against the 3.6% economists expected. Translation: prices are rising faster than hoped, so the Federal Reserve may keep interest rates high for longer. The next day, producer prices, what businesses pay for inputs, jumped 1.3% on the month and 6.3% on the year. CNBC noted those were the largest annual and monthly gains since March 2022 and December 2022, respectively, and said elevated oil prices were part of the cause.

That matters because the AI buildout runs on borrowed money. Broadcom CEO Hock Tan warned Monday that access to capital could become the industry's next major constraint, CNBC reported. In an industry that has spent years saying the only limit was chips, discovering that the limit is also money is a modest plot twist.

CoreWeave, a so-called neocloud that rents out computing power, was the week's cautionary tale. CNBC said its total debt has risen to more than $51 billion and its stock fell 25% for the week, including a 13% drop on Friday. More worrying than the stock, the network said, were rising yields on its corporate bonds and a spike in credit default swaps. Translation: a credit default swap is insurance against a borrower not paying you back, and the price of that insurance went up.

Memory chipmakers Micron, Sandisk, Western Digital and Seagate also fell after China's CXMT announced progress on a more efficient chip structure, which raised fears of more competition and a glut of supply. Cybersecurity stocks suffered too. Palo Alto Networks lost more than 18% and CrowdStrike more than 17%, partly on reports that OpenAI is in early talks to buy Trail of Bits, CNBC said.

The Cboe Volatility Index, or VIX, often called Wall Street's fear gauge, rose above 22 for the first time in four months. CNBC reported that one trader bought 9,200 November 20-strike calls for $1.7 million and 12,500 23-strike calls for $1.1 million. Translation: these are bets that the VIX will still be elevated in mid-November, which is a way of paying money to be nervous on schedule. Of the ten most popular VIX contracts Friday, nine were calls.

Not everyone had a bad week. Meta rallied on new AI models before ending the week down slightly, and Microsoft hit a record high Tuesday for the first time in nearly a year after a drop of more than a third. Goldman Sachs reported equities trading revenue up 72%, though bank shares still struggled, which CNBC's Jim Cramer attributed to worry about what lies ahead rather than what banks just earned.

The latest official data shows the VIX at 16.46 on October 15, well below the intraday highs CNBC described, so the fear gauge has already cooled. The 10-year Treasury yield was 5.1% and Brent crude was 126.55, which is a lot of reasons for the Fed to stay put. Next week Tesla, SpaceX and Meta report earnings, and the market will find out whether it can still be impressed by large numbers.
