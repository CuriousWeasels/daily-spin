OpenAI Hunts $30 Billion Privately as Anthropic Tests Whether Public Will Pay $1.5 Trillion

With OpenAI ruling out a 2026 listing and Anthropic's valuation range reportedly cut, the AI IPO season looks like a season of one.

The wire said: OpenAI races to raise $30 bln ahead of Anthropic IPO, Reuters reports · Investing.com

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 20, 2026 02:16 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Investors entered 2026 expecting a wave of mega-cap listings from SpaceX, Anthropic and OpenAI. SpaceX went public in June, OpenAI says it won't this year, and Anthropic confidentially filed its prospectus with the SEC in early June.

Why it matters

If the biggest AI companies can't comfortably tap public markets, they lean on private investors, and anyone whose retirement fund owns AI stocks is left watching one company's valuation set the mood for the whole sector.

Reuters reported, per an Investing.com headline, that OpenAI is racing to raise $30 billion ahead of an Anthropic IPO. The headline is all the source supplies, so details of the terms remain unreported here. The logic, however, is visible: when a company decides not to go public, it has to find its money somewhere else, and that somewhere is a smaller room of very wealthy people.

CNBC reported that OpenAI has effectively ruled out a 2026 debut. According to CNBC, CEO Sam Altman said last month it didn't make sense to go public amid concerns over AI safety. Translation: the company that needs enormous sums of capital has concluded that the best place to avoid scrutiny is the place where scrutiny is least required. Traders on Kalshi, a prediction market, give OpenAI a 3% chance of listing this year and only even odds of an official announcement by June 2027.

That leaves Anthropic. CNBC said it is the one major name Kalshi traders still expect to announce an IPO in 2026, at 75% odds as of Monday morning. Of 23 open companies on the market, it is the only one above 50%. Plaid fell from 50% in January to 10%. Deel went from 50-50 in February to 3%. Databricks dropped from more than 50% to 1%. Translation: a prediction market is a place where people attach money to their opinions, which is roughly what an IPO is, but with fewer lawyers.

The mood soured last week. Anthropic disclosed third-quarter revenue of $16.8 billion, which CNBC said showed weaker quarter-over-quarter growth than earlier periods. 22V Research analyst Dauvin Peterson wrote that questions emerged about the slowdown and about pricing pressure on large language models. He called the revised IPO valuation range, starting at $1.5 trillion against a prior expectation of at least $2 trillion, disappointing. It is a rare case in which a company is described as a letdown for being worth only one and a half trillion dollars.

The wider IPO picture is thinner than the headlines suggest. Renaissance Capital data cited by CNBC show proceeds are up sharply, driven by mega-cap debuts such as SpaceX, yet the number of U.S. IPOs priced this year is down 33% from 2025. Translation: the total is large because a few deals were enormous, much as an average salary looks healthy when one person at the party is a billionaire.

Market conditions are not offering much help. The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 5.09% and the 2-year at 4.74%, with the Fed funds rate at 3.88% and CPI inflation at 3.74%. Translation: safe government bonds pay well, so a company selling shares must persuade buyers that its future profits beat a guaranteed 5%. SPY closed at 766.48 on Oct. 19 and the VIX, a gauge of expected stock volatility, was 19.99, which is calm enough to hold an IPO and not calm enough to enjoy it.

What to watch is whether Anthropic's listing proceeds at the lowered range, and whether OpenAI's private round closes. If both happen, AI will have shown that it can raise money from anyone, at a price that depends on whom you ask.
