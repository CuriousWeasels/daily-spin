Tesla Roadster Flies, Drives Upside Down, Reaches 60 mph in Under a Second; Price Not Discussed

Nine years after the first announcement, Tesla's demo in Waco, Texas delivered spectacle and skipped the price, trim levels and delivery date.

The wire said: The Tesla Roadster flew, drove upside down, and hit 60mph in under a second · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 16, 2026 01:49 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Tesla first teased a next-generation Roadster in 2017, promising deliveries by 2020. Customers paid deposits, the deadlines slipped, and the car became a running joke about vaporware.

Why it matters

Early depositors have had their money tied up for years with no delivery date, and the demo gave them no new information on when or whether the car will ship. For everyone else, it is a reminder that a stunt is not a production schedule.

Tesla demonstrated its next-generation Roadster at an event in Waco, Texas, where the electric supercar drove upside down, briefly flew, and accelerated to 60 mph in under a second, according to The Verge. The event ran less than 30 minutes. It covered what the car can do and left out what it costs, which is the part most buyers ask about first.

The Verge reported that the presentation felt more like a stunt show than a product reveal, with no mention of price, trim levels or other details. Translation: trim levels are the different versions of a car you can buy. Tesla chose to show the version that exists only in a demonstration.

The history helps. The Roadster was announced in 2017 as a surprise at the Tesla Semi event, with promised specs of 0-60 mph in 1.9 seconds, a top speed above 250 mph, a 200 kWh battery good for up to 620 miles, and a $200,000 starting price. Elon Musk said deliveries would begin in 2020. The Verge notes that this sounded doable at the time.

Customers took him at his word. Early adopters put down $50,000 deposits, or $250,000 for the limited Founders Series. The Verge describes these as effectively interest-free loans that helped fund Tesla's expansion. Translation: customers lent the company money in exchange for a place in line, and the line did not move.

Meanwhile, Tesla focused on mass-market cars like the Model Y and on the complicated Cybertruck. When Musk remembered the Roadster, he raised the stakes. That led to the 'SpaceX package,' which adds cold-gas thrusters for hover capability and a 0-60 time under one second. Translation: cold-gas thrusters are compressed-air rockets. They push the car without burning fuel, which is how a car gets to leave the ground.

Musk has also played down the car's importance, calling it a 'small potato situation' that will not 'move the needle in a major way financially,' per The Verge. He has even questioned whether it is 'even a car anymore.' This is a useful position for a product that has not shipped: if it is not really a car, it cannot really be late. Some analysts, The Verge says, theorize the revival sets the stage for a rumored Tesla-SpaceX merger.

The competition did not wait. The Verge points to Rimac's records with the Nevera, Porsche's refinement of electric performance with the Taycan, and Chinese makers such as BYD building track-ready supercars at a fraction of the cost. The outlet's verdict is that the Roadster is now playing catch-up to an industry that spent nine years building what Tesla merely rendered.

The Verge's story was still marked as developing. The question for depositors is simple and was not answered in Waco: when does a car that can fly become a car that can be delivered?
