OpenAI Trims Its Price Tag to $1.25 Trillion, Which Is Still Most Expensive Ever

The ChatGPT maker is reportedly raising up to $30 billion in a bridge round led by Coatue and UAE investors, after pitching a higher valuation and meeting some fatigue.

The wire said: OpenAI working on new $30B raise at $1.25T valuation ahead of IPO · TechCrunch AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 24, 2026 02:07 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

OpenAI filed confidential IPO paperwork in June, after a March raise valued it at $852 billion post-money. Rival Anthropic filed confidentially the same month and raised $65 billion at $965 billion in May.

Why it matters

A private valuation above $1 trillion sets the benchmark for the AI IPOs expected next, which could land in the retirement accounts and index funds of ordinary investors. Reports of weaker demand suggest even AI's most generous backers have limits.

OpenAI is reportedly working on a new funding round of up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, according to CNBC, as relayed by TechCrunch. The round is said to be led by Coatue and investors in the United Arab Emirates. OpenAI declined to comment, which is the corporate equivalent of a raised eyebrow that costs nothing.

Translation: pre-money valuation is what the company is deemed to be worth before the new cash arrives. The $30 billion then gets added on top, so the post-money figure would be higher. Terms are still being negotiated, both CNBC and Bloomberg say, so every number in this story is a draft written in pencil.

The pencil has already been busy. Bloomberg reported a month earlier that OpenAI was seeking $30 billion at $1.4 trillion. The Financial Times, citing internal documents, reported that the original pitch was indeed $1.4 trillion, and that demand was weaker than in earlier rounds. Some backers were said to be suffering from what the FT called "indigestion" after heavy investments in previous fundraises. Translation: when you have written very large checks several times in a row, the next very large check feels different.

Even so, a valuation above $1 trillion would make OpenAI the most valuable private company ever, according to TechCrunch. Lowering the price from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion is a discount only in the sense that a $250,000 car is a bargain next to a $280,000 car.

The UAE piece comes with a side order. CNBC reports the funding could be paired with a commercial deal to secure more computing capacity for OpenAI through the UAE. Translation: an investor puts in money, and the company spends some of it on that investor's data centers. The arrangement is increasingly common in AI, where everyone is both customer and shareholder and the accounting must be a pleasure to read.

The round is being positioned as a bridge to an IPO, The Decoder reported. The IPO story has been less than linear. OpenAI filed confidentially with the SEC in June. CFO Sarah Friar told employees in August that the company would be public by 2027. In September, CEO Sam Altman said the timing was unfavorable for going public, something The Decoder says he had already communicated internally in June. Both statements can be true, provided one is read as a goal and the other as a mood.

Anthropic is the other half of this race. CNBC reports it is seeking a valuation of $1.5 to $2 trillion for its public debut, and TechCrunch notes that rumors point to a public S-1 filing, the detailed registration document, any day now. Anthropic's $965 billion May valuation was widely thought to be its last big private round. Apparently the word last has a flexible definition in venture finance.

What to watch: whether the round closes at $1.25 trillion or lower, and whether Anthropic files first. A public filing would finally force these companies to show their losses in a standard format, which is the one thing a private round has never required.
