Apple Unveils 'Biggest Update Ever' to Smallest iPad, Also Raises Price to $699

The 8th-generation iPad mini gets an OLED screen, a 2-nanometer chip and water resistance, and costs more than before.

The wire said: Apple reveals updated iPad mini with a bigger OLED screen for $700 · Ars Technica

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 27, 2026 02:33 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple on October 26, 2026 announced a refreshed iPad mini, its smallest tablet, with pre-orders opening October 28 and availability beginning November 6. Ars Technica reported a starting price of $699, which it says is higher than the previous model's.

Why it matters

Anyone who wants a small tablet now pays more for a better screen and a faster chip, and Apple is betting that the AI features will justify it. Buyers get a device that survives a rainy day or a poolside afternoon, which the previous model never promised.

Apple has announced the eighth-generation iPad mini, and according to Apple's own press release it is the tablet's biggest update ever. Ars Technica reports it starts at $699, a price increase over the last model. Apple, to its credit, did not put the second fact in the headline of the first.

The biggest change is the screen. The display grows from 8.3 to 8.5 inches and switches from IPS to OLED. Translation: IPS lights the whole screen from behind, while OLED lets each pixel make its own light, so black can actually be black. Apple says peak brightness reaches 1,200 nits, against 500 for the previous mini, and Ars notes the minimum is 1 nit, so bedtime reading is less like staring into a welding mask.

The screen got bigger without the tablet getting bigger, because Apple cut the bezels by 24 percent. Bezels are the borders around the display, and the company has apparently decided that empty space is only acceptable on the invoice. The device is also 5.4 mm thin, about a millimeter less than its predecessor according to Ars, and carries an IP67 rating for dust and water resistance. The last mini had no official rating, which made reading by the pool an act of optimism. A nano-texture matte option is also available to reduce glare.

Inside is the A20 Pro chip, built on a 2-nanometer process, replacing a 3-nanometer A17 Pro that Ars says was already a year old when the last mini shipped. Translation: the number describes manufacturing precision, and smaller generally means faster and more efficient. Apple claims up to 4.5 times faster AI performance, 2.7 times faster graphics and 60 percent faster multithreaded work, all measured against the previous generation.

The AI is the real selling point. The tablet runs iPadOS 27 with a new Siri AI, which Apple says can draw on personal context, answer questions on virtually any topic and understand what is on screen. Siri AI is rolling out in beta, which is a way of saying that the headline feature is the one still being finished. The 512GB configuration gets a dual 16-core Neural Engine, so the extra AI power sits behind the more expensive storage tier.

Other additions include a 12MP Center Stage front camera on the landscape edge, speakers that work through the display, Apple's N1 wireless chip for Wi-Fi 7 and Bluetooth 6, and the C2 modem, which Apple says delivers up to 50 percent faster cellular speeds. Dave Pakula, Apple's vice president of hardware engineering, called it a huge leap forward for users who love having the full iPad experience in the palm of their hand. A Smart Folio case is sold separately, because some tradition must survive.

Ars Technica also took a moment to mention that the iPhone Duo may be a bit of a risk right now, which is a polite way of saying the mid-size tablet has been handed the job of looking sensible. The mini now has a better screen, a better chip and a higher price. Whether people accept all three will be settled on November 6.
