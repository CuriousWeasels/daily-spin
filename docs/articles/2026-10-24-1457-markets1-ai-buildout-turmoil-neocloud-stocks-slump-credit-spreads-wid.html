AI Buildout Has Rocky Week; Lenders Notice Everyone Is Insuring Everyone Else

Neocloud stocks slid, default insurance on CoreWeave, Meta, Nvidia and Oracle hit distress or record levels, and SK Hynix pulled the Solidigm IPO.

The wire said: Turmoil hits AI buildout as neoclouds face pressure, credit risks mount and Solidigm pulls IPO · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 24, 2026 14:57 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Big tech has spent historic sums on AI data centers, increasingly with borrowed money and with Nvidia backing smaller 'neocloud' customers. This week, investors started asking who actually pays if the demand forecasts miss.

Why it matters

If AI infrastructure financing tightens, it could hit the stocks that dominate index funds and retirement accounts. The risk is no longer just that AI disappoints, but that the debt supporting it gets expensive.

CNBC reported that the AI infrastructure trade had a rough week. The Nasdaq fell again on Friday and ended the week down 0.7%, while neocloud stocks CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN each lost well over 30% in October. Translation: a neocloud is a company that rents out AI computing power, usually by buying Nvidia chips with borrowed money and hoping someone signs a long contract to use them.

The most telling number is on CoreWeave, the largest of the group. Its credit default swap spread passed 11%, which CNBC says is generally viewed as a distress level. Translation: a credit default swap is insurance against a company failing to repay its debt, and an 11% spread means buyers are paying a great deal for that policy. Yields on CoreWeave's 2031 and 2032 unsecured notes topped 15%, so the market is asking to be paid handsomely for lending to a company that Nvidia's CEO says does not need backstopping.

Meta supplied the large-company version. Its stock fell 7% on Thursday after it reported negative free cash flow of over $2 billion in the third quarter and raised the floor of its full-year capital spending to $135 billion from $130 billion. It also disclosed about $825 billion in planned lease and contract commitments, over $450 billion of them non-cancellable take-or-pay deals. Translation: take-or-pay means you pay whether or not you use the thing, which is a firm commitment to a forecast.

Default insurance on Meta reached an all-time high of over 124 basis points, with Nvidia at 126 and Oracle at 343. A basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point, so these are far below CoreWeave's level, but they are records. Milbank's John Williams told CNBC that hedging makes sense whether you are bullish or bearish. That is the polite way of saying the insurance is selling well to both optimists and pessimists.

The second CNBC report examined Nvidia, which has become more than a chip seller. As of its July filing it had $36 billion in off-balance-sheet backstops for neoclouds, plus a guarantee of up to $105 billion tied to a proposed 10-gigawatt OpenAI data center in Ohio. Translation: a backstop is a promise to cover the shortfall if a customer cannot pay, which lets the customer borrow more cheaply and lets Nvidia sell more chips. CNBC noted that for most of these deals Nvidia pays only if participants default.

That makes the current week an early test of whether the promise is decoration. Nvidia is also an investor in Firmus, which withdrew its IPO plans this month, and in Nscale and SB Energy, which filed to go public in September and have not updated their plans in weeks. Its stock is down 7.6% in October, on pace for its worst month since November of last year. Nvidia declined to comment.

Jensen Huang remains unbothered. He told CNBC that demand for neoclouds is 'off the charts' and that the shortage is capital, not customers. He may be right, though the lender's version of that sentence is 'we would like higher interest.' David Trainer of New Constructs put the risk plainly: if demand for compute misses lofty expectations, 'we're going to have a problem.'

Then there is SK Hynix, which scrapped a planned IPO of its Solidigm storage unit, citing market conditions and share price trends. Elice Group fell 30% on its South Korean debut and Australia's NextDC dropped 39.5% after cutting its earnings forecast. Gartner expects 80% of neoclouds to shut down or be absorbed by 2030, which at least gives investors a date to circle.

The broader market has not buckled. The Nasdaq closed at 25,955.34 on October 23, and the 10-year Treasury yield sits at 4.96% with inflation at 3.74%, which is not a gentle backdrop for companies that borrow heavily. Next week brings earnings from Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon, who will explain how much they plan to spend. Investors will be listening for the number, and checking the price of the insurance.
