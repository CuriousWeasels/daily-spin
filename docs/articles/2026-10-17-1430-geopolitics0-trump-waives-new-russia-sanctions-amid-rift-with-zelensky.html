Trump Waives Russia Sanctions He Signed, Two Days Before They Could Bite

An executive order suspends much of a congressionally passed sanctions law on 'national interest' grounds, as Zelensky says Russia is targeting Ukraine's power grid ahead of winter.

The wire said: Trump waives several Russia sanctions as row with Zelensky intensifies · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 17, 2026 14:30 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Congress passed a Russia sanctions law that Trump signed in September, with tariffs of up to 100% on top buyers of Russian oil and gas. Relations with Kyiv have since soured over a US deal to import Russian diesel and a disputed slowdown of Starlink for Ukrainian drones.

Why it matters

The waiver removes a main source of planned economic pressure on Russia while Ukraine faces strikes on its energy system. It also shows how fuel prices before the midterms can shape foreign policy.

According to the BBC, Donald Trump issued an executive order on Friday night waiving many of the Russia sanctions two days before they were due to take effect. The law was passed by Congress and signed by Trump himself in September. The order says suspending some provisions is in the US 'national interest', which is a phrase that can accommodate nearly any decision.

Translation: a sanctions law is a promise that costs will be imposed, and a waiver is the part of the law where the president decides whether to keep the promise. This one targeted Russia's economy. It gave Trump broad powers to levy tariffs of up to 100% on imports from the five biggest buyers of Russian oil and gas, most significantly China and India. It also covered senior Russian figures including Putin, Russian financial institutions, and the 'shadow fleet' of vessels used to get around existing embargoes. The BBC says many of these provisions are now waived. NPR reported that Trump told Congress the pause was in the national interest but gave no further details.

The timing follows a week of strained relations. The US struck a deal to import Russian diesel, which the BBC links to an effort to bring down fuel prices that have soared during the Iran war ahead of the midterm elections. Translation: gasoline is expensive, voters notice, and the sanctions on the country selling diesel became a complication.

Trump then claimed Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an 'energy ceasefire' to ease diesel exports. Zelensky said he was surprised, and offered to stop Ukrainian strikes if Russia did too. After three days of fresh Russian attacks, Kyiv resumed hitting Russian refineries, and Trump called the strikes a 'disgrace'. Zelensky replied that he was 'only standing up for Ukraine's interests'. A ceasefire that one side learns about from the news has a limited shelf life.

Reactions split along predictable lines. Russian envoy Kirill Dmitriev called the waiver 'an important and truly historic moment' and said the sanctions would have hurt the US and global markets. Democratic Congressman Don Beyer accused Trump of surrendering to Putin and defying congressional intent. Zelensky said the sanctions 'could have helped enormously' and told reporters he expects more sanctions to be lifted. He also said US and Ukrainian negotiators could meet next week.

The military backdrop is harsher. Zelensky said Russia hit more than 15 power generation and transmission facilities overnight, and that Russia 'is trying to cause a blackout in Ukraine' before winter. Russia's defence ministry said it targeted industries linked to Ukraine's military, including steelworks and an electrical substation. Ukrainian authorities said an ambulance driver was killed in Kherson and about 20 people were injured, and the BBC reported at least nine deaths across the country in 24 hours. DW noted that TASS reported one woman killed by Ukrainian drone strikes in Belgorod.

There is also the matter of Starlink. Ukraine says a sudden speed restriction disrupted drone missions, and that Russian strikes came during the slowdown without warning. Zelensky said US officials blamed technical issues, but Ukraine tested the system on EU territory and found no such problems. Trump denies deliberately slowing the network or halting intelligence-sharing.

The next test is whether more waivers follow, as Zelensky expects, and whether Congress, which wrote the law, does anything beyond writing angry posts. Until then, the sanctions exist in the same sense as a gym membership in February.
