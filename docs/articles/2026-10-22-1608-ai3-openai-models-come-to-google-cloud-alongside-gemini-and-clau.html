Google Cloud Adds OpenAI Models, Hosting Every Rival Its Own Gemini Team Fears

Google Cloud is now the only major provider offering Gemini, Claude and OpenAI models together, all billed on one invoice.

The wire said: Google Cloud now offers OpenAI models alongside Gemini and Claude, becoming the only major provider with all three · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 22, 2026 16:08 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google sells cloud computing to businesses and also builds Gemini, its own AI model. It has already hosted Anthropic's Claude, and now it is adding OpenAI's GPT-6 and 6.1 models too.

Why it matters

Companies can switch between rival AI models without changing vendors or contracts, which could push model providers to compete harder on price and performance. It also shows that in AI, selling the infrastructure may pay better than winning the model race.

Google Cloud will now offer OpenAI's models alongside its own Gemini and Anthropic's Claude, making it the only large cloud provider with all three, according to The Decoder and Ars Technica. Three models are involved: GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna. Google, in other words, has opened a store that stocks its competitors' best products and placed them on the shelf next to its own.

The Decoder reported that the models launch as a private preview for select enterprise customers, with sign-ups open. Astra is aimed at demanding work like coding and research. Sol is a cheaper option with similar performance, and Luna is a fast, cost-efficient model for everyday use. Ars Technica noted that Astra will naturally cost the most to run.

The models will also appear in Gemini for Business and in Antigravity, which Ars Technica describes as Google's agent-powered coding tool. Ars called that arguably the more important part. Translation: the tool Google built to show off Gemini's coding abilities will also let users pick the other company's model for the job.

The commercial logic is in the billing. According to The Decoder, all charges run through a single Google Cloud bill and count toward existing contracts. Ars Technica added that the models run entirely on Google's infrastructure, with transactions handled through the Google Cloud Marketplace. Translation: a company that has already promised Google a certain amount of spending can now use that promise to buy a competitor's AI, and Google still gets the money. Ars said this makes it easy for businesses to use GPT models in place of whatever they used before.

Google's system also supports moving workloads between models based on performance, latency and efficiency, Ars reported. In plain English, a customer can send easy tasks to the cheap model and hard ones to the expensive model, and the software can help decide. The Decoder said Google states that customer data will not be used for training.

There is an awkward side. The Decoder pointed out a recurring tension at Google: computing capacity used to serve cloud customers strengthens the biggest rivals of its own Gemini team. It said this resource conflict reportedly even pushed researcher Noam Shazeer, described as a two-billion-dollar hire, back to OpenAI. That is a notable cost of running a business that depends on both customers and competitors needing the same chips.

OpenAI, for its part, notes that Microsoft remains its primary cloud partner, according to Ars Technica. So the arrangement is less a new romance than an open relationship with a very large landlord. The thing to watch is whether Gemini customers on Google Cloud stay loyal when a rival model is one click away, and who ends up with the bigger share of the invoice.
