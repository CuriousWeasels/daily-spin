Britain Spends £100m on Lab to Defend Against Threats AI Helps Create

The Budget funds a 'world first' AI Biosecurity Lab, backed by £200m in credits from OpenAI and Anthropic, though what the lab will actually do remains to be announced.

The wire said: UK to invest £100m in 'world first' AI Biosecurity Lab · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 24, 2026 14:57 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

The UK government announced in its Wednesday Budget that it will build a national AI Biosecurity Lab and boost spending on AI-related security. This follows a recent scare in which sites were taken offline after flaws were found in the PHP programming language.

Why it matters

Governments are betting that AI can defend against the cyber and biological risks it may also make worse, and the money will shape who builds the tools and who profits. Ordinary people are meant to benefit through safer infrastructure, though the details are still to come.

The UK government says it will commit £100m to a new national AI Biosecurity Lab, which it calls a 'world first', according to the BBC. The announcement comes as part of Wednesday's Budget. The BBC also reported that the full role and remit of the lab are yet to emerge, which is an efficient way to launch a world first: the first thing it has is a name.

The lab is meant to strengthen Britain's defences against emerging biological threats. AI Minister Kanishka Narayan described it as a 'major new global institution' that would bring together 'world-leading scientific expertise'. Translation: a building, some scientists and a budget, with the purpose to be confirmed.

Private industry is helping. OpenAI and Anthropic will provide £200m worth of AI model credits, according to the BBC. Translation: model credits are prepaid vouchers for using a company's AI systems. The government is therefore getting a lab partly funded in the currency of the companies it is also meant to keep an eye on. This is generous, and also an excellent way to become a customer.

The Budget does not stop at biology. A further £120m goes to the UK's AI Security Institute, established in 2023 by the previous Conservative government to evaluate the risks of the most advanced models. The BBC said the extra money is meant to help the institute continue its work amid growing calls for technical standards and guardrails for AI development.

There is also £60m for a 'National Cyber Shield', which the government says will use agentic AI to boost cyber defences. Translation: agentic AI means software that takes actions on its own rather than just answering questions. A further £70m goes to a National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability Centre, known by the acronym NERV, which will use AI tools to detect weaknesses across critical infrastructure and the wider economy. The acronym, it must be said, is accurate.

The timing is instructive. The BBC noted that the UK government was among the organisations that took some sites offline as a precaution after critical flaws, dubbed php2shell, surfaced in PHP, a scripting language used in web development for decades. Researchers warned that platforms using it could be exposed to hacks, though no successful exploits have been reported so far.

Experts quoted by the BBC said the episode shows how quickly bugs that have lain dormant for years can be found, and possibly exploited, using AI. That is the logic behind the whole package: the same tools that dig up old flaws are the ones the state now wants to point at its own systems first. It is a race in which both sides have bought the same equipment.

John Healey, quoted by the BBC, said the government is setting out 'a distinctively British approach to AI' that does not leave workers behind. What that approach consists of will become clearer when the lab has a remit, which is expected to be soon, or at least eventually.
