Chip Stocks Tumble, Finance Minister Says Boom Will Last Until at Least Next Year

SK Hynix fell 20.6% below a key moving average as South Korea's finance minister promised strong demand through 2027, while China's CXMT posted a profit swing and still lost 6.5%.

The wire said: SK Hynix crashes -20.6% below 20-period MA: Live levels · Investing.com

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 19, 2026 01:58 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Semiconductor stocks sold off sharply this week on worries about interest rate hikes and rising Chinese competition. South Korea's market is unusually exposed because chips make up a large share of its economy.

Why it matters

When one industry is this big, its mood swings become a country's mood swings, and that affects tax revenue, pensions and anyone holding a Korea-linked fund. The memory chip cycle has historically turned from shortage to glut, and the sources show people already arguing over when.

South Korean finance minister Lee Hyoung-Il told CNBC on Saturday that the chip boom should last at least through 2027. He spoke on the sidelines of the IMF-World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok, after chip stocks fell sharply during the week. Investing.com noted that SK Hynix had dropped 20.6% below its 20-period moving average. Translation: the stock is trading far under its recent average price, which traders treat as a sign of either a bargain or a warning, depending on which they already own.

Lee's case rests on supply and demand. 'The gap between demand and supply for semiconductors is still very wide, and supply is still being expanded,' he said. This is a comforting sentence with a small wrinkle: the same sentence says supply is growing, and growing supply is what eventually ends shortages.

CNBC reported that the selloff was blamed on investor concerns about interest rate hikes and rising competition from Chinese companies. Nvidia and other U.S. giants fell along with SK Hynix and Seoul Semiconductor. The latest market data shows the Fed funds rate at 3.88% while the 10-year Treasury yield sits at 5.1%. Translation: long-term borrowing costs are well above the short-term policy rate, which makes investors less willing to pay generous prices for profits that arrive years from now.

Lee attributed South Korea's market volatility to the chip industry's outsized share of the economy compared with other countries. In other words, the market swings because the economy is, to a significant degree, a chip company with a flag. He still called the economy 'solid' and said the government expects to collect more tax than it first projected this year, leaving it in a stronger fiscal position.

Not everyone shares the long view. A Thursday opinion column in Maeil Business Newspaper urged the government to prepare for an AI slowdown. It cited two experts who expect a 'flood of new supply' that would turn the memory market 'into a red ocean' by 2029, according to CNBC's translation. Translation: so many sellers that prices are driven down and everyone loses money together.

The competition is already visible. China's CXMT said in a filing that it expects nine-month net profit of 131 billion yuan to 132.5 billion yuan, about $19.58 billion at the low end, against a loss of 5.28 billion yuan a year earlier. Revenue is forecast at roughly 265 billion to 270 billion yuan. The company credited AI-driven demand that has caused DRAM shortages and big price increases.

CXMT shares fell 6.5% on Monday anyway. Investors, it seems, had already priced in the good news, or they looked at a company that now holds 10% of global DRAM revenue, up from 5% a year earlier according to Counterpoint Research, and noticed who might be sharing the shortage premium. Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron presumably noticed too.

The finance minister may well be right that demand outlasts next year. But a forecast that the boom will last through 2027 is also a forecast that it will not last through 2028, and markets, unlike ministers, are paid to do that arithmetic early.
