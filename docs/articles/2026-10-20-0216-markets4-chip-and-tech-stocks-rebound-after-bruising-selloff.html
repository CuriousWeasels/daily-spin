Chip Stocks Bounce After Nine-Day Slide, Which Analysts Describe as 'Tepid' and Investors as 'Relief'

The semiconductor ETF is on pace to end its worst losing streak since May 2006, though CoreWeave is hitting new lows and oil near $103 is making bond traders nervous.

The wire said: Micron and other chip stocks spring back to life after bruising selloff · MarketWatch

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 20, 2026 02:16 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Tech and chip stocks fell hard last week, with the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) dropping nine sessions in a row. On Monday, investors bought the dip, though not with any great conviction.

Why it matters

Chip stocks sit inside most index funds and retirement accounts, so their swings reach ordinary portfolios. Meanwhile, rising oil and Treasury yields feed the inflation worries that keep borrowing costs high for everyone.

Stocks rose on Monday as investors bought chip shares after what CNBC's Investing Club called last week's tech carnage. The iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX) was on pace to snap a nine-session losing streak, which would end its worst stretch since May 2006. The Nasdaq was up about 1%, according to CNBC. MarketWatch reported that Micron and other chip names sprang back, while Investing.com said U.S. futures were steady after Wall Street's tech-led rebound.

Translation: a fund that holds many chip companies fell for nine straight days, and one day of gains has been declared a recovery. CNBC's own assessment was less festive. It called the bounce tepid, noting that SOXX was trading well off its best levels of the session.

The clearest sign of lingering unease was CoreWeave, which CNBC described as a market focal point. The stock flipped from positive to negative during the day and made a new low for the year under $60 per share. So even on the day the sector was rescued, one of its most closely watched names declined to attend.

Money did not appear from nowhere. It rotated out of defensive groups such as healthcare and consumer staples, which had rallied last week. Translation: investors sold the boring things they bought for safety and bought the exciting things they had just sold in fear. That is why the Dow stayed near flat while the Nasdaq climbed.

Oil complicated the mood. CNBC reported WTI crude rose about $2 to around $103 per barrel, stoking inflation concerns and a bond selloff, with the 10-year Treasury yield rising on the day. Bond prices and yields move in opposite directions, so when investors sell bonds, yields go up. The latest official data show the 10-year at 5.09% and CPI inflation at 3.74% year over year, which is the sort of arithmetic that makes a mortgage broker clear his throat.

Elsewhere, CNBC noted that Bank of America analysts raised their price target on Eli Lilly to $1,397 from $1,341 after a physician survey. The analysts said daily obesity pills and Medicare's temporary Bridge program are expanding the market, with both drawing about 70% of volumes from patients new to GLP-1 drugs. They estimated Lilly's Foundayo at 27% of oral GLP-1 new starts and expect 40% by 2028/29. Translation: more people are taking the drugs, and Lilly would like to be the one selling them.

The week ahead is crowded. Lilly reports third-quarter earnings Oct. 29, GE Aerospace, RTX, 3M and General Motors report Tuesday morning, and Meta follows Wednesday. MarketWatch pointed to Nvidia's European conference as another test for chips. If nine days of selling needed one day of buying to end, a few more earnings reports will show whether the buyers were persuaded or merely rested.
