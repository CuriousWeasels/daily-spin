Wall Street Braces for Week When Earnings, Fed and Inflation All Arrive at Once

More than a third of S&P 500 companies report as the Fed meets, midterms loom and Brent crude sits at $115.22 a barrel.

The wire said: Here are 4 big things we're watching in the stock market this week · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 25, 2026 15:06 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

The AI trade that powered this year's bull market has been showing leaks, according to CNBC, while the Middle East conflict keeps oil prices high. This week stacks the busiest stretch of third-quarter earnings on top of a Fed meeting and fresh inflation data.

Why it matters

The results from Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon will show whether huge AI spending is producing revenue, which matters to anyone whose retirement account holds the S&P 500. The Fed's tone will also signal whether borrowing costs ease or stay painful.

Wall Street enters one of its most crowded weeks of the year. CNBC calls it the busiest week of third-quarter earnings season, and MarketWatch reports that more than a third of S&P 500 companies are about to report. One strategist told MarketWatch that with sentiment cautious to negative, it could be a tricky week. Translation: everyone is about to be graded at once, and the teacher has already said the class is struggling.

The Federal Reserve is meeting too, and inflation data is due. Fed funds sits at 3.88%, the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.96% and the last reading of annual consumer price inflation at 3.74%. Translation: the 10-year yield is what the government pays to borrow for a decade, and it feeds into mortgage rates. Rates are therefore mostly what ordinary borrowers are living inside, not merely a topic for traders.

MarketWatch reports that investors want Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh to prove he can communicate the central bank's strategy. Economists quoted there say what matters is clear communication, and the headline complains that nonanswers are not working. This is a fair request of an institution whose entire job is moving markets by describing what it might do.

On the corporate side, the AI question dominates. CNBC says Microsoft's cloud backlog was $678 billion at the end of June, up 84%, and investors want to know how diversified the customers are. Translation: backlog is contracted work not yet delivered, and a customer base made of a few AI labs is a little like a restaurant booked solid by one family. Alphabet must show Google Cloud, which now includes sales of its custom TPU chips, can drive durable growth, while investors also watch its 2027 capital spending plans.

Amazon reports Thursday with estimated revenue of $202.24 billion, and CNBC says the focus is AWS growth and margins, plus how shoppers cope with higher oil prices and what it calls unrelenting inflation. Boeing opens the week with a consensus loss of 21 cents per share and free cash flow of $330 million, weighed down by a $700 million payment to the Justice Department. FedEx adds a four-month reporting period because of its shift to a calendar fiscal year, which CNBC says will create noise for analysts.

Beyond tech, GE Vernova's gas turbine orders are a proxy for whether anyone can power all those data centers, and Eli Lilly must again beat and raise to keep its stock climbing. The midterm elections are less than 10 days away, giving investors one more uncertainty at no extra charge.

The market has the backdrop to match: the S&P 500 fund SPY closed at 759.97 and the Nasdaq at 25,955.34 on October 23, with the VIX fear gauge at 19.03. Translation: the VIX measures expected volatility, and 19 means traders are nervous without yet being rude about it. The week will test whether the bull market still has fluid in the tank.
