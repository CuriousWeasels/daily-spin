Binance Loses $6.3 Billion to Hackers, Promises Everyone Is Fine

The world's largest crypto exchange says it will cover every loss, after an engineer's laptop apparently became the most expensive door in financial history.

The wire said: Crypto giant Binance hit by $6.3bn hack suspected to be largest in history · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 30, 2026 17:17 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, said on Thursday that $6.3bn in digital assets were affected by a hack believed to be the biggest on record, eclipsing the $1.5bn Bybit theft in 2025. Several analytics firms have pointed to a North Korean unit known as TraderTraitor, though this has not been confirmed.

Why it matters

If a platform holding well over $100bn in customer assets can be breached through a laptop, regulators will ask hard questions about every smaller exchange. Ordinary users should also expect a wave of scammers posing as Binance support.

Binance said on Thursday that about $6.3bn (£4.7bn) of digital assets had been affected by what appears to be the largest crypto hack ever recorded. The company suspended withdrawals and said it would "cover all losses." Being the biggest in history is normally a milestone a company announces on purpose.

The mechanism, according to the company, began modestly. Co-CEO Richard Teng said an initial investigation suggested a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop gave an attacker access to a service container, where unauthorised withdrawal transactions were submitted. Translation: someone got into the room where the money-moving buttons are, and then pressed them. Binance later said further investigation had "uncovered additional points of breach," which is the corporate way of saying the first explanation was the optimistic one.

Teng stressed this was not a single "one-click" action and that the attacker did not simply borrow one engineer's permissions. He also said investigators had found no evidence of a wallet private key leak. Check Point's Jayant Dave offered the broader lesson: attackers no longer need the vault keys, and instead "target the systems and people with authority to use them." Translation: why pick the lock when you can befriend the person with the keyring.

Illumio's Raghu Nandakumara suggested one plausible route, in which an engineer was "talked into taking a test" and malware landed on the laptop meanwhile. That is speculation from an outside expert, not a finding by Binance. Teng has said the hackers used sophisticated methods to hide their tracks, including a "self-destruct mechanism."

Attribution is where sources diverge in confidence. TRM Labs, Chainalysis and another analytics firm have linked the attack to TraderTraitor, also known as Lazarus Group, with Chainalysis claiming "high confidence." The BBC's explainer noted the link has not been confirmed, and Binance did not directly answer questions about it. North Korea has denied involvement in past hacks, while Western agencies say stolen funds help finance its weapons programmes.

On recovery, the figures differ by source. The BBC reported that about half the assets had been frozen, and Tether and Circle said they froze about $2.4bn between them. Ars Technica reported that around $3bn, mainly stablecoins, had been frozen, but that Elliptic said the attacker had converted about $3.3bn into assets that cannot be frozen and was trying to cash out. Stablecoins, tokens pegged to the dollar, can be frozen by their issuers. Other assets are less cooperative.

Binance says it has reserved over $6.34bn of company funds to cover the incident, with $5bn already held in the affected tokens, and is buying more. It cautioned that this can take longer because some tokens have low liquidity. Bitcoin fell more than 4% after industry observers flagged the problem hours before Binance confirmed it, with Ethereum and Solana also sliding. Binance.US and Coinbase said they were unaffected.

Users face a more mundane risk. NordVPN's Marijus Briedis warned of fake emails claiming funds are at risk and messages urging people to move crypto to a "safe" wallet. Translation: after the largest theft in history, the second-largest source of losses may be people being helpfully told what to do.

Binance has promised a detailed post-mortem once its investigation is complete. Until then, the company's position is that customers are safe, which will be tested the day withdrawals reopen.
