Musk Says Musk's Two Companies Are Discussing Merging, Without Musk

Tesla and SpaceX have formed special committees to study a combination that would be valued at roughly $3.5 trillion, and the man who runs both says he is not allowed in the room.

The wire said: Tesla and SpaceX are discussing a possible merger, Musk says · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 21, 2026 02:09 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

SpaceX, which absorbed xAI and X before going public this year, reported third-quarter results on Tuesday. During the call, Elon Musk, CEO of both SpaceX and Tesla, said committees at each company are weighing a merger.

Why it matters

A Tesla-SpaceX deal would be one of the largest mergers in history and would change what Tesla shareholders own, and how much of it. Anyone holding an index fund with either stock is, in a small way, a party to the discussion.

Elon Musk said on SpaceX's third-quarter earnings call on Tuesday that special committees at Tesla and SpaceX are discussing a possible merger. According to The Verge, the committees were formed at shareholders' request and consist of independent directors, whom Musk did not name. "Do I think it would be a good idea? Yes," Musk said, as quoted by both the BBC and Ars Technica. He also stressed that nothing is agreed and that there is no guarantee anything will be.

Musk described his own position with some care. Because he is chief executive of both companies, he called himself a "conflicted party" and said he cannot take part in the committees' discussions. Translation: a special committee is a group of directors with no stake in the outcome, assembled so that the deal's most interested person is not negotiating with himself. Musk called the arrangement, per The Verge, "an odd kabuki dance" in which two groups talk while he is kept out of the conversation. He is, by his own account, the only person who thinks the merger is a good idea and is also not permitted to say so in the meeting.

The BBC put the values at about $1.5 trillion for Tesla and $2 trillion for SpaceX. The Verge put the combined entity at upwards of $3.6 trillion. The sources do not fully agree on the arithmetic, but both agree the result would be enormous. SpaceX shares rose 8% in after-hours trading, and Tesla shares rose 2% after drifting lower for weeks, so the market appears to have found the idea at least interesting.

Musk's case for it is that the two companies are converging. He pointed to superintelligence software, chip design, the Terafab chip-manufacturing effort, robotics, and the Starmind satellite constellation. Ars Technica noted that a space business and a car business look like an odd pairing, but both are moving toward artificial intelligence. The BBC reported that SpaceX's fastest-growing line is now selling cloud computing capacity to companies such as Anthropic and Google, which brought in $7.5 billion last quarter. Starlink brought in $5.2 billion. SpaceX as a whole has still lost $1.1 billion this year, which is the sort of detail that tends to appear in paragraph fourteen of a merger announcement.

The mechanics matter more than the vision. The Verge reported that most analysts expect SpaceX to absorb Tesla, not the reverse, because SpaceX carries the higher valuation. Tesla would have to issue millions of new shares to buy SpaceX. Translation: current Tesla owners would hold a smaller slice of a larger pie, and the new pie would require tens of billions of dollars a year for launch ambitions. That is money not spent on car factories and battery plants.

Then there is the vote. Musk said the deal would need approval from 50% of all Tesla shares, not merely of the shares that are cast, which he called "quite a high hurdle." That may be less daunting than it sounds. The BBC and The Verge both note that Tesla investors approved a compensation package for Musk valued around $1 trillion last year, even after a Delaware judge threw out an earlier pay deal over a lack of independence. Barclays has suggested, in a client note cited by The Verge, that an announcement could come before Tesla's annual meeting on December 3, 2026.

For now, the situation is two independent committees negotiating a deal that the person who controls both sides supports and cannot attend. Watch whether the committees produce terms, and whether Tesla holders accept a smaller slice of something bigger.
