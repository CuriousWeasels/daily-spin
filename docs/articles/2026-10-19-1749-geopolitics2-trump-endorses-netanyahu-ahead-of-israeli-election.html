Trump Endorses Netanyahu, Warns Israel Not to Elect Anyone Who Would Make Him Do Things

The US president backed Israel's prime minister ahead of the October 27 vote, saying America would have to 'do this work ourselves' under any other leader.

The wire said: Trump backs Netanyahu: Is the US planning a new Iran attack with Israel? · Al Jazeera

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 19, 2026 17:49 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

The US and Israel attacked Iran in February, and the conflict is now at a standstill, with the Strait of Hormuz disrupted and a US naval blockade in place. Trump had earlier declined to endorse Netanyahu, telling Newsmax only that Israelis would like him to get involved.

Why it matters

Oil prices, shipping lanes and the risk of renewed war all hang on how Washington and Jerusalem read each other's politics. Brent crude was at $107.57 a barrel on Monday, a price ordinary drivers will eventually be invited to share.

US President Donald Trump announced on Truth Social on Sunday his 'Complete and Total Endorsement' of Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, according to Al Jazeera. He called Netanyahu a 'great warrior prime minister and an even greater partner for peace.' NPR noted that this was in the context of combating Iran. Warrior and peace partner are, in this formulation, a single job title.

The endorsement reverses weeks of reluctance. Earlier this month, TIME asked Trump twice whether Netanyahu deserved another term, and he responded by discussing his own popularity. Asked whether he trusted Netanyahu, he said, 'I don't trust anybody, honestly.' Translation: an endorsement is not the same as a reference.

The letter also contained a warning. Trump wrote that if Israel has 'the wrong Leader, which would be anyone other than Bibi, America will have to do this work ourselves, and I am not inclined to do so.' Translation: vote for the candidate who lets the US stay in the role of supporting actor in a war it helped start. Analyst Sanam Vakil of Chatham House noted that the statement makes it harder for Trump to portray the US as reluctantly drawn in.

The electoral value is uncertain. Haaretz's tracker, cited by Al Jazeera, shows the opposition alliance led by Gadi Eisenkot's Yashar party at 52 of the Knesset's 120 seats, with Netanyahu's Likud-led bloc at 51. Neither is near the 61 needed for a majority. Andreas Krieg of King's College London called the endorsement a 'political gift' that might be worth a seat or two, but said it cannot manufacture a coalition majority Netanyahu lacks.

Then there is Iran. The Strait of Hormuz, which once carried about 20 percent of global crude, was effectively shut after the war began. Traffic recovered to pre-war levels last month under US Navy escort, then was hit again by Iranian attacks on vessels early this month. Brent briefly touched $110 on Friday. Iran says its proposal to reopen the strait within days remains available if Washington meets its seven conditions, including lifting sanctions and ending the blockade.

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi responded on X that Netanyahu wants to restart attacks to 'stay out of jail,' a claim made by Tehran and not established in the sources. Axios reported last week that Trump and Netanyahu discussed a major new operation before both countries' elections, while Trump has said he would not attack before the November midterms. Several US outlets report the Pentagon has drawn up plans anyway. Plans are cheap; that is what planning offices are for.

Krieg reads the endorsement as leverage, not a declaration of war: the blockade is squeezing Iran, and Washington wants a deal it can call a victory. The risk, he said, is that Tehran decides it must escalate to improve its bargaining position. Israelis vote on October 27, and the oil market will be watching with more attention than most of the electorate.
