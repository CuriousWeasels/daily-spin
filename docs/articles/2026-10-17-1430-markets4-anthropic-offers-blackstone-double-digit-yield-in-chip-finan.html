Anthropic Offers Blackstone Double-Digit Yield to Help It Buy Chips

The AI company is reportedly paying an investor at least 10 percent to fund hardware, in a market where the 10-year Treasury already yields 5.1 percent.

The wire said: Anthropic offers Blackstone double-digit yield in chip-financing deal, FT reports · Investing.com

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 17, 2026 14:30 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Anthropic, the AI developer, has reportedly offered private-equity giant Blackstone a double-digit yield in a deal to finance computer chips, according to the Financial Times as relayed by Investing.com. The summary available gives no deal size, terms or confirmation from either company.

Why it matters

When AI firms must pay double-digit returns to finance hardware, it shows how costly the chip buildout has become and how much investors now demand to lend into it. It also hints at who ultimately carries the risk if AI revenue lags the spending.

Anthropic has offered Blackstone a double-digit yield as part of a chip-financing arrangement, the Financial Times reported, according to Investing.com. The source material is a headline and a summary, so the size of the deal, its structure and the chips involved are not known here. What is known is the price tag attached to the idea: a return of at least 10 percent.

Translation: a yield is the annual payment an investor receives for putting up money, expressed as a share of that money. Chip financing means someone else pays for the expensive hardware up front, and the company pays them back with interest. Double-digit means the lender expects to be paid at least ten cents a year per dollar, which is the sort of rate normally associated with credit cards and ambitious relatives.

The offer looks different against the market backdrop. According to the latest official data, the 10-year Treasury yields 5.1 percent and the 2-year yields 4.77 percent. Those are what the US government pays to borrow, and lenders treat them as the risk-free baseline. A yield of 10 percent or more therefore implies a premium of roughly five percentage points or higher over Uncle Sam, which is the market's way of pricing the gap between a government and a company buying very expensive chips.

The Fed funds rate sits at 3.88 percent, with CPI inflation at 3.74 percent as of September. Translation: money is not cheap, and the central bank's own rate is well below what longer-term bonds pay. A borrower looking for tens of billions, or however much this is, does not get to ignore that.

For Blackstone, the appeal is plain. It is an asset manager, and asset managers like contracts where a fast-growing counterparty pays them handsomely to hold physical equipment. For Anthropic, the logic is that chips are needed now and revenue arrives later, and a high yield is the fee for bridging that gap. Neither company's comment was included in the material available.

What to watch next: whether the terms are confirmed, and whether other AI developers start offering similar yields. If they do, the cost of capital will have become another line in the AI arms race, one that has to be paid in cash.
