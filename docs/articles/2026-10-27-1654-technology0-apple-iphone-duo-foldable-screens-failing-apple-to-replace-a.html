Apple's $2,000 Foldable Breaks Early, Which Apple Calls a 'Small Number'

Apple says it will replace iPhone Duos with failing inner screens, but has not said how many devices are affected or what happens to the warranty.

The wire said: Some iPhone Duo screens are already failing, Apple says only a "small number" are affected · Ars Technica

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 27, 2026 16:54 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple launched the iPhone Duo, its first foldable phone, last week at $1,999 and up in the US and £1,999 in the UK. Early buyers have posted photos of inner displays with bright lines, and Apple has acknowledged the problem.

Why it matters

A $2,000 phone that fails within a day tests Apple's reputation for polish, and buyers need to know their warranty position before 28 more markets launch on 30 October. How Apple handles the first batch will also shape whether people trust foldables in general, according to one analyst.

Apple has acknowledged that some iPhone Duo phones have developed screen problems days after going on sale. According to the BBC, the company said it was aware of "an issue impacting the inner display on a small number" of devices and urged affected customers to contact it for a replacement. The company did not say what "small" means, which is traditionally how a number gets to stay small.

Ars Technica reported that the damage looks much the same across devices: several bright lines start just below the middle of the inner display and run to the bottom and right edges. Some units also show dead pixels along the crease. Translation: an OLED screen makes its own light pixel by pixel, and when its internal layers are damaged, pixels can receive full voltage and stay lit at maximum brightness, or receive none and stay black. Either way the screen is finished.

Ars said most affected phones seem to fail within about a day of use. A few owners say they had only opened and closed the phone a few times, and some had not finished setup. That is a narrow window for wear and tear. Ars also reported that replacing the screen costs about half the price of the phone, so a warranty swap matters a great deal.

Some customers have had success at Apple stores. The BBC noted that one owner, Justin Lokos, posted a photo of his broken Duo next to a replacement from a New York store. Ars cautioned that spare units may be in short supply. A replacement policy works well until everyone needs one.

The extent of the problem is unclear, and the BBC pointed out that some images circulating online may have been made with AI. Francisco Jeronimo, an analyst at IDC, said that calls for caution before drawing conclusions about the whole production run. He also said the reports show that even Apple "faces a steep learning curve in foldables". Ars's iFixit teardown looked at the hinge cable, a usual suspect in foldable failures, but found its pleated design did not quite match the reported damage. No official explanation has been given.

The timing is awkward. The BBC reported that the Duo is Apple's first major iPhone redesign in almost two decades and was unveiled by new chief executive John Ternus, who said it would "redefine the experience of using a foldable phone". Redefining it is one way to put it. Samsung's Galaxy Fold also had broken screens in early reviews in 2019, so Apple is at least following tradition, if not the plan.

Jeronimo said Apple needs to clarify its warranty position quickly, since 28 more markets launch on 30 October. Apple has the loyal customers and the stores to swap devices on the spot, but not yet a public answer on how many phones are failing or why. Until then, the most expensive iPhone ever comes with a free lesson in what first generation means.
