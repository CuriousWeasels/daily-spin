Ukraine Told Warnings Are Fine, Arrive One Minute Before Missiles, Which Is Technically Before

A Zelensky adviser tells the BBC that patchy US air-raid alerts and Starshield speed limits are costing lives, while Washington says nothing has changed.

The wire said: Ukrainians dying because US restricting intelligence sharing, Zelensky adviser tells BBC · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 20, 2026 02:16 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Ukraine reported an interruption to US intelligence sharing last week, amid a row with the Trump administration over a deal to import Russian oil. Kyiv also says Starshield, the Pentagon-controlled military version of Starlink, has begun limiting how fast connected units can move.

Why it matters

Early warning of ballistic missiles is measured in minutes, and those minutes decide whether civilians reach shelter. If the support Ukraine relies on can be dialed up and down during a diplomatic dispute, Kyiv has reason to plan as if it will be.

Serhii "Flash" Beskrestnov, an adviser to President Volodymyr Zelensky, told the BBC that Ukrainians have died because Kyiv is not receiving reliable US warnings of incoming Russian attacks. "People [have] already died," he said, adding that there had not been time to warn citizens about ballistic missile strikes. The White House says there has been no change to intelligence sharing, which is a clear position, if one that Kyiv's experience does not appear to share.

Zelensky said on Monday that two ballistic missile attacks hit the capital overnight, one without warning and the second with minimal time to take shelter. In an NBC interview published the same morning, he said intelligence sharing had been restored after a three-day pause and thanked the US, noting that only Washington has the intelligence to tell Ukraine when Russia fires ballistic missiles. Beskrestnov said the information was incomplete and inconsistent, and that the latest alert came one minute before the attack.

Here the story gets specific. Beskrestnov said that other intelligence data keeps flowing and that the problem is "solely with the air raid alert component." Translation: the US is apparently still sharing what it knows about Russia, except for the narrow part that tells people when to run. He said Ukraine has its own radar for some limited warning, but it is no substitute for the American feed.

Officials in Washington offer several explanations, not all of them compatible. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there had been no "pause" and that he did not know why Zelensky was telling the press such things, then said a few nights earlier there was a software problem involving Ukraine's early warning system. President Trump, asked about intelligence sharing, said: "We've given a lot to Ukraine. They've given us nothing." A White House official said the US has provided more aid than any other country.

Then there is Starshield. Translation: Starshield is the military-grade service built for the Pentagon on the same network as Starlink, and Kyiv uses it to connect drones, helicopters and military communications. Last week it began capping the speed at which connected mobile units can travel at 100mph (160km/h), which limits Ukraine's ability to shoot down incoming drones or launch mid-range drone attacks into Russian-occupied areas. Al Jazeera reported that Zelensky warned the limits are disrupting early warning air defence systems, and that the US government has not explained the cause.

Responsibility is being handed around like a hot dish. Elon Musk said last week that the US government ultimately controls access to Starshield, suggesting it may be behind the restrictions. Trump has denied involvement. Beskrestnov said the US has given no clarity about who is responsible, and the BBC says it has contacted Starlink for comment.

Beskrestnov offered his own reading: that the two problems arriving together was probably deliberate pressure. "It's probably the United States trying to say [to] us, 'let's have the conversation, negotiation with Russia, you should find the compromise'," he said. Washington denies any change, which leaves Kyiv to infer motives from a pattern the denial says does not exist.

Meanwhile the war continued. Ukrainian officials said at least six people were killed late Monday, including a toddler and an 11-year-old girl, in a drone strike near a park café in the Kharkiv region, with another 14 injured. Zelensky called it an "absolute atrocity." NPR noted that the two sides exchanged heavy strikes over the weekend as Trump-Zelensky relations soured.

Ukraine's response is the sensible one for anyone whose critical infrastructure belongs to someone else: Beskrestnov said officials are urgently looking at alternatives to Starlink, for air defence, military communications and civilians alike. The lesson is not subtle. A lifeline that can be adjusted by a software update or a phone call is, strictly speaking, a subscription.
