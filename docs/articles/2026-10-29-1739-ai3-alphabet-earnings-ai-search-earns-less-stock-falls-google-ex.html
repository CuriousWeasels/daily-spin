Google Discovers AI Search Pays Less, Responds by Adding More Ads

Alphabet's stock fell sharply after search revenue growth slowed to 12 percent, and the company said it will spend significantly more on AI in 2027.

The wire said: Google admits AI search makes less money, but it's fixing that with more ads · Ars Technica AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 29, 2026 17:39 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Alphabet reported third-quarter results showing revenue up 25 percent to about $127.6 billion, but search growth slowed and investors worried that AI answers earn less than the old blue links. Google says its answer is more ads inside AI Mode, now going international.

Why it matters

Google's search ads fund much of the free internet's plumbing, so whether AI answers can earn as much as links affects what you see, what you pay for, and who gets paid. Investors are also being asked to fund a record AI buildout before the payoff is proven.

Alphabet reported third-quarter revenue of about $127.6 billion, up 25 percent from last year, and its shares fell anyway. Reports put the drop between roughly 8 and 12 percent depending on the outlet and the hour, which is the kind of disagreement that happens when a stock is moving faster than a copy editor. Ars Technica called it one of the largest post-earnings drops in the company's history.

The cause was search. Search revenue grew 12 percent, down from 17 percent in the second quarter and 19 percent in the first, according to TechCrunch. The Decoder reported that analysts had expected about 15 percent. Translation: the business is still growing at a rate most companies would frame and hang on a wall, but Wall Street had already priced in a nicer number.

The more interesting admission came from Chief Business Officer Philipp Schindler, who said, per Ars Technica, that each AI Mode query earns less than a comparable web search, with fewer ads that cost less. The Decoder quoted him saying the growth in AI Mode queries had an impact because of the lower monetization. In plain terms, Google built a product people like more and that pays Google less, which is a rare business model usually reserved for public libraries.

Google's proposed fix is to put more ads in it. Schindler announced that ads in AI Mode are expanding into international markets, alongside multi-turn ads in the U.S. He said click-through rates for sponsored product listings in AI Mode are already comparable to organic listings, and that user research shows people find the ads relevant. Translation: the company asked people whether they liked the ads, and the people reportedly said they were fine.

Executives also offered reasons for cheer. Sundar Pichai said search queries grew at their fastest annual rate in five years, though he did not give the figure, and that AI Mode queries run three times longer than traditional ones. The Gemini app reached one billion users. Whether longer questions become more profitable questions is the part nobody has yet demonstrated on a balance sheet.

Cloud was the bright spot. Revenue rose 103 percent to $30.7 billion, about a billion above analyst guesses, according to Ars Technica. But Google is spending to match: capital expenditures, meaning money spent on data centers and chips, hit $51.4 billion in the quarter. The full-year 2026 forecast stays at $195 billion to $205 billion, and the company expects spending to increase significantly in 2027.

Ars Technica also noted that net income was almost $9 billion lower than a year ago, and earnings per share of $2.12 trailed the $3.02 consensus. The Decoder added that management declined, even when asked twice, to disclose the profit margin on selling its TPU chip systems to outside customers. When a company is asked the same margin question twice and answers neither time, analysts tend to notice.

The investor logic is easy to follow. Google is spending record sums on AI, its flagship AI product earns less per question than the product it replaces, and the plan is to spend more next year. The bet is that cloud customers and eventually better ads make up the difference. Next quarter will show whether the ads in AI Mode are a fix or merely a bigger billboard on the same road.
