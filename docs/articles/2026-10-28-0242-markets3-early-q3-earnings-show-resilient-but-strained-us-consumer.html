Corporate America Finds Consumer 'Resilient,' Mostly Because the Word Is Free

Third-quarter earnings from Coca-Cola, UPS and Royal Caribbean point to steady spending, while Whirlpool and Sherwin-Williams report something closer to endurance.

The wire said: Early third-quarter earnings show a 'resilient' U.S. consumer, with signs of strain · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 28, 2026 02:42 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Third-quarter earnings season has started, and companies are describing the U.S. consumer as holding up despite record diesel prices, high borrowing costs and tension with Iran. CNBC reported that retail sales rose 0.8% in September after a 1.3% jump in August.

Why it matters

Consumer spending drives most of the U.S. economy, so whether households keep buying shapes jobs, inflation and the odds of a slowdown. The early reports suggest spending is intact, but some of it reflects higher prices rather than more stuff.

Several large U.S. companies reported third-quarter results this week, and according to CNBC, one word kept turning up: resilient. Royal Caribbean, UPS, Coca-Cola, Sherwin-Williams and even Whirlpool used it or something close to it. It is an efficient word, since it can describe a booming business and a shrinking one with equal confidence.

Start with the cheerful cases. Royal Caribbean carried 2.7 million guests in the quarter, up 9% from a year earlier, and said bookings for 2027 were already "up in the high teens." CEO Jason Liberty told CNBC that even through challenges, the consumer "comes roaring back." Translation: people who booked a cruise would like to keep their cruise. Shares closed up more than 5% on Tuesday.

UPS beat expectations on earnings and revenue, with U.S. revenue up 4.5% to $14.86 billion. It raised its full-year revenue forecast for the second quarter in a row, to about $92.6 billion from $91.2 billion. CEO Carol Tomé called consumers resilient heading into the holidays, though the company also expects fourth-quarter domestic daily volume to fall because it is shrinking its Amazon deliveries. That is a decline the company chose, which is the most flattering kind.

Coca-Cola beat estimates and raised its outlook, now expecting comparable earnings per share to grow at least 11%, up from 9% to 10%. North American unit case volume was flat. Translation: Americans bought the same number of cases, at higher prices, which the company classifies as steady demand. CFO John Murphy told CNBC that shoppers at every income level are "looking for value," a phrase that means they are comparing prices and still choosing soda.

Then there is the less resilient end of the resilience spectrum. Sherwin-Williams beat on revenue but missed on earnings per share. Sales rose 7.9%, which the company attributed to higher selling prices, while raw materials and supply-chain costs ate into profit. It kept its full-year guidance and said customer sentiment points to "muted demand" through 2027. In plain terms, the paint is more expensive and the walls are not getting painted faster.

Whirlpool missed on both revenue and profit, cut its full-year revenue guidance, and reported North American net sales down more than 8%. Its CFO nonetheless cited the "balance sheet resilience" of the company. Shares still closed up more than 3%, suggesting investors had expected something worse and rewarded the company for delivering it.

The backdrop explains the nervousness. CNBC reported diesel hit a record $6.52 per gallon in September, and economists feared that costly gas, high borrowing costs and U.S.-Iran tension would make shoppers pull back. Official data show Brent crude at $115.22, the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.0% and CPI inflation at 3.74% year over year. September's retail sales were also boosted by spending at gas stations, meaning part of the strength is people paying more to drive to the stores.

The pattern is a consumer who is still spending, but increasingly on cruises and cola rather than washing machines and paint. Watch the holiday-season guidance next. If "resilient" is still the word in January, it will either be true or very well rehearsed.
