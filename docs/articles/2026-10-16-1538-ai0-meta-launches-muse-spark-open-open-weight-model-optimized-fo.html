Meta Releases Giant Open AI Model, Needs Only Two $10,000-Plus Desktops to Run It

Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter model built to run on two Apple Mac Studios, is billed by Meta as the most capable US open-weight model to date.

The wire said: Meta and Apple team up to make Muse AI models run better on Macs · The Verge AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 16, 2026 15:38 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Meta, which built its AI reputation on the open Llama models, has released Muse Spark Open, a new model whose weights anyone can download. It was optimized with Apple, a company Meta usually treats as a rival.

Why it matters

Open-weight models let developers run AI on their own hardware instead of renting it, which can cut costs and keep data in-house. But the hardware bar here is high, and the license still gives Meta a hand in who profits from the model.

Meta on Thursday released Muse Spark Open, an AI model it calls the "most capable US open-weight model to date." According to The Verge, TechCrunch and The Decoder, the model has 816 billion parameters, a context window of up to 1 million tokens, and handles text, images, video and audio. Meta also says it can run on a desktop, provided your desk can hold two computers and your budget can hold a very different conversation.

Translation: an open-weight model is one whose trained numbers, the weights, are published for anyone to download and run. That differs from open source in the strict sense, because the recipe and the data stay private. Parameters are those weights, and more of them generally means a more capable model and a hungrier computer.

The Decoder reports that only 42 billion of the parameters are active at any moment, which is how a model this big can fit on consumer-adjacent hardware at all. Meta says it runs locally on two Mac Studios with Apple's M5 Ultra chip and 512 GB of unified memory each. TechCrunch, citing Engadget, put the requirement at at least 1 terabyte of RAM. For comparison, a typical laptop ships with 16 GB, so this is local AI in the sense that a private jet is personal transportation.

The collaboration is the more unusual part. Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg thanked Apple and its new CEO, John Ternus, on X, and Ternus replied that running a model of this scale "right from your desk" was amazing. Meta's chief AI officer Alexandr Wang told Axios the companies were "excited for mommy and daddy to be playing nice again." Axios noted the two have been collaborating for several months. Apple also published a technical post on MLX, its framework for running machine learning on Apple chips.

On performance, Meta's own benchmarks, which no outside party has yet verified in these sources, show Muse Spark Open scoring well ahead of rival open models Qwen 3.8, GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3 in several categories. The Decoder adds that it still falls well short of Meta's paid Muse Wonder Full models. Translation: the free one is the best free one, according to the company that made it, and the company also sells a better one.

The license is free to use but requires the label "Built with Muse Spark Open." Providers earning more than $20 million a year who want to sell the model as an API service need a separate agreement with Meta. Open, in other words, comes with a threshold, past which it becomes a negotiation.

Meta showed the model fixing a Mastodon client, from bug fixes to a full build, using its Muse Code terminal tool, and cropping footage in DaVinci Resolve. Weights are on Hugging Face and the model is available through several API platforms. The thing to watch is whether developers who can afford a terabyte of memory actually prefer it to renting a cloud model.
