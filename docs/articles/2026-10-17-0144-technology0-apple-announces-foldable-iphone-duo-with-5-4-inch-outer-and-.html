Apple Unveils $1,999 Foldable iPhone, Sells Out Before Lunch, Asks Customers to Wait Until 2027

The iPhone Duo arrives late to a market Samsung and Google already built, and Apple says demand is 'extraordinary,' which is easier to claim when delivery dates say January.

The wire said: All the news about the iPhone Duo · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 17, 2026 01:44 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple announced its first foldable phone, the iPhone Duo, at its 'Surprise and shine' event, alongside the iPhone 18 Pro and new Apple Watch models. Preorders opened Friday at 8AM ET, with in-store sales set for October 23.

Why it matters

A $1,999 starting price makes the foldable a test of how much buyers will pay for Apple's polish on a form factor rivals already sell. The repair prices show the cost of ownership does not end at the register.

Apple has announced the iPhone Duo, its first foldable phone, and according to The Verge, delivery estimates slipped into late January 2027 in less than an hour after preorders opened. Apple called demand 'nothing short of extraordinary,' a phrase that sounds better coming from a company with a product page than from one with a shelf full of units.

The device has a 5.4-inch outer screen that unfolds to a 7.6-inch inner display with a matte 'nano-texture' finish and a crease The Verge's reviewer could barely find with a finger. It starts at $1,999 for 256GB. Translation: the base model, the one with the least storage, already costs about as much as a decent used laptop, and prices rise from there.

Some specifics matter for the curious. It uses the A20 Pro chip found in the iPhone 18 Pro, a 48-megapixel main camera without variable aperture, and a wide-angle lens. It has an IP68 rating, meaning it is rated for dust and water resistance, which is a notable claim for a phone with a hinge. There is no Face ID. Instead, a Touch ID sensor sits on the power button, a fingerprint reader returning like a relative who was never really gone. Apple Pencil support is promised later this year.

The Verge's take is that the hardware is familiar and the software is the draw. Huawei, Samsung, and Xiaomi all shipped passport-shaped foldables before Apple, which The Verge attributed to Apple's slower supply chain. Apple, for its part, has long said it would make a foldable when the technology was good enough. Translation: it waited until someone else had done the embarrassing part. iOS 27 has been redesigned for the device, including a dock on the side and an opening animation when you unfold it.

Apple also addressed copycats, with new CEO John Ternus and marketing chief Greg Joswiak discussing it in an interview, according to The Verge. The Verge's explanation is that secrecy is nearly impossible when Apple and its rivals share the same global suppliers. In other words, everyone's parts come from the same few factories, so everyone's phones end up looking like cousins.

Then there is the bill after the bill. Apple's published out-of-warranty repair estimates, reported by MacRumors, are $229 for a battery, $999 for inner display damage, and $1,499 for 'other damage.' The last figure is close to the price of a new phone in the regular iPhone range, and Apple's AppleCare Plus plan is presented as the way out. The screen folds beautifully, and the invoice for it unfolds even better.

One practical note: online delivery dates are in January, but Apple typically holds back units for its stores, so walk-ins on October 23 may have a shot. Apple did not say whether any inventory would be available to people who missed preorders. Watch whether 'extraordinary demand' survives contact with a $1,499 repair quote.
