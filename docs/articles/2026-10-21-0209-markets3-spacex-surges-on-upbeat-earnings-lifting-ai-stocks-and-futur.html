SpaceX Stock Surges on Earnings, Convincing Wall Street the AI Boom Needs Rockets After All

MarketWatch says demand for SpaceX's computing power is 'off the charts,' lifting the wider AI trade and U.S. stock futures.

The wire said: U.S. stock futures rise as SpaceX surges on upbeat earnings · Investing.com

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 21, 2026 02:09 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

SpaceX is now a publicly traded company reporting earnings, and investors have been trying to work out what it is worth. Its latest results apparently contained a lot of talk about computing power.

Why it matters

If a rocket company's earnings can move the broader AI trade, index funds and retirement accounts are more exposed to a single theme than many holders realize. It also means the AI rally is being judged on demand for computing, not just on promises.

U.S. stock futures rose after SpaceX shares surged on earnings that Investing.com described as upbeat. Futures are contracts that bet on where the market will open, so this is Wall Street announcing its mood before anyone has actually gone to work. Translation: the market has decided it feels good, and the paperwork will follow.

According to MarketWatch, the reason is demand for SpaceX's computing power, which it described as 'off the charts.' The outlet said the result gave Wall Street 'a big reason to believe in the AI boom,' and that the stock was lifting the broader AI universe with it. Translation: a company best known for launching things into orbit has become evidence that the AI trade has customers, which is the kind of evidence the AI trade had been politely requesting.

The mechanism is simple. AI companies spend heavily on chips and data centers, and investors keep asking who will pay for all of it. When a company reports that customers are lining up for computing capacity, it answers that question in one line. Every company selling to or depending on that demand gets a little more credible, and a little more expensive.

The backdrop is less cheerful. The latest official data show the S&P 500 fund SPY at 769.05 and the Nasdaq at 26,430.89. The VIX, a gauge of expected market swings often called the fear index, sits at 18.44, which is roughly the sound of a market claiming to be calm. Brent crude is at 126.55, and the dollar index is at 121.4.

Rates are the other fine print. The 10-year Treasury yields 5.15% and the 2-year 4.77%, while the Fed funds rate is 3.88%. That means the government borrows for a decade at a rate well above the central bank's own benchmark. Inflation was 3.74% year over year as of September, and unemployment was 4.2%. Translation: money is not cheap, prices are still rising, and stocks are priced as though neither fact will interfere.

That is the real test for the rally. A 5.15% risk-free return makes every promised future profit worth less today, so AI valuations need results that arrive on schedule. One strong earnings report helps. Whether it counts as a trend or a good quarter is something the next few reports will settle.
