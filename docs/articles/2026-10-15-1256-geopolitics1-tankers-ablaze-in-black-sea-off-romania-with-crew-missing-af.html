Black Sea Tanker Burns Off Romania as NATO Members Weigh Which Article to Read Aloud

Eight of 12 crew on the Wine Trader are missing after a reported drone strike, as Romania and Bulgaria consider NATO consultations and the EU debates a moratorium on hitting civilian ships.

The wire said: Eight crew missing after drone attack leaves tanker ablaze off Romania's coast · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 15, 2026 12:56 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Russia and Ukraine have escalated attacks on merchant shipping in the Black Sea, now reaching waters off NATO members Romania and Bulgaria. Moscow says it targets ships carrying military cargo to Ukraine; Kyiv and its allies say civilian vessels are being hit.

Why it matters

Fewer safe shipping routes mean fewer grain and fuel cargoes, higher freight costs and food prices far from the war, and a real risk to the sailors who crew these ships. Drones landing near NATO territory also test how far the alliance's rules about consultation will be stretched.

The San Marino-flagged tanker Wine Trader caught fire about 13 nautical miles east of the Danube mouth at Sulina on Thursday morning after reporting explosions, the BBC reported. Romanian President Nicușor Dan later confirmed it was struck by drones. Eight of its 12 crew were reported missing, though DW's earlier count put the missing at seven, with five of the 12 safe. The sources disagree on the numbers, which is unsurprising when the main method of counting is waiting for helicopters.

Romania has sent two Black Hawk helicopters and two rescue boats, but the BBC said very high waves are complicating the effort, and the priority is reaching four crew members confirmed alive. A second chemical and oil tanker, the Comoros-flagged Yara J, caught fire hours earlier near the Sulina Canal, according to DW. Its crew is said to be safe. Both ships were anchored there, a spot ships use to wait out weather or congestion, which is a poor strategy when the congestion has drones.

Officials are being careful about attribution. Defense Minister Radu Miruta told Digi24 that an investigation is underway into whether a drone struck the ship, calling it a matter of 'significant gravity.' DW noted that Russian drones had strayed into Romanian airspace but that officials had not explicitly blamed them for the fires. President Dan was less hesitant on the drones, and said there will be a response from the Romanian state, without saying what that is.

The leaders of Romania and Bulgaria are due to discuss whether to invoke Article 4 of the NATO treaty. Translation: Article 4 is not the famous one about mutual defense. It simply lets a member ask allies to sit down and talk when it feels its security is threatened, which is the diplomatic equivalent of calling a meeting after the fire alarm. Dan is meeting NATO chief Mark Rutte in Brussels with Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev to discuss equipment to protect shipping.

The attack follows a bad week. On Tuesday two merchant ships were hit off Bulgaria and one sank, leaving 11 crew missing, and Bulgaria summoned the Russian envoy. DW counts at least five other cargo ships struck off Romania and Bulgaria in 10 days, with at least 23 sailors lost. Moscow says it hit two cargo ships carrying military cargo to Odesa but named neither, and the BBC notes Russia says it targets weapons and dual-use cargo, while Kyiv and its allies say grain and civilian goods are being hit. Ukraine also hit an oil tanker at Sochi last week, so nobody is the sole practitioner.

Diplomacy is busy. EU leaders at their Brussels summit will discuss a proposed moratorium on attacks on civilian ships, according to Radev, and European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen called the Black Sea 'another front of escalation.' Moscow has repeatedly rejected a naval ceasefire. India, which supplies more than 12% of the world's seafarers, per a 2026 industry report cited by DW, has proposed safe passage, and Ukraine's foreign minister called it the 'broadest' of four offers. Russia has welcomed India's peace efforts without endorsing a binding deal. A US-announced 'energy ceasefire' has unclear scope, and Ukraine alleges it has already been breached.

The economics are less mysterious than the diplomacy. DW quoted economist Lekha Chakraborty saying prolonged disruption would raise India's import bill through higher freight on Russian crude and tighter edible oil supply. That means the price of cooking oil in Mumbai now depends partly on whether a drone operator near Snake Island has a quiet night. Next to watch: whether the Brussels meetings produce a moratorium, and whether anyone agrees to honor it.
