Chip Stocks Extend Losing Streak to Longest Since 2008 as Investors Rediscover Risk

A Friday selloff hit software and semiconductors together, pushed the VIX above 22, and left traders paying millions for protection against next month.

The wire said: Chip index SOX posts longest losing streak since 2008 as AI worries deepen · Investing.com

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 17, 2026 14:30 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Stocks tied to artificial intelligence have been under pressure, and on Friday worries about the AI trade and interest rates hit both chip and software shares. CNBC reported the Nasdaq fell 2%, its worst day since June 5th, while CoreWeave dropped 13%.

Why it matters

If you own an index fund, you probably own a large helping of AI-linked companies, so a rough stretch for chips and software shows up in your retirement account whether or not you ever opened a chatbot. Rising volatility also tends to precede more of the same, or at least more expensive hedging.

Investing.com reported that the chip index SOX has posted its longest losing streak since 2008, a year most investors remember with the warmth normally reserved for dental surgery. MarketWatch added that software and semiconductor stocks, which have not traded in step much this year, fell together on Friday as AI and interest-rate fears hit the market. The headline it chose was "Nowhere to hide," which is the kind of thing a headline says when diversification has briefly gone out for lunch.

Translation: software companies and chipmakers are supposed to offer some balance to each other, the way owning both an umbrella and sunscreen covers most weather. On Friday the weather was simply bad. Both sectors fell, and the only hedge available was to own less of both.

CNBC reported that the selloff in AI-related stocks pushed the Cboe Volatility Index, or VIX, above 22 for the first time in four months. Translation: the VIX is Wall Street's fear gauge, a number derived from options prices that rises when traders pay more to protect themselves. The most recent official reading in our data, 16.46 as of October 15, is a calmer number that now looks like a historical document.

The Nasdaq lost 2%, according to CNBC, its worst day since June 5th. CoreWeave, which CNBC described as a neo-cloud provider, fell 13% amid what it called fears around the company. Translation: a neo-cloud provider rents out computing power for AI work, which makes it a useful barometer of how nervous people are about the cost of building all this machinery.

The more interesting action was in the options market. CNBC reported that one trader bought 9,200 of the November 18, 20-strike VIX calls for $1.7 million and 12,500 of the 23-strike calls for $1.1 million, a bet that the VIX closes above 23 by the third week of November. Translation: a call option is a ticket that pays off if a number goes up, so these traders are buying tickets to a worse month.

Of the ten most popular VIX contracts bought Friday, nine were calls, and CNBC said nearly $27 million in premium was spent on those nine, about 36% of all premium traded in VIX options that day. Brent Kochuba of SpotGamma, a CNBC contributor, said the speed of the rise and heavy volume suggest traders are cashing in October positions and rolling into protection against a larger downturn. In other words, some people are taking profits on fear and immediately buying more fear, which is at least consistent.

Macro conditions give the nervousness something to chew on. Our latest data show the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.1%, the 2-year at 4.77%, CPI inflation at 3.74% year over year and Brent crude at $126.55. Translation: borrowing is expensive, prices are rising faster than anyone would like, and the market is being asked to pay high multiples for companies whose profits arrive later.

Next week brings a test, as CNBC noted that Tesla, SpaceX and Meta are due to report earnings. If those results reassure investors, the VIX tickets may expire as expensive souvenirs. If not, a few traders will have been very well dressed for the occasion.
