Google Cloud Adds OpenAI Models, Hosting Every Rival Its Gemini Team Has

Google now sells Gemini, Claude and GPT-6 models on one bill, a convenient arrangement for customers and an awkward one for Google's own AI researchers.

The wire said: Google Cloud now offers OpenAI models alongside Gemini and Claude, becoming the only major provider with all three · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 22, 2026 02:03 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google Cloud already offered its own Gemini models and Anthropic's Claude. It has now added OpenAI's GPT-6 family, which makes it the only large cloud provider carrying all three leading closed models, according to Google.

Why it matters

Companies can switch between top AI models without signing new contracts or moving data, which weakens any one lab's hold on a customer. It also shows that the cloud businesses selling the computing power may profit whichever lab wins.

Google Cloud said it will offer three OpenAI models: GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna. The Decoder reported that they launch as a private preview for select enterprise customers, with sign-ups open now. Ars Technica reported that they will arrive in Google's Model Garden in the coming weeks. The sources differ slightly on timing, so readers should treat the phrase "available starting today" as meaning "available to some people, eventually to others."

Translation: Model Garden is Google's catalog of AI models, which the Ars report says holds more than 200 options. Gemini, Claude and open models such as Mistral, Llama and Gemma were already on the shelves. The new arrival is the competitor Google's own product team would presumably prefer to see left off the shelf.

The three models are sorted by price and ambition. According to The Decoder, Astra handles demanding work such as coding and research, Sol offers similar performance more cheaply, and Luna is a fast, low-cost option for everyday use. Ars notes that Astra will naturally cost the most to run, though Google's system can move workloads between models based on performance, latency and efficiency. In other words, the software can decide that your question did not need the expensive brain.

The commercial pitch is mostly about paperwork. All charges go through a single Google Cloud bill and count toward existing spending contracts, per The Decoder. Ars adds that the models can run entirely on Google's infrastructure, with transactions handled through the Google Cloud Marketplace. Translation: a company that promised Google a large sum of money can now spend it on a rival's product and still be a good customer. Google also says customer data will not be used for training.

The models will also appear in Gemini for Business and in Antigravity, Google's agent-powered coding tool. Ars called the Antigravity part perhaps the more important one. A coding tool that offers a competitor's model is a tidy admission that customers choose on results and not on loyalty.

OpenAI, for its part, noted that Microsoft remains its primary cloud partner. That is the corporate equivalent of mentioning your spouse early in the conversation.

The Decoder points to a recurring tension at Google. Computing capacity used to serve cloud customers strengthens the biggest rivals of its own Gemini team. The outlet says this resource conflict reportedly even pushed two-billion-dollar researcher Noam Shazeer back to OpenAI. The report is secondhand, but the economics are plain: every chip rented to a rival is a chip the in-house team cannot use.

What to watch is which model customers actually route their work to once the choice is a dropdown menu. If Gemini wins, Google gets to say the market chose. If it does not, Google still gets paid, which may be the point.
