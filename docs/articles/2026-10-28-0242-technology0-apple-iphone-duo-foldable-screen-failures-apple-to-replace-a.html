Apple's $2,000 Foldable Breaks Within Hours; Apple Counts 'Small Number'

Apple says it will replace iPhone Duos with failing inner screens, but won't say how many, leaving buyers to do their own math.

The wire said: Some iPhone Duo screens are already failing, Apple says only a "small number" are affected · Ars Technica

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 28, 2026 02:42 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple launched its first foldable, the iPhone Duo, last week at $1,999 and up in the US and £1,999 in the UK. Photos of devices with bright lines across the inner display soon spread online.

Why it matters

Anyone paying roughly two thousand dollars for a first-generation device is also paying to beta test it. Ars Technica reports that an inner screen replacement reportedly costs half the phone's price, so the warranty position matters a great deal.

Apple has acknowledged that some iPhone Duo handsets have developed inner-screen problems days after going on sale, the BBC reported. The company said it was aware of "an issue impacting the inner display on a small number" of phones and urged affected owners to contact it for a replacement. It did not say what the number is. Translation: small is a measurement, not a statistic.

Ars Technica described the damage as several bright lines starting just below the middle of the display and running to the bottom and right edges, sometimes with dead pixels along the crease. Translation: in an OLED screen, each pixel makes its own light. When the layers are damaged, some pixels receive full voltage and stay on at maximum brightness, while others get none and go black. The only repair is a new panel, which Ars says reportedly costs half as much as the device.

Timing is the awkward part. Ars reports that most failures seem to happen within about a day of use, though some buyers say they opened and closed the phone only a few times. Others reportedly never finished setup. Some owners have had success swapping phones at Apple stores, including one in New York, though Ars cautions that spare units may be scarce. The BBC also noted reports of cracks along the crease and a crunching noise when opening the device.

The scale is genuinely unclear, and both outlets say so. The BBC noted that some images of faulty phones may have been generated with AI, which is a modern complication: a product defect now arrives with an authenticity check. Francisco Jeronimo of IDC said caution is needed before judging the whole production run. He also said the reports show that even Apple "faces a steep learning curve in foldables."

Apple is not the first to learn this on customers' time. The BBC recalled that Samsung's first foldable, the Galaxy Fold, had broken screens in reviewers' hands in 2019. Apple has the advantage of watching that happen for years, and Android users already have a broad choice of foldables. Apple's own pitch, delivered by new chief executive John Ternus, was that others had made foldables that feel like two phones awkwardly stuck together. The Duo, he said, would "redefine the experience of using a foldable phone."

As for the cause, nobody official has said. Ars notes that iFixit has already taken a Duo apart. Foldables need a cable crossing the hinge, a usual suspect for pressing on the screen. The Duo's wide cable has accordion-like pleats, but iFixit says this does not quite match the reported damage. The mystery is therefore unresolved, which is how it stays until a company issues a statement with the word 'investigating' in it.

The review picture is mixed as well. Ars Technica's reviewer found the crease nearly invisible, but called the device technically impressive yet not always enjoyable, good as a small iPad and hard to prefer to a regular iPhone. That is a fair summary of a $2,000 purchase that may also need a second $2,000 purchase.

Next up is October 30, when a further 28 markets get the phone. Jeronimo said Apple needs to clarify its warranty position quickly. Until then, the safest accessory is a receipt.
