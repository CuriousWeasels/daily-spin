Israel Answers Checkpoint Attack With Hebron Bulldozers, Four Days Before Voting

Netanyahu pledges the largest West Bank operation since 1967 after two soldiers were killed, as the demolition of homes in Hebron displaces hundreds of families.

The wire said: Israeli PM pledges massive military operation after West Bank checkpoint attack · NPR World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 25, 2026 01:53 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Two Palestinian gunmen attacked an Israeli military checkpoint near Hebron in the occupied West Bank, killing two soldiers and injuring three before they were themselves killed. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu then announced a sweeping operation, with Israel's general election four days away.

Why it matters

Hundreds of Palestinian families have lost their homes, and talk of a far larger West Bank campaign could widen the conflict. The timing also shows how security policy and election strategy can become hard to tell apart.

Israel's military has demolished homes and businesses in the West Bank city of Hebron and displaced hundreds of families, the BBC reported, after two Palestinian gunmen killed two Israeli soldiers at a checkpoint. Three other soldiers were wounded, and both gunmen died. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu announced the operation on Friday, and the election is scheduled for four days later, a coincidence that everyone involved is expected to treat as coincidence.

The numbers differ depending on who is counting. Defense Minister Israel Katz said about 2,000 residents had been displaced, centred on the Ein Deir Baha and Farsh al-Hawa neighbourhoods, and that the military planned to displace 2,500 people from roughly 0.35 square kilometres of urban land. Hebron's governor and deputy mayor said at least 200 families had been displaced. Translation: the official tally of people who no longer have a home depends on which official you ask, and none of the figures is small.

According to the BBC, dozens of families spent the night outside, some told to leave without their vehicles, and homes were marked for demolition with spray paint. Footage shared online appeared to show at least one whole street flattened. The military has also cordoned off the city, heavily restricting the movement of its 200,000 residents. The IDF did not immediately respond to the BBC's request for comment, but said on social media that it had dozens of targets across the West Bank and that operations would last several days.

Demolishing the homes of suspected attackers is a long-standing Israeli practice. Israeli rights groups have criticised it as collective punishment, which is the legal term for penalising people for something they did not do. Netanyahu said buildings around the attack site and in the gunmen's neighbourhoods would be destroyed, and compared the operation with earlier ones in northern refugee camps, where hundreds of homes were demolished and tens of thousands displaced in 2025.

Netanyahu, who has campaigned on security, cancelled his party's Friday campaign events and went to Hebron in protective armour, posting a video from the scene. He said he had reviewed plans for the largest West Bank operation since 1967, without saying when it might happen, and it is unclear whether Hebron is part of it. Few campaign stops come with this much logistical support.

The coalition's right flank wants more. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called for a campaign in the West Bank "exactly like in Gaza or Lebanon," and National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir urged dismantling the Palestinian Authority. Netanyahu, in an i24 interview, promised the Authority "a very big surprise," which in diplomacy is not usually a birthday gift. The Authority asked for international action "beyond condemnation," a request that has seldom been answered by condemnation alone.

Hebron's mayor, Youssef al-Jaabari, said the operation was "driven by the elections" and aimed at winning far-right and settler votes. The government has framed it as a response to the killings of soldiers. Both explanations can be true at once, which is convenient for anyone who needs to defend the plan and inconvenient for the families sleeping outside.

What to watch next: whether the Hebron operation grows into the larger campaign Netanyahu described, and what happens on Tuesday, when voters will have a chance to weigh in on the plan.
