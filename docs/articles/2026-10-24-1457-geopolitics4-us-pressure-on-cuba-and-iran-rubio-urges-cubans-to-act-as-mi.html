Rubio Invites Cubans to 'Discrete Partnership' With CIA, Via Social Media Post

After Trump said a warship might stop 50 yards off Cuba on its way home from Iran, the secretary of state publicly urged Cubans to act now, days before midterm elections.

The wire said: Rubio tells Cubans ‘now is the time’ to act to see political change · The Guardian

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 24, 2026 14:57 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Trump has floated intervention in Cuba since earlier this year, and in May the administration announced criminal charges against former leader Raúl Castro. Meanwhile the US war on Iran, which Trump has long said is winding down, continues.

Why it matters

A public CIA recruitment pitch and talk of warships near Cuba raise the risk of escalation 90 miles from Florida, while voters already worried about prices from gas to groceries weigh a war that has not ended.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio published a social media post urging Cubans who want political change to act, saying "now is the time," the Guardian reported. The post came moments after President Donald Trump told a Friday rally in south Florida that US warships might intervene in Cuba on their way back from the Iran war. It is not usual for a foreign policy operation to be announced at a campaign rally and then supplemented with a help-wanted notice, but this is the one we have.

Trump was answering a shouted question from the crowd. "We'll do a little stopover," he said, adding that a ship would park about 50 yards off the coast, that Cubans would "raise up their hands," and that Rubio would then go in "to finish up the deal." Translation: the plan is a naval visit, a surrender, and a paperwork stage, in that order. The Guardian noted that Trump first raised the idea earlier this year but had not mentioned it in recent months.

Rubio's post told Cubans to seize the opportunity "through a discrete [sic] partnership with the CIA." The Guardian pointed out that "discrete" means separate, while "discreet" means secret. This matters because the entire value of a covert partnership is that it is discreet, and it is now being advertised on social media.

Havana responded with the enthusiasm one would expect. Deputy foreign minister Carlos Fernandez de Cossio wrote on X that the US was resorting to "public recruitment for the CIA," and that the agency must be desperate to sell its services "like a tube of toothpaste." That is a sharper marketing critique than most governments manage.

The timing is hard to ignore. The rally was days before midterm elections in which, the Guardian said, voters have voiced concern about the war on Iran's effect on prices of goods from gas to groceries. NPR reported separately that military reinforcements are moving to the Middle East and some personnel have left the region, pointing to a potential attack on Iran, while millions of Americans have already voted. NPR also spoke with retired Gen. Joseph Votel about US and Israeli objectives for a possible post-election bombing campaign. Translation: the war that was supposedly winding down is being prepared for a sequel, after the ballots are counted.

The administration's Cuba line has not been consistent. In September, Trump told the UN General Assembly that Rubio was "deep into negotiations with Cuba," and days later said he did not think the military would be needed because "Cuba is failing very badly." In May, he told reporters it "looks like I'll be the one that does it." Rubio, in a 29 September Fox News interview, said Cuba "has already fallen" and made no mention of the CIA. Talks with Cuban officials left the US side unimpressed, the Guardian said, and produced more sanctions.

Cuba's difficulties are real. Since 2020 its economy has shrunk, and its 10 million residents have endured blackouts, food shortages and inflation, with waves of protests and hundreds of thousands emigrating, many to the US. That is the backdrop for an appeal that asks people living under those conditions to partner with a foreign intelligence agency, using a post that anyone, including their government, can read.

What to watch: whether any warship actually stops 50 yards off the coast, and whether the Iran campaign, which has not ended yet, leaves any ships free to pass Cuba on the way home.
