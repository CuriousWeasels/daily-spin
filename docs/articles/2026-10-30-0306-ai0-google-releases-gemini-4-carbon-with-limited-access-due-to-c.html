Google Releases Gemini 4 Carbon Two Weeks After Argon, Lets Almost Nobody Use It

The new model tops an independent intelligence index, but access is limited to selected security experts while it goes through voluntary U.S. government testing.

The wire said: Google Deepmind releases Gemini 4 Carbon just two weeks after Argon with gains across the board · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 30, 2026 03:06 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google DeepMind released Gemini 4 Argon earlier this month and has now followed with Gemini 4 Carbon, a stronger version aimed at long-running coding and cybersecurity work. Carbon is rolling out in stages, starting with a small group of vetted security experts.

Why it matters

Frontier AI models are improving on a two-week cadence while the most capable versions reach the public last, if at all. Anyone choosing an AI vendor is increasingly comparing products they cannot yet try, separated by a few benchmark points.

Google DeepMind announced Gemini 4 Carbon on Wednesday, two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon. According to The Decoder, Carbon scores 59 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index v5, up from Argon's 50, which puts it first. Ars Technica summarized the release as a frontier model that you can't use, which is a fair description of the availability.

Access starts with selected cybersecurity experts, and The Verge reported it will later widen to more partners in Google's Fairwind Program. Google says the restriction reflects Carbon's cyber capabilities, and the model is going through the U.S. government's voluntary pre-release testing. Translation: voluntary pre-release testing means a company asks officials to look at its model before the public does, which is reassuring and also a sign of what the model can do.

Those capabilities are the headline. Carbon scores 88.2 percent on SEC-bench Pro, which measures finding vulnerabilities in real software, and 54.8 percent on ExploitGym, which tests whether a model can turn a vulnerability into a working exploit. The Decoder noted that Google ran some of these tests without safety guardrails. The same skill that patches a hole can, in principle, find one, so the cautious rollout is the part of the story that makes sense.

Google also says Carbon can work for days on engineering tasks, pushing through dead ends on its own. Independent numbers are less uniform. In agentic coding it scores 59 against Claude Opus 5.5's 62, still behind despite a big jump from Argon's 53. On one knowledge-work benchmark, AA-Briefcase, it trails Kimi K3.1 Pro, Muse Wonder and Grok 4.7.

The accuracy figures come with a footnote. Answer accuracy rose from 48 to 58 percent, but the hallucination rate climbed from four to 11 percent. The Decoder said that is still the second-lowest in the ranking. Translation: the model is right more often, and also confidently wrong more often, which is the tradeoff of answering more questions.

Speed and cost are mixed. Carbon uses 2.3 times as many reasoning tokens as Argon, and Finance and Tax Agent tasks take more than twice as long. Pricing is unchanged at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, half the list price Artificial Analysis uses for its calculations, because of an introductory discount that runs through the end of 2026. At list prices, a task in the index costs $7.76, about 23 percent less than Opus 5.5.

Ars Technica added some skepticism. It noted that Google presents Carbon as a new model, but for all anyone knows it could be a post-training upgrade of Argon, and that Carbon beats Argon by slim margins on every test Google has given it. It also pointed out that every frontier model lands in the same neighborhood, with gaps of a few points. Several of Google's strongest claims, including the Chrome team's tenfold patch-writing advantage over an unnamed competitor, come from internal tests that cannot be independently verified.

Google says it will open Carbon to developers, businesses and consumers once it has gathered feedback and adjusted the guardrails. No date was given. Until then, the best model on the leaderboard remains a rumor for most of its prospective customers.
