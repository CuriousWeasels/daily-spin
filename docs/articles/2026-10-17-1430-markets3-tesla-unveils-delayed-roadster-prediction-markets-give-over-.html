Tesla Shows Hovering Roadster, and Traders Bet on Merger With Rocket Company

After nine years of missed deadlines, Elon Musk's car unveiled on a SpaceX test site, and prediction markets now give a Tesla-SpaceX merger better than even odds by early 2027.

The wire said: Tesla unveils long-delayed Roadster developed with SpaceX, fueling merger speculation · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 17, 2026 14:30 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Tesla began taking $50,000 deposits for a next-generation Roadster in 2017 and missed every delivery target since. On Thursday it finally showed a prototype, built with SpaceX technology, at a SpaceX site in Texas.

Why it matters

A combination of two companies worth about $3.4 trillion would reshape major stock indexes and shareholder rights, and it would concentrate more of Musk's empire in a single set of books. Meanwhile, the car itself is not expected to move Tesla's finances.

Tesla unveiled its long-delayed next-generation Roadster on Thursday, nearly a decade after it began collecting deposits, CNBC reported. The livestream came from a SpaceX test site in McGregor, Texas. The prototype is a two-seater that, according to Tesla's own timer, reaches 60 miles per hour in under a second. It also drove up a curved ramp, clung to it upside down, flipped in mid-air and hovered. Customers waiting for a car now have visual proof that something exists.

The hovering comes from SpaceX hardware: thrusters, carbon overlay pressure vessels, diffusers and powerful fans. Translation: the car is partly a rocket company's product demo with seats. Musk said production should begin in 2028, though no price or full specifications were given. Whether it will be street-legal is unclear, and Musk said on stage, "This is not necessarily a safety-optimized car." That is a refreshing standard for a vehicle that might be sold to the public.

The Roadster will not be a financial event. CNBC noted it is expected to be a high-end, low-volume model, unlikely to have any material impact on Tesla. It also will not include Tesla's FSD driver-assistance system, and Musk said it will be the last Tesla intended to be controlled by a human driver. The company's most exciting new car is thus also its farewell to the concept of drivers.

The deposit history is the real story for anyone who is not a car enthusiast. Tesla took $50,000 deposits starting in 2017 and has missed every target date since, including a demo hoped for before the end of 2025. One customer, Lawrence O'Connor of Other World Computing, told CNBC his $250,000 Founder's Edition reservation amounted to an "interest-free loan" to the company. He said that what he saw was "off the charts." OpenAI's Sam Altman, by contrast, posted that 7.5 years "has felt like a long time to wait" and got his refund.

The more consequential speculation concerns a merger. Both companies are controlled by Musk, and they already pay each other, share some personnel and, per CNBC, have collaborated more in 2026. SpaceX went public in June, and its COO Gwynne Shotwell said before the IPO that a merger "might make Elon's life a little easier." SpaceX is valued at about $2 trillion and Tesla at $1.4 trillion, two of only 12 US companies worth at least $1 trillion.

Barclays analyst Dan Levy said in a note this week that a deal could come before the end of 2026, ahead of Tesla's annual meeting, which must be held by Dec. 6. He suggested it could be pitched as AI vertical integration, joining Tesla's Robotaxi and Optimus robot with SpaceX's Grok and Macrohard, with the Terafab chip project as a possible driver. Translation: vertical integration means owning every step of the process, which is easier to do when you already own most of the steps. Levy said the true value would be in integration rather than financial synergies.

Traders responded. On the Kalshi-linked contract, odds of a merger before March jumped to 56% by Friday morning from 29%, according to CNBC. The market resolves using news reports and the SEC's EDGAR database. CNBC disclosed that it has a commercial relationship with Kalshi, which includes a minority investment. Separately, Evercore ISI recommended buying SpaceX ahead of early November earnings and raised its price target 20% to $275.

Tesla reports third-quarter results after the bell on Wednesday. Investors may listen less for car sales than for any further remarks about overlap between the two companies. The Roadster, meanwhile, remains scheduled for 2028, which by this car's standards is practically next week.
