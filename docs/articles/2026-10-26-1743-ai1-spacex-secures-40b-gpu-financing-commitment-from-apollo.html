SpaceX Rents $40 Billion of Chips, Calls It Financing, Predicts Rivals Will Be Eaten

An Apollo-managed entity will buy the GPUs and lease them to SpaceX for about five years, while Elon Musk forecasts doom for 200-plus smaller AI landlords.

The wire said: SpaceX locks in $40B GPU financing deal with Apollo · TechCrunch AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 26, 2026 17:43 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

SpaceX disclosed in a regulatory filing a binding commitment for roughly $40 billion in financing for AI chips and data center equipment. Apollo Global Management will buy the hardware and lease it back to SpaceX, whose AI unit includes Grok, Cursor and a cloud compute business.

Why it matters

The deal shows that cheap borrowing for AI buildouts is going to companies with large, steady revenue elsewhere, which could squeeze smaller GPU-rental firms. It also shows how much of the AI boom now runs on leases rather than cash.

SpaceX said Monday in a regulatory filing that it has a binding commitment for about $40 billion to pay for AI chips and related data center gear. According to TechCrunch, an entity managed by Apollo Global Management will buy the equipment and lease it to SpaceX. SpaceX called it the "largest committed GPU financing," a category that, until recently, did not need a superlative because it barely existed.

Translation: this is a very large rent-to-own arrangement for computers. Apollo, a private equity firm, pays for the chips, SpaceX uses them, and SpaceX sends back monthly checks for roughly five to five-and-a-quarter years, starting as each batch is delivered. The leases are expected to begin in the first half of 2027, and the deal is supposed to close by year-end, subject to closing conditions.

Apollo said the money will help deliver Nvidia's next-generation Vera Rubin chips for SpaceX's "terrestrial compute buildout." The word terrestrial is doing some quiet work there, as a reminder that this is a rocket company describing its computers by where they are not.

CFO Bret Johnsen said the lease "diversifies our funding sources" and covers a notable portion of the money SpaceX needs. He also said the company is seeing "less than one-year payback periods" on its AI infrastructure investments. Translation: SpaceX says the chips earn back their cost in under a year, which makes a five-year lease sound less like a burden and more like a generous offer from the future.

The numbers explain the appetite. TechCrunch reported that SpaceX's AI segment, covering Grok, the Cursor coding tool and cloud compute, brought in $7.6 billion of the company's $14.6 billion in third-quarter revenue, more than Starlink and launches combined. Capital expenditures reached nearly $20 billion, up from $6.9 billion a year earlier, and Johnsen said on the Oct. 20 earnings call that SpaceX expects to spend $35 billion to $40 billion in the fourth quarter alone. A $40 billion lease, in that light, covers about one quarter of spending at the projected pace.

The timing matters because debt markets for AI infrastructure have been rough lately, as investors grow nervous about runaway spending and limited profits. SpaceX, by TechCrunch's account, had no trouble finding a lender. Its advantage is that it has other businesses, Starlink and rocket launches, producing steady cash, which lenders find comforting in the way people find a second income comforting.

Elon Musk noticed the advantage and pointed it out on X. Citing SpaceX's "investment-grade" status, he argued that debt markets have largely closed to companies lacking big revenue streams outside AI data centers. "That means the 200+ neoclouds are going to see guppy-level death and consolidation," he wrote, adding that some will survive, "but not many." Neoclouds are companies built mainly to rent out GPUs, which makes them, in structure, the same business Apollo just entered with SpaceX as its tenant.

Whether Musk is right depends on whether lenders keep treating chips as good collateral while also worrying about whether anyone will pay for what they produce. The deal itself offers one data point: at least one firm will fund $40 billion of hardware for a customer with rockets. Watch whether the neoclouds find similar landlords, or merely similar rent.
