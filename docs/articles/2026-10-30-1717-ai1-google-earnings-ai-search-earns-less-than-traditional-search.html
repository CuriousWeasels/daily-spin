Google Admits AI Search Earns Less, Plans to Spend Much More on AI

Alphabet's stock fell as much as 13 percent after search growth slowed and AI Mode proved less lucrative, while Amazon promised a trillion-dollar cloud and $220 billion in capex to get there.

The wire said: Google's AI search problem finally shows up in the earnings report, some investors believe · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 30, 2026 17:17 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Google has spent years rebuilding its search engine around Gemini AI models, while investors have worried that chatbot-style answers will earn less than the ad-filled links they replace. Alphabet and Amazon both reported quarterly results this week, and both said spending on AI infrastructure will keep rising.

Why it matters

The companies that run search and cloud computing are betting hundreds of billions of dollars that AI will pay for itself. If it earns less per query than the product it replaces, that bet depends on volume, new ad formats and corporate customers renting computing power.

Alphabet reported third-quarter results on Wednesday, and the stock fell sharply. According to The Decoder, shares dropped as much as ten percent. Ars Technica put the decline at almost 12 percent, after an earlier drop of about 13 percent. The sources differ on the size of the fall, but agree on the direction, which was the wrong one.

The cause was search, the business that funds nearly everything else at Google. The Decoder reported that search revenue grew 12 percent, short of the 15 percent analysts had expected. Ars Technica reported that the 12 percent figure is a slowdown from 17 percent in the second quarter and 19 percent in the first. Ars also said search revenue was flat in the quarter, a claim at odds with the 12 percent growth reported by The Decoder, so readers should treat that detail with caution.

Management explained part of the shortfall. Executive Philipp Schindler said on the call that growth in AI Mode queries had an impact "because of the lower monetization." Translation: people are using the new product a lot, and Google earns less when they do. Ars Technica reported that each AI Mode query brings in less than a comparable web search, because there are fewer ads and the queries cost more to run. That is an unusual business model, in which the customer's enthusiasm is the problem.

Google's proposed fix is more advertising. The Decoder reported that the company plans to expand its ad program for chatbots in the U.S. and internationally. Translation: the answer to "people like the new thing but it earns less" is to put ads in the new thing.

There was good news that investors declined to enjoy. Cloud revenue rose 103 percent to $30.7 billion, according to The Decoder. Ars Technica noted that it beat analyst expectations by about a billion dollars. CEO Sundar Pichai said the Gemini app has reached one billion users. Search queries grew at their fastest annual rate in five years, and The Decoder noted that traditional search has been declared dead for years and keeps growing anyway.

Then came the bill. Capital expenditures, meaning money spent on data centers, chips and the buildings that hold them, reached $51.4 billion in the quarter alone. The Decoder reported that the 2026 forecast stays at $195 billion to $205 billion, and that the company expects 2027 spending to "increase significantly." Ars Technica reported revenue of almost $128 billion, up 25 percent, with net income of more than $26 billion but almost $9 billion lower than a year earlier. Earnings per share were $2.12 against a consensus of $3.02.

Google also wants to sell AI infrastructure built on its own TPU chips to outside customers. The Decoder reported that management did not disclose the profit margin on those sales, even when asked twice. When an executive declines to give a number twice, analysts generally conclude that the number exists.

Amazon, reporting Thursday, offered the same plot with a larger figure. TechCrunch reported that CEO Andy Jassy said AWS could become a trillion-dollar revenue business in the next five to 10 years, growing north of 40 percent in both 2027 and 2028. AWS grew 39 percent last quarter and has an annualized run-rate of $184 billion. Amazon still expects about $220 billion in cash capex for 2026, mostly for AWS, and "meaningfully more" in 2027. Shares fell about 6 percent in after-market trading.

Jassy called the AI boom a "once-in-a-lifetime opportunity" and said Amazon can fund the plan. Analysts repeatedly asked him to explain the spending, the signs that growth will arrive and the expected margins, according to TechCrunch. The market's position, in short, is that it would like to see the receipt. The next several earnings calls will show whether anyone has one.
