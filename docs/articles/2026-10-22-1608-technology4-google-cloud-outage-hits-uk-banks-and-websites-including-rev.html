Google Cloud Restores London Region After Power Event Briefly Reminds Britain Where Its Money Lives

A power problem at one London data centre zone disrupted Revolut, Lloyds, Royal Mail, DPD and court video hearings before Google declared services restored.

The wire said: Google resolves cloud outage affecting UK banks and websites · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 22, 2026 16:08 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google Cloud reported an issue with multiple services in its London region, europe-west2, early on Tuesday. By evening it said a power event at a third-party facility had been resolved and services restored.

Why it matters

Banking apps, parcel booking and court hearings all turned out to share a dependency most customers had never heard of. For small firms without spare capacity, an outage of a few hours can mean lost sales and delayed perishable orders.

Google Cloud said on Tuesday that it had resolved a disruption at its UK cloud site that knocked out or degraded services at British banks, delivery firms and the courts, the BBC reported. The company attributed it to a "power event" at a third-party facility in its europe-west2 region, which is its London data centre complex. Services, it said, have been restored.

Translation: a region is a cluster of data centres that a cloud company rents out by the slice, so that customers do not have to own buildings full of computers. A zone is a smaller part of that cluster. Google said the trouble began in one smaller zone, europe-west2-b. That a problem in one zone could be felt by so many customers is the sort of detail that tends to appear in a post-incident report and not in a sales brochure.

The list of affected services was long. Revolut, Lloyds Bank and HSBC confirmed disruption, according to the BBC, and customers also flagged problems with Royal Mail, DPD and an e-bike service called Forest. HM Courts and Tribunals Service said it knew of an issue with the video platform used for remote hearings. Google's dashboard described "elevated errors and intermittent network failures," which is how engineers say that things were not working, but only some of the time.

The human cost was ordinary and inconvenient. Rachael Ellen, a Revolut customer in Glasgow, told the BBC she could not pay for parking because her account was not working properly, and the app's help bot told her there were no pending incoming transactions. Revolut said services were fully restored and that customers' money and data "remained safe throughout." Translation: the money was fine, merely unreachable, which is a distinction that is cold comfort at a car park barrier.

Small businesses felt it more sharply. Guy Baybutt of The Veg Box Company in Preston said the firm could not access its website to process orders, could not book parcels onto DPD's system, and lost sales while customers could not order. He noted that a small firm cannot easily absorb lost revenue, especially with fresh produce that has to arrive promptly. Lloyds did not confirm the cause of its own problems but said it understood the root issues had been resolved.

The BBC noted the outage came exactly one year after an outage at AWS's largest cloud site took more than 1,000 sites offline, including UK banks. Prof Feng Li of Bayes Business School said it was a reminder of how deeply economies depend on a handful of cloud providers. He added that failures will happen in complex systems, and that what matters is how far they spread, how fast services recover and whether customers have workable alternatives. He also said it was not yet clear what caused the power problem.

Google says it will publish an analysis of the incident on its service health dashboard. Readers hoping for an explanation of the power event should check there, and those hoping that every bank has a backup plan should perhaps check with the bank.
