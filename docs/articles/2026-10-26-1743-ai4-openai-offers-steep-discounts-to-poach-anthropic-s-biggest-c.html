OpenAI Discovers Customers Will Switch Vendors If You Pay Them to Leave

Discounts of up to 90 percent are pulling big accounts like Rippling, Replit and DataCamp away from Anthropic, The Information reports, at some cost to OpenAI's margins.

The wire said: OpenAI offers discounts up to 90 percent to poach Anthropic's biggest customers · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 26, 2026 17:43 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

OpenAI and Anthropic compete to supply the AI models that power coding tools and workplace software. According to The Decoder, summarizing a report from The Information, OpenAI is now using steep discounts to win over Anthropic's largest customers.

Why it matters

A price war among AI vendors lowers costs for businesses that buy the tools, but it also shows how much of the market is being bought rather than earned. Cheap models from China add pressure on both companies.

OpenAI is offering discounts of up to 90 percent to lure large customers away from Anthropic, and it appears to be working, according to The Decoder, citing The Information. Rippling, Replit and DataCamp are all moving more of their AI spending to OpenAI. In the software business this is called competition. In most other businesses it is called a sale.

Rippling CEO Parker Conrad said his company raised OpenAI's share of its coding workload from 16 percent to 52 percent. Anthropic's share fell from 57 percent to 18 percent over the same stretch. Translation: the company that was doing most of the work now does about a sixth of it, and nobody had to write a farewell email.

Replit switched after OpenAI offered more than 30 percent off a contract worth over $100 million a year. That is a discount of more than $30 million, which is the sort of number that gets a procurement meeting to end early. DataCamp CEO Jonathan Cornelissen said OpenAI's pricing cut his costs by 80 to 90 percent, a figure that suggests the previous prices were less a law of nature than an opening bid.

The generosity extends to smaller buyers. A US bank with more than $7 billion in assets had its ChatGPT seats doubled to 500 employees at no extra charge, and pays under $7 per user instead of the usual $20 or more. Translation: a seat is a license for one employee to use the software, and the bank is now getting two for roughly the price of a fraction of one.

The Decoder says OpenAI is partly buying market share at the expense of its own margins. Translation: it is selling at prices that leave less profit per sale, in the hope that customers stay once they are settled in. The report also says OpenAI has achieved real price cuts through its Sol and Luna model lines, thanks to more efficient models and infrastructure, so not every discount is charity funded by investors.

Anthropic, meanwhile, saw its growth slow from the second quarter to the third, according to an investor memo cited in the report. Both companies also face pressure from China, where new models from Deepseek and Kimi reportedly deliver comparable performance for a fraction of the cost. That leaves the two American rivals fighting over customers while a third group of competitors quietly lowers what customers think the product is worth.

What to watch next is whether the customers stay when the discounts end. Loyalty in this market appears to be measured in percentage points, and at present the going rate is about 90.
