Black Sea Tankers Burn Off Romania as NATO Members Weigh Whether to Phone Each Other

Eight crew are missing from the Wine Trader after a drone attack, and Romania and Bulgaria are discussing a NATO consultation clause that mostly consists of talking.

The wire said: Eight crew missing after drone attack leaves tanker ablaze off Romania's coast · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 15, 2026 14:38 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Russia has intensified strikes on ships in the western Black Sea, saying it targets vessels carrying weapons to Ukraine, while Kyiv and its allies say civilian grain and cargo ships are being hit. Two merchant ships were struck off Bulgaria on Tuesday, and one sank.

Why it matters

The attacks endanger seafarers and strain grain and energy routes, which can raise freight costs and food prices far from the Black Sea. They are also happening in the waters of two NATO members, which turns a shipping problem into an alliance problem.

A drone attack left the San Marino-flagged tanker Wine Trader ablaze on Thursday morning about 13 nautical miles east of the Danube's Sulina mouth, and eight of its 12 crew are missing, according to the BBC. Romanian President Nicușor Dan later confirmed drones struck the ship. DW, working from earlier reporting, gave a different count of seven missing and five safe, and noted that Romanian officials had not initially said drones caused the fire. The sources therefore disagree on the number of missing, and only one of them is certain about the cause.

Search efforts are under way with two Black Hawk helicopters and two rescue boats, though the BBC reported that very high waves complicate the work. The immediate priority is reaching the four crew confirmed alive. A second chemical and oil tanker, the Comoros-flagged Yara J, caught fire hours earlier nearby, and DW said all its crew were reported safe.

The attacks are not isolated. On Tuesday, two merchant ships were hit off Bulgaria, one sank, and 11 crew were left missing, per the BBC. DW counted drone strikes on at least five other cargo ships off Romania and Bulgaria in the past 10 days, with at least 23 sailors lost. Bulgaria suspects Russia and summoned its ambassador, which is the diplomatic equivalent of asking someone to stand in your office while you describe your feelings.

Moscow's position is that it is hitting ships carrying military or dual-use cargo to Ukrainian ports. Translation: dual-use means goods that could serve civilians or soldiers, a category flexible enough to cover most things that float. Kyiv and its European allies say grain and other civilian cargo are being targeted. Russia has repeatedly rejected calls for a naval ceasefire and blames Ukraine for attacking Russian merchant vessels, and the BBC noted Ukraine hit an oil tanker at the Russian port of Sochi last week.

Romania and Bulgaria are discussing whether to invoke Article 4 of the NATO treaty. Translation: Article 4 lets a member request consultations with allies if it feels its security is threatened. It is a meeting request, not a military response, and it sits well below Article 5, the collective defense clause. Dan said there would be a response from the Romanian state, and he and Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev met NATO chief Mark Rutte in Brussels to discuss equipment to protect shipping.

Rutte said NATO has strengthened its eastern flank, including counter-drone capabilities, and would adjust its posture as needed. Dan wrote on X that the attacks were unacceptable and had to stop. Bulgaria's prime minister said the EU would discuss a proposed moratorium on attacks on civilian ships at its Brussels summit, which proposes to solve a problem of ships being hit by asking everyone to stop hitting them.

Others are offering mediation. India, which supplies more than 12% of the world's seafarers according to a 2026 industry report, has urged both sides to guarantee safe passage and says it is talking to each. Ukraine's foreign minister called the Indian proposal the broadest of four offers Kyiv has received, DW reported, while Russia has welcomed India's peace efforts without endorsing a binding deal. US President Donald Trump announced an energy ceasefire over the weekend, though DW said its scope and mechanism remain unclear, and Ukraine alleges it has already been breached.

For ordinary people the cost arrives indirectly. An Indian economist told DW that prolonged disruption would raise import bills through higher freight on Russian crude and tighter edible oil supplies, feeding food inflation. In short, a drone strike off the Danube can eventually appear on a grocery receipt in another country. Watch whether Article 4 is actually invoked, and whether anyone's ceasefire survives contact with the shipping lanes.
