Anthropic Heads for IPO With Credit Rating Best Described as 'Almost Respectable'

Blackstone now expects a high junk rating after the AI lab's listing, reversing its earlier forecast of investment grade before 2027, the Financial Times reported.

The wire said: Anthropic expected to receive high ‘junk’ rating from credit agencies following IPO, FT report says · TechCrunch AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 31, 2026 02:52 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Anthropic, maker of the Claude chatbot, filed to go public this week after a year of enormous deals for computing power. Blackstone anchored a recent $63 billion financing of the company's chips.

Why it matters

A worse credit rating means Anthropic pays more to borrow, and the company's business depends on borrowing for very expensive computing power. How the market prices that risk will shape how freely the whole AI industry can fund its growth.

Anthropic is expected to receive a high "junk" rating from credit rating agencies after its initial public offering, according to the Financial Times, as relayed by TechCrunch. The source of the forecast is Blackstone, the anchor investor in a $63 billion deal to finance Anthropic's chips. Blackstone reportedly told investors in that deal what it now expects.

Translation: a credit rating is a letter grade for how likely a borrower is to repay its debts. Investment grade means lenders consider you safe. Junk, more politely called speculative grade, means they would like extra interest as compensation for their anxiety. The expected rating is in the double-B range, which the FT describes as the top tier of junk. It is the honors section of the remedial class.

The notable part is the reversal. According to the FT, Blackstone previously told investors that Anthropic would reach investment grade before 2027. It has now moved from "safe" to "speculative" without, as far as the sources say, any public explanation. TechCrunch reached out to both Anthropic and Blackstone. Both declined to comment, which is how companies say "we have read the headline."

Blackstone's view is a prediction, not a verdict. The agencies have not rated anything yet. But the mechanism is straightforward. A lower rating makes borrowing costlier and harder, and Anthropic has spent the past 12 months signing large compute power deals, a spending spree TechCrunch notes has worried investors. Translation: compute means the chips and data centers that run AI models, and it is the line item that does not shrink when the mood does.

Meanwhile, the competition is being conducted in a more cheerful currency. The Decoder reported that OpenAI has doubled usage limits on its Pro 100 and Pro 500 plans for Work and Codex. On the Pro 200 plan, it doubled the lower quota and reversed a planned cut after user pushback. The Plus plan stays the same. Translation: subscribers get more allowance to use the product, and in the case of Pro 200, were briefly going to get less.

OpenAI also cautioned that doubling a quota does not mean twice as many completed tasks, since consumption varies by model, task type, and settings. That is the software equivalent of an all-you-can-eat buffet noting that appetite varies. The company said it will keep shipping better models and efficiency gains, and pointed to its recent 6.1 models, Terra and Sol, which it says are much more efficient while matching or beating their predecessors.

The Decoder framed the move as coming while Anthropic prepares for its IPO, with both companies competing hard for business customers. One lab is handing out extra capacity, the other is being told by its own financiers that borrowing will be pricey. Capacity is generous when someone else's chips pay for it.

What to watch next is the actual ratings, once the agencies weigh in. Until then, Anthropic's debt is graded by the opinion of a lender who also happens to be a large investor. That is useful information, in the way a restaurant review from the owner's landlord is useful.
