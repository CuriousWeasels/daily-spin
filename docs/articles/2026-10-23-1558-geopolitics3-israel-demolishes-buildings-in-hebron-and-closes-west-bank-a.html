Israel Razes Hebron Buildings After Checkpoint Attack, Four Days Before an Election

Two Israeli soldiers were killed at a West Bank checkpoint; Prime Minister Netanyahu promised to demolish the attackers' neighbourhood and said he had reviewed plans for the largest West Bank operation in decades.

The wire said: Israeli forces begin demolishing Palestinian buildings in Hebron after two Israeli soldiers shot dead · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 23, 2026 15:58 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Two Palestinian gunmen attacked soldiers at a checkpoint outside Hebron on Friday, killing two and wounding three before being shot dead, according to the BBC. Israel's general election is four days away, and Netanyahu has campaigned on security.

Why it matters

Demolitions and cordons around a city of about 200,000 people affect civilians who had no part in the attack, and a larger West Bank operation could displace thousands. The timing means military decisions and campaign decisions are now hard to tell apart.

The Israeli military has begun demolishing Palestinian buildings in Hebron after an attack at a checkpoint outside the city killed two Israeli soldiers and wounded three others, the BBC reported. The two gunmen were shot dead at the scene. NPR reported that Israel also ordered a closure of the West Bank. Four days before a general election, the aftermath of a security incident is also a campaign event, even when the campaign events themselves are cancelled.

Netanyahu said the military would "destroy" the neighbourhood the attackers came from. Speaking from the scene, he said the army would "demolish this place and completely transform it." He compared the plan to earlier operations in West Bank refugee camps, where residential buildings were flattened and thousands of people were displaced. Translation: the target is a neighbourhood, not a person, which is the point critics keep making.

Demolishing the homes of suspected attackers is common Israeli practice, and Israeli rights groups have criticised it as "collective punishment." Translation: the family and neighbours of the accused lose their homes, whether or not they knew anything. The stated rationale is deterrence. The stated criticism is that punishing people who did nothing is not deterrence but punishment.

Netanyahu also said he had reviewed plans for the largest military operation in the West Bank in decades, without saying when it might happen. He said he had put cordons around Hebron, which has about 200,000 residents, and the nearby city of Halhul. A plan with no date is useful in an election week: it signals resolve while committing to nothing that can go wrong before Tuesday.

His coalition partners were less cautious. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called for a West Bank campaign "exactly like in Gaza or Lebanon," and National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir urged dismantling the Palestinian Authority. The Palestinian Authority is the body that administers parts of the West Bank, so the proposal is to abolish the other party to the arrangement Israel has long said it wants to deal with.

The Israeli military said it found a large quantity of ammunition in the attackers' vehicle and believes they intended a far larger attack on nearby Route 35, but ran into the checkpoint and opened fire there. The BBC said verified footage shows an intense gun battle. Two soldiers died and three were wounded, and the military's account of the intended attack is, for now, the military's account.

The politics were just as brisk. Netanyahu's Likud party cancelled Friday morning campaign events. A day earlier, Foreign Minister Israel Katz had accused opposition leaders of undermining the government by criticising Netanyahu's warnings of an attack, saying they would be "responsible" for any future one. After the attack, frontrunner Gadi Eisenkot and former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett offered condolences and praised those who killed the gunmen, which is a hard thing to blame on anyone.

The wider numbers are grim. The UN says at least 96 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli forces or settlers in the West Bank this year, while four Israelis have been killed by Palestinians there, not counting Friday's dead. Tensions were already high, with extremist settlers targeting Palestinians during the olive harvest. Watch for whether the big operation gets a date, and whether it gets one before or after the polls open.
