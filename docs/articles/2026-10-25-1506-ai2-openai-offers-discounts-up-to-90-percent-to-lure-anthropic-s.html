OpenAI Discovers Customers Love Cheaper Things, Cuts Prices Up to 90 Percent

Rippling, Replit and DataCamp are shifting AI spending to OpenAI after steep discounts, according to The Information, as Anthropic's growth slows and Chinese rivals circle.

The wire said: OpenAI offers discounts up to 90 percent to poach Anthropic's biggest customers · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 25, 2026 15:06 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

OpenAI and Anthropic compete to sell AI models to businesses, especially for coding work. The Decoder, citing The Information, reports that OpenAI is now using deep discounts to win over Anthropic's largest customers.

Why it matters

When the two leading AI vendors fight on price, business customers pay less, but the suppliers absorb the cost in thinner margins. It also shows how little loyalty exists in a market where switching vendors can cut a bill by 80 to 90 percent.

OpenAI is pulling major customers away from Anthropic with aggressive discounts, and the approach is working, according to The Decoder's summary of a report by The Information. Rippling, Replit and DataCamp are all moving more of their AI spending to OpenAI. In the enterprise software business, this is known as a sales strategy. In a grocery store, it is known as a sale.

Rippling's CEO, Parker Conrad, said his company raised OpenAI's share of its coding workload from 16 percent to 52 percent. Anthropic's share fell from 57 percent to 18 percent. Translation: a customer who once gave most of its business to one vendor now gives most of it to the other, and the change took about as much loyalty as switching checkout lanes.

Replit's move was prompted by an offer of more than 30 percent off a contract worth over $100 million a year. That is a discount of more than $30 million, which is the kind of number that makes a procurement department very friendly. DataCamp CEO Jonathan Cornelissen said OpenAI's pricing cut his costs by 80 to 90 percent.

The discounts are not limited to tech firms. A US bank with more than $7 billion in assets had its ChatGPT seats doubled to 500 employees at no extra charge, paying under $7 per user instead of the usual $20 or more. Translation: the bank received twice the product at a price that, per head, is a third or less of the list price. Most banks cannot get that deal on a checking account.

The report says OpenAI is partly buying market share at the expense of its own margins. Translation: it is selling at prices that leave it less profit per sale, in the hope that customers stay once they are plugged in. The Decoder adds that OpenAI has also achieved real price cuts through its Sol and Luna model lines, thanks to more efficient models and infrastructure, so not every discount is a subsidy.

Anthropic, meanwhile, saw growth slow from the second quarter to the third, according to an investor memo cited in the report. Both companies also face price pressure from China, where new models from Deepseek and Kimi reportedly deliver comparable performance for a fraction of the cost. Being undercut by your rival is uncomfortable, and being undercut by your rival's rival is worse.

What to watch next is whether Anthropic matches the discounts, and whether customers who left over price will stay once the discounts end. The sources do not say.
