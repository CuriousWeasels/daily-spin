Turkish Police Raid Opposition Channel on Day Its Biggest Story Begins

Authorities seized Halk TV, installed a state-appointed administrator and swapped live news for pre-recorded programs, on the day Ekrem Imamoglu's defense was due to start.

The wire said: Police raid the major pro-opposition Halk TV channel in Turkey · DW

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 16, 2026 01:49 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Halk TV is one of Turkey's best-known opposition channels and regularly criticizes President Recep Tayyip Erdogan. Istanbul Mayor Ekrem Imamoglu, a leading rival of Erdogan, has been jailed since March 2025 and faces corruption charges he calls politically motivated.

Why it matters

When the channel covering a trial is taken over on the day the defense begins, viewers lose a main source of live information about it. It is also a test of how much independent media Turkey's government will tolerate before the 2028 presidential race.

On Wednesday morning, Turkish police searched the Istanbul headquarters of Halk TV, the live broadcast stopped, and the government appointed an administrator to run the channel, according to DW. Prosecutors said 11 people were arrested and that a further 17 arrest warrants had been issued. Journalists were initially kept out of the building, and pre-recorded programs aired in place of live reports. Viewers who tuned in for the news got something calmer and considerably less current.

Prosecutors said the raid was part of an investigation into the channel's CEO, Cafer Mahiroglu, who is accused of bribery, bid rigging and money laundering. A warrant was issued for him, though he currently lives in London. Assets of companies allegedly linked to him were seized, and a trustee was appointed to run them. Translation: a trustee is a person the state installs to manage a business, which in practice means the previous owner no longer decides what the business does.

Prosecutors framed this as a financial crime case. The opposition framed it as a media case. Both can be true on paper, which is the convenience of the arrangement: a corruption investigation can end with a government-chosen administrator in charge of the newsroom, and the newsroom is then covered by the investigation's definition of normal operations.

The timing drew the sharpest criticism. Wednesday was the day jailed Istanbul Mayor Ekrem Imamoglu was scheduled to begin his defense against corruption charges. DW reports that Halk TV had been an important source of information for people following the trial. Imamoglu, who won the mayoralty in 2019 and was arrested on March 19, 2025, is accused of running a criminal organization and related financial crimes. Prosecutors are seeking 2,352 years in prison across 142 charges. That works out to roughly 16.6 years per charge, which suggests the prosecutors were being careful not to overreach on any single one. Imamoglu denies the allegations.

Ozgur Ozel, leader of the New Party, called it a dark day for Turkey and said only dictators would launch a dawn raid on a television channel and appoint a trustee. The Journalists' Union of Turkey condemned the raid on X as arbitrary. Editor-in-chief Suat Toktas tried in vain to persuade the new administrator to keep live broadcasts going and said in a video released by the channel that what was happening was illegal. DW also attributes a statement to Mahiroglu that the public's right to information was being seized for political reasons.

Halk TV began broadcasting in 2005 and drew wide attention for its live coverage of the 2013 Gezi Park protests. Mahiroglu took over the station in 2020. Imamoglu is still expected to run for president in 2028, which makes the question of who controls the cameras in the meantime a practical one.

What to watch next: whether Halk TV returns to live broadcasting under its new administrator, and whether the Imamoglu trial gets covered by anyone who was not appointed by the government. Prosecutors say the case is about bribery. The schedule says it is also about Wednesday.
