Trump Shows Off One Tanker, Floats Making Venezuela the 51st State

At a Texas rally a week before midterms, the president touted a Venezuelan oil deal he says will lower fuel prices, and mused about annexation.

The wire said: Trump shows off tanker with ‘1m barrels of oil’ and floats Venezuela as 51st state · The Guardian

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 28, 2026 17:28 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

The US attacked Venezuela in January and captured leader Nicolás Maduro, who now faces criminal charges in New York. An interim government backed by the Trump administration has since agreed an oil deal, while fuel prices have climbed since Iran closed the strait of Hormuz.

Why it matters

Gasoline prices are a main way voters judge a president, and a single tanker is not yet a supply chain. Meanwhile, elections in Venezuela have not been held since Maduro was removed, which critics say the administration is not pushing.

Donald Trump used a campaign rally in Corpus Christi, Texas, on Tuesday to show off a tanker he said carried "one million barrels of oil" from Venezuela, and to suggest the country might one day become the 51st state. According to The Guardian, he said Venezuela "will become a prosperous country, and who knows?" and added, "That's possible." It is rare for a trade deal to come with a possible change of citizenship attached, but this is a rare kind of deal.

The background is that the United States attacked Venezuela in January and captured its long-serving leader, Nicolás Maduro, who was taken to New York to face criminal charges. The oil agreement was struck with the country's interim government, which The Guardian describes as backed by the Trump administration. Translation: the party selling the oil is one the buyer helped install, which simplifies negotiations considerably.

Trump claims the deal will cover "billions" of barrels, and said the oil is being provided "at zero cost to the American taxpayer." The sources do not say how that is arranged, who is paid, or on what terms, so readers will have to take the zero on faith. One million barrels, for scale, is a visible tanker and an excellent backdrop. The Guardian's report does not compare it to total American consumption, and the rally did not either.

The urgency is domestic. Trump entered the war with Iran in February, and Iran then closed the strait of Hormuz, a passage for about 20 percent of the world's oil supplies. Translation: a narrow stretch of water became a very expensive bottleneck, and American gas prices rose nationwide. Trump posted that fuel prices "will soon come plummeting down."

The timing is hard to miss. Tens of millions of Americans vote in next week's midterm elections, and opinion polls cited by The Guardian show Trump's approval ratings at record lows, weighed down by the Iran war and tariffs on goods from much of the world. A tanker off the Texas coast is a tidy visual answer to a cost-of-living complaint, and it is easier to photograph than a tariff.

Critics, according to The Guardian, say the administration has not pushed Venezuela toward democracy; no elections have been held since Maduro was detained. Trump, meanwhile, added Venezuela to a growing list of places he has spoken of acquiring, alongside Greenland, Canada and Mexico. The list now resembles a shopping trip more than a foreign policy.

Trump also declared on Truth Social that he had made the Western Hemisphere "the Energy Capital of the World." Whether the pump price follows the slogan is the part voters will check first. They have until next week to see which arrives sooner, the cheaper gas or the next tanker.
