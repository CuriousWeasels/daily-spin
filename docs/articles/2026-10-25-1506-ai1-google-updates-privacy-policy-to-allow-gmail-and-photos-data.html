Google Amends Promise Not to Use Your Gmail for Ads, Adds the Word 'Unless'

A October 16 privacy policy update lets data from Gmail, Drive and Photos feed ad personalization if users connect it to AI features.

The wire said: Now Google says it might use your Gmail and Google Photos data to target ads · The Verge AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 25, 2026 15:06 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google released Personal Intelligence in early 2026, letting users link Gmail, Drive and Photos to Gemini and AI Mode search for more tailored answers. Its privacy policy had said it did not show personalized ads based on content from those three apps.

Why it matters

If you have connected your apps to Google's AI tools, your private content may now shape the ads you see, even if advertisers never read your inbox. The fix is a settings change, but only for people who know to look.

Google updated its privacy policy on October 16 to say it will not use your Drive, Gmail or Photos content for personalized ads, unless you choose to make that data available to other Google services that support personalized ads. The Verge and Ars Technica both reported the change, which was first spotted by Android Authority. The earlier version contained no such clause, which is the sort of detail that tends to be the whole point of a clause.

Here is the mechanism. Personal Intelligence lets users connect their Google apps to AI products such as the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. Translation: you hand the AI your email and photos so it can answer questions about your life, and in return it answers questions about your life. The Gemini app does not currently show ads, according to Ars Technica, but AI search tools may carry sponsored content. That is where the two systems meet.

Google says the change is narrower than it sounds. In a statement quoted by Ars Technica, the company said connecting apps is entirely opt-in, that ads will not be based directly on your Gmail inbox or Photos library, and that sensitive information will not be used to tailor ads. The Verge adds that advertisers do not get access to your content or to personal details such as your name or email address, and that Google will not target ads by categories like race, sexual orientation, religion or health.

Ars Technica reads the statement less generously. If you use AI Mode with Personal Intelligence on, summaries and insights from your data can shape the answers you get, and that same data is used for ad targeting. In its view, it is accurate to say your personal data is an element of ad targeting in AI search. Translation: Google says the ads are not based directly on your inbox, only on things derived from your inbox, which is a distinction that will comfort exactly the people who work in advertising.

The policy's own example is more mundane. If you search for mountain bikes in Search, you might see ads for sports equipment on YouTube. Nobody objects to that, because you typed the words yourself. The new wrinkle is that the words may now include the ones you never typed, such as those in an email to a friend about a mountain bike.

Users can opt out. According to The Verge, you can disconnect individual apps from Search's Personal Intelligence features, or switch off Personalized Recommendations entirely on the Search Services Personalization settings page. The arrangement is consistent with how such features usually work: the benefit arrives automatically, and the exit is a settings page.

Worth watching is whether Google clarifies where 'directly' ends and 'indirectly' begins, and how many of the people who connected their apps for better answers realize they also signed up for better ads.
