Musk Says Merging His Two Trillion-Dollar Companies Is 'Good Idea,' Asks Committees to Please Not Tell Him How

Elon Musk confirmed that special committees at Tesla and SpaceX are studying a combination that would be the largest merger ever, though he is not allowed in the room.

The wire said: Elon Musk confirms he is considering merging Tesla and SpaceX · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 21, 2026 16:10 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Elon Musk runs both Tesla, valued at roughly $1.5 trillion, and SpaceX, valued at $2 trillion after a June public listing. SpaceX has already absorbed xAI and X over the past year.

Why it matters

A merger would put cars, rockets, satellites, robots and AI under one share price, changing what Tesla investors actually own. Drivers are unlikely to notice much soon, but shareholders would be holding a very different company.

Elon Musk said on Tuesday that merging Tesla and SpaceX would be a good idea, according to the BBC and Ars Technica. He made the remark on SpaceX's earnings call, where he also said special committees at both companies are studying the matter. No terms have been agreed, and Musk said there is no guarantee they will be. It is the rare corporate announcement in which the person most likely to benefit says he is not allowed to take part.

Those committees are made up of independent directors, which is the corporate way of saying people who are not Musk. Translation: a special committee is a group of board members appointed to negotiate a deal when the person proposing it is on both sides of the table. Musk called himself a "conflicted party" and said he cannot join the discussions. He also described even commenting on the topic as "tap dancing through a minefield," which is a fair description of securities law.

The numbers are large enough to need their own committee. The BBC put Tesla at about $1.5 trillion and SpaceX at $2 trillion, which would make a combined company the largest merger in history. Tesla would need approval from at least 50% of its shareholders, a bar Musk himself called "quite a high hurdle." Tesla investors did, however, approve a pay package for him valued at around $1tn last year, so the hurdle may be shorter than advertised.

Musk's case, as Ars Technica reported, is that both companies are drifting toward artificial intelligence anyway. He cited superintelligence software, chip design, the Terafab chip venture, robotics and satellite connectivity. SpaceX's revenue shows the drift: the BBC reported that its cloud compute business, which sells capacity to firms such as Anthropic and Google, earned $7.5bn in the latest quarter, up from $813m a year earlier. Starlink, once the headline act, brought in $5.2bn. Translation: the rocket company is increasingly a landlord for AI computers.

Markets took the news calmly, in the sense that SpaceX shares rose 8% in after-hours trading and Tesla's rose 2%. Analyst David Nicholson of The Futurum Group told the BBC the odds of a merger are "100%," with only timing in question. Dan Sheehan of Telos Wealth Advisors was more careful, noting Tesla holders would likely own a "smaller slice of a much larger and more complex business," and that there is "no real precedent" for pricing such a deal.

Who gains is the least mysterious part. Sheehan said the deal would not add to Musk's wealth but would concentrate it in one stock, and the BBC reported it would give him more voting control than he has at Tesla. The special committee is supposed to weigh that. Regulators may also take an interest, particularly if Democrats gain seats in next month's midterm elections.

For customers, the outlook is modest. Experts told the BBC that Tesla drivers should not expect big changes soon, with cars seeming almost like "an afterthought" next to the AI ambitions. Tesla has already narrowed its focus to the Model Y and Model 3 and begun building Optimus humanoid robots in California. BYD, meanwhile, overtook Tesla as the world's biggest EV maker in January.

The next step is for two committees of people who are not Elon Musk to agree on a price for two companies that Elon Musk runs. Tesla reports its own results on Wednesday, which may offer a clue about how seriously its shareholders take the idea.
