Nvidia Invests $3 Billion in Meta, Which Plans to Spend It on Nvidia

The chipmaker is buying into Meta's stock offering as the social media giant seeks up to $95 billion to fund its AI buildout.

The wire said: Meta gets $3 billion from Nvidia and plans to spend more on Nvidia chips · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 27, 2026 16:54 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Meta wants to raise up to $95 billion through stock offerings to pay for AI infrastructure. Nvidia, its supplier, is now putting $3 billion into that offering, according to the Wall Street Journal.

Why it matters

If suppliers fund their own customers, it becomes harder for investors to tell how much AI demand is real and how much is a loan with a press release attached. Anyone holding tech stocks, directly or through a retirement fund, has a stake in the answer.

Nvidia is investing $3 billion in Meta's planned equity offering, the Wall Street Journal reported, as reported by The Decoder. In the same announcement, the two companies said they would expand their partnership around Meta's AI product Muse. Meta, for its part, plans to run more of its next generation of AI agents on Nvidia infrastructure, including Vera Rubin systems and Vera CPUs. In short, a company that sells chips is now a shareholder in the company that buys them.

Some context on the money. Meta wants to raise up to $95 billion through stock offerings to pay for its AI infrastructure buildout. Translation: an equity offering is when a company sells new shares to raise cash, which means Meta is asking investors to fund the data centers that Meta would then fill with hardware. Nvidia has now joined the line of people handing over cash.

Nvidia is not alone. According to CNBC, AMD and Broadcom signaled nonbinding interest in buying up to $5 billion in Meta shares combined. Translation: nonbinding means they have said they like the idea, which in finance is roughly the same as saying a dinner invitation sounds lovely. It costs nothing until someone books a table.

The arrangement fits a pattern investors have argued about for months. The Decoder notes that Nvidia gives money to customers like OpenAI, which then use it to build data centers packed with Nvidia chips. Critics call this circular financing and warn that it could hide how much real demand exists. Translation: if the customer's budget comes partly from the seller, the sales figures tell you less about the outside world than they appear to.

The defense of such deals is easy to imagine. Nvidia gets a stake in a large customer, Meta gets capital and a committed supplier, and both get to announce an expanded partnership. Nothing in the sources suggests the arrangement is improper, and the sources do not say whether the $3 billion is tied to any purchase commitment. The announcement says only that Meta plans to use more Nvidia infrastructure, which is the sort of plan one tends to have when the supplier is also an investor.

The practical test is whether outside buyers show up with their own money. A $95 billion raise cannot be covered by suppliers alone, and $3 billion from Nvidia plus up to $5 billion of nonbinding interest from AMD and Broadcom leaves most of the total to be found elsewhere. Watch who fills the rest, and at what price.
