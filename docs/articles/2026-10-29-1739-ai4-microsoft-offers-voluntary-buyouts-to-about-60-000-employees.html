Microsoft Offers 60,000 Employees a Way Out, Citing Strong Belief in Their Future Elsewhere

The company is extending voluntary buyouts to roughly a quarter of its workforce across four countries, as it tries to fund AI infrastructure without cutting AI infrastructure.

The wire said: Microsoft’s AI future has fewer jobs · The Verge AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 29, 2026 17:39 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Microsoft has laid off workers regularly for a decade, and CEO Satya Nadella has said AI would change how many people the company needs. This week, The Verge reported the company is offering voluntary exit packages to about 60,000 employees in the US, UK, Canada and India.

Why it matters

The offer shows a large tech employer treating headcount as the budget line that pays for AI hardware. Workers at other big companies may be watching to see how the arithmetic works out.

Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to around 60,000 employees in the US, UK, Canada and India, The Verge reported. It is the largest such offer the company has made, and it follows a retirement offer earlier this year that was limited to about 9,000 people. The stated logic, per The Verge, is to keep spending on AI infrastructure by lowering operating costs and headcount.

Translation: the data centers are expensive, and the cheapest thing left to trim is the people who were going to sit in them.

None of this should surprise anyone who was listening to Satya Nadella. Nearly two years ago, on a podcast, he said: "Our total people costs will go down, our cost per head will go up, and my GPU per researcher will go up." Translation: GPUs are the specialized chips that run AI, and the plan is fewer employees, each paid more and given more computing power. It is a staffing strategy in which the most reliably growing line item is the hardware.

The numbers show the plan is already underway. Microsoft employed about 223,000 people as of June 30th, down 5,000 from 228,000 the year before, which The Verge says is the first annual decline in a decade. The company laid off 4 percent of its workforce in July last year and, months before this week's offer, cut 4,800 more, hitting Xbox and commercial sales hardest. Layoffs used to be an annual ritual. They now arrive every few months, which is less a ritual than a subscription.

The terms of the buyout are less generous than the scale suggests. According to The Verge's sources, the offer is practically the same as Microsoft's regular severance: cash based on time at the company, accelerated stock vesting, some subsidized health coverage, and several months of career services. More than 30 percent of the 9,000 eligible for the earlier retirement offer accepted, but uptake here is expected to be far lower. Even at 5 percent, that is 3,000 people. Employees have until December to decide, and The Verge notes rumors of further layoffs in November and January. Translation: the voluntary option is partly a way to learn how many people must be involuntary later.

Some insiders think the cuts are overdue. Merill Fernando, a former Microsoft Entra product manager, told The Verge: "Microsoft needs fewer people." He described small organizations, layers on layers and silos everywhere, each with its own priorities and decision makers. Nadella has acknowledged the famous meme of Microsoft divisions pointing guns at one another, saying it defined the culture "more so than reality" while signaling real tensions. He also said social cohesion is not a goal; winning in the marketplace is.

That last point sits awkwardly beside the company's reputation for empathy-led management under Nadella, which The Verge says is now being tested by an AI future that could make many jobs unnecessary. The same report notes the broader Xbox restructuring, including the merger of Forza developers Turn 10 and Playground Games and the retirement of Turn 10 head Dan Greenawalt after almost 27 years.

Watch December, when the decision window closes, and the rumored November and January layoffs. The buyout count will tell Microsoft how many people want to leave. The next round will tell everyone else how many were asked.
