Withings Begins Charging $100 a Year to Let Your Own Data Leave

New account holders from October 13th, 2026 will need a Withings Plus subscription to sync health readings to apps like Apple Health, while existing users keep the feature free.

The wire said: Withings now charges $100 per year to sync your data to other apps · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 16, 2026 01:49 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Withings makes smartwatches, smart scales and other health gadgets that record things like weight and activity. Until now, sending that data automatically to third-party apps such as Apple Health or Google Health cost nothing.

Why it matters

Anyone buying a Withings device for the first time will pay roughly $100 a year to keep their readings in the health app they already use. It is also another example of a hardware maker turning a basic convenience into a recurring revenue line.

Withings will require new customers to pay for a Withings Plus subscription if they want automatic syncing to third-party apps. According to The Verge, which cited a discussion on Withings' support forums first spotted by MacRumors, the rule applies to accounts created from October 13th, 2026 onward. The price is $9.95 per month or $99.50 per year, which is the sort of number that looks like $100 once someone in marketing has had a quiet word with it.

Translation: you may still buy the scale, stand on the scale, and generate the weight. Moving that weight into the app where you keep your other weights now has a fee. The data is yours in the way a package is yours when the courier asks for a handling charge at the door.

There is a grace period of sorts. Devices come with a free one-month trial that includes third-party syncing. When the trial ends, syncing pauses, but The Verge reports the data already sent to those apps is not deleted. So the old readings stay put, and the new ones wait politely in the Withings app for you to reconsider.

Existing customers are spared. Anyone with an account created on or before October 12th, 2026 keeps free syncing, according to official comments from a member of Withings' support team. Because the perk is tied to the account rather than the device, those users can buy and register new watches, scales or other Withings hardware and keep the free feature.

That detail matters for how the policy works. The company is not taking anything away from people who already own its products, which avoids the loudest kind of backlash. It is instead charging the people who have not yet decided to buy, who cannot complain about a change to something they never had. Translation: a paywall is easiest to build where nobody has yet been standing.

It also creates an odd incentive. An account is now worth more than a device, and the cutoff date rewards anyone who signed up early. Whether old accounts become a quietly traded commodity is not something the sources address, but the incentive structure is at least clear.

The reaction was not subtle. The Hacker News post about the change had 161 points and 128 comments at the time of the source, and the thread linked to a Withings support forum post titled with the word 'Unacceptable.' The sources do not detail what Withings says the subscription revenue will pay for.

The next thing to watch is whether the Plus subscription gains enough new features to make the sync fee look like part of a bundle rather than a toll. Until then, the company has found a way to charge for the last step of a health journey: getting the number to the place you wanted it to go.
