OpenAI Seeks $30 Billion More Before Going Public, Which Is Apparently What Private Means Now

The ChatGPT maker is reportedly negotiating a round at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, led by Coatue and UAE investors, with an IPO still pending.

The wire said: OpenAI working on new $30B raise at $1.25T valuation ahead of IPO · TechCrunch AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 23, 2026 02:08 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

OpenAI filed confidential IPO paperwork in June, after a March round valued it at $852 billion. CNBC and Bloomberg now report it is working on yet another private raise before listing.

Why it matters

A record private valuation raises the price tag the public markets will be asked to accept, for OpenAI and for rival Anthropic. If the numbers hold, ordinary investors may get their first chance to buy in at a level private investors spent months reaching.

OpenAI is reportedly working on a new private funding round of up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, according to CNBC, which cited sources. TechCrunch reported that the round would be led by Coatue and investors in the United Arab Emirates. OpenAI declined to comment, which in fundraising is the traditional way of saying the spreadsheet is still open.

Translation: pre-money valuation is what a company is deemed to be worth before the new cash arrives. Post-money is that figure plus the cash. The distinction matters because it is the difference between a very large number and a very large number with a bonus. OpenAI's March round, for scale, produced an $852 billion post-money valuation.

The numbers are not settled. Bloomberg reported a month earlier that OpenAI was seeking $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation, so the two outlets disagree by $150 billion, a gap most people would call an error and this market calls a negotiating position. Both outlets say terms are still being worked out and could change.

CNBC also reported that the UAE money could come paired with a deal to secure more computing capacity for OpenAI from the UAE. Translation: the investor may also be the landlord for the machines the money will be spent on. This is a tidy arrangement, in that the cash has a short commute.

If the round clears $1 trillion, it would make OpenAI the most valuable private company ever, according to TechCrunch. It would also raise the bar for the IPO it filed confidentially for in June. A company that has been valued privately at over a trillion dollars cannot easily go public at a discount without someone asking questions in a quarterly call.

Anthropic is watching, with interest and presumably a calculator. TechCrunch reports it raised $65 billion at a $965 billion valuation in May, widely thought to be its last big round before an IPO. It also filed confidentially in June, and CNBC reports it is seeking a $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion valuation for its public debut. The rumor, per TechCrunch, is that its public S-1 filing could arrive any day.

Translation: an S-1 is the registration document a company files publicly before listing, the point at which it must disclose its finances to people who are not already on its cap table. Until now the valuations have been set by a small circle of enthusiastic investors. Soon a much larger circle will get to react.

What to watch: whether the final round lands nearer $1.25 trillion or $1.4 trillion, and whether either company's IPO price can match numbers that were agreed upon in private. The phrase going public, it turns out, is a promise about disclosure rather than about timing.
