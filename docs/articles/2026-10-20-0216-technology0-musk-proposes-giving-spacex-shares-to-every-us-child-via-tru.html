Musk Promises Every American Child a SpaceX Share, Delivered Via Trump Accounts

The pledge of one share per child, worth about $10.5 billion in total by CNBC's math, arrives as AI companies compete to look generous.

The wire said: Elon Musk’s proposed SpaceX stock giveaway would go into ‘Trump Accounts’ · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 20, 2026 02:16 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Elon Musk said Monday he would give one SpaceX share to every kid in America next year, and President Trump said it would flow through Trump Accounts, the tax-advantaged child accounts the White House says it has set up. The pledge followed Anthropic's announcement that it would commit 5% of its shares to a public-benefit fund.

Why it matters

If it happens, millions of children would own a sliver of a company that just set the record for the largest IPO. It also shows how AI firms are answering public worry about jobs and safety with equity rather than, say, slower development.

Elon Musk said on Monday that next year he will give one of his SpaceX shares to every child in America. According to the BBC, he wrote that it would be "the first of many." The Verge reported that President Trump then announced the shares would be delivered through Trump Accounts, and appeared to confirm this in a post on Truth Social. The plan has the rare quality of being fully announced before it has been designed.

The BBC noted that details on how each child would actually receive a share have not been disclosed. Translation: there is a pledge, a recipient list of tens of millions, and a pipe to carry the shares, but no one has yet said who fills out the paperwork. The Verge said Musk thanked Trump Account booster Brad Gerstner for "helping enable this," which suggests at least one person has thought about plumbing.

The plumbing is the interesting part. Trump Accounts are tax-advantaged accounts for children, and the White House said 60 million were created automatically in October. It later said more than 70 million existed in total. That matters because CNBC calculated that, at Friday's SpaceX share price, 72 million shares would be worth roughly $10.5 billion. That works out to about $146 per share, so each child would receive roughly the price of a nice pair of sneakers, held in an account they cannot touch for years.

Others are already contributing. The Verge listed Michael and Susan Dell, Ray and Barbara Dalio, Gerstner and SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell as having promised money to Trump Accounts. The accounts are thus becoming a kind of national tip jar for the very wealthy, with the government supplying the jar.

The timing is not accidental. According to the BBC, Musk's pledge came after Anthropic said it would put 5% of its shares into a Public Benefit Fund for donations to the "public good," with another 2% possible at its board's discretion. Anthropic said none of the fund's shares would be used for lobbying or political donations. Musk replied that shares should go directly to the public, which is the corporate equivalent of watching someone's charity gala and announcing you will simply hand out cash at the door.

Both announcements arrive while the AI industry faces intense scrutiny over safety and jobs. The BBC cited a 2024 IMF study suggesting almost 40% of jobs worldwide will be affected by AI, rising to 60% in advanced economies. Companies have cut thousands of jobs after investing in AI, many citing efficiency. Translation: a technology that may reduce the number of paychecks is now offering a small ownership stake as a consolation prize.

The money is also easy to afford at this stage. The BBC said SpaceX raised a record $85.7bn in its June IPO, and Anthropic is laying the ground for a listing that could value it above $1tn. Anthropic told prospective investors its revenue rose 45% to $16.8bn in the three months to September. Giving away a slice of that is generous, and also a reasonable thing to do before asking the public to buy the rest.

What to watch: whether Musk's single share per child survives contact with securities rules, tax treatment and account logistics. For now, every American kid has been promised a stake in a rocket company, and the first thing they will learn about equity is that it arrives after the press release.
