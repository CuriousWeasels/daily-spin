OpenAI Discovers Customers Prefer Cheaper Things, Offers Them Up to 90 Percent Off

Rippling, Replit and DataCamp are shifting AI spending from Anthropic to OpenAI, The Information reports, as a price war replaces the usual talk of benchmarks.

The wire said: OpenAI offers discounts up to 90 percent to poach Anthropic's biggest customers · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 26, 2026 01:55 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

OpenAI and Anthropic compete to sell AI models to businesses, especially for coding work. According to The Information, as relayed by The Decoder, OpenAI is now using deep discounts to win Anthropic's largest customers.

Why it matters

When AI vendors compete on price, companies that buy AI get cheaper tools, but the vendors' margins shrink, and that raises questions about how long the discounts can last. Chinese rivals offering comparable models for far less add pressure on both firms.

OpenAI is offering discounts of up to 90 percent to lure large customers away from Anthropic, and it appears to be working, The Decoder reported, citing The Information. Companies including Rippling, Replit and DataCamp are moving more of their AI spending to OpenAI. In a market where every vendor claims to have the smartest model, it turns out that a sale still gets attention.

The numbers are specific. Rippling CEO Parker Conrad said OpenAI's share of his company's coding workload rose from 16 percent to 52 percent, while Anthropic's fell from 57 percent to 18 percent. Translation: a customer that once mostly bought from one vendor now mostly buys from the other, which is the kind of thing a procurement department calls optimization and a sales team calls a bad quarter.

Replit switched after OpenAI offered more than 30 percent off a contract worth over $100 million a year. That is a discount of more than $30 million annually, or roughly what a customer might call a persuasive argument. DataCamp CEO Jonathan Cornelissen said OpenAI's pricing cut his costs by 80 to 90 percent, a figure that most finance chiefs would frame and hang on the wall.

The discounts reach smaller deals too. A US bank with more than $7 billion in assets had its ChatGPT seats doubled to 500 employees at no extra charge, and it pays under $7 per user instead of the usual $20 or more. Translation: the bank got twice the product at a third of the list price, and nobody had to wait for a holiday sale.

Someone pays for this, and per The Decoder, it is partly OpenAI. The company is buying market share at the expense of its own margins, meaning it is accepting thinner profit per sale in exchange for more sales. The report adds that OpenAI has also achieved real price cuts through its Sol and Luna model lines, thanks to more efficient models and infrastructure, so not every discount is a subsidy.

Anthropic, meanwhile, saw growth slow from the second quarter to the third, according to an investor memo cited in the report. Both companies also face a third party that has not been invited to the negotiating table: Chinese developers. New models from Deepseek and Kimi reportedly deliver comparable performance for a fraction of the cost, which makes a 90 percent discount look less like generosity and more like a reaction.

The sources do not say how long OpenAI can sustain the offers or how Anthropic plans to respond. What to watch is whether customers who moved for the price stay once the price rises, a test of loyalty that every software vendor eventually takes.
