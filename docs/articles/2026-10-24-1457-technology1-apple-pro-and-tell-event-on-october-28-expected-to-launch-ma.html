Apple Schedules Event to Finally Let You Touch the MacBook It Spent Years Telling You Not To

Apple's 'Pro and tell' event on October 28th is expected to deliver an OLED touchscreen MacBook Pro, plus possible M6 and OLED updates, and probably a higher price.

The wire said: What to expect from Apple’s ‘Pro and tell’ event · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 24, 2026 14:57 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple has announced a 'Pro and tell' event for October 28th at 1PM ET in Los Angeles, shortly after its Welcome Home event and the iPhone Duo announcement. The Verge reports that a touchscreen MacBook Pro is the expected headliner, though nothing is confirmed.

Why it matters

If the rumors hold, buyers of Apple's laptops and tablets may pay more for new screens and chips, with The Verge citing component shortages as one reason. Apple's first touchscreen Mac would also test whether a company can reverse a long-held position and call it innovation.

Apple has scheduled an event for October 28th at 1PM ET in Los Angeles, and The Verge reports that it appears to center on the MacBook Pro. Apple has not confirmed what will be shown. The event is called 'Pro and tell,' which is the company's way of announcing the product list without announcing the product list.

The main rumor is an OLED touchscreen MacBook Pro, which The Verge says has been a fixture of the rumor mill since at least early 2023. Translation: OLED is a display technology in which each pixel makes its own light, which tends to mean deeper blacks and a higher price tag. It would be Apple's first touchscreen laptop and first OLED laptop, and it is reportedly getting an iPhone-style Dynamic Island, the cutout that holds the camera and pretends to be a feature.

Apple is not expected to turn the laptop into an iPad. The Verge says to expect small touches, such as a magnification effect when you touch the screen or new gesture controls in native apps, and 'that's it.' After years of holding out, the company would be adding a touchscreen while promising it does not want you to use it very much.

The chips are where it gets fiddly. The touchscreen model is expected to stay on the M5 series, specifically the M5 Pro and M5 Max, with a possible wait until the M7 next year for higher-end upgrades. Meanwhile, the base MacBook Pro could move up to the M6, which debuted in the Mac Mini in August, and might get a slight redesign with a hole-punch camera. Apple's naming scheme thus ensures the cheaper laptop may have a newer chip than the expensive one, which is the sort of thing that is easy to explain in a keynote and hard to explain at a store.

The prices are expected to move in one direction. The Verge says the base MacBook Pro models are expected to cost more as component shortages reverberate through the Apple lineup. The iMac may get an M6 update nearly two years after its last revision, skipping the M5 entirely, with new colors as the main visible change. An OLED iPad Mini is also rumored, with a processor jump from the aging A17 to the A20 and a screen upgrade that may add $100.

A second Verge writer is already drafting wishes. After testing a Lenovo Yoga 9n, a 16-inch 2-in-1 with a stylus that works on the screen and the trackpad, he hopes Apple's version supports the Apple Pencil, magnetic attachment and wireless charging. He also doubts Apple will build a standard convertible, and notes hints in macOS 27 that Apple is adjusting the software to make touch feel more natural than poking at things designed for a mouse cursor.

The same writer is blunt about the stakes. The touchscreen MacBook Pro is 'sure to be expensive,' and Apple's recent design record includes a HomeView he calls perplexing for its lack of apps, an iPhone Duo he calls a little messy, and a butterfly keyboard fiasco that was not all that long ago. Having waited this long, Apple now has to make the wait look intentional. The company has until October 28th to work on it.
