Binance Says $6.3bn Affected in Hack, Blames Engineer's Laptop, Promises to Cover It

The world's largest crypto exchange says half the assets are frozen and that it will cover all losses, after a suspected breach that may be the biggest crypto heist on record.

The wire said: Binance says crypto worth $6.3bn impacted in suspected hack · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 30, 2026 03:06 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Binance, founded in 2017 and estimated to have more than 300 million registered users, said on Thursday that a security incident on Binance.com had affected about $6.3bn of assets. Early checks point to a compromised engineer's laptop, the BBC reported.

Why it matters

If Binance's promise to cover all losses holds, customers may be made whole, but the episode shows how much of the crypto market rests on the security of a few companies and their employees' devices. Bitcoin fell more than 4% on the news.

Binance said on Thursday that a "security incident" on its platform had affected about $6.3bn (£4.7bn) of assets, according to the BBC. The company said roughly half of that amount has been frozen with the help of other platforms. Translation: someone may have walked out with a very large sum, and the rest is being held in place while everyone checks who is holding what.

Binance's early checks suggest that a compromised laptop belonging to an engineer let an attacker make unauthorised withdrawals. This is a familiar pattern in security. A company can spend heavily on protecting its vaults, and then the whole arrangement depends on one person's computer. The company said it will "cover all losses" and will publish a full report when its investigation concludes.

If the figure holds, this would be the largest crypto heist ever, ahead of the $1.5bn hack of the Bybit exchange in 2025, which was linked to North Korea. Binance has not said who is behind this incident. It said it is working with law enforcement to identify the culprits. A separate, anonymous Hacker News post that drew hundreds of comments put the stolen amount at $5.6bn and linked to a blockchain analytics page. That is an unverified community figure, and it differs from Binance's $6.3bn estimate, which describes assets "impacted" rather than stolen.

The freezing was done by Tether and Circle, which issue the dollar-linked tokens USDT and USDC. Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino said his firm had frozen about $1.6bn, and Circle chief executive Jeremy Allaire said his company had frozen more than $800m. Translation: stablecoins are marketed as decentralised money, but their issuers can switch off particular coins when they want to. In this case that ability is being used to help Binance rather than to inconvenience it.

Binance.US, which serves American residents, said it was not affected. Changpeng Zhao, the founder who no longer runs the company but remains a key shareholder, said on X: "It does look like a hack." The BBC noted that several industry observers raised concerns hours before Binance confirmed anything, which suggests that the blockchain's public ledger is better at detecting problems than the exchange's press office.

Markets reacted as expected. Bitcoin fell more than 4%, and Ethereum and Solana also dropped sharply. Binance said in 2024 that it held more than $160bn in assets under custody, so the affected amount is a small part of the total but still a very large number for something called a security incident.

The next thing to watch is that full report. It will have to explain how a single engineer's laptop had enough access to move billions, and whether Binance can honour its promise to cover all losses without stress elsewhere in the system.
