Meta Releases Open Model That Needs Two Apple Computers and a Credit Line

Muse Spark Open has 816 billion parameters, a free license with conditions, and a surprise collaboration with Apple, a company Meta usually competes with.

The wire said: Muse Spark Open is Meta's answer to Qwen, GLM, and Kimi in the open-weight race · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 17, 2026 01:44 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Meta built its AI reputation on the open-weight Llama models, then moved toward paid, closed Muse models. It has now released an open-weight Muse model, aimed at the Chinese open models that have led that space.

Why it matters

A capable model you can download and run locally changes who controls AI costs and data, but at this size the 'local' part still means buying roughly two high-end Macs. The license terms also show how 'open' can come with fine print.

Meta on Thursday released Muse Spark Open, an open-weight AI model it calls the most capable US open-weight model to date. Translation: open-weight means the company publishes the trained numbers that make the model work, so anyone can download and run it, though not necessarily see how it was built. According to The Decoder, the weights are available on Hugging Face.

The specifications are large. The model has 816 billion parameters, of which 42 billion are active at a time, and a context window of up to one million tokens. Translation: parameters are the adjustable numbers inside a model, and a context window is how much text it can keep in mind at once. Only 42 billion working at a time is how a very large model avoids needing quite so much electricity to answer a simple question. Meta says it handles text, image, video, and audio, and is built for coding and agentic tasks, meaning jobs where the AI takes multiple steps on its own.

Meta says it beats Qwen 3.8, GLM 5.3, and Kimi K3 on several benchmarks, including JobBench, MCP-Atlas, HLE-Diamond, and ZeroBench. Translation: benchmarks are standardized tests that companies choose, run, and then report favorably. The Decoder notes that Meta's own figures also show the model falling well short of its paid Muse Wonder Full models, which is a useful reminder of why the free one is free.

The more unusual part is the hardware. Meta says the model is optimized for Apple's M5 Ultra chip and runs locally on two Mac Studios with 512 GB of unified memory each. The Verge reported that Meta calls it the first model in its class able to run on a desktop this way, using Apple's MLX framework. For ordinary people, 'on your desk' here means two professional workstations, which is a desk in the same sense that a yacht is a boat you can park.

Meta and Apple, usually rivals, are being cheerful about it. Mark Zuckerberg thanked Apple's new CEO John Ternus on X, and Ternus replied with congratulations and praise for running a model of this scale efficiently on a desk. Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang told Axios the companies were 'excited for mommy and daddy to be playing nice again.' Axios also noted the companies had been collaborating for several months.

The license is free, with conditions. Users must display the label 'Built with Muse Spark Open.' According to The Decoder, any provider earning more than $20 million a year that wants to offer the model as an API service needs a separate agreement with Meta. Translation: it is open for hobbyists, researchers, and small companies, and a negotiation for anyone large enough to be a competitor.

So Meta gets a credible answer to the Chinese open models, Apple gets a showcase for expensive Macs, and developers get a model they can run privately if they can afford the hardware. The next thing to watch is whether independent testers confirm Meta's benchmark claims, which is traditionally where the word 'leading' meets the word 'reproducible.'
