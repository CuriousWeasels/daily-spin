Zelensky Flies to Oslo to Ask Washington for Intelligence, Interceptors and Internet

Ukraine's president meets US envoys in Norway as Rubio denies withholding intelligence, fuel prices bite, and Washington's sanctions policy bends toward Russian diesel.

The wire said: Zelensky and US envoys holding Oslo talks as Rubio denies withholding intelligence · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 20, 2026 15:53 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Relations between Kyiv and the Trump administration have soured in recent weeks, with Trump saying Ukraine should get a new president or someone else to negotiate for it. Talks in Miami earlier this month reportedly ended abruptly after Trump announced Russian diesel would flow to US and other markets.

Why it matters

The outcome affects whether Ukraine gets air-defense missiles and early warnings, and whether US domestic fuel politics are starting to shape policy toward Russia. Europe, which Merz says is carrying the cost of Ukraine's defense, is watching closely.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky met US special envoy Steve Witkoff in Oslo, the BBC reported, in talks that Norway and Finland agreed to host at his request. Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Støre said the aim was to steer relations toward "a more constructive path". It is a notable arrangement: the leader of a country at war needed two Nordic governments to arrange a meeting with his main ally.

The backdrop is an argument over intelligence. Serhii Beskrestnov, a top military adviser to Zelensky known as "Flash", told the BBC that unreliable US warnings, including about ballistic missile launches, meant lives were being lost. "This looks very strange," he said, noting that Kyiv sometimes gets information before an attack and sometimes does not. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called the accusation "scandalous" and denied that anything was being withheld.

Rubio did concede, speaking in India, that Patriot interceptor missiles may indeed not have arrived in Ukraine, though he said nothing had been denied to Kyiv intentionally. Translation: the missiles are not there, but nobody decided that on purpose. The BBC notes that Patriots are the only effective weapon against Russian ballistic missiles, and that unconfirmed reports said a further transfer had not been approved. For the people under those missiles, the distinction between neglect and policy is largely academic.

Then there is diesel. According to the BBC, the Miami talks earlier this month reportedly ended when the Ukrainian delegation learned Trump had announced Moscow would supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel to the US and other markets, ending American sanctions on Russian diesel exports. Translation: sanctions are the penalty meant to make Russia's economy hurt, and this one was lifted in the middle of a negotiation about Russia. Zelensky called it "a happy birthday present for Putin".

The motive is on the gas pump. Trump has blamed Ukrainian strikes on Russian oil refineries for fuel prices that the BBC says have hit record highs since the start of the US-led war on Iran, with midterm elections next month. Vice-President JD Vance said Ukraine should stop bombing Russian energy and that there is no formal alliance with Kyiv. The war's logic, in other words, now runs through a US voter's fill-up.

Europe is not thrilled. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz spoke of "mixed pictures and messages" and noted that the US has not paid for Ukraine's defense since the start of 2025, leaving Europe and others to cover it. The Kremlin, for its part, said it greatly appreciates the American mediation efforts, and spokesman Dmitry Peskov hoped Witkoff might visit soon. NPR's coverage frames the shift as Trump's up-and-down relationship with Russia swinging back up as he seeks an end to the war.

Zelensky also raised Starlink, after a bipartisan group of US senators last week demanded an urgent investigation into speed restrictions on the network in Ukraine. Witkoff was not expected at the closing press conference, and it is unclear whether he will continue to Russia. What to watch is whether Oslo produces a single Patriot shipment. Communiqués about constructive paths are cheaper, and Ukraine has had plenty of them.
