Voters Rate Economy Poor, Trump's Economic Approval Hits Record Low, Everyone Fears the Other Party

A CNBC poll finds 80% of Americans call the economy fair or poor and Trump's economic approval at 34%, with under two weeks until the midterms.

The wire said: Trump's approval rating on economy hits all-time low as midterm looms: CNBC survey · CNBC Economy

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 25, 2026 15:06 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

CNBC's All-America Economic Survey, run by a Democratic and a Republican pollster, questioned 1,000 registered voters between Oct. 14 and 18. It was released with the midterm election set for Nov. 3.

Why it matters

Prices, not policy theory, are driving the mood, and the survey suggests that could shape control of Congress and whatever economic policy follows. Both parties also got a warning that voters distrust them nearly equally.

CNBC reported on Thursday that eight in 10 Americans rate the economy as fair or poor, the highest share since December 2023. President Donald Trump's economic approval fell to 34% from 38% in July, while disapproval rose to 65% from 60%. Translation: net approval, the gap between those who approve and those who don't, is now minus 31, the lowest of either of his terms.

The poll's most awkward detail for Republicans is where the gloom came from. Overall pessimism rose only 1 point to 63%, but Republican pessimism rose 6 points to 35%, the highest of either Trump term. "We got there not because of independents or Democrats getting more sour — it's because Republicans have," said Micah Roberts of Public Opinion Strategies, the survey's Republican pollster. When your own pollster volunteers that sentence, the data has made its point.

Prices are the engine. Eighty percent say prices are rising, up 5 points from a year ago, and 55% say faster than usual. The official figures do not argue: the latest CPI reading in our data is 3.74% year over year. Translation: something that cost $100 a year ago now costs about $103.74, and nobody has offered a refund. Brent crude sits at $115.22, which helps explain why gas is on voters' minds.

Food has overtaken gas as the bigger worry, 34% to 23%, and it bites hardest among households making $30,000 or less. It is also the top issue for Republicans who don't identify as MAGA supporters. Democrats' lead on food costs widened to 16 points from 7 in July, a rare case of a grocery bill functioning as a campaign strategist.

Voters prefer Democratic control of Congress 52% to 45%, a 3-point gain for Democrats since July. Independents lean Democratic 49% to 33%, and 72% of Democrats are highly interested in the election against 62% of Republicans. Within the 3.1-point margin of error, the lead is real but not a landslide, and both pollsters said as much.

Trump's overall approval is steady at 40%, with 59% disapproving, so voters have separated their feelings about the man from their feelings about the economy, which is a distinction few household budgets honor. The Iran war is not helping: only 46% say it was worth it to disrupt Iran's nuclear capabilities, down from 53% in April, and 35% say it was worth it given higher gasoline prices.

The one thing nearly everyone agrees on is corruption. Ninety-three percent say elected officials should not personally own or trade individual stocks of companies with federal contracts, and 89% say firms owned by a sitting president's immediate family should not get federal contracts. In a country this divided, that is the closest thing to a bipartisan bill that nobody has passed.

A companion CNBC survey shows each side fears the other. Forty-four percent worry Trump will be allowed to operate beyond his constitutional power, including 74% of Democrats. Twenty-eight percent worry about policies of the Democratic Socialists of America, including 54% of Republicans. Republican strategist Ron Bonjean summed up the brand problem: "it's not great on the Republican side."

Democratic pollster Jay Campbell sees no 1994- or 2008-style realignment, just "a return to the mean." Roberts calls it a fight between "the map and momentum." The map favors Republicans, momentum favors Democrats, and inflation of 3.74% favors whoever is not currently in charge. Watch the Nov. 3 results to see which one wins.
