France Permits Only Union-Approved Protests, Receives Protests Anyway, With 70,000 Police Attending

More than 1,300 were arrested as an estimated 410,000 to 800,000 marched across France, with clashes in several cities and similar unrest elsewhere in Europe.

The wire said: More than 1,300 arrested as clashes follow another day of mass protests across France · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 25, 2026 15:06 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

France is in a second weekend of mass protests over the cost of living and poor school conditions, following a first weekend of looting and arson that led to more than 2,500 arrests. President Emmanuel Macron decreed that only demonstrations organised by recognised trade unions would be permitted.

Why it matters

The unrest is unfolding six months before a presidential election in which both the hard right and the radical left blame Macron, so how the government handles it may shape the vote. Businesses face repair bills the CCI put at hundreds of millions of euros, and people in affected cities face tear gas, curfews and closed streets.

More than 1,300 people were arrested across France on Saturday as hundreds of thousands took part in a second weekend of protests, according to the BBC. Most marches were peaceful until later in the day, when police fired tear gas and water cannon in Paris and other cities. The scenes were less severe than last weekend's, which the BBC described as some of the worst disorder in modern French history.

The crowd size depends on whom you ask. The interior ministry counted 410,000 protesters, while the unions said about 800,000. Translation: both sides agree a very large number of people walked outside, and disagree only on whether it was a lot or a great deal. It is the traditional French method of settling a dispute by publishing two figures and letting the evening news choose.

The government came prepared. About 70,000 police were deployed, restrictions were imposed in several cities, and Macron decreed that only union-organised demonstrations would be allowed. Last week, the BBC reported, officers were outnumbered, hundreds of businesses were looted and buildings were torched. Hundreds of people have since been jailed after fast-track court hearings, a system that works quickly in the sense that its name says so.

The prevention was partial. In Paris, a peaceful march from Porte d'Italie toward Porte de Vincennes ended in clashes, with police including an elite unit that normally handles organised crime and counter-terrorism. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said several thousand radical activists gathered at the rear of the march, including around 500 he identified as rioters. Paris recorded 142 arrests, fewer than half of last Saturday's total. In Marseille, barricades burned, and in Lyon police used tear gas on a group of students gathered in an area the prefecture had declared off-limits.

The human cost was real. The interior ministry said 53 officers were hurt. Nuñez said two protesters were seriously injured, in Toulouse and Clermont-Ferrand. Franceinfo reported that the 20-year-old in Toulouse had several fingers torn off by a nearby explosive. Last week, according to AFP, a demonstrator's hand was blown off by a police crowd-control grenade, and about 1,000 officers were injured.

The protesters' complaints are fairly ordinary. A 26-year-old who gave her name as Zelie told the BBC that people were taxed heavily and felt unable to speak. Two teenagers said their generation felt abandoned. A demonstrator said looting was wrong but blamed poverty and police violence, and said the protests would end when demands were answered.

Politics is doing what it does six months before an election. Macron leaves office next spring, and polls suggest voters will choose between Marine Le Pen of the National Rally and a candidate from the radical-left La France Insoumise. Both camps blame Macron. The right also criticises rioters, while the left backs the union-led marches. Jean-Luc Mélenchon said police had created a setup designed to provoke chaos. The BBC did not report the government's response to that claim.

France was not alone. The BBC reported more than 120 arrests in Barcelona after a student protest, tear gas in Tirana, and clashes in Brussels. In Milan, hundreds of soldiers joined police to keep protesters from gathering at all. That is one way to guarantee a peaceful demonstration: ensure it does not occur.

What to watch next is whether a permitted-protests-only rule can hold when the grievances are about prices and schools, neither of which a decree lowers. Nuñez said the arrest total would likely rise.
