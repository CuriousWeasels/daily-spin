Black Sea Ships Keep Catching Fire While Governments Draft Strongly Worded Calls for Them to Stop

Two tankers burned off Romania with seven crew missing, as NATO, the EU and India all search for a way to protect shipping that none of them controls.

The wire said: Romania: 2 ships on fire in Black Sea, 7 crew missing · DW

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 16, 2026 01:49 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Russia's invasion of Ukraine has moved shipping lanes in the Black Sea into the line of fire, with attacks on merchant vessels rising in recent months. Romania and Bulgaria, both NATO members, sit along the routes Ukrainian ships now favor.

Why it matters

Attacks on commercial ships endanger crews from many countries, cut grain shipments from two major exporters, and raise freight costs that eventually show up in food and fuel prices.

Two chemical tankers caught fire near the mouth of the Sulina Canal off Romania's Black Sea coast overnight into Thursday, and seven crew members from one of them remain missing, according to DW. The vessel, the San Marino-flagged Wine Trader, sent an emergency alert; of its 12 crew, five were reported safe. The other ship, the Comoros-flagged Yara J, burned some hours earlier, and all of its crew are said to be safe.

The fires followed Russian drones straying into Romanian airspace. Romanian officials have not explicitly said the drones caused the blazes. Defense Minister Radu Miruta said an investigation is underway into whether the Wine Trader was struck, and called the matter one of significant gravity because it occurred in Romanian territorial waters. This is the careful phrasing of a government that has a strong suspicion and a NATO treaty to consult before it says so.

The context is not reassuring. DW reports drone strikes on at least five other cargo ships off Romania and neighboring Bulgaria in the past 10 days, with at least 23 sailors lost. Bulgaria summoned the Russian ambassador after a ship sank off its coast on Tuesday. Translation: a summoned ambassador is a diplomatic way of asking someone to come in and listen to a complaint they will not act on.

Romanian President Nicusor Dan met NATO chief Mark Rutte and wrote on X that the attacks are unacceptable and must stop. Rutte replied that NATO has strengthened its eastern flank, including counter-drone capabilities, and will adjust its posture as needed. Translation: counter-drone capabilities means equipment to shoot down cheap drones, ideally at a cost lower than the drone's price tag, though neither statement addressed how that math works out.

Ships were anchored by the canal because that is where they wait out weather or congestion. The congestion exists partly because Ukrainian vessels increasingly use the Danube and its ports while Russian forces attack Ukraine's own Black Sea ports. Ukrainian ships are also hugging the Romanian and Bulgarian coasts. A safe route for one side has become an exposed one for everybody nearby, which is not how routes are supposed to work.

Diplomacy is moving, at about the speed diplomacy moves. Bulgarian Prime Minister Rumen Radev said EU leaders at their Brussels summit will discuss a proposed moratorium on attacks on civilian ships. India, which supplies more than 12% of the world's seafarers, has urged both Russia and Ukraine to guarantee safe passage. Ukraine's foreign minister called India's plan the broadest of four offers Kyiv has received, while Russia has welcomed India's peace efforts without endorsing a binding safe-passage deal.

Then there is the energy ceasefire US President Donald Trump announced over the weekend, whose scope and enforcement mechanism DW describes as unclear. Ukraine alleges Russia has already breached it. Analysts quoted by DW say it could be a confidence-building first step, but a shipping deal would need explicit commitments on merchant vessels, ports and routes. A truce with no defined terms is, at least, easy to violate without noticing.

The costs reach well beyond the Black Sea. Lekha Chakraborty of India's National Institute of Public Finance and Policy said prolonged disruption would raise India's import bill via higher freight on Russian crude and tighter supplies of soft oils, with edible oil prices and food inflation feeling it first. Sailors pay with their lives, and shoppers pay at the checkout.

Watch the Brussels summit for whether a moratorium moves from discussion to text, and whether Romania will say outright what hit the Wine Trader. Until then, the ships will keep anchoring where the weather is calm and the airspace is not.
