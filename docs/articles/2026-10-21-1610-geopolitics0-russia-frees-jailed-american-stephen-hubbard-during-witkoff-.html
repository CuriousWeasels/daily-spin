Russia Frees 74-Year-Old American, Receives Praise, Diesel Deal and Possible Summit in Return for Nothing

Stephen Hubbard is heading home after Putin's talks with Steve Witkoff, and Trump says no swap was needed, though he also credits suspended sanctions.

The wire said: Russia frees jailed American Stephen Hubbard during Witkoff talks · DW

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 21, 2026 16:10 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Stephen Hubbard, a 74-year-old American from Michigan, was captured in Ukraine in 2022 and sentenced by Russia in 2024 to almost seven years for allegedly being a mercenary, which he denies. His release came as US envoy Steve Witkoff held talks with Vladimir Putin in St. Petersburg on Wednesday.

Why it matters

A man in poor health is going home, which is the part nobody disputes. The surrounding questions are what Washington gave up to get there, and whether it changes the path to ending the war in Ukraine.

Russia released Stephen Hubbard on Wednesday, with Putin announcing during talks with Witkoff in St. Petersburg that the American was at the airport. Hubbard had been jailed after being accused of serving in a Ukrainian defence unit, according to the BBC. CBS News, the BBC's US partner, reported that he was in poor health and expected to be flown to Texas for treatment.

President Trump said on Truth Social that he had asked Putin directly in a phone call last week, and that there was no 'swap' necessary. Translation: a swap is when each side hands over a prisoner, which is how Americans have mostly left Russian custody in the past. Trump's point is that this time Washington paid nothing, which would be remarkable if it were the whole story.

It was not quite the whole story. In a follow-up post, Trump said that had sanctions gone ahead, the negotiations would have come to a complete and total halt. The BBC notes he suspended sanctions on Russia two days before they were due to take effect. So the release was free of charge, provided one does not count the sanctions that were not imposed. Translation: a concession counts as a concession even when it is described as a absence of one.

Witkoff then told Putin, 'you are a humanitarian also sir,' according to the BBC. Republican lawmakers were not charmed. Representative Don Bacon called it 'another embarrassment', and Senator John Cornyn said Putin 'is a stone cold killer'. The Kremlin's own account is that Putin acted for humanitarian reasons after American officials had asked for some time.

Hubbard's family kept to the human side of things. His son, Joseph Coleman, an Orthodox priest, said he received his father's freedom 'not as a political victory, but as a gift of mercy'. His lawyer, Martin De Luca, said the human dividends of peace need not wait for a final agreement.

The diplomacy around him was less tidy. Witkoff brought papers with 'some ideas' for a peace deal, he told Putin, according to DW. The Kremlin afterward said the proposals 'lacked substance'. Putin did agree 'in principle' to resume talks with Russian, Ukrainian and US delegations after November 10, focused on a post-war security system, according to adviser Yuri Ushakov.

There was also commerce. Putin thanked Trump for the deal struck earlier this month to import Russian diesel, with the first shipment due to leave in two to three days, the BBC reported. That deal infuriated Zelensky, and the BBC says Trump has blamed Ukrainian strikes on Russian energy sites for fuel prices that have risen since the US-led war on Iran, ahead of November's midterm elections. Putin also floated selling large quantities of fertiliser to the American market, and Russian officials said a first-ever meeting of the Chinese, Russian and US leaders was being prepared.

Zelensky, meanwhile, told Fox & Friends that the front line should be frozen as a first step toward a ceasefire. The BBC notes that Putin has so far rejected every proposal to do that. Norway's prime minister said the Russians may be looking for diplomacy, and Witkoff plans to return to Oslo. One American is home, and the question is what the invoice looks like when it arrives.
