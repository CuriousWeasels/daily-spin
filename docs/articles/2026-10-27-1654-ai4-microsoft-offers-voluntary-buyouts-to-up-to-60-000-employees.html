Microsoft Invites 60,000 Employees to Leave, Insists This Is Not a Layoff

The company is offering voluntary exit packages to up to 60,000 staff in four countries, months after cutting 4,800 jobs the old-fashioned way.

The wire said: Microsoft offers buyouts to 60,000 employees · The Verge AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 27, 2026 16:54 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Microsoft has about 223,000 employees and has been trimming its workforce. It laid off 4,800 people months ago, mostly in Xbox and commercial sales, and earlier this year offered a voluntary retirement program to about 9,000 long-serving staff.

Why it matters

A buyout lets a company shrink headcount without calling it layoffs, and it signals how aggressively big tech is reshaping its workforce around AI. Workers who are offered one face a decision with real money, and little information, attached.

Microsoft is offering voluntary exit packages to as many as 60,000 employees in the US, UK, Canada, and India, according to The Verge. In a message to staff, chief people officer Amy Coleman described it as a chance to leave the company "with financial support and career planning resources." That is roughly a quarter of a workforce of about 223,000, presented as an opportunity.

Translation: a voluntary exit offer is a buyout. The company puts money on the table and hopes enough people take it that nobody has to be fired. It is a layoff in which the employee signs the paperwork, which tends to improve the mood of the press release.

Eligibility is uneven. Sources told The Verge that staff in AI experiences, Copilot, CoreAI, Windows + Devices, Xbox, finance, and human resources are eligible. Cloud + AI, MicrosoftAI, and corporate, external, and legal affairs are partially participating. Marketing, commercial business, discovery and quantum, and security are not included at all. Those sitting out may want to note that the company has, in effect, told them it would like them to stay.

The offer follows two earlier moves. Months ago Microsoft laid off 4,800 people, mostly in Xbox and commercial sales. Earlier this year it ran a voluntary retirement program for about 9,000 US employees whose age plus years of service totaled 70 or more. This time, according to an internal note seen by The Verge, the offer is not based on age, length of service, or performance. In other words, nearly everyone in the eligible teams is invited, and nobody can read it as a verdict on them personally.

Coleman's memo was candid in one respect: "The Voluntary Exit Offer is new for us," she wrote, adding that "We'll learn from it." It is reassuring to see a company run an experiment on 60,000 people and promise to take notes.

There is a catch for anyone hoping to wait it out. Microsoft says it may consider another voluntary exit offer "where there is a business and workforce need." Fortune also reported that Xbox CEO Asha Sharma is planning new layoffs. So the choice for some employees is between a package now and an unspecified arrangement later, which is a negotiating position economists call leverage and employees call Tuesday.

The Verge's reporting does not say how many people are expected to accept or what the packages are worth, and Microsoft's memo does not say. Those numbers will determine whether this was a gentle trim or a prelude to the other kind of announcement.
