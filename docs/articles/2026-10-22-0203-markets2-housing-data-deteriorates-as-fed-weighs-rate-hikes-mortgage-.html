Housing Market Enters Deep Sleep as Fed Weighs Making the Mattress More Expensive

Pending home sales hit a record low and mortgage rates sit near 7.4%, yet inflation leaves the Federal Reserve eyeing further rate hikes.

The wire said: Housing is flashing warning signs just as the Fed weighs more interest rate hikes · CNBC Economy

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 22, 2026 02:03 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

The Fed has been trying to bring down inflation, which has stayed stubborn, and markets now price in possible quarter-point hikes in December and March 2027. Mortgage rates have climbed to roughly 7.4%, about double the roughly 3% of five years ago.

Why it matters

Anyone hoping to buy, sell or refinance a home is facing borrowing costs that make all three unattractive, and further Fed hikes could push them higher. Meanwhile the central bank's main tool for fighting inflation is the same one that is flattening housing.

The National Association of Realtors reported Tuesday that its pending home sales index fell 7.3% in September, the lowest reading in records going back to 2001, according to CNBC. Wall Street had expected a decline of 0.4%. Translation: economists predicted a mild dip, and the housing market delivered a cliff.

Pending sales count contracts that have been signed but not yet closed, which makes the index a forward-looking measure. It was down 11.3% from a year earlier. Separate Commerce Department data showed housing starts falling 5.2% on the month, against an expected 0.4% drop, while single-family permits totaled 845,000, the lowest since 2023.

The cause is not mysterious. The 30-year mortgage rate spent most of September above 7% and ended the month near 7.6%, according to Mortgage News Daily. Five years ago it was closer to 3%. NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun said market conditions weakened because of fast-rising mortgage rates, which is the kind of finding that required a chief economist and a press release.

The Mortgage Bankers Association added more evidence on Wednesday. Total mortgage applications fell 5.3% last week, even though the average 30-year rate slipped to 7.41% from 7.50%, the first decline in over two months. Purchase applications dropped 7% and were 18% lower than a year ago. Refinancing was 59% below last year, because few people can profit from swapping a cheap loan for an expensive one.

That points to the lock-in effect, which Goldman Sachs economist Ronnie Walker cited. Translation: homeowners with cheap old mortgages will not sell and take on a pricey new one, so they stay put, supply stays thin, and buyers face both high prices and high rates. Goldman's note says more than 95% of borrowers have rates below the current level, and 65% are at least 2 percentage points below. Goldman forecasts existing home sales of 3.9 million in 2027, 26% below 2019.

Some buyers are turning to adjustable-rate mortgages, whose share held at 10.5%. Translation: you get a lower rate now in exchange for letting the market choose your rate later. The MBA's Joel Kan noted these rates run almost a full percentage point below the 30-year fixed.

In an ordinary downturn, the Fed might cut rates to help. This is not an ordinary downturn. CNBC noted that wholesale final demand goods prices rose 2.6%, the fastest pace in a data series going back to December 2009, and that officials are watching inflation tied to heavy artificial intelligence investment. The Fed also believes that AI spending could offset weakness in sectors like housing, which is a theory in the way a lottery ticket is a retirement plan.

Markets expect the Fed to hold rates at next week's meeting, but futures pricing implies a strong chance of a quarter-point hike in December and another in March 2027, according to CME Group's FedWatch tool. The one optimist is Yun, who notes that mortgage rates have already risen far more than the Fed's own hikes, so they may be topping out. Buyers and sellers, he said, may be waiting for rates to move favorably, which is also what they were doing last month.

The S&P 500 ETF, SPY, stood at 773.09 as of October 21. Whether stocks keep ignoring the housing market is the next thing to watch, along with whether the housing market keeps ignoring everyone.
