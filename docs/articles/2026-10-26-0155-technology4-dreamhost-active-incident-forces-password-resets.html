DreamHost Resets Passwords After 'Issue Affecting Some Systems,' Declines to Say Which

The web host has reset email and FTP passwords for some customers since October 23, while its status page describes the cause only as an incident.

The wire said: Active incident at Dreamhost forcing password reset · Hacker News

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 26, 2026 01:55 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

DreamHost, a web hosting company, began investigating File Manager connectivity problems on October 23, 2026. Within hours the problem grew into signed-out webmail users and a precautionary password reset for some email and FTP/SFTP accounts.

Why it matters

Anyone who hosts a website or email through DreamHost may find old passwords suddenly rejected by webmail and email apps. The company's repeated warnings about reset emails are also a reminder that incidents like this are a favorite occasion for phishing.

DreamHost, the web hosting company, said on its status page that it is resetting passwords for some email and FTP/SFTP users after an incident that began on October 23, 2026. The company describes the cause only as an issue affecting some of its systems, including FTP/SFTP access and webmail. It has not said what the issue was, which is a choice, and a common one.

The timeline is instructive. At 2:20PM Pacific, the status page reported connectivity issues with the File Manager. Translation: a tool customers use to look at their files was not working. By 5:20PM, the company said the File Manager was offline, some webmail users had been signed out, and it was resetting some email passwords as a precaution.

A precaution, in this context, is an action taken because something might have gone wrong, without confirming that it did. Translation: the company has chosen the inconvenient option in case the alternative turns out to be worse. The sources do not say whether any data was accessed, and this article will not guess.

The reset has real consequences for ordinary users. If an account was reset, webmail and email apps such as Outlook, Apple Mail and phone clients stop accepting the old password. Only the account owner can set a new one, so email and FTP users must contact whoever pays the bill, or in some offices, whoever is least able to hide.

The instructions also changed as the incident went on. On the evening of October 23 the company told customers to wait for its email before changing anything, because changes made before the reset finished would be overwritten. By the morning of October 24, it said the reset for affected email and FTP/SFTP users was complete and owners could set new passwords immediately in the control panel. The new password may take up to 15 minutes to work.

The panel is not the only thing that was in flux. The status page listed email hosting, webmail and the File Manager as service disruptions, while web hosting, VPS, databases, domain registrations, security certificates and cloud services showed as operational. Customers' websites were, by this account, mostly fine. Their ability to read mail and upload files was the part under repair.

DreamHost also repeated one message in nearly every update: it will never email you asking for your password, and its notice will not ask you to log in through a link. Customers should go directly to panel.dreamhost.com. If a reset email arrives that they did not request, the company says to ignore it. This is sound advice, and also a quiet acknowledgment that an email telling people to reset passwords is exactly what a scammer would write.

By the latest update, a minority of Professional Email customers had seen a "Failed to edit" error when setting a new password, which the company says is fixed. Emails to account owners were still going out, with completion expected that day, and the File Manager remained unavailable. What remains unanswered is the cause, and customers will have to wait for a post-incident explanation, assuming one arrives.
