Serbia Raises Terror Alert to Red After Knife Attack at Israeli Embassy

Police shot dead a teenage attacker in Belgrade who was already on security services' radar, and the country moved to its highest threat level.

The wire said: Serbian shot dead after attacking officer outside Israeli embassy, minister says · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 29, 2026 03:01 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Early on Wednesday, a knife-wielding attacker rushed a police officer guarding the Israeli embassy in Belgrade and was shot dead, according to Serbia's interior minister. It is the second attack on the embassy in under three years.

Why it matters

A red alert means visible changes for ordinary Serbians, from tighter checks to more police at crowded places and infrastructure. The case also raises the question of what monitoring a known extremist sympathizer actually accomplishes.

Serbian police shot dead a young man outside the Israeli embassy in Belgrade in the early hours of Wednesday, after what authorities called a terrorist attack, according to the BBC and DW. Interior Minister Ivica Dacic said the attacker ran at a police officer with two knives while shouting "Allahu Akbar." The officer suffered a minor knife wound to the face, shot the attacker, and by the minister's account did not need hospital treatment.

Dacic said the suspect had not been formally identified but was a Serbian citizen and a minor. DW reported he was believed to come from the western town of Sabac. The minister said security services had been monitoring him as a known supporter of the Islamic State, and that he had posted an image of the group's flag on his Instagram account.

Translation: being monitored means the state knew who he was and what he was posting. It does not mean the state could stop him from walking to an embassy with two knives. Surveillance is a way of knowing about a problem in advance, which is not quite the same as preventing it. Dacic said an investigation is ongoing, and the sources do not say what, if anything, the monitoring led to before Wednesday.

Israel's foreign ministry confirmed what it called an attempted terrorist attack near the mission. It said no embassy personnel were hurt, and the BBC reported the building was closed at the time. Armed guards were seen outside the embassy on Wednesday morning.

Dacic raised Serbia's terrorism threat level to red alert, the highest tier. Translation: more identity checks, more uniformed police, and a visible presence at infrastructure sites and places where crowds gather. In practice, that mostly means ordinary people will notice extra officers on the way to work and at big events, which is the government's way of showing the alert exists.

This is the second attack on the embassy in less than three years. In June 2024, a man shot a police officer in the neck with a crossbow. The BBC said that officer survived and killed the assailant. DW described that attacker as a 25-year-old Islamic convert from a village near the capital who had become radicalized, and said the officer was seriously wounded. The two outlets differ only in emphasis, not in the basic account.

The timing adds context but no established link. The BBC noted the attack came one day after Israeli elections. DW added that it followed key early parliamentary elections in Serbia days earlier, and that Israel's vote had no clear result in sight. Neither source says the attack was connected to either vote, and officials have not suggested otherwise.

What to watch: whether the investigation explains how a minor on a watchlist got within knife range of an embassy guard, and how long red alert lasts before the extra checkpoints quietly go back to being ordinary Wednesdays.
