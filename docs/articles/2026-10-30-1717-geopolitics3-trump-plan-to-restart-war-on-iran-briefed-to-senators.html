Senators Briefed on Plan to Restart Iran War; At Least One Calls It 'Insane'

Al Jazeera reports a closed-door briefing on renewed war with Iran, while NPR says Hegseth removed an Army general as the U.S. prepares for possible attacks.

Catch up

The U.S. and Israel have been in a war with Iran, and Al Jazeera's coverage now carries links asking why strikes have resumed and what is happening in the Strait of Hormuz. The sources here are thin, consisting of one headline and one short summary.

Why it matters

A decision to restart a war with Iran would be made by a small number of people, and everyone else, including energy consumers and shipping companies, would find out the consequences afterward. The reported removal of a senior officer at the same moment suggests the planning is not happening in a calm environment.

According to Al Jazeera, senators have been briefed on President Donald Trump's plan to restart the war on Iran, and the outlet's headline describes the plan as 'insane.' The word is in quotation marks, which in journalism means someone else said it and the outlet would like you to know that. The source text does not say who used the word, so the identity of the critic remains a mystery for now.

The briefing is the part of the process in which Congress is told what the executive branch intends to do. Translation: a briefing is not a vote. Senators are informed, they react, and then events proceed on the schedule set by whoever has the authority to order the strikes. It is a bit like being invited to a meeting about a decision that has already been made, except the meeting has classified slides.

Separately, NPR reported that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth ousted an Army general 'as the U.S. prepares for potential attacks on Iran.' NPR's summary does not name the general or give a reason for the removal, and neither does Al Jazeera's page, so readers should resist the urge to supply one. The timing is reported, the motive is not.

The two reports fit together in a way that is uncomfortable but not complicated. When a country is preparing for possible military action, it generally wants its senior officers in place, and removing one is the sort of detail that makes observers ask whether everyone agrees on the plan. Whether that is what happened here is not established by the sources.

Al Jazeera's page also carries navigation links asking 'Did Iran capture a US submarine?' and 'Why have strikes resumed?' These are headlines, not confirmed facts, and the sources provided do not answer either question. The second one does suggest that the word 'restart' may already be partly out of date, though the material does not say what has been struck, by whom, or when.

What to watch is what the briefed senators say once they are outside the room and whether any of them can vote on the matter before the strikes begin. The more reliable prediction is that someone will eventually describe the plan as having been thoroughly explained.
