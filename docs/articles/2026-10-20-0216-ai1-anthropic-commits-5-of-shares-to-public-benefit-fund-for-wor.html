Anthropic Pledges 5% of Itself to Public, Retains 100% of Say in How

The AI company's new benefit fund is a wholly owned LLC, not a nonprofit, and arrives just ahead of an IPO that could value it near $2 trillion.

The wire said: Anthropic plans fund for workers threatened by AI, Musk counters with SpaceX shares for children · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 20, 2026 02:16 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Anthropic announced a public benefit fund holding 5 percent of its shares, aimed partly at workers displaced by AI. Elon Musk responded by promising one SpaceX share to each child in the U.S.

Why it matters

If AI does eliminate jobs, someone will have to pay for the fallout, and companies are now competing to say they will. How much control the donors keep over the money will decide whether these pledges function as a safety net or as marketing.

Anthropic said on Sunday that it plans to put 5 percent of its shares into a new Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, pending shareholder approval. According to The Decoder and TechCrunch, the fund could make large donations to traditional nonprofits or explore direct payments to specific populations, with a focus on workers whose jobs AI may threaten. It is a generous-sounding gesture from a company that sells the technology it is generous about.

The fund could receive another 2 percent of shares for every 50 percent rise in Anthropic's valuation above a set baseline. Translation: the pledge grows as the company gets richer, which is how most charitable pledges work and also how most executive stock packages work. Anthropic pitches it as the opposite of the latter. The Decoder reports that the top-ups are at the board's discretion, and the milestones that would trigger them have not been named.

The structure is the interesting part. Instead of a tax-exempt nonprofit, the fund will be a wholly owned LLC subsidiary. Translation: a limited liability company that Anthropic owns, so the donor and the recipient are the same entity. Anthropic says this gives it flexibility, and it promises not to use the money for lobbying or political donations in the US or abroad. It has not said how the fund will be governed, only that details will follow in the coming months.

Control at the top is similarly settled. The Decoder reports that the Long-Term Benefit Trust, which holds no economic stake, appoints a majority of the board, and that after the IPO the founders are set to hold 50.1 percent of votes on major decisions through a special share. The timing is not subtle: the report says the IPO could raise up to $100 billion at a valuation near $2 trillion, with filings expected within weeks. TechCrunch notes the announcement lands while the industry faces souring public opinion, which CEO Dario Amodei has called a crisis of trust.

The Decoder offers OpenAI as the cautionary comparison. Its nonprofit foundation holds 26 percent of the for-profit and appoints the board, yet when that board fired Sam Altman in November 2023, he was back within five days and the board was gone. Oversight, it turns out, works until it is used. Anthropic's fund, being a company-owned LLC, probably will not face even that level of outside scrutiny.

Then came Elon Musk. He told Anthropic on X to give the shares directly to the public, and on Monday said he would give one SpaceX share, currently about $146, to each of roughly 72 million U.S. children next year. That comes to about $10.5 billion, and he called it the first of many. He did not explain how the distribution would work. The Decoder adds that Musk's xAI dropped its public benefit corporation status in 2024, which makes this his second position on corporate benevolence in two years.

Google, for its part, sticks to conventional philanthropy such as its AI Opportunity Fund, which teaches basic AI skills and makes no direct payments. Anthropic is therefore the only major lab to name direct payments as an option, though naming an option and exercising it are different activities. Watch for the governance details, the IPO filing, and whether anyone is ever actually handed a check.
