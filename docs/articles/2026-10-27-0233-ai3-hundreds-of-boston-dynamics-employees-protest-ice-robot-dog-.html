Boston Dynamics Staff Ask Company Not to Sell Robot Dogs to ICE; Company Declines

More than 300 employees signed a petition against a $1.3 million deal, but executives say the dogs will not be weaponized and the contract stands.

The wire said: ICE plan to buy robot dogs spurs protest from hundreds of Boston Dynamics employees · Ars Technica AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 27, 2026 02:33 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

In September, DHS awarded a $1.3 million contract to Connecticut firm Radeco to supply ICE with four modified Boston Dynamics Spot robots. Hundreds of Boston Dynamics employees then petitioned executives to halt the sale.

Why it matters

The dispute shows how little control engineers have over where their products end up once a reseller is involved. It also shows how a company's sales terms become its main ethical safeguard.

More than 300 employees at Boston Dynamics signed a petition asking executives to stop a sale of the company's robot dogs to US Immigration and Customs Enforcement, according to Ars Technica, which cited reporting by The Boston Globe and WBUR. Executives said no. The signatures, half of them anonymous, represent roughly one in five of the company's approximately 1,400 workers.

The contract, awarded on September 25, is worth $1.3 million and goes to Radeco, a Connecticut company, rather than to Boston Dynamics directly. Radeco modifies Spot robots and sells them as a platform called Cerberus. Translation: the company's robot is going to ICE through a middleman, which allows everyone involved to say that someone else is the vendor.

The contract covers four robots. It describes them as tools to detect radiation, toxic gas and explosive threats in urban environments or disaster zones. They come with an oxygen sensor for checking air in confined spaces such as tunnels, a camera with 30-times zoom, 360-degree cameras and infrared thermal imaging. An optional arm lets the robot open doors and handle objects, and the contract says it can run repeatable autonomous missions with minimal human intervention.

ICE's stated justification is support for Homeland Security Task Forces working against cross-border tunnels and human smuggling and trafficking networks. Boston Dynamics stressed that the robots would mainly be used by Homeland Security Investigations, the unit focused on transnational criminal organizations, according to WBUR. That is where the employees object. The Brennan Center for Justice has documented the unit as frequently involved in deportations and workplace raids during Trump's first term, and the Immigration Policy Tracking Project says thousands of its agents were diverted to immigration enforcement after a 2025 executive order redefined its mission.

The petition cites a September 4 raid at a Hyundai Motor Group battery and vehicle facility in Georgia, where 475 workers, mostly South Korean, were detained. Hyundai has owned Boston Dynamics since June 2021. The petition said employees could not be proud of work used by an agency that brutalizes civilians, including some of their own colleagues at Hyundai. Hyundai, for its part, has kept the Georgia plant running and still plans to deploy thousands of Boston Dynamics Atlas humanoid robots at its US facilities.

Management's answer was an email from Amanda McMaster, the company's former interim CEO, quoted by the Globe and WBUR. She wrote that the decision fits the company's purpose of ethically advancing intelligent machines in service of humanity, and urged staff to respect and accept it even if they disagree. Translation: the company has listened carefully and would like everyone to continue.

The company's main assurance is its terms of sale, which prohibit using Spot to harm or intimidate any person or animal, as a weapon, or to enable a weapon. A spokesperson told Ars there have been no cases of a customer violating them, and that a violation would bring consequences including voiding the warranty, refusing software updates, terminating the license, legal action and likely never selling to that customer again. Whether losing software updates is the deterrent one wants against an immigration agency is a question the company did not address.

The timing is awkward. Boston Dynamics is building a $100 million robotics and AI facility in Waltham, Massachusetts, that its press release says will create 1,250 jobs by 2033. A company that plans to hire that many people may eventually need a way to explain its customer list to them. For now, it has a warranty clause.
