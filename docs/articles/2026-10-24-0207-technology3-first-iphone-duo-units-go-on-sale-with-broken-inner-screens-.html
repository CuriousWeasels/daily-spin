First Folding iPhone Duo Reaches Customers, and a Few Screens Fold Early

Hours after the iPhone Duo went on sale, Reddit users posted photos of cracked inner displays near the hinge, while The Verge's review scored it 7 out of 10.

The wire said: The first broken iPhone Duos are here · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 24, 2026 02:07 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple's first folding phone, the iPhone Duo, went on sale this week after years of speculation. The Verge reviewed it and gave it a 7 out of 10.

Why it matters

Anyone considering a first-generation folding phone is about to learn whether the hinge survives ordinary use, which is the main thing a folding phone must do. Early failure reports are anecdotal, but they are the first real data on durability.

The iPhone Duo officially went on sale this week, and according to The Verge, a few units already have broken screens. Reddit posts about the affected phones describe a similar failure point: a problem on the inner display along the hinge, and bright lines across the right side of the screen. A folding phone has now been folded, and some of it has noticed.

One user in Australia described the experience in detail. They had owned the phone for about six hours and, by their own estimate, had opened it fewer than 20 times. While showing it to a friend, they felt something around the internal hinge beneath the screen, then heard an audible crack as the display broke along the hinge. They reported no marks or damage on the inner screen's surface or the external hinge that would suggest something had been caught.

Translation: this was not a drop, a pocket of loose change, or a toddler. It was the phone doing the single thing it was built to do. Twenty openings in six hours is also roughly the pace of a person who has just bought a folding phone and wants to show it to everyone they know, which is a use case Apple presumably anticipated.

The user said the Apple Store replaced the phone with what they called a brand-new one on the spot. That is the part of the story Apple will want repeated. A fast swap is good customer service, but it is also the standard first response when a company wants a problem to stay a single customer's problem.

The scale remains unclear. Another Reddit post showed an image of a second broken screen, with a comment containing a video of yet another, and a separate thread gathered more pictures. Apple did not immediately reply to The Verge's request for comment. A handful of Reddit posts is not a failure rate, and millions of phones will sell against a few cracked ones. It is, however, how hardware problems usually surface first.

Meanwhile, the reviewing class is still deciding what the Duo is. The Verge gave it 7 out of 10, and on its Vergecast, hosts David and Nilay debated whether that was right and whether the device would have been better received as an iPad rather than an iPhone. They also noted that Daring Fireball's John Gruber suggested the outer screen might be the worst iPhone ever. The Verge's own hosts said it is easy to see how the Duo will improve over time, though not whether it will ever be the phone for everyone.

That is the usual arc of a first-generation product: early adopters pay for the research, reviewers adjust their scores, and version two arrives with the hinge fixed. The open question is how many first-generation hinges fail between now and then, and how quickly the replacements keep coming.
