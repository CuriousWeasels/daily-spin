New Jersey Deputy Attorney General Arrested in AI Child Abuse Imagery Case, Detectives Say

Bucks County Detectives say a tip to a national clearinghouse, a Discord account and some ordinary internet records led them to a state prosecutor's home.

The wire said: “thegentlemanpervert” producing AI CSAM is a NJ prosecutor, say cops · Ars Technica

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 18, 2026 01:40 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Since at least 2024, US law enforcement has promised to pursue AI-generated child sexual abuse material, with the Justice Department stating that such material is still CSAM. On Thursday, Bucks County Detectives announced felony charges against Brian Richard Fitzgerald, a deputy attorney general in New Jersey.

Why it matters

The case shows that AI-generated abuse imagery built from photos of real children is being treated as a serious crime, and that old-fashioned tip lines and subscriber records can still unmask anonymous accounts. It also puts a law enforcement official on the wrong side of the kind of case his profession exists to bring.

Brian Richard Fitzgerald, 51, a New Jersey deputy attorney general, was arrested on felony charges related to AI-generated child sexual abuse material, Bucks County Detectives said in a press release Thursday, according to Ars Technica. Officials accused him of using AI to turn photos of real minors taken from social media into sexualized images, then distributing them on social media. These are allegations, and no court has ruled on them.

The case began with the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children, a nonprofit clearinghouse that receives reports of suspected child exploitation online. Translation: when a platform or user spots something illegal, this is where the report goes, and from there it is passed to police. According to the press release, the center received a tip that a Discord user had shared an illicit image in July.

Detectives then used what they described as digital forensics and subscriber records tied to the Discord account and associated Internet protocol addresses. Translation: an IP address is the numerical label your internet connection carries, and the company that assigned it knows which household it was assigned to. A username is an alias. A billing address is not.

Police say they traced the account to Fitzgerald's home and searched it Thursday. According to the release, they recovered prompts and scripts used to create artificially generated abuse material with an AI program. They also reportedly found Discord chat logs in which a user named "thegentlemanpervert_2160_07833" allegedly admitted making multiple illicit images with AI and sharing them with others.

The legal backdrop is not new. In 2024 the Department of Justice made one of its earliest arrests in this area, charging a Wisconsin man with spreading thousands of realistic images of prepubescent minors across Telegram and Instagram. The department then told law enforcement that AI-generated CSAM is still CSAM, a sentence that exists because some people evidently hoped the word artificial would work as a legal defense.

The images in this case are alleged to have been built from photographs of real minors, which is the part that removes any argument that nobody was involved. The technology changes how the material is produced. It does not change who is harmed by it, or who has to be protected from it.

What happens next is the usual slow machinery of a criminal case: charges, court appearances and, eventually, a plea or a trial. It will be watched for an additional reason. The accused works for the office that supervises law enforcement in the state, and the same office will presumably need to explain how it handles the situation.
