Musk Says Ukraine Broke Starlink Rules; Ukraine Says Rules Already Said No

SpaceX capped Starlink speeds on drones, Musk accused Kyiv of using the service for strikes, and Zelenskyy says the new limits have been lifted.

The wire said: Musk accuses Ukraine of violating Starlink terms with deadly drone strikes · Ars Technica

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 27, 2026 02:33 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Ukraine's military relies heavily on Starlink, SpaceX's satellite internet service, for communications. A restriction on drone speeds sparked a dispute that began almost two weeks before this weekend, according to Ars Technica.

Why it matters

A private company's terms of service can shape how a wartime military communicates, and the company's owner can change them in public on a Saturday. That is a procurement risk for any government that depends on a commercial network.

Elon Musk said on Saturday that Ukraine's military violated Starlink's terms of service by using the civilian network for offensive drone strikes, Ars Technica reported. The accusation followed a SpaceX restriction that stops Starlink from working on drones flying above certain speeds. Translation: the company installed a speedometer on a war and is now arguing about who was speeding.

Musk wrote that using Starlink, a civilian commercial service, to kill people "has never been allowed, nor should it be." He drew a line between Starlink and Starshield, a different system he said is controlled by the US government and is up to Washington. He added that the loophole was closed last week, which is an unusual thing to say about a rule he also says always existed.

Ukrainian officials disagreed. Serhii Beskrestnov, a defense technology advisor to President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, wrote on Telegram on Sunday that all Starlink terminals over Ukrainian territory, including Russian-occupied areas, already had a movement speed limit of 70 to 90 kilometers per hour. He added that Starlink does not function over Russian territory. The article's own subhead quotes a claim of something being "physically impossible."

One current and one former Ukrainian official also told Ars Technica that earlier SpaceX limits already prevented the military from using Starlink the way Musk described. Separately, Ukrainian officials have accused Musk of throttling their use of Starshield, the military variant of Starlink that Ukraine accesses through a deal with the US government. Translation: throttling means deliberately slowing a connection, which is a harder sell to a customer when the customer is a country at war.

Zelenskyy said over the weekend that the new satellite restrictions have been lifted, possibly ending the controversy. The sources do not say what changed or whether SpaceX has confirmed it. Musk and Ukraine therefore agree the restriction existed, disagree on whether it was necessary, and now seem to agree it is gone.

What to watch next is whether SpaceX confirms the lifting of the limits, and whether the Starlink-versus-Starshield distinction Musk drew holds up in practice. Until then, one of the most important communications networks in the war depends on a terms-of-service document that its owner reads aloud when he feels like it.
