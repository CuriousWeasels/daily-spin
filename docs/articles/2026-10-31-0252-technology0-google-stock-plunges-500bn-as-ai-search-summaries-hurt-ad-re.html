Google's AI Answers Questions So Well Nobody Clicks the Ads; Investors Lose $500bn

Alphabet fell 12% after admitting its AI search is eating its ad business, and by Friday Intel, Reddit and Amazon had joined the sell-off.

The wire said: Google value plunges by $500bn as its own AI search hits ad sales · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 31, 2026 02:52 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Big tech companies are spending hundreds of billions of dollars on AI infrastructure. This week's earnings reports from Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft and Intel gave investors their first sustained look at what that spending is doing to the businesses that pay for it.

Why it matters

Index funds and pension funds hold these companies, so a wobble in AI spending confidence reaches ordinary savers. The question of whether AI earns back its cost, or merely replaces products that already made money, will shape tech valuations and jobs.

Alphabet lost just over $500bn in market value on Thursday, according to the BBC, after saying its AI summaries were cutting into the search advertising revenue that has long paid for most of Google. Its shares fell more than 12% to a six-month low, taking its market value from $3.97 trillion to $3.46 trillion. Put plainly, Google built a product that answers questions so well people stop clicking on the ads beside the answers, and the market noticed.

The numbers were not bad in the ordinary sense. Revenue rose 25% to $127.6bn in the quarter to September. The trouble is that analysts had predicted more, especially in advertising. Translation: the company grew very fast, just not as fast as people who had already spent the money in their heads.

Philipp Schindler, Google's chief business officer, said the company's AI mode was eating into ad money, yet he felt "very good about our trajectory". Kim Scott, a former head of Google's advertising business, told the BBC the company would rather eat its own lunch than let someone else eat it. This is the corporate version of burning down your own house to stop a rival from buying it.

Alphabet expects to spend up to $205bn this year, almost all of it on AI, the BBC reported. Forrester analyst Tracy Woo said the results highlight the challenge of funding massive AI investments while keeping established businesses strong. That is analyst language for paying for a new restaurant with the profits of the old one, while the new one is open next door and gives away the food.

Amazon offered a more vivid example. Sales grew 11% to $201bn, but free cash flow came in at negative $42bn, meaning it spent far more than it brought in, with spending up six-fold in three months. Translation: the company is borrowing against the future. Chief executive Andy Jassy said two businesses could each reach $1 trillion, and conceded it "would be more convenient" if the spending were spread over 25 years. "That's not the world we live in," he added. Amazon has committed to $220bn this year, and its shares fell about 6% in after-hours trading before recovering to a 1.4% decline on Friday.

Microsoft got the gentlest treatment. Shares slipped less than 1% after revenue rose 19% to $92.3bn, cloud revenue grew 30%, and AI-related investment was about $5bn lower than before. It also announced plans to cut thousands of workers. Usually lower spending and rising sales earn applause, so the muted reaction shows investors have shifted from asking whether AI is being bought to asking who ends up keeping the money.

On Friday the damage spread. Intel fell 28%, shedding more than $130bn, after saying it would not generate more cash than it spends this year and forecasting weak fourth-quarter revenue. Its finance chief, David Zinsner, expects the PC slowdown to last "at least through the first half of next year", yet Intel plans to spend more on equipment. Reddit fell 29% after warning that Google's AI-driven search was hurting referral traffic, and Roblox fell 18% as daily users dropped. The Nasdaq closed 2.8% lower and ended October with its worst monthly slide since March 2025.

The BBC noted that Meta, Alphabet, Oracle, Amazon and Microsoft have committed to roughly $800bn of spending between them in 2026. XTB's Kathleen Brookes said investors "have turned ruthless" toward any earnings disappointment. Context helps: the Nasdaq is still up 7.8% over the past year, and Amazon is up nearly 8%, while Alphabet is down 1.3%. Brookes also noted that November is typically a strong month for stocks, so the verdict on whether this is a bubble or a bad week arrives soon.
