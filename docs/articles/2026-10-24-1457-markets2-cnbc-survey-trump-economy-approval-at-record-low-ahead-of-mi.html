Voters Rank Corruption Above Groceries, Which Is Saying Something at Current Grocery Prices

CNBC's final pre-midterm survey finds Trump's economic approval at a record low of 34% and corruption narrowly topping food costs as the leading worry.

The wire said: Trump's approval rating on economy hits all-time low as midterm looms: CNBC survey · CNBC Economy

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 24, 2026 14:57 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

CNBC's All-America Economic Survey, run jointly by a Democratic and a Republican pollster, polled 1,000 registered voters Oct. 14-18, less than two weeks before the Nov. 3 midterm elections. It follows months of Democratic efforts to tie economic frustration to allegations of corruption.

Why it matters

Economic mood is a major driver of midterm results, and the poll suggests Republicans are losing ground even with their own voters. With the Fed funds rate at 3.88% and inflation at 3.74%, the cost-of-living complaints behind that mood have not gone away.

CNBC reported Thursday that President Donald Trump's approval rating on the economy has reached its lowest point in either of his terms: 34% approve and 65% disapprove, for a net of minus 31. Eight in 10 Americans rate the economy as fair or poor. Translation: net approval is approval minus disapproval, so a president can be unpopular in a way that fits on a single line of arithmetic.

The drop is sharp. In July, 38% approved and 60% disapproved, so the president lost 4 points of approval and gained 5 of disapproval in three months. By CNBC's account, the 65% disapproval figure is the highest for any president the survey has measured going back to Barack Obama. It is also 1 point worse than Joe Biden's worst showing, a record nobody campaigns on.

His overall approval is steadier, at 40% approve and 59% disapprove. Net approval is minus 19, compared with minus 8 a year ago. So voters are not newly unhappy with the president in general, only with the part of the job that involves their money.

The more interesting movement is inside his own party. Overall, 63% of the public is pessimistic about the economy, up 1 point from July, but that includes 35% of Republicans, up 6 points and the highest Republican pessimism of either Trump term. Micah Roberts, the Republican pollster on the survey, said the shift came "because Republicans have" soured, not independents or Democrats. When the Republican pollster is the one delivering that line, it is not a rumor from the other side.

Voters were also asked what worries them most. Government corruption came first at 36%, followed by food and groceries at 34%, and protecting democracy and gas prices at 23% each. CNBC noted that the survey splits cost-of-living concerns into separate items. Translation: if you cut the cost of living into three slices, no single slice wins, but together food, gas and housing are the top concern for 57%. Prices are the main worry whichever way you count them.

Corruption's rise matches other polls CNBC cited. A Washington Post-ABC News-Ipsos poll found about two-thirds think Trump is using the presidency to enrich himself. A Fox News poll found 60% extremely concerned about government corruption. Both parties have noticed. Trump said Tuesday his administration has waged an "unprecedented war on corruption and fraud," and a task force led by Vice President JD Vance targets fraud. Democrats, though, are the ones with a pitch linking high costs to Washington's behavior.

Part of that pitch is plain. About 93% of respondents say elected officials should not be allowed to personally own or trade individual stocks in companies with federal contracts, and 89% say companies tied to a sitting president's immediate family should not receive federal contracts. These numbers are unusually bipartisan for 2026, which suggests the idea is popular precisely because nobody on the ballot is proposing it.

The scoreboard is not a landslide. Voters prefer Democratic control of Congress 52% to 45%, a 7-point edge and a 3-point gain since July. On who handles corruption better, Democrats lead only 35% to 31%, and 41% of independents think neither party would do a good job. Both pollsters were cautious: Roberts called it a contest between "the map and momentum," and the Democratic pollster, Jay Campbell, sees a return to the mean rather than a realignment.

The backdrop is not helping incumbents. CPI inflation was 3.74% year over year in September's reading, Brent crude sits at $115.22, and 80% of poll respondents say prices are rising. Only 46% now say the Iran war was worth it to disrupt Iran's nuclear program, down from 53% in April. Eleven days remain to change minds, and groceries are not cooperating.
