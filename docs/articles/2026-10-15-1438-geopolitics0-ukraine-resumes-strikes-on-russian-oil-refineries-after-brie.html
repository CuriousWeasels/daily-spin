Ukraine Ends Refinery Pause After Truce Only One Side Seemed to Notice

Kyiv says its three-day restraint on Russian oil targets is over, citing continued Russian strikes and a US diesel deal it calls investment in Moscow's war.

The wire said: Ukraine resumes attacks on Russian oil refineries, officials say, after brief truce · NPR World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 15, 2026 14:38 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Donald Trump announced an "energy ceasefire" on Sunday, and Volodymyr Zelensky said Ukraine would honor it if Russia did. The Kremlin never said it agreed, and the two sides have since traded accusations.

Why it matters

Fuel prices, US midterm politics and Ukraine's air defenses are now tangled together, so a drone strike on a refinery south of Moscow touches the cost of diesel far beyond the battlefield. Ukraine's access to US intelligence and Starlink, if truly used as leverage, affects how well it can defend its cities.

Ukrainian drones struck a refinery in Russia's Ryazan region early Thursday, hours after Zelensky said a three-day pause on such attacks was over. NPR reported the strike. Russia's defence ministry said four missiles and dozens of drones were intercepted and that falling debris started a fire at the site. A ceasefire that lasts three days and binds one party is, strictly speaking, a favor.

Zelensky told the Financial Times that he restricted attacks at the request of the US while Russia kept hitting Ukrainian energy infrastructure. "We're done," he said. The BBC noted that the Kremlin never said it agreed to the deal, which explains why it was so hard to violate. Zelensky said strikes since the announcement left hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians without power.

Then there is the diesel. Last week Russia and the US agreed to release 300,000 tonnes of Russian diesel to the American and global market. Translation: Washington wants cheaper fuel before the midterm elections, and Russia happens to sell some. The BBC said the Iran war pushed up petrol and especially diesel prices and hurt Trump's standing with voters. Al Jazeera reported that Russia says its diesel has begun reaching global markets, and that Ukraine's strike contradicts US-brokered efforts.

Zelensky called the deal the US effectively investing in Russia's war effort. "Maybe it will be gas. Definitely it will be gas," he said of the next one. Kyiv argues refinery strikes are vital to cutting the revenue that funds Russia's military, so a deal that restores some of that revenue looks to Ukraine like a conflict of interest with a gas pump attached.

The more alarming allegation concerns support. Zelensky said US counterparts told Ukrainian officials: "if you will not stop [the strikes], we will block intelligence." Ukraine relies on US early warnings of incoming aerial attacks to organize air defense. Trump denied halting intelligence-sharing, according to the BBC.

Starlink adds a second dispute. On Tuesday a speed limit hit drones using the satellite service, and some suggested a technical glitch. Elon Musk appeared to deny that, pointing to the Trump administration, per the BBC. Zelensky said: "It was not a technical problem, it was the will." Ukrainian defence ministry sources told the BBC that Starshield's operation on the move had been restored and that officials were still trying to establish why the restrictions occurred. The BBC has asked the White House and the Defense Department for comment.

None of this paused the war itself. Zelensky said more than 850 drones were launched at dozens of Ukrainian cities in the past day, and local officials reported at least 13 deaths across the country. About 360 drones hit Kirovohrad in a 10-hour barrage, its largest attack since the 2022 invasion, with at least three killed there. Zelensky said Russia exploited eased sanctions to escalate.

Zelensky now says he will halt oil strikes only if the US supplies more Patriot missiles, which are scarce because the Iran war consumed stockpiles. Britain's prime minister, Andy Burnham, announced faster delivery of lightweight multirole missiles, and European leaders meet in Brussels on Thursday. The price of the pause has been set: one missile type, delivered, rather than one ceasefire, announced.
