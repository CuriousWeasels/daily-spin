Serbia Raises Terror Alert to Red After Knife Attack at Israeli Embassy

Police shot dead a minor who attacked a guard in Belgrade, and the interior minister said the attacker was already on security services' radar.

The wire said: Serbian police shoot dead assailant at Israeli Embassy · DW

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 28, 2026 17:28 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Serbian police guard the Israeli embassy in Belgrade, which was attacked once before, in June 2024. The latest incident came overnight into Wednesday, days after a Serbian parliamentary election and a day after Israel's own vote.

Why it matters

Serbia has moved to its highest terrorism threat level, so residents should expect tighter checks and more police at infrastructure sites and crowded places. The case also raises the old question of what it means for someone to be monitored and still reach a guard post with knives.

A man armed with knives attacked a police guard outside the Israeli embassy in Belgrade overnight into Wednesday, and the guard shot him dead, according to Serbian Interior Minister Ivica Dacic. DW and the BBC both reported that the officer suffered a minor wound. The BBC said it was to the face, and that the officer did not need hospital care.

Dacic described the attacker as a minor and a Serbian citizen. According to DW, he was believed to come from the western town of Sabac. The BBC reported that the suspect had not yet been formally identified. Dacic said the young man shouted "Allahu Akbar" during the attack, and that police are treating the episode as terrorism.

The minister also said security services had been monitoring the attacker as a known supporter of Islamic State. As evidence, he cited an Instagram account that displayed the group's black flag. Translation: the authorities knew who he was, which is the sort of fact that is reassuring in a briefing and less so in a postmortem. Neither source says what the monitoring involved or how the attacker came to be outside the embassy.

Israel's Foreign Ministry confirmed what it called an attempted terrorist attack. It said no embassy personnel were hurt. The BBC added that the mission was closed at the time, which meant the only people on the receiving end were the police officers whose job is to stand there.

Serbia responded by raising its terrorism threat level to "red alert," the highest on its scale. Dacic said this means tighter security checks and a larger police presence at sensitive sites, including infrastructure and places where large crowds gather. Translation: more officers and more bag checks, possibly in places you were already going.

The incident echoes one from June 2024, when an attacker with a crossbow seriously wounded a police officer guarding the same embassy. The BBC says the officer was shot in the neck, survived, and shot the attacker dead. DW described that attacker as a 25-year-old Islamic convert from a village near the capital who had been radicalized. Taken together, the two attacks in under three years suggest the embassy's guards are performing the same duty at the same address, and facing the same risks.

The timing was awkward in the usual way. DW noted that the attack came days after a key early parliamentary election in Serbia and a day after Israel's vote, which has no clear result yet. Neither source links the attack to either election, and officials have not said that any connection exists. What to watch next is the investigation into how a monitored teenager got within knife range of a guarded building.
