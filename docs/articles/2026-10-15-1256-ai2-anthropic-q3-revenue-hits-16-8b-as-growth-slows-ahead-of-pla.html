Anthropic Posts $16.8 Billion Quarter, Investors Wonder Why It Isn't Bigger

The AI lab is profitable on an adjusted basis and growing 45 percent a quarter, yet bankers are reportedly trimming IPO hopes from $2 trillion to under $1.5 trillion.

The wire said: Anthropic's Q3 revenue hits $16.8B but growth slows ahead of planned November IPO · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 15, 2026 12:56 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Anthropic, maker of the Claude models, plans to go public in November, with a private valuation that hit $965 billion in May. It shared third-quarter figures with prospective investors at a private meeting in San Francisco, according to US media reports cited by The Decoder.

Why it matters

Anthropic's listing is billed as the biggest test yet of whether public markets will pay AI-boom prices. The result will shape how much money flows to AI companies and how exposed ordinary investors become to them.

Anthropic told prospective investors that its third-quarter revenue reached $16.8 billion, according to multiple US media reports cited by The Decoder. That is roughly 45 percent above the second quarter and more than 3.6 times the company's entire 2025 revenue. Some investors were reportedly disappointed anyway, which is one way to describe the experience of growing 45 percent in three months.

The disappointment is about the second derivative. Translation: nobody is upset about the speed, only that it is no longer increasing. In the second quarter, demand for the coding tool Claude Code had pushed revenue up more than 140 percent over Q1. The Decoder offers several explanations for the cooldown: a summer lull, when people work less and use fewer tokens (the small units of text AI models are billed by), and a strong September rival from OpenAI called Astra. Anthropic reportedly countered only in late September with Opus 5.5.

The profit picture is better than the growth picture. The Information reported an adjusted operating margin of 11 percent for the year through Q3. Translation: profit after leaving out the expensive bits, such as stock-based compensation, which is the part of pay that arrives as shares. The Financial Times reported it was the second straight quarter of adjusted profitability, and that the company is cash-flow positive, earning $1.70 in revenue for every dollar spent on compute, the computing power that runs the models.

The Decoder calls this good news for the whole industry, since it suggests trained models can make money despite months of price wars. The caveats are the ones listed in the same report: steep training and staff costs, and the risk that open-weight alternatives, whose trained parameters anyone can download, start replacing proprietary models like Claude.

Then there is distillation. Translation: using a company's AI outputs to train your own model. Anthropic estimates that 5 to 7 percent of its revenue comes from it. The company has repeatedly accused Chinese labs of copying its models this way, and has now rolled out effective countermeasures. The Decoder notes this raises the question of why it lobbied so long against distillation if it could have stopped it all along, a question that is easier to ask when the customers in question are paying.

Anthropic told investors that distillation revenue will probably fall and weigh on the current quarter. The long-term bet is that blocking it makes it harder for rivals to match Claude. Put plainly, the company is choosing to shrink a revenue line slightly now in order to keep its product from being cheaply cloned later, and has disclosed this just before asking the public to buy shares.

The valuation math is where the mood shifts. Anthropic was worth under $20 billion two years ago and $965 billion privately in May. Some bankers floated a $2 trillion IPO, but several investors now reportedly expect advisers to lower expectations to possibly under $1.5 trillion. The company did not discuss its September annualized revenue rate, the current monthly pace multiplied by twelve, or offer a full-year outlook. In July that rate was reportedly $65 billion.

The backdrop is not generous. The Decoder lists high inflation, rising borrowing costs, public sentiment toward AI that is more negative than positive, unsolved cybersecurity risks, and a months-long price war with OpenAI and Google while open models close the performance gap. The IPO is set for November, before Thanksgiving, according to The Information.

The consolation is that people will almost certainly use more AI rather than less. Whether that is worth $2 trillion, $1.5 trillion, or something less will be settled the old-fashioned way, by strangers with brokerage accounts.
