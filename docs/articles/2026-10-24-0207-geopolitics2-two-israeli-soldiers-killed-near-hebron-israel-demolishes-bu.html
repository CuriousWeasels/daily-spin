Israel Begins Demolitions in Hebron After Deadly Attack, Four Days Before Election

Netanyahu vows to 'destroy' the neighborhood the two gunmen came from, as a campaign built on security meets a checkpoint shooting that killed two soldiers.

The wire said: Israeli forces begin demolishing Palestinian buildings in Hebron after two Israeli soldiers shot dead · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 24, 2026 02:07 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Two Israeli soldiers were killed and three wounded at a checkpoint outside Hebron in the occupied West Bank on Friday, according to the BBC. The two Palestinian gunmen were shot dead, and Israel votes in four days.

Why it matters

Demolitions and cordons around a city of about 200,000 people affect civilians who had no part in the attack, and a larger West Bank operation could widen a conflict already pushing oil prices higher.

The Israeli military has begun demolishing Palestinian buildings in Hebron after two Israeli soldiers were killed at a checkpoint outside the city, the BBC reported. Three other soldiers were wounded before the two gunmen were shot dead. The attack came four days before a general election, a detail that has not gone unnoticed by anyone involved.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu said the military would "destroy" the neighbourhood the attackers came from and said Israel was ready for "extensive action" in the West Bank. He also said he had reviewed plans for the largest military operation there in decades, though he did not say when it might happen. Al Jazeera reported that Israeli forces launched what the military called a "targeted and thorough" operation in Hebron. Translation: the word "targeted" is doing a great deal of work next to a plan to transform an entire neighbourhood.

Demolishing the homes of suspected attackers is a long-standing Israeli practice. Israeli rights groups have criticised it as collective punishment. Translation: the building is punished for something that the people living in it, in many cases, did not do. Netanyahu likened the plan to earlier operations in West Bank refugee camps, where residential buildings were demolished and thousands were displaced.

He also said he had imposed cordons around Hebron, which has about 200,000 residents, and the nearby city of Halhul. NPR reported that Israel ordered a closure of the West Bank. Translation: a cordon is a polite word for a ring of soldiers around people who are expected to carry on with their day.

The politics sit in plain view. Netanyahu has built his campaign on a promise of security, and his Likud party cancelled Friday morning campaign events. Finance Minister Bezalel Smotrich called for a West Bank campaign "exactly like in Gaza or Lebanon," while National Security Minister Itamar Ben Gvir called for dismantling the Palestinian Authority. Both are members of the government, which is the sort of detail that makes the word "proposal" feel optimistic.

The opposition was drawn in as well. On Thursday, Foreign Minister Israel Katz accused opposition leaders of undermining the government by criticising Netanyahu's warnings of an attack, saying they would be "responsible" for any future attack. Opposition chiefs, including frontrunner Gadi Eisenkot and former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett, offered condolences and praised those who killed the attackers. That is a hard position to attack, though it has not stopped anyone from trying.

The military said it found a large quantity of ammunition in the attackers' vehicle. It believes they planned a much larger attack on nearby Route 35 but ran into the checkpoint and opened fire there instead. The BBC said verified footage shows an intense gun battle.

The backdrop is grim and was grim before Friday. The UN says at least 96 Palestinians have been killed by Israeli forces or settlers in the West Bank since the start of the year, and four Israelis have been killed by Palestinians there, not counting Friday's attack. Extremist settlers have also been targeting Palestinians during the olive harvest, according to the BBC. Al Jazeera separately reported Brent crude at $117.30 a barrel, a four-year high, amid fears of a resumption of the US-Israeli war on Iran.

What to watch is whether the larger operation Netanyahu described arrives before or after Tuesday's vote. Voters will then decide whether a security promise made in a campaign still counts as one once the campaign is over.
