Drones Hit St Petersburg Oil Depots Days After Trump's 'Energy Ceasefire' Meets Energy

Ukraine resumed strikes on Russian oil facilities, killing at least one person in St Petersburg, while Washington buys Russian diesel and Moscow says it downed 1,000 drones.

The wire said: Oil facilities hit in deadly overnight attack on St Petersburg · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 18, 2026 14:56 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Donald Trump declared an "energy ceasefire" last week, and Ukraine paused strikes on Russian oil sites for three days while accusing Russia of carrying on. Kyiv resumed attacks earlier this week, annoying Trump, who wants a truce to support a diesel supply deal with Vladimir Putin.

Why it matters

Fuel prices, sanctions policy and winter heating in Ukraine are now tangled in the same war, and the US midterms are part of the arithmetic. Civilians on both sides bear the cost of attacks on energy infrastructure as winter approaches.

An overnight attack on St Petersburg hit two of the city's oil facilities and killed at least one person, the BBC reported. Governor Alexander Beglov said fires were being put out at the St Petersburg Oil Terminal and the Ruchyi oil depot, and that at least five people were injured. Kyiv has not commented, which in this war is a form of commentary in itself.

The strikes also reached commercial targets. A logistics centre belonging to Ozon, which the BBC describes as Russia's Amazon, caught fire, and the company said staff were evacuated and nobody was hurt. It added that it was not accepting seller shipments there because the site was "currently serving other operational purposes". Translation: the warehouse is on fire, but the press release must be written by someone who has met a lawyer.

A regional data centre of VKontakte, the Facebook equivalent, was also targeted by a drone. VK said its services were available "as usual", though users reported outages in the early hours. Beglov said 12 apartment buildings and two homes were damaged, Pulkovo Airport was temporarily closed, and mobile internet might be disrupted. Russia's defence ministry said it shot down more than 1,000 Ukrainian drones overnight into Sunday.

The reason Ukraine hits oil is simple: oil is the main revenue stream for Moscow's war effort, according to Kyiv. Ukraine paused those strikes for three days after Trump's announcement, while saying Russia kept hitting its energy sector. President Volodymyr Zelensky said Russia had targeted Ukrainian energy 76 times since the declaration. A ceasefire in which one side checks the other's compliance by counting incoming drones is a demanding format.

Resuming the strikes drew Trump's ire. He wants a truce to facilitate a deal he struck with Putin to supply diesel, part of an effort to bring down US energy prices that have skyrocketed during the Iran war, ahead of the midterm elections, the BBC reported. Zelensky suggested the arrangement amounted to the US investing in Russia's war effort. Trump has also temporarily suspended a raft of sanctions on Russia, which has drawn criticism, including from some US Republicans.

The result is a strange symmetry. Washington wants Russian fuel to flow, Kyiv wants it to stop flowing, and Moscow would like the money either way. Meanwhile each side is hitting the other's energy infrastructure with increasing intensity before winter, as the full-scale invasion enters its fifth year.

The human cost sits mostly on the Ukrainian side of the ledger. The UN noted on Friday that the first two weeks of October were the deadliest for Ukrainian civilians in years. Zelensky said Russia launched nearly 2,700 drones, 1,700 aerial bombs and 66 missiles in the past week, mostly at energy facilities, and that tens of thousands of people remain without power. Russia says it targets sites used by Ukraine's military.

What to watch next is whether the energy ceasefire ever becomes more than a press-release category. At present it is the only ceasefire in history that has been declared, suspended, resumed and violated before anyone could locate the paperwork.
