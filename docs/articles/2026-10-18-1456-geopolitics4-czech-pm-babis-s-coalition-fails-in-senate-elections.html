Czech Prime Minister Wins Zero of 27 Senate Seats, Calls It Catastrophic

Andrej Babis's ruling coalition was shut out of Saturday's Senate vote, leaving opposition parties in control of the upper house and his broadcaster overhaul facing a tougher road.

The wire said: Populist Czech PM Babis goes empty-handed in Senate elections · DW

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 18, 2026 14:56 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Babis, a populist billionaire, returned as Czech prime minister after winning parliamentary elections in October 2025. The Senate vote was his first electoral test since then.

Why it matters

The Senate cannot block most ordinary legislation, but it has a big say over constitutional amendments and Constitutional Court judges, so an opposition-run chamber limits how far the government can reshape the country's institutions.

The ruling coalition of Czech Prime Minister Andrej Babis did not win a single seat in Saturday's Senate elections, according to DW. Candidates tied to opposition parties took 22 of the 27 seats on offer, and the pro-Western opposition keeps control of the 81-member upper house. It is a rare case of a governing party running in an election and finding, on the evidence, that voters had been paying attention.

Babis's centrist ANO movement (the name means YES) did not add anything to its tally, leaving it with 14 senators. Translation: one third of the Senate is renewed at each election, so the seats that were up for grabs were only part of the picture. The opposition did not need to conquer the chamber, only to keep it, and it managed that with room to spare.

Babis himself called the result "catastrophic." He also said, in DW's account, that nobody in his camp had expected to win nothing at all, which is a sentence that works well as both a concession and a polling critique. He then vowed to carry on with the government's agenda regardless.

That agenda includes an unpopular plan to revamp funding for the country's public broadcasters. Translation: the government wants to change how the outlets that cover the government are paid for. DW describes the plan as unpopular, which may help explain why the voters who were asked about the government on Saturday answered the way they did, though the report does not say how much the plan drove the outcome.

The coalition, which DW describes as euroskeptic, has also ended direct military support for Ukraine as it defends itself against Russia's invasion, and has opposed European Union climate policies. Those are positions the opposition parties, which are pro-Western, are now better placed to argue against from the chamber they control.

The Senate is the junior partner in Czech lawmaking. The lower house does the main legislating, and it remains in the hands of Babis after his October 2025 victory. But the Senate has a big say in constitutional amendments and in approving Constitutional Court judges, which are the two tools a government needs if it wants to rewrite the rules rather than merely follow them.

So the practical picture is this: Babis keeps the government, keeps the agenda, and has promised to proceed. What he has lost is the ability to assume the upper house will go along with the parts that matter most. Watch for the broadcaster plan and any judicial appointments, where the new arithmetic will be tested first.
