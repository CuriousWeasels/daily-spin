Apple's Foldable Iphone Duo Cracks in Same Spot, Teardowns Suggest Hinge

Two separate teardowns point to a complex hinge as the likely culprit behind broken Duo screens, while Apple has yet to comment.

The wire said: A YouTuber dissected an iPhone Duo and thinks he found the cause of crackgate · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 26, 2026 01:55 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple's new foldable, the iPhone Duo, has drawn reports on social media and a dedicated subreddit of screens cracking, sometimes within hours of purchase. Two teardowns, one by a YouTuber and one by iFixit, have now offered theories about why.

Why it matters

Anyone paying foldable prices should know that the most delicate part of the phone is also the part that moves, and that repairing a folding display is, per iFixit, an expensive nightmare. If the hinge is the problem, the fix may involve more than a software update.

Reports of the iPhone Duo's screen breaking have been piling up on social media and the iPhone Duo subreddit, according to The Verge. Some owners say they had the phone for only a few hours before the hinge seemed to catch on something, made an audible crack, and broke the display. The cracks reportedly appear in the same place each time, which is the kind of consistency engineers call a pattern and customers call a refund request.

YouTuber CuriousHagi managed to reproduce the broken screen during durability testing, then took the phone apart to find out why. The Verge reported that the Duo's hinge is quite complex, with a gear-like central hinge and two different types of linking hinges. Translation: there are a great many small moving parts between you and a working screen, and each one has to cooperate.

The part that failed was one of the links in the middle of the phone. It is thinner than the links above and below it, and it snapped at a small notch, which is exactly where a thin part with a notch would be expected to give up. He also found marks where the hinge meets the support plate, suggesting a place where it may have gotten caught. A weak link, in this case, was a literal description.

iFixit, the repair site that dissects each new phone, arrived at a related but not identical theory. It suspects part of the hinge sometimes fails to descend properly. It also noted an accordion cable that passes over that same spot near the edge of the battery, which could concentrate pressure and produce the audible crack. So the sources agree on the location and disagree, politely, on the exact villain.

The teardown was not all bad news for Apple. iFixit gave the Duo a repairability score of 5 out of 10, which The Verge notes is the best the site has given a foldable. Translation: it is the most fixable of a category that is generally not designed to be fixed. The matte nano-texture cover on the inner screen is relatively easy to replace without touching the OLED underneath, though repairing the display itself remains, in iFixit's assessment, an expensive nightmare.

There were also some design details worth admiring. According to iFixit, the nano-texture cover scatters reflections to mask the crease, and the display's oval subpixels, arranged at various angles, are believed to hide it further. Dust did get into the USB-C port, speaker grills, and hinge, making it a bit crunchy, though less glow dust got in than with the Z Fold8 or Pixel 11 Pro Fold. Apple has done an impressive job concealing the crease, and apparently an inconsistent one concealing the crack.

Apple did not immediately return a request for comment. The open question is whether the failures are a manufacturing variance or a design flaw, and only Apple knows how many units are affected. Until it says something, owners can treat the hinge with the care normally reserved for a sleeping cat.
