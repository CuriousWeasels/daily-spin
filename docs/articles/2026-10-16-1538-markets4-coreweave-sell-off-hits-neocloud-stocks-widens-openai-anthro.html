CoreWeave Slides, Taking OpenAI's Unlisted Price Tag Down With It, While Anthropic's Rises

Perpetual futures on a crypto exchange show OpenAI slipping under $1.5 trillion and Anthropic near $2 trillion, as the GPU landlord CoreWeave hits a one-year low.

The wire said: Here's how the CoreWeave sell-off is affecting OpenAI vs. Anthropic valuations · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 16, 2026 15:38 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

OpenAI and Anthropic are private, so nobody can buy their shares on an exchange. Traders on the crypto platform Hyperliquid have instead built futures contracts that guess what each company is worth.

Why it matters

These contracts are tiny and informal, but they show how closely AI valuations are tied to the debt-funded data center businesses that supply the computing power. If the landlord struggles, its biggest tenants start to look a little less invincible.

CNBC reported that OpenAI's implied valuation fell more than 4% in 24 hours, briefly dipping under $1.5 trillion, according to perpetual futures pricing on the decentralized exchange Hyperliquid. Anthropic's contracts rose 2.4% over the same period and imply a valuation just over $2 trillion. In other words, a company that has not sold a share to the public is being repriced every few minutes by people who cannot own it.

Translation: a perpetual future is a bet that never expires. Traders go long or short, and a funding rate keeps the contract price near a reference "oracle" price. For private companies, CNBC said, that oracle estimates value from things like private funding rounds and secondary market transactions, which is to say, from the last few times anyone actually bought a piece of the company.

The market is also small. Open interest, the total of long and short positions, is more than $41 million for Anthropic and under $7 million for OpenAI, per CNBC. For scale, a valuation in the trillions is being signaled by positions that would not cover the catering budget of a mid-sized data center. The listings come from a deployer called Entropy IO, not TradeXYZ, the platform's most prominent developer, which David Schamis of Hyperliquid Strategies said typically waits for public S-1 filings.

Schamis said liquidity should deepen once TradeXYZ lists these companies, and that such contracts generally trade at a premium to the IPO price but predict post-IPO levels well. That is a reassuring claim about a product whose subjects have not yet gone public, and so cannot yet be checked.

The reason OpenAI fell more is CoreWeave, which CNBC describes as a GPU rent-a-center. Its shares touched a new one-year low on Thursday, extending a 54% decline over that period, as worries grow about how it finances itself with debt. Its OpenAI relationship dates further back and was worth at least $22 billion as of September 2025, including a five-year agreement for data centers and technology.

Anthropic is also a CoreWeave customer. The company announced in April a deal to power Claude products, though it did not disclose the value. So Anthropic has exposure to the same landlord, just with less paperwork showing how much. Both companies' contracts remain down roughly 15% from last month's highs, according to Hyperliquid pricing.

The broader backdrop is a debt story. An Investing.com roundup framed CoreWeave's stress as a test of private credit's AI financing boom, meaning the lenders who funded the building of all this computing capacity are now watching one of its borrowers. The sources do not detail the terms, so the extent of the risk is unclear.

What to watch: whether CoreWeave's debt worries spread to its lenders, and whether the two trillion-dollar valuations survive contact with an actual stock exchange.
