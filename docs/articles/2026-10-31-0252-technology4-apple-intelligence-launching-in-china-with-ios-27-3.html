Apple Intelligence Arrives in China, Bringing Everything Except the Part Everyone Was Waiting For

Apple will ship notification summaries, translation and photo tools with an iOS 27.3 beta on November 4th, but not the new Siri AI, and Alibaba's Qwen will fill the chatbot slot.

The wire said: Apple Intelligence is finally coming to China with iOS 27.3 · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 31, 2026 02:52 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple Intelligence, the company's suite of AI features, has been unavailable in China since it debuted elsewhere. The Verge reports it was approved in China in July, and Reuters reported in August that Apple had trained its own language model specifically for the market.

Why it matters

China is one of Apple's largest markets, and AI features are now a standard part of the pitch for a new phone. Apple's choice of a local chatbot partner also shows how a global product gets rebuilt to fit one country's rules.

Apple said on its Chinese newsroom website that it will launch Apple Intelligence in China with iOS 27.3, according to The Verge, citing MacRumors. A beta containing the features is scheduled for November 4th, with a full release planned for December. Chinese iPhone owners, who have watched the feature list from the sidelines for a long while, may now watch it from slightly closer.

The first batch is the older material. The Verge reports it includes notification summaries, real-time translation, rewriting and proofreading tools, visual intelligence, and the ability to remove objects from photos. Translation: these are the features that already exist elsewhere, now arriving in a market where they did not.

What is missing is the new Siri AI, which the launch does not bring to China, according to The Verge. Customers will receive the AI assistant's supporting cast without the lead. It is the technology equivalent of a film premiere that includes the trailers, the credits and a very capable catering team.

For the chatbot slot, Apple says it worked with Alibaba to support its Qwen chatbot in some experiences. The Verge notes this sounds similar to the integration with OpenAI's ChatGPT that launched in 2024. Translation: Apple has once again decided that the best way to build a chatbot is to let someone else's chatbot appear in the menu.

The timeline was not exactly a surprise. Apple Intelligence was approved in China in July, which the Verge says signalled the launch was getting near. Reuters reported in August that Apple had trained its own large language model, or LLM, specifically for China. Translation: an LLM is the text-predicting engine behind a chatbot, and in this case it was built to order for one country's regulatory environment.

The sequence is instructive. A company with a famously tight grip on its products spent months obtaining approval, building a local model and lining up a local partner, all to deliver features that are, by now, a few steps behind. That is not a criticism so much as a description of what the cost of entry looks like.

Next to watch is the November 4th beta, and whether the new Siri AI follows in a later release. Apple has not said it will, which leaves it as the one feature in China still described as a future event.
