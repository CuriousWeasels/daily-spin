Amazon Will Fund Indiana Nuclear Plant, Pending Reactors, Licenses, Timeline

The company plans 12 unproven small reactors on the site of a dead coal plant, with no construction date and no disclosed price.

The wire said: Amazon is funding a new nuclear plant in Indiana · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 19, 2026 17:49 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Amazon has signed an agreement with X-energy and American Electric Power to fund a nuclear facility in Fairbanks, Indiana, on the site of the defunct Breed coal plant. It is the second such project for Amazon and X-energy, after one planned in Washington state.

Why it matters

Data centers for AI are straining power grids, and tech firms are betting on nuclear power that does not yet exist at commercial scale. Ordinary ratepayers and neighbors will be told the benefits are coming for many years before they can verify them.

Amazon has agreed with reactor developer X-energy and utility American Electric Power to fund a new nuclear facility in Fairbanks, Sullivan County, Indiana, The Verge reported. The plant would sit where the Breed Generating Station, a coal plant, used to operate, and is meant to deliver up to 960 megawatts of carbon-free electricity to the regional grid and to Amazon's data centers. No timeline for construction or operation has been announced, which is a bold way to announce a power plant.

The design calls for 12 Xe-100 small modular reactors. Translation: SMRs are nuclear reactors built smaller than the traditional kind, in the hope that smaller means cheaper and faster. The Verge describes the Xe-100 as advanced but unproven, still undergoing development and licensing, so Amazon is funding a plant whose main component is currently a plan with paperwork attached.

Amazon says the reactors can be cooled by air, water, or a hybrid of both, which it argues would let them operate in water-constrained regions without affecting local water demand. This is a deliberate answer to a common complaint about data centers, which is that they are thirsty neighbors. Amazon also says the project could create 1,000 construction jobs and 100 permanent roles. The word is could, and it is carrying real weight.

Indiana has some history here. The Marble Hill Nuclear Power Plant was abandoned half-finished in 1983 after $2.5 billion had been spent on it, and it has been under demolition since 2008. That means the state has spent longer taking down its last nuclear ambition than some utilities spend building a gas plant. Terms of the Amazon deal have not been disclosed, so no one can compare costs between the two projects, which may be the point.

This is the second project for Amazon and X-energy. The pair have set a collective goal of working with regional utilities to deploy more than 5 gigawatts of new US nuclear capacity by 2039. Their first, the Cascade Advanced Energy Facility in Washington, also plans 12 advanced reactors, but construction is not expected to begin until the end of this decade.

Amazon is hardly alone. Meta, Microsoft, and Google are all exploring nuclear projects to feed AI data centers. Investing in grid infrastructure was also among the commitments in a lengthy Amazon blog post earlier this month, which aimed to soften growing negativity about data center projects. The company is, in effect, answering the question of where the electricity will come from with a promise that arrives sometime after the electricity is needed.

What to watch next is the licensing process, the first disclosed price tag, and whether the 12 reactors outnumber the completed ones. At present the count of completed Xe-100 plants in this story is zero.
