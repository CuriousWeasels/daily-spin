US Accuses Canada of Election Interference for Objecting to Tariffs Out Loud

A US embassy fact sheet cites a minister's tariff remarks, governor meetings and a Reagan ad as evidence, though it does not explain how they amount to interference.

The wire said: US accuses Canada of election interference ahead of midterms · Al Jazeera

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 22, 2026 02:03 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

The US and Canada have been in a trade war since early in Trump's second term, with each side taxing the other's imports. Relations have also been strained by Trump's repeated talk of making Canada a 51st state.

Why it matters

The dispute shows how tariff fights can spill into questions of sovereignty and foreign influence, just as the November 3 midterms approach. It also muddies the line between ordinary lobbying and election meddling, which matters for any country that wants to talk to foreign politicians.

The United States has accused Canadian politicians of trying to influence its midterm elections, citing Canada's efforts to push back against President Donald Trump's tariffs. According to Al Jazeera, the US embassy released a fact sheet on Wednesday saying the Canadian government "brushes off concerns" about interference. The document's central theory appears to be that Canadians have been too visible while disagreeing with American policy.

The exhibits begin with Industry Minister Melanie Joly. In August she said Canada was targeting products that would affect US states, adding, "we're being wise and strategic to put political pressure." Translation: retaliatory tariffs are designed to annoy the people who can change the tariffs, which is how retaliatory tariffs have always worked, and why they are not usually filed under espionage.

The embassy also objected to Joly's September meetings with Democratic officials in Boston and Chicago. Asked what might happen to tariffs if Democrats won the midterms, she replied that this was "exactly why I'm meeting so many governors and potential presidential candidates." Joly rejects the interference charge, saying Canada would never meddle in US elections and that her meetings with Democrats and Republicans were about attracting investment.

Next came Ontario Premier Doug Ford, whose government ran an ad campaign against the tariffs last year. The ads used clips of Ronald Reagan criticizing tariffs in a 1987 radio address, which prompted Trump to call off trade talks in October 2025. The embassy listed Ford's calls for retaliatory measures too. Al Jazeera noted that the sheet laid out several examples of Canadian officials trying to influence trade policy but did not explain how that amounted to election interference.

The sheet also mentioned Liberal MP Anthony Housefather, who said he campaigned in Florida for Democratic Representative Debbie Wasserman Schultz ahead of her August primary. That is the item closest to actual electioneering, though it concerns one MP and one primary. The document further named 11 Canadians charged or sentenced for voting between 2012 and 2024, a span of 12 years that includes several elections. Al Jazeera reported that the sheet offered no evidence linking any of them to a Canadian government effort.

The embassy closed with a line that works as both policy and slogan: "Americans decide America's elections." It came a week after US Ambassador Pete Hoekstra said on a podcast that Washington was monitoring Canada and that "if some of it becomes election interference then America will respond to it." He did not, in the quoted remarks, say which part of it might.

The timing is awkward. Al Jazeera reported that separatist leaders from Alberta had reportedly sought support from Trump administration officials before a failed independence referendum this week. Prime Minister Mark Carney said before that vote that Canada expects foreign governments, the United States included, to stay out of its elections, and that the same goes in reverse. Al Jazeera said it had asked the Prime Minister's Office, Global Affairs Canada, Ford's office and Joly for comment.

What to watch is what "respond" means. If criticizing tariffs counts as interference, the list of suspects includes anyone who has complained about them, and the fact sheet has not yet said who is exempt.
