Amazon Pledges to Build Nuclear Plant in Indiana, Date and Price Both Pending

The company, X-energy and AEP plan 12 unproven reactors on a former coal plant site, with no timeline and undisclosed terms.

The wire said: Amazon is funding a new nuclear plant in Indiana · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 20, 2026 02:16 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Amazon has agreed with X-energy and American Electric Power to fund a proposed nuclear facility in Fairbanks, Indiana, on the site of the defunct Breed coal plant. It is Amazon's second such project with X-energy, after one planned in Washington state.

Why it matters

AI data centers are consuming enormous amounts of electricity, and tech giants are betting on new nuclear power to supply it. But the reactors are still in development and licensing, so ordinary ratepayers and communities are being asked to wait years for any benefit.

Amazon has signed an agreement with X-energy and American Electric Power to fund a new nuclear facility in Indiana, The Verge reported. The plant would replace the defunct Breed Generating Station in Fairbanks, Sullivan County, and would supply up to 960 megawatts of carbon-free power to the regional grid and to Amazon's data centers. No timeline for construction or operation has been announced, which is a bold approach to a press release about energy.

The plan calls for 12 Xe-100 reactors. These are small modular reactors, or SMRs. Translation: nuclear reactors designed to be smaller and built more repeatably, in the hope that doing something many times makes it cheaper. The Verge describes the Xe-100 as advanced but unproven, and still undergoing development and licensing. Amazon is therefore pledging to buy a product that is, for now, mostly a very confident drawing.

Amazon says the reactors can be cooled with air, water, or a hybrid of both, which it says should let them operate in water-constrained regions without affecting local water demand. This matters because data centers have become unpopular partly over resource use, and a plant that does not compete with the neighbors for water is a more agreeable neighbor.

Amazon also says the project could create 1,000 construction jobs and 100 permanent roles. The word doing the work there is could. Terms of the agreement have not been disclosed, so nobody outside the deal can say what it costs.

Indiana offers a cautionary footnote. The Marble Hill Nuclear Power Plant was abandoned half-finished in 1983 after $2.5 billion had been spent on it, and has been under demolition since 2008. That means the state has now spent longer taking down an unfinished nuclear plant than many people spend in a career. Because the Fairbanks terms are secret, The Verge notes, the two projects' costs cannot be compared.

This is the second project in a partnership between Amazon and X-energy, which aims to work with regional utilities to deploy more than 5 gigawatts of new US nuclear capacity by 2039. The first, the Cascade Advanced Energy Facility in Washington, also plans 12 advanced reactors, with construction not expected to start until the end of this decade. Meta, Microsoft and Google are exploring nuclear projects of their own to feed AI data centers.

The announcement also fits a broader public relations effort. Amazon recently published a lengthy blog post, according to The Verge, meant to ease growing negativity around data center projects, and it listed investment in grid infrastructure among its commitments. A nuclear plant is a persuasive example of grid investment, especially one that will be ready when everyone has stopped asking.

What to watch next: licensing progress for the Xe-100, any disclosure of costs, and whether Indiana gets its reactors before the Marble Hill demolition finally finishes.
