Google Ships Gemini 4 Argon, Discovers Meta Launched Yesterday and Ruined the Mood

Google's new flagship model undercuts rivals on price and tops one leaderboard, but free and Plus users get the cheaper Flash models instead.

The wire said: Gemini 4 Argon is now available worldwide as Google's most capable frontier model · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 15, 2026 14:38 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google unveiled Gemini 4 Argon a few weeks ago, saying it needed to proceed carefully, and began a wide rollout on Tuesday. Meta launched its own frontier-class model, Muse Wonder Full, the day before.

Why it matters

Cheaper frontier models could lower costs for developers and businesses, while the tiered rollout shows the best AI is increasingly reserved for people who pay for it. A crowded race also makes it harder for any one lab to hold a lead.

Google began rolling out Gemini 4 Argon worldwide on Tuesday, making its new flagship model available to developers through its API, and to AI Ultra and Pro subscribers through the Gemini app and Search's AI mode, according to The Decoder. Ars Technica noted that Google had said weeks earlier that it needed to move cautiously because of the potential for misuse. Apparently the caution took about as long as a long weekend.

Not everyone gets in. Free users and paying Plus subscribers now receive only the weaker Flash models under a new tier structure, The Decoder reported. Translation: Google has introduced a premium product and asked you to demonstrate your enthusiasm with a larger monthly payment. Argon replaces the Pro class as the company's top series, and Google says more Gemini 4 models will follow.

The pricing is the more aggressive part. The introductory rate is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached inputs 95 percent cheaper. Translation: a token is a small chunk of text, and a million of them is roughly a small library that the model reads or writes. The regular price of $4 and $20 is still below GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1, which both list at $10 and $50. The Verge said Google is undercutting rivals by as much as 80 percent during the promotion.

Google also supplied internal results, which are self-reported and should be read as such. Engineers using Argon increased agent-driven code submissions by more than 35 percent, and rollbacks fell by nearly a third. In Chromium, a refactor across hundreds of files that would have taken months reportedly took a few weeks. Ars Technica added that Argon found redundant emoji rendering in YouTube comments, and removing it cut server load by 45 percent. Somewhere, a very expensive supercomputer was quietly spending its days drawing emoji twice.

On safety, Google calls Argon's architecture its strongest yet. It says external red teaming in the bio domain got past fewer than 1 percent of tested safety checks, and an automated monitor watches the agent's reasoning in real time. On a prompt injection benchmark, Argon had a 0.7 percent successful attack rate, versus 8.5 percent for GPT-6 Astra and 4.8 percent for Claude Opus 5 in an earlier test. The Decoder points out that Google's write-up does not make clear whether those tests were identical, which is a fairly important detail in a comparison.

The scoreboard is less flattering than the press release. Argon tops the Vals Index but still trails Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5 in independent overall rankings, per The Decoder. Because Google waited about two weeks to ship, it also now sits behind Meta's Muse Wonder Full, which arrived Monday. The Verge framed the pair of launches as the AI race being back on, after Google had looked like a company drifting from the frontier amid executive departures and reshuffled teams.

The Decoder also reports that Google is already testing a successor codenamed Carbon, said to be better at coding. Argon is thus the current best model for roughly as long as it takes to read this sentence, which is how frontier AI now works. Watch whether the Flash models reach free users soon, and whether Argon's price stays low after the promotion ends.
