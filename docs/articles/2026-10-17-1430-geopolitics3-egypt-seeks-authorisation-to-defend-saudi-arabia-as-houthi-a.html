Egypt Asks Parliament for Permission to Help Saudi Arabia, Should Saudi Arabia Ask

President Sisi wants lawmakers pre-approving defence aid to Riyadh as Houthi strikes on airports and oil sites escalate, though no request has been made.

The wire said: Egypt’s Sisi seeks authorisation for defence assistance to Saudi Arabia · Al Jazeera

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 17, 2026 14:30 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis have been striking Saudi airports and oil facilities, including an attack on Riyadh's main airport that killed 14 people and wounded more than 300, according to Al Jazeera. The fighting sits inside a wider US-Iran war that began with US and Israeli strikes on Iran in February, DW reported.

Why it matters

The attacks threaten Saudi Arabia's economic diversification plans and Red Sea shipping, which also feeds Egypt's Suez Canal revenue. How much real military help Riyadh gets, and from whom, will shape how far the conflict spreads.

Egyptian President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi has asked parliament to convene an emergency session so he can approve defence assistance to Saudi Arabia, if Riyadh requests it, Al Jazeera reported. He spoke on Saturday at a graduation ceremony at the Egyptian Military Academy. Translation: Egypt would like the legal paperwork finished before the phone rings, which is the most bureaucratic form of loyalty available.

Sisi was careful about the scope. Egypt would take any measure to protect the kingdom, he said, in line with the directives of King Salman and Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman. He added that he was not speaking so that Egypt could fight or attack anyone, only for defence. This is a standard diplomatic arrangement: a commitment large enough to be noticed and conditional enough to be cancelled.

The backdrop is grim. Al Jazeera reports the Houthi strikes have mainly hit infrastructure on the outskirts of Riyadh but appear to be edging toward the city centre. Besides King Khalid International Airport, attacks have been reported at the airports in Dammam, Abha, Jazan and Najran. DW adds that Houthis hit Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran on Tuesday and that a cruise missile was intercepted over the Medina region on Thursday. It remains unclear who launched that one.

Egypt has reasons beyond friendship. The Houthis recently seized Yemen's Red Sea coast and gained significant control over the Bab al-Mandeb strait, the southern gateway to the Suez Canal, according to Al Jazeera. Analysts told the outlet that Egypt's Suez revenue depends on the same shipping lanes. Translation: when Egypt says Saudi security is part of its national security, the national security in question includes a toll booth.

Saudi Arabia is collecting partners. In August it signed a defence pact with Pakistan and Turkey, and earlier this month the three agreed to activate deterrence measures and rapidly deploy forces, per Al Jazeera. DW notes, however, that Pakistan and Turkey have offered intelligence and defence support while making clear they will not put boots on the ground in Yemen. France and the United Kingdom offered limited help. Egypt so far has offered words.

The conspicuous absence is Washington. DW reports the US signalled willingness to share intelligence and offer targeting assistance, but has stopped short of direct strikes, apparently to avoid opening another front against Iran. Analyst Timothy Kaldas told DW that Riyadh is prioritising the symbolic value of its partners, which is a polite way to describe an alliance that is long on photographs.

The costs are not only military. Several airlines suspended flights to Riyadh, and the Future Investment Initiative, usually a gathering of foreign leaders and executives, plans remote participation, DW reported. Kaldas noted that the economic diversification effort depends on tourists seeing the kingdom as safe. Saudi Arabia accuses Iran of encouraging the attacks; Tehran denies it and has offered help to halt the conflict.

What to watch is whether parliament convenes and whether Riyadh ever asks. An authorisation nobody uses is cheap deterrence. One somebody does use is a different kind of document.
