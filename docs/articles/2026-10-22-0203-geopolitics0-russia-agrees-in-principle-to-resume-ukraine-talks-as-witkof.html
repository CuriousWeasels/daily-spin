Russia Frees Jailed American, Declines to Read Peace Proposal Handed Over in Same Meeting

Putin released Stephen Hubbard during talks with Steve Witkoff in St. Petersburg, then the Kremlin said the envoy's peace ideas 'lacked substance.'

The wire said: Russia frees jailed American Stephen Hubbard during Witkoff talks · DW

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 22, 2026 02:03 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Stephen Hubbard, an American held in Russia, was sentenced to seven years in 2024 at a closed trial on mercenary charges he denies. Washington has been pursuing a peace deal between Russia and Ukraine, with envoy Steve Witkoff shuttling between the parties.

Why it matters

A prisoner release can be real relief for one family and still say little about whether a war is ending. The talks now hinge on whether Moscow, Kyiv and Washington will actually sit in one room after November 10.

Russian President Vladimir Putin announced on Wednesday that Stephen Hubbard, a jailed American, was being released, according to DW. The news came during talks in St. Petersburg with US special envoy Steve Witkoff, and Putin said Hubbard was already at the airport. NPR described Hubbard as a 74-year-old Michigan native and reported that the release followed a request from President Trump. Witkoff said Hubbard was 'going home to his family,' which is the part of this story with no catch.

Hubbard was captured by Russian forces and convicted of being a mercenary, a charge he denies. DW reported that he received seven years at a closed trial in 2024. Translation: a closed trial is one where the public is not invited, which tends to reduce the number of people who can check the math.

Trump said on Truth Social that he had asked Putin directly in a phone call last week. He claimed the US 'was not required to give anything in return' and that no 'swap' was needed. In a follow-up post, he also said 'numerous people' had called to apologize for criticizing his suspension of a new round of sanctions, which he described as a necessary concession. Translation: sanctions are economic penalties on a country, so pausing them is, in the ordinary sense of the word, giving something. The two claims sit in neighboring posts, which makes them easy to compare.

The goodwill gesture arrived alongside a diplomatic agenda. Witkoff told Putin he had brought 'some papers,' described as ideas for a peace deal, to be read 'at your leisure.' After the meeting, the Kremlin said the proposals 'lacked substance' and would be of no real use. This was the ninth meeting between Witkoff and Putin, so the two sides have had considerable practice at exchanging documents and politely disagreeing about them.

Not everything was a rejection. Putin agreed 'in principle' to resume talks among Russian, Ukrainian and US delegates after November 10, DW reported. Kremlin adviser Yuri Ushakov said those talks should focus on building a post-war security system for Russia and Ukraine. He also said a possible first-ever meeting of the leaders of China, Russia and the United States was being prepared in Beijing. Translation: 'in principle' means yes, as long as the details never arrive.

Witkoff had met Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Norway a day earlier. On Wednesday Zelenskyy told Fox & Friends that the front line should be frozen as a first step toward ceasefire talks, saying 'freeze and stop.' German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said any capitulation by Ukraine would be 'a capitulation by all of us.' The European Union, which had delegates at the Oslo talks, said it expected Washington to brief it afterward, which is how the continent most affected by a war learns what its allies decided.

Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store said Witkoff planned to return to Oslo, and suggested the Russians may be 'looking for opportunities for diplomacy.' The next test is November 10, when a promise made in principle must become a chair, a table and a delegation. Until then, one man is on a plane home, which counts.
