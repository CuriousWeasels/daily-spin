Trump Pauses Russia Sanctions Two Days Before Launch, Citing Reasons He Declines to Specify

A law Congress passed and Trump signed in September is suspended for 60 days, as the president's fuel-price worries collide with his Ukraine policy.

The wire said: Trump waives several Russia sanctions as row with Zelensky intensifies · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 18, 2026 14:56 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Congress passed a Russia sanctions law that Trump signed in September, giving him broad powers to tax buyers of Russian energy. Relations with Kyiv have since frayed over a US deal to import Russian diesel, a disputed energy ceasefire and Starlink restrictions.

Why it matters

The pause removes pressure on Russia's energy revenue just as Ukraine faces a winter of attacks on its power grid. It also shows that fuel prices, and the midterm elections, are shaping US foreign policy.

Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday night suspending several Russia sanctions two days before they were due to take effect, the BBC reported. The sanctions came from legislation that Congress passed and Trump himself signed in September. The order said the suspension was in the US national interest, and NPR noted that Trump gave Congress no further details.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the move amounts to a 60-day pause so the administration can analyse the effect on the US and global economy. Translation: the government wrote a law, signed it, and will now spend two months checking whether it works. The law gave Trump power to levy tariffs of up to 100% on imports from the top five buyers of Russian oil and gas, chiefly China and India. It also targeted senior Russian figures including Putin, Russian banks, and the shadow fleet, meaning the vessels used to get around existing embargoes.

The first to applaud was Kirill Dmitriev, a Russian envoy, who called the order an important and truly historic moment. He said the sanctions would have done serious damage to the US and global markets. When the sanctioned party and the sanctioning party's critics agree about who benefits, the analysis tends to write itself.

Volodymyr Zelensky said it was unfortunate that the US had decided to completely disregard the sanctions, which he said were supposed to pressure Russia to end the war. He also said he believed the administration would lift more of them. DW quoted him calling it a US sanctions blackout that rewards Putin financially.

The pause draws on a background of fuel prices. The BBC said US energy prices have skyrocketed during the Iran war, and Trump has spent months dealing with the political cost ahead of the midterms. Last week the US struck a deal to import Russian diesel, which Zelensky suggested amounted to investing in Russia's war effort. Trump then announced an energy ceasefire, which Zelensky said surprised him. After three days of fresh Russian attacks, Kyiv resumed strikes on Russian refineries, which Trump called a disgrace.

Criticism came from both parties. Ted Cruz, a Trump ally, called the waiver a mistake and said other ways exist to address fuel prices. John Cornyn called it reckless, and Thom Tillis called it beyond irresponsible, arguing Trump could cut energy prices by ending the Iran war. Don Bacon indicated that lawmakers will now resist funding Trump's agenda. Congress, it turns out, can write a sanctions law, but the executive order is what decides whether it applies.

The war continues in the meantime. Zelensky said Russia hit more than 15 critical power generation and transmission facilities overnight, one of its biggest attacks on the energy sector since the full-scale invasion began, and warned of an attempted blackout before winter. Russia's Defense Ministry said it struck infrastructure targets including a substation in Zhytomyr and steelworks in Kryvyi Rih and Zaporizhzhia. Ukrainian authorities said an ambulance driver was killed in Kherson and at least nine people died across the country since Friday, with about 60 injured, according to the BBC.

There is also a dispute over Starlink. Ukraine says the satellite service abruptly limited the speed at which drones can fly while staying connected. US officials cited technical problems, but Zelensky said tests on EU territory found no such problems. Trump denied deliberately slowing the network or withdrawing intelligence-sharing. The pause lasts 60 days, so the review should finish around the time the heating season is in full swing.
