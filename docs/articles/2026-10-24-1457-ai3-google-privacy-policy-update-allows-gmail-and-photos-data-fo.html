Google Amends Promise Not to Use Your Gmail for Ads by Adding the Word 'Unless'

A privacy policy update dated October 16 means data you share with Google's AI features may now help decide which ads you see.

The wire said: Now Google says it might use your Gmail and Google Photos data to target ads · The Verge AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 24, 2026 14:57 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google's privacy policy used to say flatly that it does not show personalized ads based on content from Drive, Gmail, or Photos. Its Personal Intelligence feature, released in early 2026, lets users connect those apps to AI tools such as AI Mode in Search.

Why it matters

Anyone who linked their email, files, or photos to Google's AI for better answers may also be feeding ad targeting. The only protection is knowing which settings to switch off.

Google updated its privacy policy on October 16, according to The Verge and Ars Technica, both of which credited Android Authority with spotting the change. The old text said Google does not show personalized ads based on your content from Drive, Gmail, or Photos. The new text adds an exception: unless you choose to make data from these services available to other Google services that support personalized ads.

Translation: the firewall between your inbox and the ad machine now has a door, and the door is labeled 'your choice.' It is a famously durable design in the technology industry, in which the user is always in charge of a decision made by clicking through a screen designed by the company.

The mechanism is Personal Intelligence, which lets users hand data from Gmail, Drive, and Photos to Google's AI products in exchange for more tailored answers. Ars Technica noted that the Gemini app currently has no ads, but AI search tools can show sponsored content. So data shared with AI Mode for a better response can also help shape the ads beside it. The Verge put it plainly: sharing data for better AI recommendations also opens it up for more personalized ads.

Google disputes the framing, at least in spirit. In a statement quoted by Ars Technica, the company said connecting apps is entirely opt-in and that ads 'will not be based directly on your Gmail inbox or Photos library.' It added that sensitive information will not be used to tailor ads. Ars Technica read this as a roundabout confirmation that the data may inform ads, since the same data feeds the answers and the ad targeting.

Translation: Google is not reading your email aloud to advertisers. It is reading your email, forming an impression, and letting the impression do the talking. The word 'directly' is carrying a lot of weight in that sentence, and it has not asked for a raise.

There are guardrails, per The Verge. Advertisers do not get access to your Drive, Gmail, or Photos information, or to personal details like your name or email address. Google also says it will not show ads based on sensitive categories such as race, sexual orientation, religion, or health.

Users who would rather skip all this have options. They can disconnect individual apps from Search's Personal Intelligence features, or disable Personalized Recommendations entirely through the Search Services Personalization settings page. The policy has been updated, and so, in a sense, has the homework.

What to watch next is whether people connect their apps in large numbers, and whether regulators think a setting counts as consent when the setting is the only thing standing between a user and a mountain bike ad on YouTube.
