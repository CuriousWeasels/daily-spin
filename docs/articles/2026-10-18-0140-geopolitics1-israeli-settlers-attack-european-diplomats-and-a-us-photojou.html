Masked Men Attack European Diplomats; Israel Notes Visit Was Not Cleared in Advance

Belgium, France, Poland and the Catholic Church were targeted in Taybeh, while Netanyahu's office questioned whether the video proves what it shows.

The wire said: European diplomats attacked by Israeli settlers in West Bank · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 18, 2026 01:40 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Israeli settlers have carried out near-daily attacks on Palestinians during the olive harvest in the occupied West Bank. A day before the diplomatic incident, settlers injured Palestinians and a Washington Post photographer in a separate village attack.

Why it matters

When settler violence reaches diplomats and US journalists, it becomes harder to dismiss as a local dispute, and it strengthens Belgium's push for EU sanctions. For Palestinian farmers, who face this violence daily with far less media attention, the question is whether anyone will be held accountable.

A delegation of European diplomats was attacked on Saturday by masked Israeli settlers in Taybeh, a largely Christian village near Ramallah in the occupied West Bank, according to the BBC. The group included envoys from Belgium, France, Poland and the Catholic Church. They had come to assess conditions after near-daily settler attacks on Palestinians trying to bring in their olive harvest. They were, in effect, attacked while investigating attacks.

Video from the scene showed masked men confronting the convoy, and reports suggest stones were thrown. Belgium said a window of its consulate's car was smashed. A French mayor was assaulted, and footage showed a man in a French tricolor sash slumped beside a vehicle. The mayor of Aulnoye-Aymeries told AFP that settlers kicked him and beat him with iron bars. "It's the gratuitous nature of the violence that stuns me," he said.

Belgian Foreign Minister Maxime Prevot condemned the attack and said Israel, as the occupying power, must protect Palestinians, diplomats and journalists. Translation: under international law, whoever controls the territory is responsible for the people in it. He added that Belgium will keep pressing for EU sanctions against the violent settlers and those who support them. Translation: asset freezes and travel bans, which are the diplomatic equivalent of a strongly worded letter that also affects your bank account.

Israel's response came in several registers. Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar condemned the violence by what he called "Israeli rioters", then observed that the visit had not been coordinated with the IDF in advance, which could have helped safeguard the visitors. The IDF also condemned the attack and said that, according to a preliminary inquiry, no soldiers were present when it happened. That is a useful fact, though it does not obviously argue against the need for coordination.

Prime Minister Netanyahu's office took a third approach. It urged "caution before drawing conclusions", said social media images do not establish the full sequence of events or individual responsibility, and raised concerns about a "coordinated campaign to smear Israel" during the olive harvest. It also said police and forensic investigators had launched an inquiry. So the position is that the attack is under investigation, and also that reports of it may be a smear. Both can be held at once, provided nobody asks which one governs.

The incident followed a Friday attack in another village in which several Palestinians and Heidi Levine, a Washington Post photographer, were injured, according to the BBC and NPR. Secretary of State Marco Rubio called that attack "disturbing" and "not a good look". US Ambassador Mike Huckabee, a staunch ally of Israel, called it a "despicable act of terror carried out by masked cowards" and demanded prosecution. Al Jazeera described the broader pattern as unprecedented settler pogroms during the harvest.

The sources disagree mainly on framing, not on the basic events: Israeli officials and European governments both use the word violence, and differ over how much the video proves and what it means politically. Watch whether the police investigation produces any arrests, and whether Belgium finds enough EU partners for sanctions. The olive harvest, meanwhile, is still going on.
