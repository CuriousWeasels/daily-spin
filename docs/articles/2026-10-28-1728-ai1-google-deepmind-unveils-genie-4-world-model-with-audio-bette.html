Google's Genie 4 Builds Convincing Worlds That Last Two Minutes, Then Forgets Them

DeepMind's new world model adds audio, sharper physics and live prompt editing, but its simulations still lose track of how they began.

The wire said: DeepMind unveils Genie 4 world model promising more realistic physics and humans · Ars Technica AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 28, 2026 17:28 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google DeepMind's Genie 3 world model, which generates playable virtual environments from text prompts, rattled gaming stocks last year. Genie 4 is the follow-up, released in research preview to trusted testers.

Why it matters

World models are being pitched as a way to train robots, prototype games and storyboard films without building anything physical. The consolidation around them, including acquisitions by AMD, Amazon talks and Rivian, suggests big companies think the technology is worth owning.

Google DeepMind released Genie 4, its newest world model, in research preview on Tuesday, according to TechCrunch. The model generates interactive worlds from a text prompt, now with matching audio, and it can keep a scene consistent for about two minutes, up from one minute for Genie 3, The Verge reported. It is a significant improvement, provided your goals can be accomplished in the time it takes to microwave a burrito.

Translation: a world model does not load a pre-built level the way a video game does. It generates the experience frame by frame as you move through it, so there is no map, no scripted objects and no artist who spent three years on a tavern. You describe what you want and the model improvises it. Ars Technica notes that this is exactly why Genie 3 made the gaming industry nervous.

The new version also lets users change things midstream. You can alter the weather, move the camera, or add people and objects by typing a new prompt while the simulation runs. Google says physics are better and characters are more humanlike, and the model produces music, ambient noise and interactive dialogue.

Under the hood, Gemini 3.8 Flash acts as what TechCrunch calls a director, planning steps and checking each frame while Genie draws the visuals. Translation: one AI proofreads the other AI's hallucinations in real time. Ars Technica says this helps the simulation stay on track as environments change. Tejas Kulkarni, a research director at Google, told reporters he expects more of these hybrid models, with Gemini writing code and text while Genie simulates reality.

The commercial pitch is broad. Google suggests gaming, interactive learning, and robotics, where a Gemini Robotics experiment had a model break tasks into steps that Genie 4 simulated, as a way to test robot scenarios before they happen in the real world. Trusted testers are also pointing to film prototyping, and Google's Diego Rivas cited storyboarding and rapid ideation. One speculative learning example is stepping into a historical painting and chatting with its subjects, who presumably have opinions about the framing.

Google is unusually candid about the limits. Details and objects drift the longer a simulation runs, the worlds forget how they started after a couple of minutes, and the team has not figured out how to guarantee accurate audio or legible in-world text. So the current product is a convincing world with unreliable signage and short-term memory. The Verge's writer has not tried it, but did previously use Genie 3's Project Genie to generate what he called bad Nintendo knockoffs.

Competitors are not idle. TechCrunch notes that Runway released its second world model, GWM Worlds 2, a couple of months ago, while Fei-Fei Li's World Labs is being acquired by AMD, Amazon is in talks to buy Decart, and Rivian acquired Third Dimension for self-driving simulation.

Access is staged. Genie 4 is available to trusted testers now, and in the coming weeks it will roll out to Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie and to early access partners of the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. The Verge says a waitlist is open. Translation: the infinite worlds are available at a price, and for now they come with a two-minute timer.
