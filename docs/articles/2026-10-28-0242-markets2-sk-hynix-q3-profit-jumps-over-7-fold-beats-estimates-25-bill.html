SK Hynix Earns 83 Trillion Won in a Quarter, Then Gives Much of It Back

The South Korean memory maker posted record third-quarter profit and promised about 100 trillion won to shareholders, as AI demand keeps outrunning the supply of chips.

The wire said: SK Hynix shares soar over 8% as profit beats, chipmaker to return $25 billion to investors · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 28, 2026 02:42 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

SK Hynix makes memory chips, including the high-bandwidth memory that sits next to AI processors in data centers. On Wednesday it reported third-quarter results that beat analyst expectations and announced a large shareholder payout.

Why it matters

Memory is a bottleneck for the AI buildout, so SK Hynix's results suggest demand is still strong and that prices for chips in servers and devices may stay high. For investors, a payout this size is a signal about how much cash the company thinks the boom will keep producing.

SK Hynix reported record quarterly results on Wednesday, with revenue of 104.62 trillion won ($78.86 billion) against the 98.34 trillion won that LSEG SmartEstimates had expected, CNBC reported. Operating profit came in at 83.33 trillion won versus 76.72 trillion expected. Shares rose more than 8%, ahead of the Kospi's 6.25% gain, which is what happens when a company is both the news and the index.

Translation: LSEG SmartEstimates weight the forecasts of analysts who have been right more often, so the company beat the estimates of the people who are good at estimating. Revenue rose 327.9% from a year earlier and operating profit climbed 632.1%. Sales passed 100 trillion won for the first time, a milestone that required a number with a lot of zeros to be called small by the time of the next report.

The company then decided what to do with the money. It plans to return about 100 trillion won to shareholders, including at least 30 trillion won in cash dividends and 60 trillion won in buybacks and cancellations. Translation: a buyback is when a company purchases its own shares, and cancelling them means the remaining shares each own a slightly bigger slice of the business. It is a way of saying that the best investment available is itself.

The third-quarter dividend is 14 trillion won, including a special dividend funded by gains from SK Hynix's investment in Japanese chipmaker Kioxia. CEO Kwak Noh-Jung said the company intends to return those gains to shareholders. A further 20 trillion won buyback is planned, half of it to be cancelled this year, after a previously announced 40 trillion won buyback finished last week. The company also raised its shareholder return target to more than 50% of cumulative free cash flow from 2025 to 2027.

One thing it will not be doing is taking its U.S. subsidiary Solidigm public. Kwak cited financial market volatility and a correction in semiconductor stocks, and said an IPO would not fully deliver the anticipated benefits at this time. Translation: the market was in a mood, and the company had enough cash that it did not need to ask strangers for more.

The operating details explain the cash. HBM, or high-bandwidth memory, is the specialized memory used alongside AI processors to move large amounts of data. SK Hynix expects its sixth-generation HBM4, which began mass production and shipments in the third quarter, to surpass HBM3E in both shipments and revenue in the fourth quarter. Average selling prices for DRAM rose by a high-20s percentage from the previous quarter, and shipments of server DDR5 chips rose more than 40%.

For customers, the outlook is less comfortable. The company said demand outpaces supply in the memory market and expects that imbalance to continue for the time being. It has long-term agreements with 14 major customers covering more than 50% of its 2027 and 2028 capacity, and expects demand to outpace supply through 2030. Translation: buyers are reserving chips years before they exist, which is the semiconductor version of booking a restaurant table two years out.

The company also plans to significantly increase investment next year, which sits a little awkwardly beside a 100 trillion won payout. Shareholders get cash now, the factories get expanded later, and the open question is what happens to both promises if the AI spending that funds them slows. For now, SK Hynix says the shortage is the plan.
