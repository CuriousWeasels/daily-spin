OpenAI Seeks $30 Billion More Before Going Public, Which Is Apparently How One Prepares

The ChatGPT maker is reportedly negotiating a round at a $1.25 trillion valuation, led by Coatue and UAE investors, with terms still moving.

The wire said: OpenAI working on new $30B raise at $1.25T valuation ahead of IPO · TechCrunch AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 24, 2026 14:57 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

OpenAI filed confidential IPO paperwork in June, after a March raise valued it at $852 billion post-money. Anthropic, its chief rival, also filed confidentially in June after raising $65 billion at a $965 billion valuation in May.

Why it matters

Private valuations set the bar that public-market investors will be asked to clear, and ordinary shareholders, including anyone in an index fund, may eventually be asked to pay for that bar. If the figures hold, the two leading AI labs would debut at prices that leave little room for disappointment.

OpenAI is reportedly working on a new private funding round of up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, according to CNBC, as relayed by TechCrunch. Coatue and investors in the United Arab Emirates are said to be leading it. OpenAI declined to comment, which in fundraising is the traditional way of saying the spreadsheet is still open.

Translation: pre-money valuation is what the company is deemed to be worth before the new cash arrives. Post-money is that figure plus the cash. At $1.25 trillion pre-money, a full $30 billion raise would put the post-money number at $1.28 trillion, a rounding error only at this altitude.

The figure is not settled. Bloomberg reported a month earlier that OpenAI was seeking $30 billion at a $1.4 trillion valuation. Both outlets say terms are still being negotiated, so the number has either dropped by $150 billion or is simply being tried on in different sizes. Either way, it is higher than the $852 billion post-money valuation from March.

CNBC also reports that the UAE money could be paired with a deal to secure more computing capacity for OpenAI from the UAE. Translation: an investor puts in cash, and part of the arrangement may be the investor supplying the machines the cash will be spent on. It is a tidy loop, and the sort of circularity AI financing has made routine.

If the round closes above $1 trillion, TechCrunch notes, OpenAI would become the most valued private company ever. This is also the awkward part of the plan. The company has already filed confidentially for an IPO, which is the stage where a company ordinarily stops raising private money and starts asking the public for it. A bigger private valuation simply raises the price of admission for those public buyers.

Anthropic is watching the same ladder. According to CNBC, the latest reports have it seeking a $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion valuation for its public debut. That would be a large step up from the $965 billion it was valued at in May, a round many believed would be its last before going public. Rumors, TechCrunch says, suggest Anthropic's public S-1 filing could arrive any day. Translation: an S-1 is the registration document a company must publish before selling shares to the public, and the first time outsiders see the actual financials.

The incentives line up neatly. Private investors want a high mark now so their earlier stakes look wise. The companies want a high mark so the IPO can be priced above it. Public investors, who have not yet been consulted, will be the ones to decide whether the mark was a valuation or a hope.

What to watch next: whether the final terms land nearer $1.25 trillion or $1.4 trillion, and whether Anthropic's S-1 finally shows what a trillion-dollar AI lab earns when it is not describing the future.
