RealReal Teaches Its Website to Tell Shoppers From Shopping Robots After Cart Loophole Exposed

After The Verge showed Meta's Muse agent could keep designer items in a cart for hours, the resale retailer tightened its bot defenses.

The wire said: The RealReal won’t let AI agents hog designer clothing · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 26, 2026 17:43 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

The RealReal, a luxury consignment retailer, puts any item in a shopper's cart on hold for 20 minutes. The Verge reported that Meta's Muse AI agent could re-add items the moment each hold expired, blocking other buyers.

Why it matters

As AI agents become mainstream shoppers, retailers must decide whether to welcome them or block them, and ordinary customers may find popular items held by software that never gets tired. Systems built on the assumption that humans are impatient are starting to break.

The RealReal, a secondhand luxury fashion retailer, said it has updated its bot defense system to make it harder for AI agents to shop on its site. The move followed a Verge report showing that an agent could exploit the company's cart hold system. The retailer, in other words, has discovered that a rule written for people applies poorly to something that does not need lunch.

The mechanism is simple. According to The Verge, when an item goes into a shopper's cart on The RealReal, it goes on hold for 20 minutes, during which nobody else can buy it. Translation: the hold is a polite way of saying the handbag is yours for a moment, provided you make up your mind. Using Meta's Muse, The Verge instructed the agent to re-add listings each time the hold expired, which kept other shoppers out for hours or longer.

Chief technology officer Luke Friang said in an email that the company had addressed the Muse use case The Verge flagged. Its bot defense configuration now makes it much more difficult for all agents to log in, add items to cart, and check out, he said. Agents can still surface listings and send Muse users to product pages, so the robot may browse the window but not touch the merchandise.

Friang added that the company will keep strengthening protections as AI-powered shopping evolves. Translation: this is a patch, and everyone involved expects to be patching again. Meta did not immediately respond to a request for comment, according to The Verge.

The timing is awkward for everyone except Meta. The company said on its Q3 earnings call this month that shopping is the most common use case for Muse, with purchases averaging over $200, and that 10 million people in the US downloaded the app in 40 days. Tech companies are pitching agents as always-on assistants for billions of people, and it turns out that always-on is exactly the problem when a system depends on people eventually getting bored.

The larger tension is commercial. If shoppers reach websites through chatbots, retailers become one step removed from their customers, which threatens revenue such as advertising. The Verge notes that Amazon, among others, has blocked agents from shopping on its sites. Retailers must therefore choose between a new sales channel and a middleman that may quietly hoard the inventory.

The Verge observed that a person has a realistic limit on how long they will add and re-add designer clothing to a cart. An agent has no such limit. For now, The RealReal has answered with a firewall, which is the traditional response when a new customer arrives who is both very enthusiastic and not a customer.
