Alleged Ransomware Gang Member Books Holiday in Japan, Gets Itinerary Revised by Police

A 28-year-old Russian suspected of belonging to the Qilin gang was arrested on arrival in Japan, and German detectives are now linking him to the 2024 attack that disrupted London hospitals.

The wire said: Russian man linked to deadly London hospitals hack arrested · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 17, 2026 14:30 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

In June 2024, the Qilin cyber-crime gang hacked Synnovis, a blood-testing provider serving London hospitals, forcing cancelled operations and diverted emergency patients. King's College Hospital later confirmed that one patient had "died unexpectedly" and that a long wait for a blood test result was a contributing factor.

Why it matters

Prosecuting a cyber attack as a contributor to a death would set a precedent that treats hospital ransomware as violence rather than a financial nuisance. It also tests whether gangs sheltered by Russia can still be reached when their members travel.

German police have detained a 28-year-old Russian man, named by German press as Vladimir K, who is accused of being a core member of the Qilin cyber-crime gang, the BBC reported. He was arrested by Japanese police in May after arriving in the country for a holiday. German officials said his travel plans were intercepted, which is one way of describing a vacation that went to the authorities before it went to the beach.

He was originally wanted in Germany over a September 2024 attack on a logistics company, in which data was encrypted and about $160,000 in cryptocurrency was demanded. Translation: criminals locked the firm's files and offered the key for a fee. The BBC now says it can confirm that German detectives are also folding the 2024 Synnovis hack into their investigation of him and his associates.

Synnovis is a pathology partnership between several London hospitals and the German company Synlab. When Qilin struck on 3 June 2024, a critical incident was declared, blood transfusion services were severely disrupted, operations were cancelled and emergency patients were sent elsewhere. In total, more than 10,000 appointments were disrupted over weeks. The BBC describes it as the first cyber attack in history directly linked to a death.

The hackers threatened to publish the stolen data on their dark net website unless a ransom was paid. No ransom was paid, and they published the highly private data anyway. They told the BBC at the time that the attack was not only about money but also a protest against the UK's lack of help in Russia's war on Ukraine. That explanation was dismissed as an attempt to distance themselves from Qilin's known extortion tactics. It is an unusual business model that combines a political manifesto with an invoice.

Qilin has been tracked since 2022. It has allegedly hacked more than 4,000 organisations since 2024 and extorted more than $140m (£105m). Its targets include schools, hospitals, companies, councils and health organisations, and it was behind the October 2025 hack of Japanese beer maker Asahi. Japan, it seems, is a place Qilin has both attacked and, in one alleged member's case, tried to visit.

The BBC also reports that British police are examining whether its own inquiry could lead to an unlawful killing case, potentially a world first for cyber-crime. There is a cautionary precedent. A 2020 attack on a Düsseldorf hospital led to a negligent homicide investigation after an elderly patient died, but it was dropped. Medical experts concluded the patient would have died regardless, and prosecutors could not prove the attack was the direct cause.

The larger obstacle is geography. The BBC notes that hackers are rarely arrested in Russia, whose government refuses to co-operate with Western law enforcement requests. That leaves international travel as the main enforcement mechanism, a system that depends on suspects choosing to holiday somewhere with an extradition treaty. For now, the most effective tool against ransomware gangs is their own taste in destinations.
