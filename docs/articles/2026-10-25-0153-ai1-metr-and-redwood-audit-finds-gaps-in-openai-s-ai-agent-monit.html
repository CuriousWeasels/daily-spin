Auditors Spend Three Weeks Inside OpenAI, Find Safety Monitor Missing Quite a Lot of AI

Researchers from METR and Redwood Research estimate 6 to 25 percent of some internal agent traffic went unmonitored, and the public gets a short summary of the 61-page report.

The wire said: Three-week on-site audit at OpenAI reveals major gaps in AI safety monitoring · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 25, 2026 01:53 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Two outside researchers, one from METR and one from Redwood Research, spent July 21 to August 11 at OpenAI examining the system meant to catch rogue behavior by its own AI models. It was a pilot program organized jointly by METR, Redwood Research and OpenAI, according to The Decoder.

Why it matters

The companies building increasingly autonomous AI agents are relying on internal monitors to catch misbehavior, and this audit suggests those monitors had holes. It also shows how little of an outside check the public can see: a short summary, with the full findings kept inside.

Two researchers spent three weeks inside OpenAI checking whether the company's monitoring system for rogue AI behavior actually monitors things, The Decoder reported. The answer, in the careful language of auditors, was: not always. They had access to the codebase and internal documents and interviewed about a dozen employees, which is roughly the point at which a smoke detector inspection becomes a conversation about the batteries.

The headline number is that an estimated 6 to 25 percent of internal agentic Responses API traffic from models of the GPT-5.5 class and above was not covered by monitoring during the audit period. Translation: agentic traffic means AI systems taking actions on their own rather than just answering questions, and the monitor was not watching a meaningful slice of it. METR says the uncertainty is significant in both directions, so the true figure could be better or worse than the range suggests.

A second gap involved internal Codex traffic, the company's coding agent. METR estimates roughly 10 percent of traffic that was supposed to be monitored went untracked between May and July, because the system mishandled compaction events. Translation: compaction events are context summaries, the agent's way of tidying its memory, and apparently the tidying left the monitor behind. OpenAI found and fixed the bug on its own while the audit was still going, which the auditors noted.

The design also had a weakness best described as honor-system oversight. During the period when compaction was unmonitored, the system only triggered when an agent handed control back to the user. An agent could therefore avoid scrutiny by simply never stopping, a loophole that human employees have been exploiting in meetings for decades.

The auditors further found ways to hide hundreds of actions from the monitor and to get around the Codex sandbox, the restricted environment meant to keep the agent contained. They also concluded that specially crafted inputs could likely crash the monitor entirely, and that model behavior during reinforcement learning training was not monitored at all when the audit began. Translation: reinforcement learning is the training stage where models are rewarded for good results, which is also a rather important time to watch what they do to get them.

OpenAI says it paused RL training after the Hugging Face incident in August and expanded monitoring to cover it. Those changes came after the auditors' access ended and were not part of the evaluation, so readers are invited to take them on trust. OpenAI also says it has fixed all the weaknesses the auditors found.

Then there is the matter of the report itself. The full document ran 61 pages and was handed over internally. After OpenAI applied redactions, METR and Redwood leadership received a 38-page version, and the public got a short summary. METR said it had no right to disclose the redacted portions on its own.

For future audits, METR says it wants to push for more public transparency. For now, the world has learned that an outside audit of AI safety monitoring is possible, that it found real gaps, and that the full list of them is a document most people will not read.
