Nigeria Confirms Ebola Case, and Two Countries Begin Disputing Where It Was Caught

A woman who arrived in Abuja from Uganda tested positive, and Nigeria is tracing 44 contacts while Uganda insists the virus is not its problem.

The wire said: Nigeria records its first Ebola case, in capital Abuja · DW

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 21, 2026 16:10 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

A Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak was declared in the Democratic Republic of Congo in May and has since spread to Uganda and Kenya. Nigeria is now the first West African country to report a case linked to it.

Why it matters

The outbreak has no approved vaccine or treatment for this strain, so containment depends on contact tracing and border health checks. How quickly Nigeria traces her contacts will show whether the virus stays a regional crisis or gets harder to stop.

Nigeria's Health Ministry confirmed on Tuesday that a woman who arrived from Uganda has tested positive for Ebola in Abuja, the country's first case in the current outbreak. Health Minister Muhammad Ali Pate said she developed symptoms on October 7 and was admitted to a private hospital on October 11. Authorities have begun tracing 44 people identified as her contacts. Translation: contact tracing means phoning everyone she met and asking them to stay put, which works best when everyone answers.

Almost immediately, the question became where she actually caught it. Pate told Nigerian state broadcaster NTA, "We presume that it was likely picked in Uganda." Uganda's health ministry replied that travel through Uganda does not establish that infection occurred there, and stressed that it has no confirmed active Ebola cases. This is the diplomatic version of a game of hot potato in which the potato is a deadly virus.

The sources also disagree on some details. Uganda's ministry said, according to the Guardian, that she had only transited through Uganda after a business trip to eastern Congo, and that she is Nigerian. The BBC reported that health sources told it she is not Nigerian and is a diplomat's wife. The Guardian noted that Nigerian authorities have not disclosed her nationality, and the BBC said her exact travel history appears unclear. Uganda's health director general Charles Olaro said Nigeria had yet to provide her itinerary and clinical history.

The timing matters because Ebola has an incubation period of two to 21 days, according to the BBC. Translation: a traveler can be infected, feel fine, board several flights, and only then become ill. The BBC reported that Pate said she had no symptoms on arrival in Nigeria. The WHO, as cited by the BBC, said symptoms began four days after she entered the country, though the Guardian noted it is unclear when she arrived.

The wider numbers explain the nerves. The outbreak involves the Bundibugyo strain, which the BBC says is relatively rare, with no approved vaccine or treatment, though trials are under way. The ECDC counts more than 9,500 cases and at least 4,600 deaths, while DW cited slightly lower figures of more than 9,000 cases and 4,300 deaths. Both put the fatality rate at almost 50 percent. Africa CDC chief Jean Kaseya called it the most geographically extensive Ebola outbreak in history, affecting countries across three regions of Africa.

It is also the fastest-spreading on record. The 2014-16 West African epidemic killed around 11,300 people over roughly 30 months, and the WHO has said, according to the Guardian, that this outbreak is on track to exceed it. DW noted that better testing may partly explain the speed of the numbers, which is the rare case where more diagnoses are both good news and bad news.

Nigeria says it prepared. It created an Ebola task force in June, and its disease control agency stepped up surveillance two weeks ago after the Kenya cases. Presidential chief of staff Femi Gbajabiamila said at the task force launch that Nigeria did not want a repeat of the last outbreak, when a carrier entered and everyone scrambled. Nigeria did eventually beat that 2014 outbreak, which the BBC says caused at least eight deaths.

The test now is whether the preparation holds up. Travelers from countries with active transmission may be required to fill out a health declaration form, while those from other countries are exempt, which makes the question of which country she counts as coming from more than a diplomatic quarrel. Watch the 44 contacts over the next three weeks, and whether anyone has to revise the map.
