Microsoft Offers 60,000 Employees a Chance to Leave Before It Has to Ask

The company is extending voluntary buyouts to about a quarter of its staff in four countries, as it tries to protect AI spending by shrinking payroll.

The wire said: Microsoft’s AI future has fewer jobs · The Verge AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 30, 2026 17:17 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Microsoft has cut jobs repeatedly over the past couple of years while spending heavily on AI infrastructure. This week it began offering voluntary exit packages to around 60,000 employees in the US, UK, Canada and India.

Why it matters

The buyout is an early, concrete example of a large employer trading headcount for computing power. Workers across the tech industry can read it as a preview of how AI spending may be funded.

Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to around 60,000 employees in the US, UK, Canada and India, The Verge reported. That is roughly a quarter of the company, and it is the largest such offer Microsoft has made. An earlier retirement offer this year was limited to about 9,000 people.

The stated logic is arithmetic. The Verge describes the effort as a way to keep investing in AI infrastructure by lowering operating costs and headcount. Translation: data centers are expensive, and people are the line item that can be edited fastest.

Satya Nadella, the CEO, more or less announced this plan almost two years ago on a podcast. "Our total people costs will go down, our cost per head will go up, and my GPU per researcher will go up," he said. Translation: fewer employees, paid more, each with a larger share of the expensive chips. A GPU is the specialized processor that AI systems run on, and in this model it is the one colleague guaranteed a raise.

The numbers support the direction of travel. Microsoft employed about 223,000 people as of June 30th, down 5,000 from 228,000 in 2025, which The Verge says is the first year-over-year decline in a decade. The company laid off 4 percent of its workforce in July last year and another 4,800 people just months ago, hitting Xbox and commercial sales hardest. Layoffs that were once an annual ritual now arrive every few months, which makes them less a ritual than a subscription.

The offer itself is less generous than it sounds. According to the report, citing sources familiar, it is practically the same as regular severance: cash based on tenure, accelerated stock vesting, some subsidized health coverage and several months of career services. Translation: the company is asking people to accept the terms they would get if they were fired, but to say yes first. More than 30 percent of eligible staff took the earlier retirement offer, but the Verge expects far fewer to accept this one.

Even so, scale does the work. If only 5 percent accept, that is still 3,000 people. Employees have until December to decide, and The Verge notes rumors of further layoffs in November and January. The acceptance count will presumably inform how many people the company then has to remove involuntarily, which is the polite reason the word voluntary is in the name.

Some insiders think the cuts are overdue. Merill Fernando, a former Microsoft Entra product manager, told The Verge that Microsoft needs fewer people and described "layers on layers" and "silos everywhere." Nadella has acknowledged internal tensions himself, saying last year that "social cohesion is not a goal, winning in the marketplace is a goal."

That sits awkwardly beside the empathy-centered culture Nadella is credited with building. The Verge asks whether he can reshape the company for AI without undoing it. Employees weighing a December deadline will have to decide how much of that culture is a policy and how much is a slogan.

One more thing to watch: whether the 3,000 figure holds, and whether the next round of cuts arrives right on schedule.
