Binance Loses $6.3 Billion to Hackers, Promises Everything Is Fine

The world's largest crypto exchange says it has frozen about half the stolen funds, while analytics firms point to North Korea's Lazarus Group.

The wire said: Crypto giant Binance attempting to recover $6.3bn stolen in hack linked to North Korea · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 31, 2026 02:52 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Binance, the largest cryptocurrency exchange, reported a hack that accessed about $6.3bn in assets, which the BBC describes as a record heist. Two analytics firms, TRM Labs and Chainalysis, say they have high confidence that a North Korean state-backed hacking unit is responsible.

Why it matters

Millions of users keep money on exchanges that promise to cover losses, but experts say such promises may not be legally binding. The episode also shows how a sanctioned state can fund itself by stealing from the private sector.

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, said this week that about $6.3bn (£4.7bn) was accessed in a hack that the BBC describes as a record heist. The company says it has secured about $3bn, which it puts at roughly half, though a spokesperson later cited 80% of stolen assets as secured. The BBC's reporting contains both figures and does not reconcile them, which is a useful reminder that in a crisis, arithmetic is also a communications strategy.

Binance's first explanation was that an engineer's laptop had been compromised, allowing unauthorised withdrawals. It later said the attack was wider. Co-CEO Richard Teng said the incident was "not carried out by one person" and that the investigation had "uncovered additional points of breach." Translation: the first answer was a single laptop, the second answer was several problems, and the third answer is still being compiled.

The attackers also reportedly used a self-destruct mechanism that wiped out tooling and traces as they went, according to Teng. This is what security professionals call making the investigation harder, and what everyone else calls cleaning up after yourself. TRM Labs said it is likely the attacker gained access to Binance's hot wallet signing system. Translation: a hot wallet is the internet-connected account used for day-to-day transfers, and a signing system is what approves them, so the thieves reached the part that says yes.

Attribution points to the Lazarus Group, also known as TraderTraitor, which TRM Labs and Chainalysis both identified "with high confidence." Chainalysis also said the laundering patterns were ones it had "not seen before." The BBC notes that several elements resembled the Bybit hack, which the FBI blamed on North Korea last year, when $1.5bn was stolen. North Korea has long denied involvement in crypto theft, though the BBC says the evidence from cryptocurrency flow analysis and defector interviews is compelling.

The incentives are straightforward. North Korea is a heavily sanctioned country with very little industry, and experts quoted by the BBC say the regime uses cybercrime to bring money in. Reports say it recruits promising university students into hacking groups overseen by its intelligence agency. It is, in effect, the only state with a recruiting pipeline from the math department to the heist.

Binance told users that "your money is safe, and we're covering every loss," and said it had set aside more than $6.34bn in company funds. The BBC points out that crypto history watchers noted FTX's Sam Bankman-Fried offered similar reassurance before FTX collapsed in 2022. Daniel Tunkel of the law firm Edwin Coe said recovery would come down to what customers were promised in their contracts. Translation: a reassuring tweet and a binding contract are different documents, and only one of them can be taken to court.

Markets reacted with their usual composure. Bitcoin fell 5% to $76,800 on Friday afternoon in New York, Coinbase dropped 12% (partly on earnings that slightly missed expectations), and Robinhood slid 7.8%. The US Secret Service said it was working around the clock to identify, freeze, trace and recover the funds, and Binance said it is working with the FBI. Binance has more than 300 million registered users and said in 2024 that it held over $160bn in assets, so the next thing to watch is whether the promise to cover every loss survives contact with the other half of the ledger.
