Meta Releases Open AI Model That Runs at Home, Provided Home Contains Two Mac Studios

Muse Spark Open has 816 billion parameters, a free license with a branding requirement, and a hardware list that costs more than most people's cars.

The wire said: Muse Spark Open is Meta's answer to Qwen, GLM, and Kimi in the open-weight race · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 16, 2026 01:49 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Meta built its AI reputation on the open-weight Llama models, then moved toward paid, closed Muse models. Muse Spark Open is its return to releasing weights the public can download, this time in partnership with Apple.

Why it matters

Open-weight models let developers run AI on their own hardware instead of renting it from a vendor, which can cut costs and keep data in-house. The catch is that 'your own hardware' currently means a very expensive desk.

Meta on Thursday released Muse Spark Open, an AI model it calls the most capable open-weight model in the United States. According to The Decoder, it has 816 billion parameters, 42 billion of them active at a time, and a context window of up to one million tokens. The weights are on Hugging Face, which makes it available to anyone, in the way a yacht is available to anyone.

Translation: 'open-weight' means the trained model's numbers are published so you can download and run them yourself. It is not the same as open source, where you would also get the training data and recipe. 'Parameters' are the adjustable values inside the model, and 'context window' is how much text it can consider at once. One million tokens is roughly several novels, which is useful if your codebase is several novels.

Meta says the model handles text, image, video and audio, and is built for coding and agentic work. Translation: 'agentic' means the software carries out multi-step tasks on its own rather than merely suggesting what you might do. TechCrunch reported that Meta paired it with its Muse Code terminal tool and used it on bug fixes, new features and a full build for a Mastodon client. It also had the model review and crop footage in DaVinci Resolve, the video editing program.

Now the hardware. Meta says the model is optimized for Apple's M5 Ultra and runs locally on two Mac Studios with 512 GB of unified memory each. TechCrunch, citing Engadget, put the requirement at at least 1 terabyte of RAM. Those figures agree with each other, which is more than can be said for the word 'desktop,' which in this context means 'two desktops.' The Verge noted Meta's claim that it is the first model in its class to do this.

On performance, Meta says Muse Spark Open scores well ahead of Qwen 3.8, GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3 on several benchmarks, including JobBench, MCP-Atlas, HLE-Diamond and ZeroBench. These are Meta's own results. The Decoder adds that the model still falls well short of Meta's paid Muse Wonder Full models. So Meta has released the best free model it is willing to compare to everyone else's, while keeping a better one to sell.

The license is free, with conditions. Users must display the label 'Built with Muse Spark Open,' and any provider earning more than $20 million a year that wants to offer the model as an API service needs a separate agreement with Meta. Translation: it is free until you are successful at it, at which point it becomes a conversation.

The partnership is the more unusual part. Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg thanked Apple and its new CEO, John Ternus, on X, and Ternus replied with congratulations and praise for running a model of this scale from a desk. Meta's chief AI officer, Alexandr Wang, told Axios, 'We're excited for mommy and daddy to be playing nice again.' The Verge reported that the companies had collaborated for several months, and Apple published a technical post on its MLX framework.

The incentives are tidy. Meta gets its model on a premium platform and a credible local-AI story without building any hardware. Apple gets a flagship demonstration of why a very expensive Mac Studio is a sensible purchase. Developers get a capable model they can control, provided they can control about $20,000 worth of computer, a figure that is our estimate and not in Meta's materials.

What to watch next is whether anyone outside well-funded labs and studios actually runs it, and whether independent testing agrees with Meta's benchmark charts. Until then, it is open to everyone, in the sense that a sealed vault is open to anyone with the combination.
