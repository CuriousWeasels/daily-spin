Anthropic Pledges 5% of Itself to Public Good, Retains Control of Who Decides

The AI company's new public benefit fund is a wholly owned subsidiary, not a nonprofit, and the governance details are due in the coming months.

The wire said: Anthropic says it’s committing 5% of its shares to a ‘public benefit fund’ · TechCrunch AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 19, 2026 17:49 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Anthropic announced on Sunday a "public benefit fund" holding 5% of its shares, to support societal projects including help for workers affected by AI. The company is reportedly preparing an IPO that could value it at around $2 trillion.

Why it matters

If AI does disrupt jobs, who pays and who decides becomes a practical question for workers, not a philosophical one. The fund's structure suggests the company that causes the disruption will also control the response, at least for now.

Anthropic said on Sunday it will commit 5% of its shares to a new "Anthropic Public Benefit Fund," according to TechCrunch. The fund could make large donations to traditional nonprofits or explore direct payments to specific populations. The Decoder reported that shareholders still have to approve the plan.

The timing is worth noting. Anthropic's CEO, Dario Amodei, has described the AI industry's public standing as "fundamentally a crisis of trust," TechCrunch reported. The company has also been warning for some time about job market upheaval, and has already committed $200 million to study it. The announcement says that, given the pace of AI progress, "bolder approaches may be needed." Translation: studying the problem is going well, and the problem is going faster.

The fund can grow. Anthropic said it may add another 2% of shares for every 50% rise in the company's value, though The Decoder reported that these top-ups are at the board's discretion and the milestones have not been specified. So the generosity scales with success, and the schedule for that success remains unpublished. The company pitches this as the opposite of the stock packages tech firms usually give executives. Add the co-founders' pledge of 80% of their shares and an employee donation-matching program, and Anthropic says about 20% of its equity goes toward "the public good."

Now the structure. The fund is a wholly owned LLC subsidiary, not a tax-exempt nonprofit. Translation: Anthropic owns the fund that gives away Anthropic's money. The company says this offers flexibility, and it promises not to use the capital for lobbying or political donations in the US or abroad. It has not said how the fund will be governed, only that more is coming in the months ahead.

Control of the company itself sits elsewhere. The Decoder reported that the Long-Term Benefit Trust holds no economic stake but appoints a majority of directors, and that after the IPO the founders are set to hold 50.1% of the votes on major decisions through a special share. The Decoder's comparison is OpenAI, whose foundation holds 26% of the for-profit and appoints its board. That board's power was tested in November 2023, when it fired Sam Altman and he was back within five days while the board was replaced. A company-owned LLC, The Decoder noted, is unlikely to face the same outside oversight.

Elon Musk replied on X, advising Anthropic to give the shares directly to the public and saying he would do the same. On Monday he said that next year he plans to give one SpaceX share, currently worth about $146, to each of roughly 72 million US children, about $10.5 billion in total. He called it "the first of many" but did not explain how it would work. The Decoder noted that his own AI company, xAI, dropped its Public Benefit Corporation status in 2024.

The Decoder reported that Anthropic is expected to file IPO paperwork within weeks, with an offering that could raise up to $100 billion. Google, by comparison, has no comparable stock-linked structure and relies on conventional corporate philanthropy such as its AI Opportunity Fund, which does not include direct payments.

What to watch: the governance document. Until it appears, the fund is a large number attached to a promise, with the question of who decides still marked to be announced.
