Kremlin Says Europe Not Invited to Abu Dhabi Talks, Finland Says Otherwise

Moscow and Helsinki gave conflicting accounts of the same 90-minute call, while Britain and the Netherlands pledged billions and a Kherson truce held.

The wire said: Kremlin says European countries not included in next Ukraine talks · Al Jazeera

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 28, 2026 17:28 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Russia, Ukraine and the United States are planning trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi, and Kyiv wants Europe at the table. Separately, a US- and Vatican-brokered ceasefire in Kherson region is meant to let aid reach the cut-off, Russian-occupied city of Oleshky.

Why it matters

Who sits at the negotiating table usually determines who has to pay for, and live with, whatever gets agreed. Europe funds much of Ukraine's defence and reconstruction, so being excluded from talks that decide the terms would be an awkward arrangement for everyone except Moscow.

The Kremlin says European countries will not take part in the next round of talks on Ukraine in Abu Dhabi, according to Yuri Ushakov, a senior foreign policy aide to Vladimir Putin, as quoted by Interfax. Ushakov said European participation had not been discussed during a 90-minute phone call on Tuesday between Putin, Finnish President Alexander Stubb and Norwegian Prime Minister Jonas Gahr Store. Finland's president, who was on the call, remembers it differently.

Stubb told the Nordic Council in Helsinki that European countries would in fact be involved in the November talks. Al Jazeera reported both accounts without picking one. Translation: two parties attended the same phone call and left with different understandings of what was said, which is a useful preview of how the negotiations themselves may go.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said in the Netherlands that the trilateral talks with Russia and the United States would run from November 6 to 10 in the United Arab Emirates. He said Kyiv wants Europe included, and argued that unity and stronger pressure on Russia were needed. The logic is straightforward: Europe is paying a large share of the bill, and a seat at the table is the usual reward for that.

Europe is making its case in the currency it has available. Dutch Prime Minister Rob Jetten pledged an extra 500 million euros ($556m) in military support this winter for drones and other material. The Dutch government also announced a 1.2 billion euro ($1.33bn) package over three years, plus 40 million euros ($44.5m) for the Ukraine Energy Support Fund by the end of the year.

Dutch trade and development minister Sjoerd Sjoerdsma said in Kyiv that support for recovery and reconstruction cannot wait for a ceasefire. He added that however successful forces are on the battlefield, energy systems, hospitals and food production must keep working. Translation: wars are fought at the front but lost or survived in the power grid.

Britain went larger. Zelenskyy thanked Prime Minister Andy Burnham for a new package worth 10 billion pounds ($13.3bn), including 500 million pounds ($665m) from the Ministry of Defence. Downing Street said that portion is meant to plug urgent gaps in Ukraine's air defences this winter. Zelenskyy called the move timely, as Russia needs to see that Ukraine will not be left alone.

Meanwhile, a smaller diplomatic effort is under way in the south. The BBC reported that a ceasefire, brokered according to the White House and the BBC by US First Lady Melania Trump and Pope Leo XIV, has been extended across Kherson region. It was initially meant to get food and medicine into Oleshky, where an estimated 1,700 to 2,000 people remain with no reliable electricity, water or gas. Both sides say it is being observed, and no breaches have been reported so far.

The plan calls for road clearing, aid deliveries and then evacuations planned for Friday. Many details remain unclear, and residents told the BBC they fear Russia may not let them return to Ukraine. Zelenskyy has warned that routes cleared by Russia will lead people toward Russia, while the Dnipro bridges are destroyed and the riverbank is mined. The ceasefire is scheduled to last until Sunday, 1 November, and one Ukrainian official called it the largest de-escalation since 2022.

The thing to watch is whether a truce built to move bread and bandages can survive until Friday, and whether the Abu Dhabi guest list gets settled before November 6. At present, one side says Europe is coming, the other says it is not, and both have a phone call to cite.
