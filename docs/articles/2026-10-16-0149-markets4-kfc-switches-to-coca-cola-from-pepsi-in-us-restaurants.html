KFC Leaves Pepsi After Four Decades, Citing a Comeback That Requires New Beverages

The Yum Brands chain will pour Coca-Cola in all U.S. restaurants by the end of the first quarter of 2027, ending a relationship that began when Pepsi bought it in 1986.

Catch up

KFC's U.S. restaurants have served PepsiCo drinks since Pepsi acquired the chain in 1986, and stayed loyal even after Pepsi spun it off into Yum Brands. The chain has since lost U.S. diners to rivals such as Chick-fil-A and Popeyes.

Why it matters

Fountain contracts are a major sales channel and a source of brand visibility for soft drink makers, so each defection shifts revenue and bargaining power. For diners, the practical result is a different cola and, if KFC delivers, a wider menu of drinks.

KFC said Thursday that its U.S. restaurants will switch from PepsiCo to Coca-Cola beverages next year, the first change in drink supplier in decades, according to CNBC. The rollout is expected to finish by the end of the first quarter of 2027. The chain has poured Pepsi since Pepsi bought it in 1986, which makes this less a vendor change than a very long marriage ending with a memo.

The memo, obtained by CNBC, came from KFC U.S. President Catherine Tan-Gillespie, who called the move "a critical step in the Kentucky Fried Comeback." Translation: when sales are soft, a chain looks for something it can change quickly, and the soda fountain is easier to replace than the chicken's reputation. She said a new partner gives KFC "a platform to innovate," which is how one describes a cup.

The backstory is mostly loyalty. Pepsi later spun off KFC, along with Pizza Hut and Taco Bell, yet the combined Yum Brands kept buying from its former parent. Per CNBC, KFC was once Coke's second-largest fountain account, behind only McDonald's, so Coke is not so much winning a new customer as recovering an old one with interest.

The incentives are fairly legible. Fountain accounts are a sales channel and a billboard: every cup of cola is a small advertisement for the brand on it. Restaurants have also discovered that drinks, from refreshers to so-called dirty soda, can draw customers in. CNBC reported that KFC recently showed new iced coffees and shakes at its innovation restaurant in McKinney, Texas, and Coke said it has helped many restaurant partners create new drinks.

Pepsi is not leaving the Yum family entirely. Tan-Gillespie said Pepsi remains an important partner for the parent company and for KFC's international markets. CNBC also noted that Taco Bell's Mountain Dew Baja Blast depends on Pepsi, which is a strong argument for keeping the relationship cordial.

The loss lands in a broader pattern. CNBC noted that big chains rarely switch, but it is not unprecedented: Pepsi took Subway from Coke in 2024, while Coke has won Culver's, Arby's and Panda Express in recent years. MarketWatch reported that as more customers move to Coke, some analysts are growing more worried about Pepsi. Neither source gives figures on how much revenue the KFC contract represents, so the size of the hit is unknown.

The sources do not say whether the switch will bring diners back. Fried chicken customers who left for Chick-fil-A or Popeyes were presumably not driven away by the cola. Still, a new drink lineup is a cheaper pivot than a new recipe, and it comes with a press release. What to watch is whether the Kentucky Fried Comeback shows up in sales, or only in the beverage cooler.
