Meta Releases Open AI Model That Runs at Home, Provided Home Has Two Mac Studios

Muse Spark Open, an 816-billion-parameter model built with Apple's help, can run on a desktop, as long as the desktop is plural and has 1 terabyte of memory.

The wire said: Meta launches an open-weight AI model that runs on Macs · TechCrunch AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 17, 2026 14:30 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Meta has returned to open-weight AI models, which anyone can download, after its Llama series. The new model, Muse Spark Open, was announced Thursday and optimized for Apple's M5 Ultra chip.

Why it matters

Running a frontier-class model on your own hardware means less dependence on cloud providers, but the hardware bill currently limits that freedom to people who can afford it. The license terms also show that open and free are not quite the same thing.

Meta on Thursday released Muse Spark Open, which it calls the most capable open-weight model in the United States. Open-weight means the trained model files are public, so anyone can download and run them. Translation: you get the finished cake, but not the recipe, the kitchen or the ingredients list.

The model has 816 billion parameters, of which 42 billion are active at a time, according to The Decoder. Translation: parameters are the adjustable numbers that hold what the model has learned, and only a fraction of them switch on for each task, which is how a very large model avoids being completely impractical. It supports text, image, video and audio, and a context window of up to 1 million tokens, meaning it can keep a very long document or codebase in view at once.

The home-computing part comes with arithmetic. Meta says the model runs locally on two Mac Studios with 512 GB of unified memory each, using Apple's M5 Ultra chip and its MLX framework. Engadget, cited by TechCrunch, put the requirement at at least 1 terabyte of RAM. Translation: it runs on your desktop, if your desktop is two desktops and you have stopped looking at the price tag.

Meta says the model beats Qwen 3.8, GLM 5.3 and Kimi K3 on several benchmarks, including JobBench, MCP-Atlas, HLE-Diamond and ZeroBench, according to The Decoder. It also says the model still falls well short of its own paid Muse Wonder Full models. This is a normal arrangement in the industry: the free product is impressive enough to attract developers and conveniently not good enough to cancel the paid one.

Meta's demonstrations were practical. TechCrunch reported that the company used the model with its Muse Code terminal to work on a Mastodon client, including bug fixes, new features and a full build. It also had the model review and crop footage in DaVinci Resolve, the video editing software.

The licensing is where free gets an asterisk. According to The Decoder, use is free but requires the label "Built with Muse Spark Open," and providers earning more than $20 million a year who want to sell the model as an API service need a separate agreement with Meta. Translation: everyone may use it, and larger companies that would like to charge others for it must first have a conversation with Meta.

The collaboration is the other story. The Verge reported that Meta and Apple, typically rivals, worked together on the launch, and their chief executives praised each other publicly. Mark Zuckerberg thanked new Apple CEO John Ternus on X, and Ternus replied with congratulations. Meta chief AI officer Alexandr Wang told Axios, in remarks The Verge relayed, that the companies were excited for "mommy and daddy to be playing nice again." The sources do not say which company is mommy.

The incentives are tidy. Apple gets a headline model showing off what its expensive desktops can do. Meta gets a large open model that runs on hardware it does not have to build or host. The only party asked to bring a terabyte of memory is the customer.

Watch for whether anyone outside well-funded developers actually buys the second Mac Studio.
