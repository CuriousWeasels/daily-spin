Egypt Offers Saudi Arabia 'Any Measure' of Defence, Stresses It Is Not Fighting Anyone

After a fresh wave of Houthi drone attacks, President Sisi pledges help if Riyadh asks, while insisting this is a purely defensive form of involvement.

The wire said: Saudi-led coalition intercepts drones as Egypt weighs sending help · Al Jazeera

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 18, 2026 01:40 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Fighting between Yemen's Houthi rebels and the Saudi-backed Yemeni government flared in July, and Riyadh's King Khalid airport has since been attacked at least 20 times. The Saudi-led coalition says it shot down four more drones on Saturday.

Why it matters

A regional power offering military help to Saudi Arabia raises the odds that a Yemeni conflict becomes a wider one, and travel warnings and airport risks are already disrupting daily life in Riyadh. Separate reports of burning tankers near the Strait of Hormuz add to concern about energy shipping.

The Saudi-led coalition said on Saturday that it intercepted and destroyed four drones over Riyadh, the Eastern Province and the south of the country. Spokesperson Major-General Turki al-Maliki also posted footage of what he described as interceptions of cruise missiles and drones, and strikes on Houthi ballistic missile launchers. It was a busy Saturday, though the coalition's own account suggests it is now simply what Saturdays look like.

Egypt then entered the conversation. President Abdel Fattah el-Sisi said he was willing to carry out "any measure to protect" Saudi Arabia if the kingdom asks. In the same breath he said Egypt was not looking to intervene in regional wars, and that he was speaking "only about defence." Translation: Cairo is offering a very large umbrella while explaining that umbrellas are not weapons.

The distinction is doing real work. Sisi said it was "inconceivable" for Egypt to stand by while Saudi Arabia was harmed, which is a commitment, and also said nobody would be attacked, which is a limit. Both statements can hold until someone asks for the first one. Egypt's parliament meets in emergency session on Sunday at the president's request, which is how governments convert a sentence into a procedure.

The attacks Egypt is reacting to are not abstract. According to Al Jazeera, King Khalid airport in Riyadh has been attacked at least 20 times since July, including once last week in an attack that killed 13 people. On Thursday, at least five people died in missile strikes near the presidential palace in Aden, the seat of Yemen's internationally recognised government, which blamed the Houthis. On Friday, the government said two more missiles were fired at Aden's airport.

On the ground, Saudi Arabia is backing a government counteroffensive called Dawn of Yemen and has carried out air strikes on the Houthis, including on an oil facility in Sanaa. Government forces said they advanced in Marib province. Several embassies in Saudi Arabia have restricted travel and warned citizens not to use airports, and the Saudi education ministry extended remote learning for students in Riyadh and nearby Diriyah.

Saudi Arabia accuses Iran of encouraging the escalation. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran supports a "comprehensive political process" as the only sustainable way to resolve the conflict. Support for a political process is the kind of position every party can hold while the drones continue to fly.

Al Jazeera's live coverage adds that Iran's navy reported an oil tanker on fire in the Sea of Oman, after the IRGC said a second supertanker struck a naval mine in the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday. The sources do not link these incidents to the Yemen fighting, so no connection should be assumed. Still, a region where oil facilities, airports and shipping lanes are all making the news at once is not short of reasons to read a defensive pledge carefully.

What to watch is Sunday's parliamentary session, and whether Riyadh actually asks. Until it does, Egypt holds the rare diplomatic position of being fully committed and fully uninvolved.
