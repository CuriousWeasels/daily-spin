Machado Returns to Venezuela After Washington Apparently Finds the Right Form to Sign

The Nobel laureate came home to huge crowds and no arrests, while Trump said he knew nothing about it and Rubio took credit.

The wire said: Exiled opposition leader María Corina Machado returns to rapturous reception in Venezuela · The Guardian

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 26, 2026 17:43 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

María Corina Machado left Venezuela in December to collect her Nobel Peace Prize, and weeks later US forces seized President Nicolás Maduro. Washington then backed his deputy, Delcy Rodríguez, as interim president, leaving Machado abroad until now.

Why it matters

Venezuela's next election timeline depends on whether Washington prefers a popular opposition leader or a profitable working relationship with the current government. Oil markets and 'transition' diplomacy are both watching.

Venezuelan opposition leader María Corina Machado announced on Sunday that she is back in Venezuela after roughly ten months in exile. 'I'm here. With you,' she wrote on X, according to The Guardian and the BBC. She is, by the standards of this particular story, the one person who appears to have planned her own schedule.

Footage showed her at a church in Barquisimeto, in Lara state, about 220 miles west of Caracas, surrounded by supporters. The Guardian reported that the trip to the capital then took eight hours because crowds blocked streets and highways. Tens of thousands greeted her as she entered Caracas, leaning out of a car sunroof. Her ally Edmundo González confirmed the arrival and said he would soon follow.

The notable detail is what did not happen. Members of Venezuela's government had long hinted she would be arrested if she returned, but the Guardian said there was no sign of security services bothering her. Translation: when a government that threatened to detain you instead lets you pass, someone with more leverage has probably made a phone call. The BBC noted her return was always contingent on approval from the Trump administration, which appears to have been granted.

Washington's public position on all this is a masterclass in distributed responsibility. Asked about it, President Trump said, 'I don't know anything about it,' then added that she is 'a very nice woman' who gave him her Nobel prize, 'so how can I say anything else?' Secretary of State Marco Rubio, meanwhile, said her safe return was the direct result of US-supported reconciliation talks. So the White House knew nothing, and the State Department made it happen.

Some background explains the awkwardness. According to the BBC, Machado won an opposition primary in 2023 with more than 90 percent of the vote, was barred from running by Maduro's government, and her ally González ran in her place in 2024. The electoral authority declared Maduro the winner without releasing detailed tallies, while the opposition published copies of voting tallies it said showed González won. The Guardian says her movement is widely believed to have won.

After Maduro was seized in January, many expected Trump to hand power to Machado. He backed Rodríguez instead, who the Guardian says now governs what is widely seen as a US protectorate. The Guardian also reported that the administration had reportedly opposed Machado's return, fearing it would complicate its cosy relationship with Rodríguez. The BBC said reports of US involvement in delaying her return were denied by the US. Sources therefore disagree on whether Washington was an obstacle, though both agree it was relevant.

Machado has asked for fresh presidential elections by July 2027. The Guardian said it is unclear whether the administration supports that timeline, given that Trump called the oil agreement with Venezuela 'the biggest oil deal in world history.' Translation: an election might change who signs the contracts, which is a risk if the current signatories are pleasing.

Machado has praised Trump repeatedly, and in January gave him her Nobel medal, though the Nobel committee had said the prize cannot be revoked, shared or transferred. Her spokesman thanked Trump and Rubio on Sunday. Whether that gratitude is returned in the form of an election date is the thing to watch.
