France Arrests 1,800 After Protest About Underfunded Schools Ends With Several Underfunded Town Halls on Fire

About 550,000 people marched over school conditions and living costs, and a violent minority dominated the coverage, leaving the government to fix a budget and a message at once.

The wire said: French police arrest 1,800 after violent clashes and looting mar protests · NPR World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 18, 2026 14:56 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Student-led protests began in France in mid-September over overcrowded classrooms, teacher shortages and the cost of living. Saturday's nationwide day of action was the largest so far.

Why it matters

The violence risks burying the protesters' grievances just as the government tries to pass a deficit-cutting budget, a combination that historically makes French politics harder, not easier. It also raises the stakes for a presidential election next year.

French police arrested about 1,800 people on Saturday after nationwide protests turned violent in several cities, according to NPR, the BBC and Al Jazeera. Authorities put turnout at 550,000, while unions and organisers said 800,000. Most marchers were peaceful, a fact that has so far struggled to compete with the fires.

The marchers were demonstrating against rising fuel prices, the cost of living and deteriorating public services, including schools. Al Jazeera reported that the movement began in mid-September over overcrowded classrooms, teacher shortages and run-down buildings. Translation: students asked for more teachers and got a very expensive lesson in news cycles.

Then came the other crowd. The BBC described groups in black, masks and balaclavas setting fire to cars and buildings in Paris, Marseille and other cities, while looting hit Paris, Marseille and Lille. Al Jazeera saw masked young men smashing shop windows with what appeared to be golf clubs. In Lille, police said a McDonald's was set alight and an Apple store was looted.

The damage was broad. France's interior ministry said more than 500 police officers, gendarmes and firefighters were injured, and the Retail and Trade Federation said around 1,500 shops were damaged. In Limoges, a town hall dating back 143 years was set on fire and partly collapsed, and officials said it was largely lost. Lyon's tourist office also burned, and the mayor called it a tragedy. Separately, the mayor of Saint-Étienne said a young man had his hand blown off by a small explosive.

Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez said demonstrating is a fundamental right but blamed several thousand far-left individuals, later joined by delinquents who looted. A Paris shop owner told the BBC the looters were not genuine protesters and that he saw no police during roughly an hour and a half of CCTV footage. Marseille's mayor, Benoit Payan, said students with real claims are being drowned out by people smashing everything.

Police conduct is also under scrutiny. Al Jazeera reported that the police inspectorate had opened 36 investigations into alleged police violence since the protests began, a figure that predates Saturday. Human rights lawyers described some injuries to young protesters as comparable to conflict zones, and a lawyers' union leader warned that heavy crowd-control tactics inflame tensions. The government is therefore being criticised for too little policing by some and too much by others, which is a rare consensus on at least the volume of the problem.

The politics are awkward. Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu wants to cut the deficit through spending restraint and tax measures, which Al Jazeera said will likely deepen public anger. Jean-Luc Mélenchon accused the government of opening the door to chaos, and Lecornu replied that this was his critics' political responsibility. President Emmanuel Macron, whose final term ends next year, was due to address the nation Sunday evening.

Graffiti reading tax the rich appeared on shopfronts, while shopkeepers swept up glass outside Gucci and Versace. The schools that started all this remain overcrowded, and nobody has yet announced a plan for the glass.
