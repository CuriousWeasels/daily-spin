Instagram, Long Opposed to Long Videos, Discovers Long Videos

The platform is testing widescreen 'Features' in the US and Brazil, a format its own boss spent years arguing against.

The wire said: Instagram chases YouTube with a push for longer, widescreen videos · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 26, 2026 17:43 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Instagram built its business on photos and short Reels, while YouTube dominates longer video, including on living-room TVs. Instagram launched a TV app late last year and is now adding a longform format of its own.

Why it matters

If the test works, the app you open to see a friend's lunch may also want your evening, and creators will face another platform asking for longer content. For Instagram, the bet is that more watch time on TV screens matters more than keeping the feed a place for sharing.

Instagram announced a push into longform horizontal video, a format it calls 'Features,' according to The Verge. The test is expanding to the US in small form and is running with some creators in Brazil, spokesperson Megan Buckley told the outlet. It is a notable reversal for a company whose product has spent years teaching users that anything over a minute is a commitment.

The reversal has a personal dimension. Instagram head Adam Mosseri said in a video that he has opposed longform video on the platform 'for a long time.' His stated worry was keeping Instagram about connecting with friends and not only entertainment. Translation: people rarely send a friend a forty-minute video, and a video nobody sends is a video that generates fewer likes and shares, which are the things Instagram sells to advertisers as proof that people are enjoying themselves.

The solution, according to Mosseri, is to make shortform and longform 'symbiotic.' His examples were podcasts and comedy specials, which can exist as one long piece and also be chopped into clips. Translation: the long video is the main course, the short clips are the free samples handed out in the feed, and everyone pretends this was always the plan.

The timing makes more sense with the TV context. The Verge notes that Instagram launched a TV app late last year, starting as a Reels-focused pilot on Amazon Fire TV devices in the US. It has since expanded to Google TVs, Samsung TVs and Apple TV, and added features such as watching Stories. A vertical phone video stretched across a television is an awkward guest, and widescreen is the obvious fix.

Instagram is testing other pieces as well: clips that point viewers to a long video, a way for creators to build a 'series' of Reels, and better discovery for live video. Together they form a pipeline in which short videos lead to long ones, and long ones produce more short ones. It is a familiar loop, and YouTube has been running it for years.

Whether creators and viewers go along is unproven, since this is a small test in two countries. Mosseri's own hesitation is a reasonable benchmark. A company that spent years worrying longform would hurt friend-to-friend sharing now plans to find out by shipping it. Watch for whether the feed starts looking less like a group chat and more like a channel guide.
