Musk and Anthropic Pledge Share Giveaways Ahead of Very Large Stock Listings

Anthropic will put 5% of its shares into a public fund while Musk promises one SpaceX share per American child, details to follow.

The wire said: Musk and Anthropic pledge public share giveaways · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 20, 2026 15:53 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

AI companies face growing scrutiny over safety and job losses, even as they raise record sums. SpaceX, which owns xAI, set the record for the biggest IPO in June, and Anthropic is preparing a listing that could value it above $1tn.

Why it matters

Pledges of free shares may ease public suspicion of AI firms, but the details that determine who actually benefits have not been published. Meanwhile, the jobs concerns driving the goodwill campaign remain unresolved.

The BBC reported that Anthropic will commit 5% of its shares to a Public Benefit Fund that makes donations for the "public good", and that its board may add another 2% at its discretion. Elon Musk replied by saying he would give one SpaceX share to every child in America next year. Two companies worried about public trust have thus discovered the oldest remedy in finance: handing out equity.

Anthropic said none of the fund's shares would be used for lobbying or political donations, in the US or abroad. Translation: the money is meant to be charity, and the company would like that in writing before anyone asks. The timing is notable, as Anthropic is preparing a stock market listing that could value it at more than $1tn (£750bn).

Musk, for his part, said he recommended giving shares "directly to the public", which is what he would be doing. The BBC noted that details of how each American child would receive a share have not been disclosed. That leaves open questions such as how a share is delivered to a child, who holds it, and whether anyone has located every kid in America. A share of a company that raised $85.7bn in the biggest IPO in history is, at least, a gift with a price tag the recipient can look up.

The pledges arrive as the AI industry faces intense scrutiny over safety and employment. The BBC cited IMF research from 2024 suggesting almost 40% of jobs worldwide will be affected by AI, rising to as much as 60% in advanced economies. Companies, mostly in tech, have cut thousands of jobs after investing in AI, citing efficiency. Translation: the efficiency is real, and so are the people it is efficient at removing.

Anthropic told prospective investors last week that revenue rose 45% to $16.8bn (£12.6bn) in the three months to the end of September. Like other AI firms, it is trying to persuade investors that enormous profits are coming despite billions spent on development and infrastructure. A public benefit fund is a handy exhibit in that pitch: a company confident enough in its future to give some of it away.

The safety backdrop is less cheerful. In September, a researcher who left Anthropic told the BBC that people working on the technology were "genuinely frightened" about the speed of progress. The BBC reported that Dario Amodei has called for slower AI development, though some question his motives, and that Sam Altman and Musk say they agree with industry-wide deceleration, regulation and independent monitoring. Everyone favors slowing down, which is an unusual position for people currently accelerating.

In Washington, the BBC said President Trump and AI company heads signed a "morally binding" document making them responsible for the safety of their own technology. Trump has repeatedly rejected laws to control the technology, and has named Jay Clayton to lead a new AI taskforce. A morally binding document differs from a legally binding one mainly in who can sue.

What to watch: whether Musk publishes the mechanics of his giveaway, and whether the Anthropic fund's shares end up valued as generously as the listing hopes. Gratitude, like any asset, depends on delivery.
