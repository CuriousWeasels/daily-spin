Trump Pauses Russia Sanctions for 60 Days to Study Whether Sanctions Work

Two days before a congressionally passed sanctions law was due to bite, the president suspended parts of it, citing the national interest and a need for analysis.

The wire said: Trump waives several Russia sanctions as row with Zelensky intensifies · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 18, 2026 01:40 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Congress passed a Russia sanctions law that Trump signed in September, giving him broad powers to hit buyers of Russian oil and gas with tariffs of up to 100%. On Friday night he issued an executive order suspending parts of it, amid a worsening dispute with Ukraine's president, Volodymyr Zelensky.

Why it matters

The pause eases pressure on Russia's energy revenues just as Ukraine faces heavy strikes on its power grid ahead of winter. It also tests how much a law passed by a bipartisan majority can be shelved by a signature.

US President Donald Trump blocked numerous Russia sanctions on Friday night, two days before they were due to take effect, the BBC reported. The sanctions came from a law Congress passed and Trump himself signed in September. Signing a law and then suspending it is a sequence that carries a certain administrative elegance, provided nobody asks about the middle part.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Saturday that the suspension is a 60-day pause to analyze what imposing both sanctions and tariffs would mean for the global economy, and even the US economy. Translation: the sanctions were designed to hurt Russia's economy by hurting the economies that buy from it, and someone has now noticed that economies are connected. NPR reported that Trump told Congress the pause was in the national interest but offered no further details.

The law gave the president power to levy tariffs of up to 100% on imports from the top five buyers of Russian oil and gas, most significantly China and India. Translation: a tariff is a tax on imports, so the plan was to punish Russia's customers until they stopped being customers. The law also targeted senior Russian figures including Vladimir Putin, Russian financial institutions, and the so-called shadow fleet, the vessels Russia uses to get around existing embargoes.

The timing is easier to follow with the diesel. Last week the US struck a controversial deal to import Russian diesel, the BBC reported, in hopes of lowering US energy prices that have skyrocketed during the Iran war. Trump then claimed Russia and Ukraine had agreed to an energy ceasefire to ease those exports. Zelensky said he was surprised, and offered to stop strikes if Russia did. After three days of fresh Russian attacks, Kyiv resumed hitting Russian refineries, which Trump called a disgrace.

Russia was pleased. Presidential envoy Kirill Dmitriev called the order an important and truly historic moment and said the sanctions would have done serious damage, primarily to the US and global markets. It is unusual for a sanctioned party to describe its own relief as a favor to the sanctioning country's economy, but the argument is at least consistent with the one Washington is now making.

Criticism came from both parties. Democrat Don Beyer accused Trump of surrender to Putin. Republican Senator Ted Cruz, described as a Trump ally, said he wished the president hadn't done it. Senator Thom Tillis called it beyond irresponsible and said Trump could lower energy prices by solving the Iran problem rather than doing a solid for Putin. Representative Don Bacon said many lawmakers would now resist funding the president's agenda.

Zelensky said the sanctions could have helped enormously. He pointed to Russian overnight attacks on more than 15 power generation and transmission facilities, which he called one of the largest strikes on the energy sector since the full-scale invasion began in February 2022. Russia's Defense Ministry, per DW, said it hit several infrastructure targets, including a substation in Zhytomyr and steelworks in Kryvyi Rih and Zaporizhzhia, which it said supplied the Ukrainian military. Ukrainian authorities said an ambulance driver was killed in Kherson, with 19 or 20 others injured depending on the report, and at least nine people killed nationwide since Friday.

A further irritant is Starlink. Ukraine says a sudden speed restriction on drones disrupted battlefield missions. Zelensky said US officials blamed technical issues, but that tests on EU territory showed no such problems. Trump has denied deliberately slowing the network or halting intelligence-sharing.

Zelensky said he expects the administration to lift more sanctions, and that negotiators from both sides could meet next week. The pause lasts 60 days, which is roughly the time it takes to run an analysis, or to find out whether anyone intends to resume the original plan.
