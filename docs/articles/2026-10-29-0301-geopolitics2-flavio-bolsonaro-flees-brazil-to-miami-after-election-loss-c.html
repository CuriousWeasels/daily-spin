Flávio Bolsonaro Loses Election, Flies to Miami, Revises Title Three Times

The senator who lost Brazil's runoff on Sunday landed in Florida on Wednesday and declared himself 'leader of Brazil,' a downgrade from 'true president' that took roughly one edit.

The wire said: Flávio Bolsonaro calls himself the ‘leader of Brazil’ after fleeing to Miami · The Guardian

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 29, 2026 03:01 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Flávio Bolsonaro, a far-right senator and son of jailed ex-president Jair Bolsonaro, lost Sunday's runoff to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva. Brazilian police had asked the Supreme Court to retain his passport amid criminal investigations, and he left the country before that happened.

Why it matters

A losing candidate leaving the country while disputing a result that his own party accepts tests Brazil's institutions and could deepen friction between Brasília and Washington. It also leaves open how the investigations against him proceed once he is abroad.

Flávio Bolsonaro, who lost Brazil's presidential runoff to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva on Sunday, arrived in Miami on Wednesday morning by private jet and published a letter calling himself the country's 'true president,' according to The Guardian. He later deleted it and posted another calling himself 'the leader of Brazil.' Translation: the title was reduced after review, which is how most promotions work in reverse.

The facts around the claim are not in dispute. Brazil's electoral court, its supreme court, the presidents of both houses of Congress and international observers all accepted Lula's win, The Guardian reported. Bolsonaro's own party said on Monday: 'We recognise the presidential result.' DW added that the head of the electoral authority said the vote proceeded normally and in full accordance with the law. Bolsonaro, without evidence, repeated that there had been fraud.

The trip itself was an itinerary of some ambition. The Guardian reported that he vanished for more than 24 hours after Monday evening, then entered Argentina by land on Tuesday night, boarded a Gulfstream parked at Puerto Iguazú, and flew with his wife and two daughters to Guayaquil, Ecuador, reportedly to avoid Brazilian airspace. From there they continued to Miami-Opa Locka, a private aviation airport. Source descriptions of the aircraft differ: The Guardian said Gulfstream IV, DW said Gulfstream G400. Both agree it was a Gulfstream, which suggests the plane was the one thing everyone could see clearly.

The timing matters. DW reported that on Monday Bolsonaro posted on Instagram that he would not leave the country and would hand over his passports. By then, the Supreme Court had been asked to take them. DW, citing AFP, said he could still travel because he also holds Italian citizenship, his Brazilian and diplomatic passports having been canceled. Translation: a second passport is the part of the plan that does not require a press release.

There is also a legal reason to be somewhere else. The Guardian reported he is under investigation in at least two criminal cases, one involving a suspected money-laundering scheme worth about $905 million tied to betting companies. DW noted that he loses his immunity from prosecution in February, when his Senate term ends. Bolsonaro calls the investigations a political witch hunt. O Globo reported the escape had been planned for weeks, with his brother Eduardo, who lives in self-imposed exile in the US, as its main organiser.

Washington's role is, at best, ambiguous. Trump said Tuesday night that he would approve the family's move and called them 'fine people,' then said Wednesday that he would not comment on the claim but that 'we gave him a pass to come into the country, so we'll see.' He also called the election 'very strange,' and DW noted he earlier called extended voting times a 'very bad look.' Staying out of it, in this case, includes issuing the visa.

Lula, speaking at an event for his 81st birthday, called the flight 'shameful.' Brazil's three main newspapers described the declaration as 'coup-mongering,' The Guardian reported. Brazil's federal police are now being criticised for failing to stop him from leaving.

The family has history here. Jair Bolsonaro spent three months in Florida after losing in 2022, and is now jailed after a conviction tied to the attempted coup. About a dozen allies have also left Brazil while facing charges, according to The Guardian. What to watch next: whether the Supreme Court pursues the cases in his absence, and whether 'leader of Brazil' survives another draft.
