Apple Updates MacBook Pro, iMac and iPad mini, Charges Extra for the Trackpad

The M6 arrives across the Mac lineup with faster chips, 24-hour battery claims and a $50 fee for a trackpad that used to be free.

The wire said: Apple introduces the new 14‑inch MacBook Pro with M6 · Hacker News

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 27, 2026 16:54 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Apple announced a wave of products ahead of its "Pro and Tell" event: a 14-inch MacBook Pro and an iMac with the M6 chip, plus an iPad mini with an OLED screen. Pre-orders open October 28, with availability from November 6 for the Macs.

Why it matters

Anyone with an older Mac gets a real performance case for upgrading, but the iMac's new configuration fees show that the headline price is only the opening bid.

Apple on Tuesday introduced a 14-inch MacBook Pro and an iMac, both running its new M6 chip, along with an 8th-generation iPad mini. Per Apple's press release, the MacBook Pro offers a 12-core CPU, a 12-core GPU and up to 24 hours of battery life, which the company calls the longest ever in a Mac. Pre-orders begin October 28, and the machines ship November 6. It is a rare product cycle in which the main surprise is how many things are called the fastest.

The MacBook Pro's gains depend heavily on what you are comparing it to. Apple says the M6's GPU is up to 30 percent faster at graphics and up to 25 percent faster at AI workloads than the M5. Against the M1, those figures become up to 11x and 13x. Translation: if your laptop is old enough, nearly any new laptop looks like a miracle, and Apple has helpfully run the numbers on the oldest one it can justify.

Apple leans hard on on-device AI, citing additional Neural Accelerators in the GPU and a dual 16-core Neural Engine. Translation: the chip has dedicated circuitry for running AI models on your own computer instead of renting someone else's server, which Apple says preserves privacy. The release also mentions running LLMs locally, meaning large language models, the same technology behind chatbots. The pitch is that your laptop can now do the thinking, and Apple does not have to see it.

The iMac got the same chip, and The Verge reported that the design is otherwise largely unchanged. Prices start at $1,499 for a 10-core CPU and 10-core GPU, the same as the old 8-core M4 model, and $1,699 for the 12-core version. The M6 is Apple's first 2nm chip, and Apple claims up to 5x faster on-device AI than the M4 iMac. The refresh also skips the M5 entirely, so this is a two-year jump rather than a yearly nudge.

The fine print is where the iMac gets interesting, according to The Verge. Swapping the Magic Mouse for a Magic Trackpad now costs $50, though it was free on the previous model. Upgrading Ethernet from 1 gigabit to 2.5 gigabit adds $30. Apple also will not let buyers configure a Magic Keyboard with Touch ID but no numeric keypad, even though its own announcement page shows one. The colors are the same as the M4's, and some of the marketing images were reportedly reused with new content on the screen.

The iPad mini is the more substantial redesign. Ars Technica reported that it starts at $699, up in price, and gains an 8.5-inch OLED display, up from 8.3 inches, with peak brightness of 1,200 nits versus 500 on the old model. It is 5.4 mm thick, carries an IP67 rating for water and dust resistance, and uses the 2nm A20 Pro chip from the iPhone 18 Pro. Apple calls it the most significant update ever for the little tablet, and the price tag agrees.

Ars Technica also noted that the iPhone Duo may be a bit of a risk right now. The iPad mini, a mid-size touchscreen with a better display, is positioned as a safer way to scratch the same itch. Whether that is a compliment to the mini or a review of the Duo is left to the reader.
