Google Discovers AI Search Pays Less Than Search, Plans to Fix It With Ads

Alphabet's stock fell sharply after search revenue growth slowed to 12 percent, even as cloud revenue doubled and capital spending hit $51.4 billion in a single quarter.

The wire said: Google's AI search problem finally shows up in the earnings report, some investors believe · The Decoder

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 30, 2026 03:06 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Alphabet reported third-quarter results on Wednesday, with revenue of about $127.6 billion, up 25 percent. Investors have long worried that AI chatbots would erode Google's search advertising business, and this report gave that worry some numbers.

Why it matters

Google's search ads fund much of the company and a large share of the internet's free services. If AI answers earn less per question than the old blue links, the economics of how people find information online, and who pays for it, start to change.

Alphabet's stock fell as much as 12 percent after its third-quarter report, according to Ars Technica, which called it one of the largest post-earnings drops in the company's history. TechCrunch put the decline at 8 percent following the results, and The Decoder reported a drop of up to ten percent. The sources disagree on the exact figure, but they agree on the direction, which is the one shareholders dislike.

The cause was search. Google Search revenue grew 12 percent year over year, down from 17 percent in the second quarter and 19 percent in the first, per TechCrunch. Analysts had expected 15 percent, The Decoder reported. Translation: the company is still growing at a pace most businesses would frame and hang on the wall, but Wall Street had already priced in a better pace and is now sending the bill.

The more interesting admission came from Chief Business Officer Philipp Schindler. He said that "the strong growth in AI mode queries had an impact because of the lower monetization," The Decoder reported. Ars Technica summarized his explanation: each AI Mode query earns less than a comparable web search, because there are fewer ads and the queries cost more to run. Translation: Google built a more helpful product that people use more, and which makes less money per use. This is a recognizable problem for any restaurant that decides to give away the main course.

Google's answer is more ads. Schindler said the company is expanding ads in AI Mode to international markets for the first time, alongside multi-turn ads and AI Mode in the U.S., TechCrunch reported. He said click-through rates on sponsored product listings in AI Mode are already comparable to organic listings. Translation: users click on the ads about as often as they click on regular results, which is the sort of sentence advertisers have been waiting to hear and users have been waiting not to hear.

Executives also offered a counterargument. CEO Sundar Pichai said search queries grew at their fastest annual rate in five years, though he did not give the figure. He said AI Mode queries are on average three times longer than traditional ones. The Decoder noted that traditional search has been declared dead for years and keeps growing anyway. The sources do not reconcile how query volume can hit a five-year high while revenue growth slows, but the lower-monetization explanation is the obvious candidate.

Elsewhere the numbers were strong. Cloud revenue rose 103 percent to $30.7 billion, and the Gemini app reached one billion monthly users. Ars Technica noted that cloud beat analyst estimates by about a billion dollars. But Ars also reported that net income was almost $9 billion lower than a year earlier, and earnings per share of $2.12 fell short of a $3.02 consensus. Revenue up, profit down is what economists call investing, and what shareholders call a concern.

The spending explains much of it. Capital expenditures reached $51.4 billion in the quarter alone, The Decoder reported. Google kept its 2026 forecast at $195 billion to $205 billion and said spending will increase significantly in 2027. The company also wants to sell AI infrastructure built on its own TPU chips to outside customers, but management did not disclose the profit margin on those sales, even when asked twice, according to The Decoder. When a company is asked the same question twice and declines both times, the answer is usually available, just not in the transcript.

What to watch next: whether ads in AI Mode close the gap per query, and whether the 2027 spending number arrives with a matching revenue story. Until then, Google is asking investors to fund the future of search while conceding that, so far, the future pays less than the past.
