Anthropic Shows Investors Its Profits and Its Warnings, Hopes They Read Only One

The AI firm's revenue hit $16.8bn in a quarter, even as its own researchers put the odds of human extinction above 10%.

The wire said: AI giant Anthropic woos potential investors ahead of stock market debut · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 15, 2026 12:56 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Anthropic, maker of the Claude AI models, is expected to list on the stock market and has been courting investors at an event in San Francisco. The pitch arrives during a week of falling tech shares and renewed doubts about AI spending.

Why it matters

If you own a pension fund or index fund, you probably own a slice of the AI boom, so whether these companies can turn spending into profit affects your savings. A listing by Anthropic or OpenAI would also put the safety debate on a quarterly reporting schedule.

Anthropic has been persuading investors that it can make money, according to the BBC, which reported that the company's revenue rose 45% to $16.8bn in the three months to the end of September. Revenue has risen each quarter this year, from $4.7bn in the first to $11.6bn in the second. The company also told attendees at its San Francisco headquarters that it made a third-quarter profit on an adjusted basis.

Translation: adjusted profit is profit calculated after leaving out the costs the company would prefer you consider unusual. It is a legitimate accounting method, in the same way that a diet is legitimate if you do not count weekends. Investors, the BBC reported, left the event optimistic about the growth and the path to profitability.

The same event also emphasised safety, which is an unusual thing to stress to people you are asking for money. Chief executive Dario Amodei has called for the pace of AI development to slow down. In September one of the company's researchers warned of a greater than 10% chance that AI could "kill all humans" within a decade, and a former researcher said people in the field were "genuinely frightened" by the speed of progress.

Anthropic also said in September that it had disrupted attempts to use its model for "malicious activity" that could support the development of biological weapons. This is the rare risk disclosure that doubles as a product feature: the software is dangerous, and the company is good at stopping that. The business model, in short, is to sell the technology and the caution together.

President Donald Trump has taken a different view, saying researchers have been "bringing up things that won't happen" and that in the race with China, "whoever wins AI, wins". Rival OpenAI, which is eyeing a listing that could value it at more than $1tn, said through Sam Altman that it will not go public this year while it addresses safety concerns. So one company is holding its offering back over safety, and the other is showing investors its revenue chart.

The timing is awkward. Tech stocks fell on Wednesday, with chip makers AMD, Nvidia and Intel dropping sharply. In Asia, SoftBank fell more than 9%, Samsung 9.5% and SK Hynix 11%. The slide followed remarks by Broadcom chief executive Hock Tan, who said many investors have "no clue" and have set "extreme expectations" only to find it is "almost like they are in a casino".

Inflation data added to the gloom. US price rises accelerated by more than expected in September, and investors worry that the Federal Reserve will respond by raising the cost of borrowing. Translation: expensive money makes it harder to justify spending billions today on profits promised for later. That is the precise problem an AI listing is meant to solve.

Anthropic's numbers are therefore doing double duty, as the BBC noted: they are a gauge of whether the whole industry can pay for the infrastructure it is building. The evidence so far is a quarter of rapid growth and an adjusted profit. What to watch is whether the market treats that as proof, or as one more table at the casino.
