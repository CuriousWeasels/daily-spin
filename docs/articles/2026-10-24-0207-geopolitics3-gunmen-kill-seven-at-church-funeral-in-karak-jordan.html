Gunmen Kill Seven at Church Funeral in Jordan; Officials Revise Toll Twice

At least 34 others were wounded in Karak governorate, and the interior minister called the attack unparalleled in the kingdom's history.

The wire said: Masked gunmen kill seven in attack on church funeral in Jordan · Al Jazeera

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 24, 2026 02:07 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Jordan is a majority-Muslim country with a sizeable Christian community in Karak, and deadly attacks there are rare. On Friday, gunmen opened fire during a funeral at a Greek Orthodox church, and no group has claimed responsibility.

Why it matters

An attack on mourners in a country known for relative stability raises hard questions about security and sectarian tension. Jordan's response in the coming days will shape how safe minority communities feel there.

Seven people were killed and at least 34 wounded on Friday when gunmen attacked a funeral at a church in Hmoud, in Jordan's Karak governorate, according to the BBC and Al Jazeera. The attackers reportedly arrived by car, entered the church, opened fire and fled. No one has claimed responsibility.

The death toll moved a little on the way to its final figure. The BBC reported that initial accounts said seven were dead, a government spokesperson then said six, and officials later returned to seven. Translation: in the first hours after an attack, the official number is a draft, and the draft is edited in public.

The BBC verified CCTV footage that appears to show three gunmen walking from a road toward the church. Two enter what looks like a vehicle entrance to the grounds, retreat briefly, then return with a third man. All three appear to be carrying handguns. Al Jazeera, working from earlier reporting, referred to two masked gunmen, so the sources differ on the number of attackers.

Witnesses described a scene of terror. Khalid al-Mdanat told BBC Arabic the attack lasted about 15 minutes, with people trying to flee while shooting continued. Others described people throwing themselves under pews and away from windows, and bodies lying in the courtyards. One woman said the gunfire began seconds after a prayer.

Interior Minister Mazen al-Faraya described the perpetrators as a "group of extremists" and the attack as "unparalleled in the history of the Hashemite Kingdom of Jordan." King Abdullah II said authorities would confront the act "with the harshest penalties", and Al Jazeera reported that he urged national unity. Security forces are hunting the suspects.

At the hospital treating the wounded, the BBC reported heavy security, closed roads and an atmosphere of anger among residents. Some voiced frustration at what they described as a failure to take a tougher approach toward terrorists. Lebanon's President Joseph Aoun condemned what he called a "heinous terrorist attack" that violated the sanctity of death and of mourners.

The government has now promised severe punishment and national unity, both easier to announce than to deliver. What to watch is whether the suspects are caught, whether anyone claims the attack, and what officials do to protect places of worship.
