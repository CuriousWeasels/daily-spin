Google Cloud Power Event Reminds Britain Its Banks Live in One Building

A power problem at a London data centre zone knocked out Revolut, Lloyds, HSBC, Royal Mail and court video hearings for much of Tuesday.

The wire said: Google resolves cloud outage affecting UK banks and websites · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 21, 2026 02:09 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google Cloud reported an issue with multiple services at its London region, europe-west2, early Tuesday, disrupting UK banks, delivery firms and courts. The company says services have been restored and that it will publish an analysis of the incident.

Why it matters

When a handful of cloud providers host much of the internet, one failed data centre zone can stop people paying for parking, shipping vegetables or attending court hearings. Businesses are being reminded to ask whether they have a workable plan B.

Google Cloud said it has fixed a disruption at its UK cloud site that caused an outage for banks, businesses and the courts, according to the BBC. The company blamed a "power event" at a third-party facility in its europe-west2 region, which is its London cloud site. Services have been restored, a spokesperson said on Tuesday evening, which is the cloud industry's way of saying the lights are back on and everyone should move along.

Translation: a cloud region is a cluster of data centres that a provider rents out to customers who would rather not own their own computers. Google said the trouble began in one of the region's smaller zones, europe-west2-b. Its dashboard first described "elevated errors and intermittent network failures," a phrase that in practice means the thing you wanted to use did not work.

The list of affected services was long. Revolut, Lloyds Bank and HSBC confirmed disruption, and customers also flagged problems with Royal Mail, DPD and the e-bike service Forest. HM Courts and Tribunals Service said it was aware of an issue with the video platform used for remote hearings. More than 1,500 reports about Revolut were logged on an outage monitor shortly after 08:00 BST.

The human cost was mundane and therefore familiar. Rachael Ellen, a Revolut customer in Glasgow, told the BBC she could not pay for parking at a shopping centre, and the app's help bot told her no incoming transactions were pending. Revolut said services were fully restored and that customers' money and data "remained safe throughout." Lloyds did not confirm the cause, but said it understood the root issues had been resolved.

Smaller firms felt it more sharply. Guy Baybutt of The Veg Box Company in Preston told the BBC his site was unreachable, so he could not process orders, could not book parcels onto DPD's system, and lost sales on perishable produce. He noted that small businesses cannot easily absorb lost revenue, a point that a bank with a press office and a spare data centre may find hard to appreciate.

The BBC noted the outage came exactly a year after an outage at AWS' largest cloud site knocked more than 1,000 sites offline, including UK banks. Prof Feng Li of Bayes Business School said the incident shows how deeply economies depend on "a handful of cloud providers." He said it was not yet clear what caused the power problem, and that failures in complex systems will happen. What matters, he said, is how far they spread and whether customers have alternatives.

That last point is the commercial heart of the matter. Customers choose a single provider for convenience and cost, and then discover that convenience is the thing that fails in unison. Google has promised a published analysis on its service health dashboard, so the explanation for what caused the power event should arrive shortly, probably in a document as detailed as it is calm.
