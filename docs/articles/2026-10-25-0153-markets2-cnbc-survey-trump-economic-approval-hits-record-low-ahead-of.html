Voters Name Corruption Top Worry, Narrowly Ahead of Groceries, Which Are Also Corruption

CNBC's final pre-midterm survey finds Trump's economic approval at a record low of 34% and corruption edging out food prices as the leading concern.

The wire said: Trump's approval rating on economy hits all-time low as midterm looms: CNBC survey · CNBC Economy

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 25, 2026 01:53 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

CNBC's All-America Economic Survey, run jointly by a Democratic and a Republican polling firm, questioned 1,000 registered voters Oct. 14-18, ahead of the Nov. 3 congressional elections. It covers the economy, President Trump, Congress, AI and the Iran war.

Why it matters

Voters are tying high prices to questions of government integrity, and that link could shape who controls Congress and how much economic policy gets contested after Nov. 3. Investors should watch whether the political mood shifts the odds on tariffs, spending and energy policy.

CNBC reported Thursday that President Trump's economic approval rating has fallen to 34%, with 65% disapproving, the lowest of either of his terms. That is down from 38% approval in July. Translation: net approval, meaning approval minus disapproval, is now -31, which in polling is the number that makes campaign consultants start speaking in careful, quiet voices.

Overall, 80% of respondents rate the economy as fair or poor, the highest since December 2023. Some 63% are pessimistic about the economy and its outlook, and the notable movement came from Republicans: 35% of them are now pessimistic, up 6 points since July and the highest in either Trump term. Micah Roberts, the survey's Republican pollster, said the shift happened because Republicans soured, not because Democrats or independents did.

Trump's overall approval is steadier at 40% approve and 59% disapprove, a net of -19 versus -8 a year ago. So voters are not enthusiastic about him in general, but they have reserved a special, record-setting level of disapproval for his handling of the thing they spend money on every week.

The sharper story is what voters say worries them most. A separate CNBC report found government corruption is the top issue at 36%, edging out food and groceries at 34%, with protecting democracy and gas prices at 23% each. CNBC noted a caveat: the poll lists several cost topics separately, so if prices were lumped into one category they would likely rank first. Taken together, concern about food, gas and housing is the top worry for 57%. In other words, the margin of victory for corruption is partly a matter of how the menu was printed.

Democrats have spent months arguing the two are the same problem. Sen. Jon Ossoff said in an April campaign video that corruption is why things do not work for ordinary people, and Sen. Mark Warner said Americans have spent over $100 billion extra on gas because of the Iran war. That figure is Warner's claim on X, not a number from the survey. Republicans respond that they are fighting fraud, and Trump said Tuesday his administration has waged an unprecedented war on corruption.

The price picture gives the argument some traction. According to the survey, 80% say prices are rising, up 5 points from a year ago, and 55% say they are rising faster than usual. Official data show CPI inflation at 3.74% year over year as of September, with Brent crude at $115.22 a barrel. Translation: the gas pump is doing much of the campaign messaging.

Democrats lead on corruption only narrowly overall, 35% to 31%, but the lead grows to 17 points among voters who call it their top issue, and independents favor Democrats by 15 points, though 41% of them think neither party would do well. Democrats also lead the generic ballot for Congress, 52% to 45%, a 3-point improvement since July. On food costs the Democratic advantage widened to 16 points from 7. Republicans still lead on border security and immigration, though that edge shrank to 9 points from 15.

There is one issue on which the country is almost unified: 93% said elected officials should not personally own or trade stocks in individual companies that hold federal contracts, and 89% said companies owned or backed by a president's immediate family should not receive federal contracts. It is rare for a poll to find 90% of Americans agreeing on anything, and the thing is that Congress would have to vote on it.

Both pollsters cautioned against reading too much into the numbers. Roberts called it a contest between the map and momentum, noting that a big change requires Democrats to flip many places Trump won handily two years ago. Hart's Jay Campbell said he does not see a realignment like 1994 or 2008, but a return to the mean. The poll has a margin of error of 3.1 points, and the eight competitive Senate states favor Democrats by only 4.

Markets were calm on Friday, with the VIX at 19.03 and the S&P 500 proxy SPY at 759.97. Investors seem to have decided the election is a problem for November. What to watch next is whether voters connect the dots at the ballot box, or whether the map wins.
