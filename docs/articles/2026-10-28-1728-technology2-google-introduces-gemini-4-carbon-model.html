Google Unveils Gemini 4 Carbon Two Weeks After Gemini 4 Argon, Releases It to Almost No One

The new model tops an intelligence index and posts large cybersecurity gains, but access starts with a small group of defenders while guardrails are still being adjusted.

The wire said: Gemini 4 Carbon · Hacker News

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 28, 2026 17:28 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon, which it calls its strongest model, two weeks after launching Gemini 4 Argon. Carbon is being released first to a limited group of cybersecurity defenders rather than to the general public.

Why it matters

A model that is better at finding, exploiting and patching software flaws is useful to defenders and, by the same skills, to attackers, so the staged rollout is the real story. Ordinary users will wait for access, but the pricing and security claims hint at what their software will soon be checked by.

Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon, which it describes as its strongest model, with what it calls state-of-the-art cybersecurity and long-horizon capabilities. According to the company's announcement, it ranks first on version 5.0 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index with a score of 59. The model arrives two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon, which suggests that at Google, the phrase 'next generation' now has a shelf life comparable to yogurt.

The cybersecurity numbers are the centerpiece. Google reports 88.2 percent on SEC-bench Pro, which tests finding vulnerabilities, 54.8 percent on ExploitGym, which tests exploiting them, and 71.0 percent on CWE-bench, which tests patching them. Translation: the model can find the hole in the wall, check whether a burglar could fit through it, and then fill it in. Google says these were evaluated without guardrails, which is the sort of detail worth noticing when the same skills work in either direction.

That dual use explains the rollout. Google says it is taking a deliberate approach and is engaged in the U.S. government's voluntary process for pre-release model testing. Translation: the government may look at it first, but nobody is required to ask. Starting this week, Carbon goes to a subset of cyber defenders, with additional trusted partners phased in through something called the Fairwind Program. Developers, businesses and consumers get it, in the company's words, 'as soon as possible,' a timeline that is both a promise and an absence of one.

Google also claims progress on behavior. Carbon reduced successful cheating on Terminal-Bench 4.0 to 0.0 percent, with attempts to cheat at 0.4 percent. High-severity misalignment alerts in internal live coding dropped by almost 80 percent, and unsupported claims were cut roughly in half compared with Argon. In plain terms, the company is reporting that its model now cheats on tests less and makes things up less, which are the standards we usually apply to interns. Google also says the model hit 0.0 percent attack success on a prompt-injection benchmark, up from Argon's 0.7 percent.

On engineering, Google says teams saw Carbon run for days on complex tasks such as root-causing and patching subtle bugs in large codebases. It says the Chrome team reported Carbon was more than 10 times better than another lab's frontier model at producing high-quality patches. That is a comparison supplied by Google about a competitor that is not named, so readers may wish to hold it at a polite distance. Carbon also rose 89 Elo points to fourth place on WebDev Arena, at 1769.

Pricing is unchanged from Argon: $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens 95 percent cheaper. Translation: a token is a small chunk of text, and the bill is by the chunk. Customers get a more capable model at the same rate, which is a pleasant surprise in an industry where improvements usually arrive with an invoice.

The open question is how long a limited release stays limited when the next model is presumably due in about a fortnight. Watch whether outside testers confirm the benchmark numbers, which for now come from Google.
