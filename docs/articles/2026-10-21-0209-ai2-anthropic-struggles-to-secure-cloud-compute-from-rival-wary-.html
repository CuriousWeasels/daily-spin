Anthropic Cannot Find Enough Cloud, Plans to Ask the Public for Money

A leaked S-1 shows $750 billion in possible cloud spending, an $80.7 billion half-year loss, and landlords who increasingly view their biggest tenant as a rival.

The wire said: Anthropic’s cloud partnerships are getting messy · TechCrunch AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 21, 2026 02:09 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Anthropic, maker of the Claude chatbot, is reportedly targeting a November IPO, and a draft S-1 (the document a company files with regulators before going public) has leaked to The Wall Street Journal. TechCrunch summarized its contents alongside a Bloomberg estimate of early investor Eric Schmidt's stake.

Why it matters

If Anthropic goes public, ordinary investors and index funds could end up owning a company that loses money on a vast scale and depends on suppliers who are also competitors. The filing shows how much of the AI boom rests on rented computers.

Anthropic is having trouble securing enough cloud capacity from Amazon Web Services, Google, and Microsoft, according to a leaked S-1 filing reported by The Wall Street Journal and summarized by TechCrunch. The reason, per the filing, is that those companies increasingly regard Anthropic as a competitor rather than a customer. This is a reasonable concern for a landlord to have about a tenant who is also building a rival apartment complex, and who keeps asking for more floors.

The filing adds detail to figures Reuters and the Financial Times reported in late September: Anthropic believes it could spend more than $750 billion on cloud contracts, some still under negotiation. Translation: cloud contracts are long-term commitments to rent computing power, and this one is priced like a small country's defense budget. TechCrunch notes that even this is not enough.

So Anthropic is leasing its own data centers and working with third-party operators. The company worries that those operators have a "limited operating history," which could make them less reliable than the big three. In plain English, the backup plan is a set of newer landlords with fewer references, which is the sort of risk factor lawyers insist on printing and everyone else skims.

The money side is equally direct. Anthropic reported an $80.7 billion net loss in the first half of the year on $16.3 billion in revenue, according to the WSJ, with two customers accounting for about a fifth of sales. Losing roughly five dollars for every dollar earned is a business model, though one usually described in a footnote. Customer concentration means that if either of those two customers reconsiders, a fifth of sales reconsiders with them.

There is also a liability warning. Building on earlier disclosures about catastrophic risks, the filing says Anthropic could be held liable for damages from harmful AI behavior in amounts exceeding its insurance. Translation: the company is telling prospective shareholders that some mistakes may be too large to insure, which is candid, if not exactly a selling point.

On the bright side, Anthropic last week told prospective investors that third-quarter revenue alone reached $16.8 billion, up 45% from the previous quarter, according to TechCrunch. That single quarter already exceeds the entire first half's revenue, which is the kind of chart that makes an IPO roadshow possible.

The winners in the meantime are early backers. Bloomberg reports that Eric Schmidt, the former Google CEO, owns about 1% of Anthropic after investing tens of millions in its 2021 Series A, when PitchBook data valued the startup at $461 million. That is about half the 2% held by CEO Dario Amodei. At the top-end market estimate of a $2 trillion valuation, Schmidt would gain as much as $20 billion on a net worth Bloomberg puts at $57.5 billion.

Neither Anthropic nor Schmidt responded to requests for comment. Watch the November listing, if it happens: investors will be asked to buy a company that cannot get enough computers, from suppliers who want to compete with it, at a price that assumes they eventually will.
