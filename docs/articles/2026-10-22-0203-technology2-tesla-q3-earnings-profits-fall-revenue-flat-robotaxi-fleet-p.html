Tesla Reports Flat Revenue, Shrinking Profit, and a Roadster That Briefly Left the Ground

Third-quarter revenue held at $28 billion while operating income fell 56 percent, and the company set a Q2 2027 date for letting owners put their cars into its robotaxi fleet.

The wire said: Tesla profits drop 43% in Q3 · Ars Technica

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 22, 2026 02:03 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Tesla sold fewer cars in Q3 after the $7,500 federal EV tax credit expired on September 30, 2025, and Elon Musk had already warned of 'a few rough quarters'. The company is also spending heavily to reposition itself as an AI and robotics business.

Why it matters

Tesla's valuation rests on autonomy and robotics more than on selling cars, so each earnings report is a test of whether the cash-burning future is arriving on schedule. For owners, the robotaxi date is a promise worth noting and not yet worth planning around.

Tesla reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of about $28 billion, essentially unchanged from a year earlier, according to Ars Technica and The Verge. Revenue beat Wall Street's expectation of $27.9 billion, per LSEG data cited by The Verge, but fell short of Tesla's own compiled forecast of $28.3 billion. In other words, the company outperformed the people who guess and underperformed the people who are paid to know.

Automotive revenue was $20.5 billion by The Verge's count and $20.6 billion by Ars Technica's, a 3 percent decline either way. Deliveries fell 2.1 percent from a blockbuster Q3 2025, a drop the market largely expected once the federal tax credit disappeared. Translation: customers who had planned to buy an electric car eventually bought it early, and the quarter after the discount ended is when that bill arrives.

Energy generation and storage, a business that had been growing, fell 16 percent to $2.9 billion. Services and other revenue, which covers charging, maintenance, parts and insurance, rose 33 percent to $4.6 billion. The part of Tesla that earns money after you buy the car is doing better than the part that sells you the car, which is also how many printer companies operate.

Costs are where the quarter turned unpleasant. Operating expenses rose 39 percent to $4.8 billion, and operating income fell 56 percent to $718 million, leaving an operating margin of 2.6 percent against 5.8 percent a year earlier. Translation: of every $100 in sales, Tesla kept about $2.60 after running the business, before taxes and other items.

The sources disagree on the bottom line. Ars Technica reported net profit of $787 million, down 43 percent. The Verge reported net income of $1.7 billion against $1.4 billion a year earlier, yet described that as a 4 percent drop, which does not add up on its own terms. The operating figures match across both outlets, so readers can treat the net number as unsettled until the filing is parsed more carefully.

Capital expenditures rose 110 percent to $4.7 billion as Tesla spends on AI, autonomous vehicles and robotics. Free cash flow was $1.5 billion, down 62 percent. Musk has promised a capex year of at least $25 billion, but The Verge noted Tesla had spent only $13 billion in the first three quarters, so the company may miss its own target for spending money, a rare case of restraint arriving by accident.

On the earnings call, Ashok Elluswamy, Tesla's vice president for autonomous driving, said owners could add their own cars to the robotaxi fleet around Q2 2027. The Verge noted that Musk promised this in 2019, along with a million robotaxis by 2020. Tesla currently has a few hundred Robotaxis in Texas and Florida, some with human safety monitors, and several dozen driverless Cybercabs in Austin.

There is real progress to report. Over 75 percent of new North American buyers chose Full Self-Driving (Supervised), up almost 20 points from the prior quarter, and Tesla says it has driven 3.3 million autonomous miles, up from 380,000 in July. Still, unsupervised ride-hailing needs local and state permits, insurance may be hard to find, and in most places it is illegal to run your own driverless taxi.

The quarter also included a Roadster demo in Waco, Texas, in which a prototype flew using SpaceX cold-gas thrusters. Skeptics remain unconvinced it will reach production in 2028, and Musk said special committees at Tesla and SpaceX are discussing a merger he would not be involved in. Watch whether the Q2 2027 robotaxi date holds, or whether it joins the Roadster in the category of things that are always about to leave the ground.
