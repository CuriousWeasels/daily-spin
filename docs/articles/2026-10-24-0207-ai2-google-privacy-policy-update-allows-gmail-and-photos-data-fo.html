Google Amends Promise Not to Use Your Gmail for Ads, Adds the Word 'Unless'

A October 16 privacy policy update lets data from Gmail, Drive and Photos feed ad personalization if users connect it to Google's AI features.

The wire said: Now Google says it might use your Gmail and Google Photos data to target ads · The Verge AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 24, 2026 02:07 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google has long said it would not use content from Gmail, Drive or Photos to personalize ads. Its Personal Intelligence feature, released in early 2026, lets users connect those apps to Gemini and AI search tools for more tailored answers.

Why it matters

Anyone who links their email and photos to Google's AI for better answers may now also be feeding the ad system, so the opt-in settings matter more than the old promise did.

Google updated its privacy policy on October 16, and the sentence that used to say it would not show personalized ads based on content from Drive, Gmail or Photos now has a qualifier. According to The Verge and Ars Technica, which both credited Android Authority with spotting the change, the new text adds: "unless you choose to make data from these services available to other Google services that support personalized ads." It is a short clause. Clauses of this size tend to carry a lot of luggage.

Translation: the wall between your inbox and the ad system still exists, but there is now a door in it, and Google has handed you the key and asked you to confirm that you really wanted to be handed a key.

The door is Personal Intelligence, a feature that lets Gemini and AI Mode in Search read your Gmail, Drive and Photos so its answers are more relevant to you. Ars Technica reported that while the Gemini app currently has no ads, AI search tools can show sponsored content. So if you connect your data to AI Mode, summaries and insights drawn from it can shape the answers, and the same data can inform the ads next to them.

Google disputes the framing, sort of. In a statement quoted by Ars Technica, the company said connecting apps is entirely opt-in, and that ads "will not be based directly on your Gmail inbox or Photos library." Ads will instead stay relevant to your query, the context of the response and your interests. Ars Technica's read is that this is a roundabout confirmation that the data may inform ads. The word doing the work is "directly," which is a lot like saying the soup does not directly contain the onions, only the broth that onions were in.

Some limits remain, according to The Verge. Advertisers do not get access to your Drive, Gmail or Photos content, or to details like your name or email address. Google also says it won't target ads using sensitive categories such as race, sexual orientation, religion or health.

The incentive is not mysterious. Google is the world's largest advertising company, as Ars Technica put it, and it has built an AI feature that works best when people hand over more personal data. Users get more helpful answers. Advertisers get a more useful audience. The only party asked to do any reading is the user.

Opting out is possible. You can disconnect individual apps from Search's Personal Intelligence features, or switch off Personalized Recommendations entirely through the Search Services Personalization settings page, The Verge reported.

What to watch next is whether Gemini itself gets ads, since that would put the new clause to work in the one place it currently has no job. Until then, the privacy policy has become a document you now have to read, which Google may regard as a feature.
