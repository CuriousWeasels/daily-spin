AI Buildout Has Rough Week as Lenders Discover Neoclouds Are Mostly Debt With Chips Attached

CoreWeave's default insurance costs top 11% and SK Hynix pulls Solidigm's IPO, while Nvidia, which has guaranteed much of the sector's borrowing, says demand is 'off the charts.'

The wire said: Turmoil hits AI buildout as neoclouds face pressure, credit risks mount and Solidigm pulls IPO · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 25, 2026 15:06 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Two years of record AI spending have been funded increasingly by debt, with Nvidia backing smaller 'neocloud' data center firms and OpenAI projects through guarantees and investments. This week, neocloud shares and credit prices fell hard, and several listings were pulled or stalled.

Why it matters

If the companies renting out AI computing cannot borrow cheaply, the buildout slows and the guarantees behind it get tested. Because AI stocks make up a large share of the Nasdaq, retirement accounts and index funds are exposed to the outcome.

CNBC reported that the AI infrastructure trade had a rough week. The Nasdaq fell again on Friday to finish down 0.7%, Alphabet slid 6% ahead of its earnings, and shares of neoclouds CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN dropped to losses of well over 30% each for October. Translation: a neocloud is a company that rents out AI computing power, usually bought with borrowed money, to customers who also need to borrow money.

The credit market was less polite than the stock market. CoreWeave's credit default swap spread, which is the price of insurance against the company failing to repay, widened past 11%, a level CNBC says is generally viewed as distress. Yields on its 2031 and 2032 unsecured notes topped 15%, which is what lenders charge when they would like to be paid for worrying. CoreWeave carries roughly $51.6 billion in total debt, according to CNBC.

The wobble was not limited to small names. Meta fell 7% after reporting negative free cash flow of over $2 billion in the third quarter, meaning it spent more than it took in, while raising the floor of its capital spending forecast to $135 billion from $130 billion. It also disclosed about $825 billion in planned lease and contract commitments, more than $450 billion of them non-cancellable take-or-pay agreements. Translation: take-or-pay means you pay whether or not you end up needing the thing.

Meta's five-year CDS spread hit a record above 124 basis points, with Nvidia at 126 and Oracle at 343, also records. A basis point is one hundredth of a percentage point, so these are small numbers that investors treat as big news. Milbank derivatives lawyer John Williams said hedging is appropriate whether investors are bullish or bearish, which is a polite way of saying nobody wants to be the only one without insurance.

Abroad, Elice Group, a South Korean cloud provider, fell 30% in its market debut, and Australia's NextDC dropped 39.5% after cutting its earnings forecast. SK Hynix then withdrew the planned IPO of its Solidigm storage unit, citing market conditions and share price trends. Nvidia-backed Firmus also pulled its offering this month, and Nscale and SB Energy have not updated their September filings in weeks, according to CNBC.

This matters for Nvidia because it has become the sector's financier as well as its supplier. CNBC reported $36 billion in off-balance-sheet backstops for neoclouds as of July, a guarantee of up to $105 billion tied to a proposed OpenAI data center in Ohio, and a commitment to buy up to $6.3 billion of CoreWeave's compute if no one else will. Translation: Nvidia sells the chips, helps customers borrow to buy them, and promises lenders it will cover some of the gap if things go badly. For most deals it pays only if participants default.

Jensen Huang remains unbothered. He told CNBC that demand for neoclouds is 'off the charts' and that the missing piece is working capital. That may be true, though it is also the sort of sentence a chip seller would say, and a chip seller's main customer base is now people who cannot buy chips without a loan the chip seller helps arrange. Gartner, for its part, predicts 80% of neoclouds will shut down or be absorbed by 2030.

Nvidia declined to comment. Its stock is down 7.6% in October, on pace for its worst month since November of last year. Investors will watch Alphabet, Microsoft and Amazon earnings next week for signs of whether the spending continues. As Equity Armor's Brian Stutland asked, can you drop a bowling ball in one end of the pool and expect no waves at the other?
