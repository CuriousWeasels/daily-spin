Stellantis Shares Jump 12% After Losing Slightly Less Cash Than Feared

The Chrysler and Jeep owner posted a 1% revenue gain and reaffirmed its 2026 guidance, which on a bad year counts as a triumph.

The wire said: Stellantis stock rises 12% as automaker's turnaround plan, cost cuts gain traction · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 28, 2026 17:28 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Stellantis, the European automaker behind Chrysler and Jeep, announced a 60 billion euro turnaround plan in May under CEO Antonio Filosa. On Wednesday it reported third-quarter results, its latest check on whether that plan is working.

Why it matters

Investors are watching whether legacy automakers can fix their finances while Chinese rivals take market share. If Stellantis hits its cash-flow target, it suggests the turnaround is real; if not, the 60 billion euros starts to look like a very large bet.

Stellantis shares rose roughly 9% in Milan on Wednesday and were up 12% in early New York trading, after the company reported third-quarter revenue of 37.6 billion euros, up 1% from a year earlier, according to CNBC. Adjusted operating income rose 184% to 256 million euros from 90 million. The company, in other words, grew sales by roughly the amount a rounding error would, and investors responded as if it had found oil.

The 184% figure deserves context. Translation: when profit starts from 90 million euros on a business that size, almost any improvement looks heroic in percentage terms. Industrial free cash flow, meaning the cash the car-making business generates after paying for its operations and investments, improved to negative 4 billion euros from negative 4.3 billion. That is still negative 4 billion euros, but it is a more tasteful negative.

The real news was what Stellantis did not do. It reconfirmed its 2026 guidance, which includes a mid-single-digit increase in net revenue, a low-single-digit adjusted operating margin and a target of positive free cash flow next year. RBC Capital Markets analyst Tom Narayan wrote that the third quarter was soft but expectations were far lower, and that guidance held while other automakers and suppliers are cutting on elevated costs. Translation: the bar was on the floor, and the company stepped over it.

Filosa told investors the company is ahead of schedule on the plan and expects to beat its earlier 6 billion euro cost-cutting target by 2028, including 2.4 billion euros to start 2027. Pressed on whether the cash-flow target is realistic, he said, "We are very confident in reaffirming our '26 guidance." Investors have heard confident statements before, but this one came with a number attached, which is the preferred format.

The longer plan involves investing 36 billion euros across its brands, with 60% going to the struggling North American business. It also aims to lift revenue about 23%, from 154 billion euros last year to 190 billion euros by 2030, with a 7% adjusted operating margin. Today's margin target is low single digits, so the company is promising to roughly double or triple its profitability while selling more cars in a market that is not asking for them.

The backdrop is China, where CNBC noted Xiaomi, BYD and Geely are gaining ground against European brands. Porsche said this week it would reduce its China exposure, and Mercedes-Benz blamed a slump in third-quarter sales there on a challenging market environment. Filosa, for his part, has asked European regulators to reward carmakers that actually design and engineer cars in the region, a request that is both a policy position and a polite description of the competition.

The stock's jump should be kept in proportion. CNBC noted it remains sharply down since the start of the year, so Wednesday's rally recovers some ground rather than reaching new territory. The next test is whether a company that is burning 4 billion euros can turn cash-positive in 2027, a promise with a very clear deadline.
