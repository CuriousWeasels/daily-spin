Trump Pauses Russia Sanctions for 60 Days to Keep Moscow Talking, Study Economic Damage

Two days before penalties were due to take effect, the president suspended them, while Russia hit Ukraine's power grid and Kyiv warned of a winter blackout.

The wire said: Trump says suspension of Russia sanctions is to keep peace negotiations going · BBC World

By The Daily Spin Desk · GeopoliticsOct 19, 2026 01:58 UTC · 3 min read

Catch up

Congress passed, and Trump signed in September, a package of sanctions and tariffs aimed at Russia's energy revenues. It was due to take effect on a Sunday, and on the Friday night before, Trump suspended it.

Why it matters

Sanctions are the main non-military pressure on Russia's war budget, and pausing them changes the incentives of every party in the talks. For ordinary people, the story links Ukrainian winter heating, US energy prices and the Iran war in one ledger.

US President Donald Trump blocked a set of Russia sanctions on Friday night, two days before they were due to take effect, according to the BBC. He said on Truth Social that he is negotiating peace and did not want to "foolishly" drive Russia away from the bargaining table. The sanctions, it should be noted, were passed by Congress and signed into law by Trump himself in September, so the president has paused a law he signed, in order to protect a negotiation.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio described the move as a 60-day pause, during which officials would analyse the potential impact of the sanctions on the US and global economies. Translation: the penalties designed to hurt Russia's economy are being reviewed for whether they hurt everyone else's too. Kirill Dmitriev, a Russian presidential envoy, called the suspension "an important and truly historic moment" and said the measures would have caused "serious damage" to the US economy and global markets. When the sanctioned party and the sanctioning party agree the sanctions are painful, the disagreement is mostly over who should feel it.

The context is a deteriorating relationship with Kyiv. The BBC reported that ties frayed after the US struck a deal to import Russian diesel, a move meant to lower US energy prices that have skyrocketed during the Iran war. Ukraine and its European allies criticised it, and Volodymyr Zelensky said it amounted to the US investing in Russia's war effort. Trump, for his part, said Zelensky was "more difficult to deal with" than Putin, and wrote that Zelensky "doesn't want to end this war, and probably never did." Zelensky has said repeatedly that it is Putin who does not want peace.

Sanctions have never fully stopped Russian oil from leaving the country. According to the think-tank CREA, cited by the BBC, China has taken about half of Russia's crude exports and India about 37 percent, often at attractive discounts. Translation: the oil still sells, just to fewer customers who negotiate harder. The pause matters because this package targeted energy revenues, the part of the budget that pays for the war.

Meanwhile the war did not pause. DW reported that Zelensky said Russia carried out one of its biggest attacks on Ukraine's energy sector since the full-scale invasion began in February 2022, with more than 15 critical power generation and transmission facilities targeted. He said Russia was trying "to leave millions of our people without electricity and heating" before winter and urged allies to apply pressure. Russia's Defense Ministry said it struck infrastructure targets, including a substation in Zhytomyr and steelworks in Kryvyi Rih and Zaporizhzhia, which it said supplied the Ukrainian military. Ukrainian authorities said an ambulance driver was killed and 19 people, including a child, were injured in a Russian strike on Kherson. TASS reported that one woman died in Ukrainian drone strikes in Russia's Belgorod region.

Zelensky said a "US sanctions blackout" and financial rewards for Putin only encourage him to keep attacking, and told NBC News that siding with Putin would be a "historic mistake." He also said he expects the Trump administration to lift more sanctions. The BBC added that concerns have been heightened by disruption to Starlink, the US-provided satellite system Ukraine uses. Zelensky said US officials blamed technical issues, but that Ukraine tested it in EU territory and found faster speeds. Trump has denied deliberately slowing the network and denied that the US stopped sharing defence intelligence.

The 60-day clock is now the thing to watch. If talks produce something, the pause will look like patience. If they do not, it will have given Moscow two months of reduced pressure, which Moscow will describe as historic, and which Kyiv will be spending in the dark.
