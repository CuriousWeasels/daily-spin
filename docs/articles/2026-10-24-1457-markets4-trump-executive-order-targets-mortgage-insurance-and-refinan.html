Trump Order Targets Mortgage Insurance, Hopes 7.32% Rate Will Look Smaller From Far Away

An executive order signed less than two weeks before the midterms would cancel mortgage insurance automatically on some future loans and make cheaper refinancing permanent.

The wire said: Trump's executive order targets mortgage insurance, refinance costs. What it means for borrowers · CNBC Markets

By The Daily Spin Desk · Markets & EconomyOct 24, 2026 14:57 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

President Trump signed an order titled "Decreasing Housing Costs for the American People" as rising rates and inflation squeeze buyers. It follows earlier orders, including one restricting Wall Street firms from buying single-family homes.

Why it matters

The changes could trim a few hundred dollars from some borrowers' costs, but the biggest drivers of housing affordability, home prices and mortgage rates, are untouched. Analysts quoted by CNBC say the full effect is hard to judge without more details.

President Trump has signed an executive order directing federal agencies to cancel mortgage insurance "proactively" on eligible future loans and to make a cheaper refinancing program permanent, CNBC reported. The order arrives less than two weeks before the Nov. 3 midterm elections, at a time when housing costs rank high among voter concerns. Timing, as ever, is a matter of coincidence.

Start with the insurance. Translation: private mortgage insurance, or PMI, is a fee borrowers pay to protect the lender, not themselves, when they put down less than 20% of a home's price. Currently, borrowers can generally ask to drop it once their balance falls to 80% of the original value, and servicers may remove it automatically at 78%. The order tells the Federal Housing Finance Agency to build a pilot program that cancels it without being asked once equity hits a certain threshold. It would apply only to mortgages originated after the pilot launches, so anyone currently paying is invited to admire it from the sidewalk.

The stakes depend on how much PMI costs. Fannie Mae puts it at 0.2% to 2% of the loan annually, depending on down payment, credit score and other factors. Freddie Mac's calculator, per CNBC, shows about $351 a month on a $600,000 home with 10% down and a 7.3% 30-year mortgage. FHFA director Bill Pulte said on X that "millions of Americans will save thousands and thousands of dollars." Keith Gumbinger of mortgage site HSH said it is hard to know the consumer impact without more details.

The industry's reaction was gracious. Seth Appleton of the mortgage insurers' trade group said it commends the focus on lowering costs and that the industry is "already delivering on that goal." Translation: the people who collect the fee are pleased that someone is looking into the fee.

The second piece concerns refinancing. The order makes permanent an FHFA pilot that waives the lender's title insurance policy, which protects the lender against disputes over who actually owns the house, and allows cheaper alternatives. Title-related charges are generally the largest part of closing costs, according to a 2025 Urban Institute report. Former FHFA director Sandra Thompson estimated in 2024 that borrowers could save $500 to $1,500 on loans under the pilot, which was limited to certain states and loans with a loan-to-value ratio below 80%.

Gumbinger was measured about the whole package. "I don't know that it's necessarily a game-changer," he said, noting that housing costs are dictated by home prices and mortgage rates. "Every little bit certainly helps," he added.

The rates are the larger problem. Freddie Mac put the 30-year fixed rate at 7.32% as of Oct. 23, and refinance demand was down 59% in mid-October from a year earlier. The 10-year Treasury yield, which mortgage rates tend to follow, was 4.96% on Oct. 22, and CPI inflation was 3.74% in September. The Federal Reserve's latest survey found the monthly payment on a median-priced home took about 33% of median family income in 2025, up from 26% in 2022.

So the order shaves the edges of a cost that is mostly set elsewhere. Watch for the FHFA's pilot details, including the equity threshold and the start date, since those will show whether this is a savings plan or a press release with a closing date.
