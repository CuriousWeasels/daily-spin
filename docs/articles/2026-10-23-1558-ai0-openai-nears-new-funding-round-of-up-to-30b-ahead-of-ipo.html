OpenAI Seeks $30 Billion at Slightly Smaller Trillion-Dollar Price Tag

The ChatGPT maker is reportedly trimming its valuation target from $1.4 trillion to $1.25 trillion as it raises a private round it describes as a bridge to an IPO.

The wire said: OpenAI working on new $30B raise at $1.25T valuation ahead of IPO · TechCrunch AI

By The Daily Spin Desk · AIOct 23, 2026 15:58 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

OpenAI filed confidential IPO paperwork in June and last raised money in March at an $852 billion post-money valuation. It is now reportedly raising up to $30 billion more, led by Coatue and UAE investors, while rival Anthropic prepares its own listing.

Why it matters

A record private valuation sets the benchmark that public-market investors, and eventually retail shareholders and index funds, will be asked to accept. If demand is softening even at these prices, the AI funding boom may be less bottomless than the headlines suggest.

OpenAI is reportedly close to raising up to $30 billion at a $1.25 trillion pre-money valuation, according to CNBC, with Coatue and investors in the UAE leading the round. Terms are still being negotiated, and OpenAI declined to comment. It is a rare case of a company being described as lowering its expectations while asking for the gross domestic product of a mid-sized country.

Translation: pre-money valuation is what a company is deemed to be worth before the new cash arrives. Post-money is that figure plus the cash. At $1.25 trillion pre-money, the company would be worth roughly $1.28 trillion once the $30 billion lands, assuming the full amount is raised. Rounding errors at this scale are larger than most startups.

The lowered figure is the interesting part. Bloomberg reported a month earlier that OpenAI was working on $30 billion at $1.4 trillion, and the Financial Times, citing internal documents, said that was the original pitch to investors. The Decoder, summarizing the FT, said demand was weaker than in earlier rounds and that some backers were experiencing what the paper called "indigestion" after heavy investments in previous fundraises. Translation: the people who already bought a great deal of OpenAI are less hungry for a second helping, particularly at a higher price.

Even so, a valuation above $1 trillion would make OpenAI the most valuable private company ever, according to TechCrunch. The round is being positioned as a bridge to an IPO, which is a financing term meaning money to keep you going until the money you actually want arrives. CNBC also reported that the UAE funding could come with a deal to secure more computing capacity for OpenAI from the UAE. In other words, some of the investment may return to the neighborhood as a purchase order for chips and data centers.

The IPO timeline is, charitably, flexible. OpenAI confidentially filed a prospectus with the SEC in June. The Decoder reports that CFO Sarah Friar told employees in August that the company would be public by 2027, while CEO Sam Altman said in September that the timing was unfavorable for going public, a view he had already shared internally in June. Both statements can be true if you squint at the calendar, and a bridge round is a convenient place to do the squinting.

Then there is Anthropic. It raised $65 billion at a $965 billion valuation in May, which many had assumed would be its last big round before listing, and it also filed confidentially in June. CNBC reports it is now seeking a $1.5 to $2 trillion valuation for its public debut, and TechCrunch notes that its public S-1 filing could arrive any day. Whichever company goes first will help set the price tag the other has to justify, which makes the race to go public partly a race to avoid being the comparison.

What to watch next: whether the final number lands at $1.25 trillion or slips again, and whether Anthropic's filing shows what a public-market investor is actually being asked to pay for. A private valuation is a negotiation among friends. An S-1 is where the numbers have to show up in writing.
