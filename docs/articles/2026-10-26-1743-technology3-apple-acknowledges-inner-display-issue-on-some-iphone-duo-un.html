Apple Acknowledges 'Issue' With iPhone Duo Screens, Declines to Say How Many 'Small Number' Is

Apple says a small number of foldables have cracked inner displays and tells owners to call AppleCare, a month into a new CEO's tenure.

The wire said: Apple acknowledges iPhone Duo display ‘issue’ after reports of cracked screens · The Verge

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 26, 2026 17:43 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Apple's iPhone Duo, a foldable phone, launched recently as one of the first major products since John Ternus succeeded Tim Cook as CEO. Users soon began posting photos of inner screens that cracked or showed a bright line after the hinge was opened.

Why it matters

Anyone who paid foldable-phone prices should know that Apple is offering replacements through AppleCare, and that early reports point to a possible design flaw rather than user error. It also tests whether a new CEO's first big hardware launch can avoid an old Apple pastime: the quiet recall-shaped statement.

Apple has confirmed that it is aware of "an issue impacting the inner display" on a "small number of iPhone Duo units," according to statements the company gave Bloomberg and 9to5Mac, as reported by The Verge. Owners of affected phones are advised to contact AppleCare for a replacement. The statement did not say how many units are in the small number, which is how small numbers usually stay small.

The reported symptoms are specific. Users described a hinge that tightened, followed by a crack when the phone was opened, and a distinctive bright line running from the center of the display to the right side. Translation: the part of the phone that exists to fold is the part that is breaking when folded.

Apple's handling has varied by location, according to the reports. One user in Australia said an Apple Store replaced their unit "on the spot," which suggests that at least some employees have been given the authority to treat the problem as real. The Verge said it contacted Apple and had not received an on-the-record response of its own, so the company's position on the cause currently comes from statements to other outlets.

Then there is iFixit, the repair site, which published a teardown of the phone over the weekend. In cases where damage occurred, iFixit showed how the foldable screen might be pushing against a center bar. The source describes this as a possible explanation, not a confirmed diagnosis, and Apple has not said what causes the cracks. A teardown is essentially an autopsy performed by enthusiasts with small screwdrivers, and it has a way of making official statements look brief.

The timing is awkward. These are among the first devices to launch since John Ternus took over from Tim Cook, so the company's reaction will be read as an early indicator of his style. Apple's usual approach to hardware trouble is to acknowledge a narrow problem, offer a narrow fix, and avoid any word that sounds like a recall. A replacement program fits that pattern neatly.

What to watch next is whether the small number grows, whether Apple explains the cause, and whether replacement units fix the problem or simply restart the clock on it. A replaced phone with the same hinge is, after all, the same phone with a fresh opportunity.
