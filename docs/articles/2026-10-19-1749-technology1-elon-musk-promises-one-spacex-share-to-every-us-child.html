Musk to Give Every American Child One SpaceX Share, Details to Follow

The pledge, worth roughly $10.5 billion by CNBC's count, arrives as Anthropic sets aside 5% of its shares for the public good and AI firms face scrutiny.

The wire said: Elon Musk promises to give one SpaceX share to every child in the US · The Verge

Catch up

Elon Musk said on Monday he will give one SpaceX share to every child in the US next year. The post followed Anthropic's announcement that it would commit 5% of its shares to a public benefit fund.

Why it matters

If carried out, roughly 72 million children would get a stake in a newly listed company, but the mechanics are undisclosed. The pledges also show AI and space companies trying to answer public worry about the technology's effect on jobs and society.

Elon Musk said on Monday that next year he will give one of his SpaceX shares to every child in America. In a post on X he wrote that this "will be the first of many." According to CNBC, as cited by The Verge, there were about 72 million US children as of July 2025, which puts the total at around $10.5 billion in shares. The BBC reports that details of how each child would actually receive a share have not been disclosed.

Translation: the headline number is settled, but the delivery method is not. Seventy-two million recipients, many of whom cannot yet read a brokerage statement, is the sort of logistics problem that a press release does not solve and a post on X does not either.

The timing matters. Musk's post followed Anthropic's announcement on Sunday that it would commit 5% of its shares to a Public Benefit Fund that makes donations for the "public good", with another 2% possible at its board's discretion. Anthropic said none of the fund's shares would be used for lobbying or political donations. Musk replied to that news with: "I recommend giving the shares directly to the public. This is what I will be doing."

Both pledges arrive as the BBC describes Big Tech as under pressure over AI's possible effect on humanity. The IMF said in 2024 that almost 40% of jobs worldwide will be affected by AI, rising to as much as 60% in advanced economies. Companies, mostly in tech, have cut thousands of jobs after investing in AI. A share handed to every child is, among other things, a way of saying the gains will be widely shared, shortly after the gains arrive somewhere else.

The money involved is large on both sides. SpaceX set the record for the biggest IPO in history in June, raising $85.7bn, according to the BBC. Anthropic is preparing a possible listing that could value it above $1tn, and told prospective investors that revenue rose 45% to $16.8bn in the three months to September. Translation: an IPO is when a private company sells shares to the public. Giving some away beforehand is a reasonably inexpensive way to be remembered fondly while doing so.

The Verge notes that the giveaway follows similar moves toward Trump Accounts, the tax-advantaged investment accounts for American children. SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell said she would give SpaceX shares to 2 million of them. Dell's founder put $6.25 billion into the accounts, and Micron announced $250 million. The Verge also notes Musk gave $158 million to the Senate Leadership Fund PAC in September, according to CBS News, plus $76 million to America PAC. A super PAC is a political committee that can raise unlimited sums, which makes the Verge's juxtaposition of generosity and electioneering hard to miss.

Whether a single share changes anyone's life is a different question. At roughly $146 each by the CNBC arithmetic, it is a modest gift per child and a very large one in aggregate, which is the sort of ratio press coverage tends to reward. What to watch next: whether the shares arrive through Trump Accounts, some other vehicle, or a follow-up post.
