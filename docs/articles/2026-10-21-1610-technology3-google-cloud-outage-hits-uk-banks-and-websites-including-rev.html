Google's London Data Centre Loses Power, Taking Banks, Couriers and Courts With It

A 'power event' at one zone of Google Cloud's UK region disrupted Revolut, Lloyds, HSBC, Royal Mail and remote court hearings before services were restored Tuesday evening.

The wire said: Google resolves cloud outage affecting UK banks and websites · BBC Technology

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 21, 2026 16:10 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Google Cloud runs a London region called europe-west2, which hosts services for many UK companies. On Tuesday morning it reported elevated errors and intermittent network failures, which spread to well-known consumer and business services.

Why it matters

When a few cloud providers carry much of the internet, one failed power system can stop people paying for parking, shipping vegetables or attending court hearings. Businesses, especially small ones, are left to decide whether backup plans are worth the cost before the next outage rather than after it.

Google Cloud said on Tuesday that it had resolved a disruption at its UK cloud site that knocked out services for banks, businesses and the courts, according to the BBC. The company said the cause was a 'power event' at a third-party facility in its europe-west2 region. Translation: somewhere in London, the electricity did something it was not supposed to do, and a surprising amount of the country noticed.

The BBC reported that Revolut, Lloyds Bank and HSBC were among the services that confirmed disruption on Tuesday morning. Royal Mail, DPD and the e-bike service Forest also drew complaints, and HM Courts and Tribunals Service said it was aware of a problem with the video platform used for remote hearings. More than 1,500 reports about Revolut were logged on an outage monitor shortly after 08:00 BST.

A 'region,' in cloud terminology, is a cluster of data centres in one geographic area that a provider like Google, Amazon or Microsoft rents out to customers. Google's dashboard said the trouble came from a 'power event' in one of the region's smaller zones, europe-west2-b. Translation: the problem started in one room of the building, and the building's customers discovered they had all been living in the same room.

The human cost was ordinary and irritating. Rachael Ellen, a Revolut customer in Glasgow, told the BBC she could not pay for parking because her account was not working, and that the app's help bot reported no pending incoming transactions when a relative had sent money. Revolut said services were fully restored and that customers' money and data 'remained safe throughout.' Lloyds did not confirm the cause of its problems, but said it understood the root issues had been resolved.

Small businesses took a different kind of hit. Guy Baybutt of The Veg Box Company in Preston told the BBC his firm could not access its website to process orders, could not book parcels onto DPD's system, and lost sales while customers could not order. Perishable produce, he noted, does not wait for the cloud to reboot, and a small firm cannot easily absorb lost revenue the way a larger company can.

Google apologised, thanked customers for their patience, and promised to publish an analysis of the incident on its service health dashboard. The BBC noted the outage came exactly a year after an outage at Amazon Web Services' largest cloud site knocked more than 1,000 sites, including UK banks, offline for hours. Prof Feng Li of Bayes Business School said the outage was a reminder of how deeply economies depend on a handful of providers, adding that it was not yet clear what caused the power problem.

His other point is the practical one: failures will happen, and what matters is how far they spread, how fast services recover and whether customers have workable alternatives. For now, the main alternative on offer appears to be waiting for the post-incident analysis, which will arrive on the same dashboard that customers use to check whether the cloud is working.
