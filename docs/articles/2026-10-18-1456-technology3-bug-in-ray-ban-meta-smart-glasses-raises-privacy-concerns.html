Meta Says Glasses That Recorded Without Being Asked Have Been Fixed, Declines to Say What Was Wrong

Some Ray-Ban Meta users reported audio being captured without the "Hey, Meta" phrase, and the company says the issue is resolved while leaving the cause and scale unexplained.

The wire said: Bug turns one of the biggest fears about Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses into reality · Ars Technica

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 18, 2026 14:56 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses have a microphone, a camera and an AI assistant that is meant to wake only when the wearer says "Hey, Meta." This week, users reported online that some pairs were recording conversations anyway.

Why it matters

Anyone wearing a device with an always-available microphone is, in effect, asking the people around them to trust a firmware update. A bug that records without consent shows how much of that trust rests on the company's word.

Some Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses were recording conversations without the wearer knowing, and without anyone saying the wake phrase "Hey, Meta," according to Ars Technica. Meta says the bug has been fixed. This is the standard sequence in consumer technology: the problem is discovered by customers, then resolved by the company, and the interval between the two is described as nothing.

The reports surfaced this week. A Reddit user going by Kindly-Audience-137 said it happened across three pairs of Meta Ray-Bans, even with the "Respond without Hey Meta" setting turned off. Translation: the user had specifically told the glasses not to listen unprompted, and the glasses took this as a suggestion. The user said the Meta AI app saved audio recordings and text transcripts of the conversations in their conversation history.

The same user wrote that customers "should not have to wonder" whether private conversations are being captured when they haven't activated the assistant. It is a modest request, and notably one that the product's own settings menu appeared to be making on the user's behalf.

Android Authority reported the discussions on Tuesday and later that day published a statement from Meta: "This issue has been resolved." The company added that people can manage, review and delete voice logs of their interactions with Meta AI in settings at any time. Translation: if your glasses recorded something you didn't mean them to, you are welcome to go and tidy up after them.

What Meta has not said is more interesting than what it has. It has not explained what caused the recordings. The Reddit user said they first noticed the behavior after a recent firmware update, but Meta has not confirmed whether a faulty update was responsible. Firmware, for the uninitiated, is the software that lives inside a gadget, and is updated automatically on the theory that more software fixes things.

Nor is it known how many pairs were affected. There are only a few reports online, and Android Authority said it could not replicate the problem before Meta announced the fix. This leaves the public with an unverifiable bug and an unverifiable repair, which is a tidy symmetry. Ars Technica said it asked Meta how many devices were affected and what it is doing to prevent a repeat, and would update if it heard back.

The episode lands on ground already crowded with worry. Privacy advocates had raised concerns about Meta's glasses and those of other vendors before this week, partly because the glasses look inconspicuous, making it easier for someone to record another person in secret. A bug that records the wearer's conversations by accident is, in a sense, an equal-opportunity version of that fear.

Watch for whether Meta answers Ars Technica's questions. A fix that arrives the same day as the complaint is reassuring, but only if someone eventually says what was broken.
