EA Closes BioWare, Citing Desire to Align Talent With Opportunity by Removing Talent

The studio behind Mass Effect and Dragon Age is shut after more than three decades, as its newly private, debt-laden owner trims 400 jobs company-wide.

The wire said: RIP BioWare: EA shuts down storied RPG studio after over three decades · Ars Technica

By The Daily Spin Desk · TechnologyOct 22, 2026 16:08 UTC · 2 min read

Catch up

BioWare, founded in Edmonton in 1995, was bought by Electronic Arts in 2007 and went on to make Mass Effect, Dragon Age and Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic. EA was taken private last year in a $55 billion deal led by Silver Lake, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund and Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners.

Why it matters

Players of long-running story-driven series lose a studio and the next Mass Effect, while the closure shows what private ownership with heavy debt can mean for creative teams. It also hints at where the money is expected to come from: sports franchises, live services and microtransactions.

Electronic Arts has closed BioWare, the studio behind Mass Effect, Dragon Age, Neverwinter Nights and Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic, according to Ars Technica. BioWare announced the news on social media Wednesday afternoon, saying that "after more than three decades of shared adventures, BioWare is closing and early development on the next Mass Effect project is ending." It is rare for a company to describe a shutdown as an adventure that has concluded, but it is a genre BioWare knows.

The announcement landed minutes after Bloomberg reported 400 job cuts across EA, including 50 at BioWare, citing a person familiar with the company's plans. Translation: the leak arrived first, and the official statement followed to confirm that the leak had been modest. Ars did not say what happens to the remaining staff beyond the 50 named in that report.

EA executives, according to Bloomberg, told staff the company is making "deliberate choices to align talent and investments where we see the greatest opportunities for players and long-term growth." Translation: talent will be aligned with opportunity, and where no opportunity can be found, the talent will be aligned with the exit. In this case the opportunity was apparently not Mass Effect.

The history makes the corporate phrasing more vivid. EA bought BioWare in 2007, before Mass Effect and Dragon Age had become what they are now. Then-CEO John Riccitiello called it one of "the most respected studios in the industry" and said the deal would help "drive long-term value for our shareholders." That was a reasonable thing to say at the time.

Those shareholders are now gone. EA went private last year in a $55 billion deal led by Silver Lake, Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund and Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners. Ars reports that analysts warned then to expect layoffs, studio closures and sales of intellectual property, since the company was now heavily in debt and would lean harder on live services, annual sports franchises and microtransactions.

The mechanism is not mysterious. A buyout of this size is typically financed in part with borrowed money, and the debt belongs to the company, not the buyers. Interest must be paid whether or not anyone finishes a thirty-hour role-playing game, a product that takes years, costs a great deal and cannot be sold again every August with updated rosters.

For fans, the practical result is that a franchise with a devoted following loses its original home just as early development on its next installment stops. For EA's owners, the bet is that players will spend more on games that can be monetized continuously. Whether that bet pays off will be visible in future earnings, though now that the company is private, fewer people will be shown them.
